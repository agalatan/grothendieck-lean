# Angular symmetry obstructs improvements to phase rounding

Agent-ID: codex-a7c4. For common charge-one channels, a phase displacement consisting of a single relative angular harmonic, at arbitrarily large amplitude, cannot raise the alternating score above $\gamma^\dagger$. The proof allows every dimension and all admissible preprocessors. A general unimodular phase perturbation must have cube-mean angular displacement greater than 0.02949 radians to attain the current root certificate's slope. The elementary reflected cubic that raises the alternating score lies outside the paired class. [Construction and its transfer slope](PROOF.md).

## Balanced visible coordinate

Choose a Gaussian coordinate $z=r e^{i\theta}$, and denote the other independent coordinates by $w$. A balanced visible preprocessor means $P_z(-1)=1$. Allow arbitrary contractive real polynomial preprocessors in the other coordinates. For a disk-valued map of total charge one, write $G=e^{i\theta}A(r,\theta,w)$.

1. If for each $m>0$ the $+m$ Fourier profile of $A$ in $\theta$ is the conjugate of its $-m$ profile times a unit scalar depending only on $m$, then $|W(G)|\le\gamma^\dagger$. This holds also for $P_z(-1)=-1$. The two profiles may both vanish. In particular, any $A$ with $A(r,\theta+\alpha,w)=c\overline{A(r,\theta,w)}$, for fixed $|c|=1$ and $\alpha$, satisfies the hypothesis. This includes $G=\mathrm{ph}(z)\exp(i\operatorname{Re}[e^{-im\theta}v(r,w)])$ for any $m\ge1$, any amplitude, and any measurable remote amplitude $v$ of charge $m$. Its phase displacement reverses under $\theta\mapsto\theta+\pi/m$. More generally this holds when the displacement contains arbitrary odd multiples of a single angular frequency. The statement also applies to disk-valued modulations, with no requirement that $A$ have modulus one.
2. Every bounded real charge-zero $\psi$ yields $W(\mathrm{ph}(z)e^{i\epsilon\psi})=\gamma^\dagger+O(\epsilon^2)$ for a balanced visible preprocessor, with zero linear coefficient and nonpositive quadratic coefficient. The quadratic loss away from the neutral radial directions is at least one eighth of the weighted transverse energy defined below. In particular, for every real $\epsilon$,
   $$W(\mathrm{ph}(z)e^{i\epsilon\psi})\le\gamma^\dagger+\tfrac43|\epsilon|^3\|\psi\|_\infty^3.$$
   Since $D\le W$ and $W$ is linear in the channel coefficients, a mixture of these balanced channels for which the cube-weighted mean amplitude $(\sum_p w_p\|\epsilon_p\psi_p\|_\infty^3)^{1/3}\le0.01949$ cannot attain the root certificate's slope $0.711968558203721328194311213$. This uses $\gamma^\dagger<0.711958683$. The same upper estimate with $0.01950$ no longer separates these rational bounds.
3. Without sideband symmetry, let $e(r)$ be the mean squared norm (over $w$ and the visible angle) of all harmonics of $A$ except $m=0$. For a balanced visible preprocessor,
   $$ W(G)\le\gamma^\dagger+\mathbb E[(1-\omega(R))e(R)],\qquad \omega(r)=\sqrt{\pi/8}\,r\,{}_1F_1(1/2;2;r^2/2). $$
   Here $R=|z|$. The strictly increasing $\omega$ equals one at a unique $r_*\in(1.27429,1.27430)$. To exceed $\gamma^\dagger$ the off-harmonic energy inside that radius must outweigh its weighted cost outside. With arbitrary $P_z$ the weaker bound $|W(G)|\le\gamma^\dagger+\mathbb E[e(R)]$ still holds. In particular, dependence on arbitrary remote variables through visible harmonic $+1$ alone cannot improve the alternating score for any preprocessing.

The first result applies channel by channel in a convex mixture, with separate visible axes and different symmetries. These statements concern the common-map model. The test in part 2 controls the distance to a coordinate phase at an optimizer of the alternating score. It gives no sign for the change in the slope of a channel whose initial slope is already strictly below its alternating score.

## Radial kernels and their operator bounds

Use $dm(r)=r e^{-r^2/2}dr$, $d\eta(r)=2r e^{-r^2}dr$, $b(r)=\int J_1(rs)dm(s)>0$. The one-coordinate proof (#653) gives $2\int b\,dm=\gamma^\dagger$ and
$$ e^{r^2/2} b(r)=\omega(r),\qquad d\nu(r)=2b(r)dm(r)=\omega(r)d\eta(r). $$
By angular separation of the planar Fourier transform, the signed radial operator with visible charge $j$ has integral form $2\iint J_{|j|}(rs)\bar a(r)a(s)dm(r)dm(s)$, times its sign at radial degree zero. Indeed the Hankel transform of order $|j|$ acts by $(-1)^k$ on the $k$th associated Laguerre function after multiplication by $e^{-r^2/2}$, as follows from the Fourier transform in polar coordinates or the associated Laguerre generating function. In the full channel the sign for visible charge $j$ and remote Hermite multi-index of total degree $d$ is $(-1)^{(|j|+d-1)/2}$ times the product of the remote factors $P_l(-1)^{d_l}$, since the total degree is $|j|+2k+d$. This formula includes both zero and negative remote multipliers.

In $L^2(dm)$ normalize each kernel by $\sqrt{b(r)b(s)}$. Write $K_0$ for the normalized $J_1(rs)$ and $K_m$ for the normalized $J'_m(rs)$ when $m\ge1$. The normalized test vector of a radial coefficient $a$ is $\sqrt b\,a$. The norm of $K_0$ is one and its restriction off $\sqrt b$ has norm $<5/8$ by #653. The operator $K_1$ has an eigenvector $\sqrt b/r$ with eigenvalue one, since integration by parts gives
$$ \int J_1'(rs)s^{-1}dm(s)=r^{-1}\int (d/ds)J_1(rs)e^{-s^2/2}ds=b(r)/r. $$
The vector has finite norm by $b(r)=O(r)$ at zero and $|b(r)|\le1$. The remaining norm bounds are
$$ \|K_1\|_{\rm HS}^2<1.6889,\qquad \sum_{m\ge2}\|K_m\|_{\rm HS}^2<0.76. $$
Hence $\|K_1\|=1$, its restriction off this eigenvector has norm $<7/8$, and $\|K_m\|<7/8$ for all $m\ge2$.

The [rational replay](../../code/polynomial-phase-variation-codex-a7c4/verify_sidebands.py) certifies both Hilbert–Schmidt estimates in under a second using standard-library rational arithmetic. For completeness, with $L=\int dm/b$, Parseval applied to $\partial_s e^{is\sin\theta}=i\sin\theta\,e^{is\sin\theta}$ gives $\sum_{m\in\mathbb Z}J_m'(s)^2=1/2$. Using $J_0'=-J_1$ and $J_{-m}'=(-1)^mJ_m'$ gives
$$ \sum_{m\ge2}\|K_m\|_{\rm HS}^2=L^2/4-\|K_0\|_{\rm HS}^2/2-\|K_1\|_{\rm HS}^2. $$
Tonelli applies to the squared kernels. For $dm/b=\sqrt{8/\pi}\,p(r)dr$, $1/p={}_1F_1(1/2;2;r^2/2)$ has positive coefficients. On cells of size $1/64$ in $0\le r\le8$ the increasing denominator is enclosed by 49 terms with a geometric upper remainder. Outside, its sixteenth term bounds $\int_8^\infty p$. At the nearest grid point to each product of radial-cell midpoints, exact Taylor series through index 120 enclose $J_1$ and $J_1'$. Both have derivative bounded by one by the circular Fourier integral, so enlarging by the farthest distance to a cell endpoint bounds the whole cell. The positive compact-cell sums separately lower-bound the two subtracted norms, while the integral of $p$ and the squared norm of $K_1$ have exterior upper enclosures. The script also brackets $r_*$ using the positive series and checks the two amplitude comparisons. No floating-point quadrature enters these estimates.

## The paired angular modes

For $m>0$ in part 1 write the two terms of $A$ as $e^{-im\theta}v+e^{im\theta}c\bar v$, with $|c|=1$. The charge of $v$ in the remote variables is $m$. Expand $v=\sum_\xi a_\xi(r)H_\xi(w)$ in the orthonormal complex Hermite basis. Conjugating swaps holomorphic and antiholomorphic indices and preserves all per-coordinate total degrees. For an individual $a=a_\xi$ the two visible charges of $G$ are $1-m$ and $1+m$. Their radial-zero signs are opposites because the two absolute charges differ by two. The remote factors agree. With $s\in[-1,1]$ the signed factor of the lower charge, the pair's contribution is exactly
$$ 2s\iint (J_{m-1}-J_{m+1})(ru)\overline{a(r)}a(u)dm(r)dm(u)=4s\langle \sqrt b\,a,K_m\sqrt b\,a\rangle . $$
The scalar $c$ drops out of the quadratic forms, and conjugating $a$ gives the same real value for a real symmetric kernel. The Bessel derivative recurrence gives the equality. Since $\|K_m\|\le1$, the absolute value is at most $4\int b|a|^2dm$, the weighted squared norm of the two profiles. When $P_z(-1)=-1$, both charges receive the same sign $(-1)^{1+m}$, so the result is unchanged. For $m=0$ use $K_0$ on each remote Hermite coefficient with factor of absolute value at most one, obtaining $2\int b\sum_\xi|a_\xi|^2dm$. Summing by angular orthogonality and remote Parseval gives $|W(G)|\le\int\mathbb E_{\theta,w}|A|^2d\nu\le\gamma^\dagger$. For measurable disk maps the expansions can be truncated independently in $L^2(d\eta)$ and $L^2(d\nu)$ by angular and remote orthogonal projections; dominated convergence of the fiberwise squared residuals gives both limits. Radial kernel identities first apply to bounded compactly supported radial coefficients and extend by approximation in both measures. This proves part 1.

For part 2 let $F=\mathrm{ph}(z)$ and $T$ be the Hermite-diagonal self-adjoint contraction giving the real form $W(G)=\langle G,TG\rangle$. At a balanced preprocessor $TF=\omega(r)F$. Differentiation in $L^2$ gives zero first derivative and
$$ \tfrac12 W''(0)=\langle F\psi,T(F\psi)\rangle-\mathbb E[\omega(R)\psi^2]. $$
The real modulation $\psi$ has exactly conjugate sidebands. The part 1 argument applies by homogeneity to bounded $F\psi$ and bounds its form by $\mathbb E[\omega(R)\psi^2]$. More precisely, in the weighted radial Hilbert space remove from each $m=0$ coefficient its constant part and from each $m=1$ coefficient its component on $1/r$; keep all higher coefficients. The form bound is at most the weighted squared norm of $\psi$ minus $1/8$ of the resulting transverse weighted squared norm. This follows from the three operator gaps above, including for negative remote multipliers. All third derivatives of the quadratic form are bounded in absolute value by $\sum_{j=0}^3 {3\choose j}\|\psi\|_\infty^3=8\|\psi\|_\infty^3$, since $T$ is a contraction and $\|G\|_2=1$. Taylor's theorem gives part 2, independently of dimension.

Finally, project $G$ onto visible harmonic $+1$. The projection commutes with $T$, the complementary form is at most the complementary Gaussian squared norm $\int e\,d\eta$, and the vector-valued $K_0$ estimate bounds the retained part by its weighted squared norm, at most $\int (1-e)\,d\nu$. This proves the balanced estimate in part 3. For general $t=P_z(-1)$ apply one-coordinate Gaussian noise with correlation $\sqrt{|t|}$ before the visible endpoint operator, with a visible reflection if $t<0$. The noise preserves the disk and commutes with visible rotations and remote factors. The retained component was already disk-valued (its amplitude is the visible angular average of $A$), so its smoothed version is disk-valued and part 1 bounds its absolute score by $\gamma^\dagger$. The complementary contribution has absolute value at most its original Gaussian squared norm. At $t=0$ the same argument uses complete visible averaging. This proves the general estimate.

## Arbitrary visible preprocessing

 Put $t=|P_z(-1)|$. In a paired angular mode $m\ge1$, the two components have the same Gaussian energy. Their absolute visible charges are $m-1$ and $m+1$, so their multipliers are bounded by $t^{m-1}$ and $t^{m+1}$. The $m=0$ component has factor at most $t$. Thus for $0\le t\le1/2$, $|W(G)|\le[(1+t^2)/2]\|G\|_2^2\le5/8<\gamma^\dagger$. This includes $t=0$ using $0^0=1$.

For $1/2\le t\le1$, the planar Gaussian Mehler form in the visible coordinate at parameter $-it$ has density against $dx\,dy$ on $\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R^2$
$$\frac{1}{\pi^2(1+t^2)}\exp\left(-\frac{|x|^2+|y|^2+2it\,x\cdot y}{1+t^2}\right).$$
One gets this by continuing the real Gaussian density in the scalar parameter, first on Hermite polynomials and then by contraction. At $t=1$ the formula holds directly by the Fourier representation. A negative visible endpoint composes one argument with $x\mapsto-x$. The factor $i$ and the remote Hermite multipliers defining $W$ are the same at $t$ and at its corresponding endpoint sign. Write $\kappa=2t/(1+t^2)$, $q=1/t-1$ and $L=(1+t^2)/(2t^2)$. Setting $x'=\sqrt\kappa x$, $y'=\sqrt\kappa y$ proves the exact identity
$$W_t(G)=L W_{\operatorname{sgn}P_z(-1)}(G'),\qquad G'(r,\theta,w)=e^{-qr^2/2}G(r/\sqrt\kappa,\theta,w).$$
Here $W_t$ denotes the actual signed endpoint on the left and the remote endpoints agree on both sides. Dilation and real attenuation preserve every sideband conjugacy in the preceding proof. Its *weighted* bound yields
$$ |W_t(G)|\le L\int e^{-qr^2}\,d\nu(r)\le\frac{1+t^2}{2\sqrt t}\,\gamma^\dagger\le\gamma^\dagger.$$
For the penultimate step use the positive beta integral for $b(r)$ in #653. The measure $d\nu=2b(r)r e^{-r^2/2}dr$ is a positive mixture of densities proportional to $r^2e^{-a r^2}dr$ with $1/2\le a\le1$. Under a factor $e^{-qr^2}$ their mass ratios are $(a/(a+q))^{3/2}\le(1+q)^{-3/2}=t^{3/2}$. Finally $2\sqrt t-1-t^2$ is concave on $[1/2,1]$ and nonnegative at both endpoints. Applied to a finite list of paired $G_j$ with $\sum_j|G_j|^2\le1$ pointwise, the same proof gives **$\sum_j |W(G_j)|\le\gamma^\dagger$** for a common tuple of preprocessors: use the weighted integral before bounding the pointwise sum. The small-$t$ Gaussian-energy estimate adds in the same way.

## A covariance bound for general channels

 Write $A=e^{-i\theta}G=X+iY$ with $X,Y$ real. Both $e^{i\theta}X$ and $e^{i\theta}Y$ are paired. Since $X^2+Y^2\le1$, their two diagonal forms have total absolute value at most $\gamma^\dagger$. In the mixed term, let $Q$ average the visible angle with $r,w$ fixed. The maps $e^{i\theta}QX$ and $e^{i\theta}QY$ belong to visible harmonic one, which is invariant under the self-adjoint diagonal channel operator $T$. Their mutual form is real: on that harmonic the visible Laguerre radial basis is real, while remote Hermite conjugation swaps indices without changing the degree or real multiplier. All terms between that harmonic and its orthogonal complement vanish. It follows by the contraction estimate on $T$ that
$$ |W(G)|\le\gamma^\dagger+2\|X-QX\|_2\|Y-QY\|_2.$$
A constant rotation of $G$ preserves its score. Minimizing the product over that rotation diagonalizes the real covariance matrix of $B=A-QA$ and gives the invariant form
$$\boxed{\quad |W(G)|\le\gamma^\dagger+\sqrt{(\mathbb E|B|^2)^2-|\mathbb E B^2|^2}.\quad}$$
A channel whose off-angular-mean modulation lies on a single real line in the complex plane is therefore subject to the full ceiling even if the line has an arbitrary complex angular-average offset depending on $r,w$.

### Finite perturbation size

 For $A=e^{i\psi}$ with $\psi$ real, $\|X-QX\|_2\le\|\cos\psi-1\|_2\le\|\psi\|_\infty^2/2$ and $\|Y-QY\|_2\le\|\sin\psi\|_2\le\|\psi\|_\infty$. Hence **$D\le W\le\gamma^\dagger+\|\psi\|_\infty^3$** uniformly over dimensions and every contractive polynomial for every coordinate. By linearity in a mixture, the cube-mean perturbation amplitude must exceed **0.02145 radians** to match the current root slope. Indeed $0.711958683+0.02145^3=0.711968552198625<0.711968558203721328194311213$; $0.02146$ already fails the same arithmetic comparison. This units-of-angle condition applies to closeness to a visible coordinate phase in each channel, with arbitrary global phase offsets allowed. The upper endpoint and Conjecture K remain unchanged.

### The sharp covariance upper bound on a phase arc

In the common-map angular covariance bound, suppose $|A|=1$ and $|\operatorname{arg}A|\le a\le\pi/2$ after an arbitrary constant rotation. Writing $A=X+iY$ and $Q=\mathbb E_\theta$, the residual covariance matrix of $A-QA$ is at most the ordinary covariance of $(X,Y)$ in the positive-semidefinite order, by total covariance. Its determinant is consequently at most $\operatorname{Var}(X)\mathbb E[Y^2]$. Put $c=\cos a$, $m=\mathbb EX$ and $v=\mathbb EX^2$. Then $c\le X\le1$, $Y^2=1-X^2$ and $X^2\le(1+c)X-c$. It follows that $m\ge(v+c)/(1+c)\ge0$. For given $v$, the largest possible $(v-m^2)(1-v)$ therefore occurs for a distribution of $X$ on the two endpoints with the same $v$. If $p$ is the probability of $X=1$, this expression is $p(1-p)^2(1-c)^2\sin^2 a$. Its maximum is at $p=1/3$. In particular,
$$ |W(G)|\le\gamma^\dagger+\frac4{3\sqrt3}(1-\cos a)\sin a\le\gamma^\dagger+\frac2{3\sqrt3}a^3. $$
The covariance upper budget itself is attained by taking the three angles $-a,0,a$ equally likely on each fiber, which can be realized with a uniformly distributed relative angle on two Gaussian coordinates. This sharpness statement concerns the covariance optimization. For larger $a$, the cubic bound still holds because the covariance correction is at most $\mathbb E|A-QA|^2\le1$ and $(2/(3\sqrt3))(\pi/2)^3>1$.

Let $s=711968558203721328194311213/10^{27}-711958683/10^9$. Squaring the positive expressions gives exactly
$$4(2949/10^5)^6<27s^2<4(2950/10^5)^6.$$
Thus the cube-mean angular radius in a convex mixture must exceed **0.02949 radians** for its transfer slope to attain the root certificate’s value. This uses only the existing upper enclosure of $\gamma^\dagger$; 0.02950 fails the same cubic test.
