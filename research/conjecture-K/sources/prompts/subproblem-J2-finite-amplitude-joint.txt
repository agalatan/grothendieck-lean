THE REDUCTION. Apply (2) with the even base q' = q + (T-1) r_A, then (1):
   Lambda(q + A + B) <= Lambda(q) + 2 Re<r_A, c_A(q)> + 2 Re<r_B, c_B(q)> - 0.24|r_A|^2 - kappa_B|r_B|^2
                        + 2 Re< r_B, c_B(q + (T-1) r_A) - c_B(q) >.
So the entire finite-amplitude joint question is the LAST term: the change of the B content when the A-shell is added.
At q = 0 and to first order in r_A it equals -2m Re<r_A, r_B> with m = +0.118 (this is where the positive sign comes from).

WHAT WE WANT. Uniformly over remote cubics q with u <= 0.0463 (at least on the certified classes: XP(Y), the Gaussian-factor
class, the self-interaction family) and |r_A|^2 <= 0.0113:
   (J2)   Re< r_B, c_B(q + (T-1) r_A) - c_B(q) >  <=  - (m - delta) Re<r_A, r_B> + delta |r_A| |r_B|     for all r_B,

second-order remainder in |r_A|^2. Equivalently: the derivative of the B content along the A direction is, up to delta, minus
m times the identity, uniformly in q. Then the signed joint cost holds at finite amplitude with (kA, kB, m) replaced by
(0.24, kappa_B, m - delta), and by (5) the A+B core closes with Lambda(q + A + B) <= S + O(1e-5) on the certified classes.

How to attack. c_B(q + (T-1) r_A) is explicit: for each w, E_z[(z^2/sqrt2) conj ph(L + q(w) + (T-1) r_A(w))], a two-dimensional
