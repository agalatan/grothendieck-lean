# An exact cubic class beyond fixed unitary decoupling

Agent-ID: codex-a7c4. Research task [#687](https://github.com/agalatan/grothendieck-lean/issues/687), related to [#509](https://github.com/agalatan/grothendieck-lean/issues/509). The proof and symbolic replay are **P + C, author only**.

Ledger 3.24 asks whether the class with zero contraction $DF(z)F(z)=0$ is larger than the cubics that separate into holomorphic and antiholomorphic variables under a fixed unitary change. It is strictly larger. A five-coordinate example has an elementary conditional Gaussian law. A circle action extends zero first-chaos content to a whole family, including cubics whose contraction is nonzero. At the peak row a positive deficit on that family gives an explicit bound for arbitrary nearby remote cubics.

## A two-point test for fixed decoupling

For a pure bidegree-$(2,1)$ Wick cubic $q$, let $F=\bar\partial q$ be its vector of holomorphic homogeneous quadratics. Normal ordering gives $q=\sum_j\bar z_j F_j(z)-\operatorname{div}F(z)$. In fixed decoupled coordinates, the outputs of $F$ belong to one complex subspace and its inputs to the orthogonal complement. The following equivalence does not require guessing the coordinates:
$$
 q\text{ has a fixed unitary decoupling}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 DF(z)F(z')=0\quad\text{for every }z,z'.
$$
Indeed let $E=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C}\{F(z')\}$. The two-point condition says that the derivative vanishes on $E$ at every base point. Integrating along a line shows that $F$ depends only on the projection onto $E^\perp$. Its values lie in $E$ by construction. In an adapted orthonormal basis its divergence vanishes, giving exactly the stated separation. The converse follows by differentiating in an output direction. Under $z=U\zeta$ the field becomes $U^*F(U\zeta)$, so the test is invariant. Here decoupling means equality of the Gaussian polynomials after a fixed change of coordinates. The distinction matters because different polynomials can share a law.

For five independent standard complex Gaussians $(x,y,u,v,w)$ take
$$
 q=x^2\bar u+xy\bar v+(xv-yu)\bar w,\qquad F=(0,0,x^2,xy,xv-yu).
$$
Each monomial is Wick ordered. The only possibly nonzero component of $DF(z)F(z')$ is the fifth, equal to $x'(xy'-yx')$. It vanishes on the diagonal, but its value at $(e_2,e_1)$ is $-1$. This gives strict inclusion in 3.24. The diagonal identity is the quadratic quasi-translation condition. The two-point test above concerns the coordinate splitting used for Gaussian chaos. [Original ledger derivation, §4](../deficit-sign-2026-11-16/REPORT.md) · [de Bondt, *Quasi-translations and singular Hessians*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1501.05168).

Five is the smallest complex dimension admitting separation of these two conditions. For completeness, the diagonal identity makes $z+F(z)$ a quasi-translation. The derivation $D=F\cdot\nabla$ annihilates each component of $F$, so its polynomial flow is $z+tF(z)$ (on coordinates $D^2z_j=0$, and the Leibniz rule extends the substitution to all polynomials). Its inverse is $z-tF(z)$. In at most four variables, de Bondt's Corollary 5.6(ii) now bounds the dimension of the output span by two. For a one-dimensional span choose the last unit vector along it. The diagonal identity becomes $g\,\partial_n g=0$, giving the two-point condition directly. A zero span gives $q=0$.

For a two-dimensional span use orthonormal coordinates with the last two along it. Theorem 5.7 in the same paper writes the two nonzero components as $(b g,a g)$, where $g$ is their greatest common divisor, $a,b$ are polynomials in the outside coordinates, and $g$ belongs to the ring generated by those coordinates and $a z_{n-1}-b z_n$. Homogeneity makes $a,b$ homogeneous of the same degree, which is positive because the output components are linearly independent. Thus their invariant $a z_{n-1}-b z_n$ has degree at least two, while $g$ has degree at most one. Taking the homogeneous components of degrees zero and one of its polynomial expression shows that $g$ involves only outside coordinates. Both output components therefore depend only on the orthogonal complement of the span. This proves minimality for degree two fields. [Corollary 5.6(ii) and Theorem 5.7, pp. 19–20](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1501.05168#page=19).

Condition on $x,y$. The forms $P=\bar x u+\bar y v$ and $Q=xv-yu$ are jointly proper Gaussians with variance $s=|x|^2+|y|^2$ each and zero cross-covariance. Thus $q=x\bar P+Q\bar w$, conditionally on $x,y,Q$, is proper Gaussian of variance $V=|x|^2s+|Q|^2=\|F\|^2$. Equivalently $V$ has the law $(S+T)(S+H)$ for independent unit exponentials $S,T,H$. Direct Wick integration of the original polynomial gives $\mathbb E|q|^2=5$ and $\mathbb E|q|^4=152$, agreeing with $\mathbb EV$ and $2\mathbb EV^2$.

## The full circle-weight subspace

Let $X,U,W$ be independent standard complex Gaussian blocks. Rotate them by weights $0,1,2$ respectively. Within the pure $(2,1)$ chaos the entire weight $-1$ projection has the form
$$
 p=\sum_i A_i(X)\bar U_i+\sum_j (M(X)U)_j\bar W_j
$$
with holomorphic homogeneous quadratics $A_i$ and a matrix $M$ of holomorphic linear forms. These are exactly the ways two holomorphic weights minus one antiholomorphic weight can equal $-1$. For every radial response $h$ with the indicated expectations finite, $p h(|p|^2)$ still has weight $-1$. Every holomorphic coordinate has weight $0$, $1$ or $2$, so every first-chaos content coefficient $\mathbb E[\bar z_j p h(|p|^2)]$ vanishes.

The field of $p$ is $(0,A(X),M(X)U)$. Its diagonal contraction vanishes exactly when $M(X)A(X)=0$ identically. Its fixed decoupling criterion instead requires $M(X)A(X')=0$ for all $X,X'$. For example replacing $xv-yu$ by $xv-2yu$ in the five-coordinate cubic gives diagonal contraction $-x^2y$ in the last component, while all first contents still vanish.

Write $d=\dim_{\mathbb C}X+\dim_{\mathbb C}W\ge1$, $u_0=\mathbb E|p|^2$, and $S_d\sim\mathrm{Gamma}(d,1)$. For any independent proper standard complex Gaussian $G$ and every $v\ge0$,
$$
 2\mathbb E|\sqrt v G+p|\ \le\ \sqrt\pi\,\mathbb E\sqrt{v+u_0 S_d^2/[d(d+1)]}.
$$
To see this, condition on the bath $(X,W)$. The two real components of $p$ are linear in the real Gaussian coordinates of $U$. Diagonalize their conditional covariance, with eigenvalues $\lambda_1,\lambda_2\ge0$. Polar coordinates for a standard real Gaussian pair yield $|p|^2\stackrel d=E_1 H$, with $E_1$ an independent unit exponential and $H=2(\lambda_1\cos^2\Theta+\lambda_2\sin^2\Theta)$, the angle uniform and independent of the bath. The unconditional law of $p$ is circular by its global Wick charge. Hence $p\stackrel d=\sqrt H G'$ for a proper standard Gaussian $G'$ independent of $H$, including singular conditional covariances. The coefficients of the real-linear map are degree two under real dilation of the bath. The covariance eigenvalues are homogeneous of degree four, so $H=S_d^2 J$ with $S_d=\|(X,W)\|^2$ independent of $J$. As $\mathbb E S_d^2=d(d+1)$ and $\mathbb E H=u_0$, Jensen over $J$ gives the displayed bound. At $v=0$ it gives $\mathbb E|p|\le(\sqrt\pi/2)\sqrt{d/(d+1)}\,\|p\|_2$. This refines the Gaussian comparison for this subspace at every buffer. [Conditional Gaussian carrier estimate, ledger 6.3.16b](../real-driver-blocks-codex-a7c4/PROOF.md).

## An explicit perturbation bound for the peak row

Use the row of #509, $a=699/1250$, $b=179/1250$, $L=z(a+b|z|^2)$. By ledger 2.7, $L$ has the law of a centered proper Gaussian of random variance $V_L$. Its mean variance is $m=a^2+4ab+6b^2$. Write $D(r)=2\mathbb E|L+\sqrt{\mathbb E|r|^2}G|-2\mathbb E|L+r|$ and use the raw content $C$ of #509. Suppose $d=3$. For $p$ as above let $Y=u_0 S_3^2/12$. It has mean $u_0$. Subtracting the square root from its tangent at $v+u_0$ gives exactly
$$
 \sqrt{v+u_0}+\frac{Y-u_0}{2\sqrt{v+u_0}}-\sqrt{v+Y}
 =\frac{(Y-u_0)^2}{2\sqrt{v+u_0}(\sqrt{v+Y}+\sqrt{v+u_0})^2}.
$$
On $S_3\le2$ one has $Y\le u_0/3$, and the event has probability $1-5e^{-2}>3/10$. The tangent gap there is at least $u_0^2/[18(v+u_0)^{3/2}]$. Average over the independent $V_L$ and use convexity of $(v+u_0)^{-3/2}$. The preceding buffer comparison gives
$$
 D(p)\ge\frac{\sqrt\pi\,u_0^2}{60(m+u_0)^{3/2}} > u_0^2/60
 \qquad (0<u_0\le463/10000).
$$

Now allow any remote cubic $r$ in these blocks with total energy in the band of #509. Let $p$ be its weight $-1$ projection, $h=r-p$, and $e=\mathbb E|h|^2$. If $0<u_0$ and $e\le u_0^2/21960$, then **$C(r)^2\le D(r)/10$**. The dimension of $U$ is arbitrary.

Here are the regularity constants for this estimate. The radial map $z\mapsto L$ has real Jacobian $(a+b|z|^2)(a+3b|z|^2)\ge a^2$. The law of $L$, and the pushforward weighted by $||z|^2-1|$, both have densities at most $1/(\pi a^2)$ and mass at most one. For the weighted measure use $|t-1|e^{-t}\le1$ and $\mathbb E||z|^2-1|\le1$. For either measure $\mu$,
$$
 \sup_y\int |x+y|^{-1}\,d\mu(x)\le 2/a+1/a=3/a,
$$
by splitting at radius $a$. The derivative of the real phase map $s\mapsto s/|s|$ has norm $1/|s|$ away from zero. Integrating its derivative on a segment and using Fubini yields a Lipschitz bound $3/a$ for the raw response $\Psi(y)=\mathbb E[(|z|^2-1)\operatorname{ph}(L+y)]$ and $6/a$ for the gradient of $\Phi(y)=2\mathbb E|L+y|$. The set of translates for which the segment hits zero has planar measure zero.

Since $\Phi$ is radial, $\nabla\Phi(p)$ has weight $-1$, and its real pairing with $h$ has expectation zero. The quadratic Taylor bound gives $\mathbb E\Phi(r)\le\mathbb E\Phi(p)+(3/a)e$. The Gaussian comparison payoff is increasing in energy by the mixture law of $L$. Hence $D(r)\ge D(p)-6e$ as $a>1/2$. The response at $p$ has zero first projection and the projection is contractive, so $C(r)^2\le(3/a)^2e\le36e$. The asserted ratio follows from $366e\le u_0^2/60$. This is a sufficient energy condition on the projection residual. General remote cubics outside it remain open.
