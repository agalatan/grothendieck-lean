\subsection{Lifting the new complex-anchor cover to arbitrary dimension}
\label{sec:complex-anchor-complement}

\paragraph{Status and inputs.} \textbf{[P,C]} The following finite matrix
check is independent of the core cover's numerical result. It says that
any valid core cover using the eight anchors
\[
 c_0=6/25,\qquad
 \alpha_0\in\{0,3/50,9/50,-9/50,9i/50,17/50,-17/50,17i/50\},
 \qquad\beta_0=0
\]
automatically admits the complement estimate below. Its numerical input
is the successful replay of \texttt{check\_complex\_complement.py}.
The core bound $B$ must itself be certified; a floating estimate cannot
be substituted for it.

Let $J\in\mathcal H_{2,1}$ satisfy $\mathbb E[J\mid Z,X]=0$, on
arbitrarily many additional Gaussian coordinates. The fresh buffer $U$
is independent of all those coordinates. Let $J_{rs}$ denote the
orthogonal component with remote Hermite degree $(r,s)$. Then every
core bound $\Lambda(Q+gU)\le B$ obtained from the displayed anchors,
their conjugates, square completion and parameter interpolation lifts to
\[
\begin{split}
 \Lambda(Q+J+gU)\le B
 &+\frac{19}{25}\|J_{10}\|_2^2
 +\frac{81}{100}\|J_{01}\|_2^2\\
 &+\frac{27}{25}(\|J_{20}\|_2^2+\|J_{11}\|_2^2)
 +\frac{32}{25}\|J_{21}\|_2^2.
\end{split}
\]
The simpler additional cost $(32/25)\|J\|_2^2$ is therefore valid.
No independence of $J$ and $(Z,X)$ is required.

\paragraph{Why complex anchors still reduce to small blocks.}
The cell tangent argument and remote Hermite expansion from
Section~\ref{sec:uniform-complement} are unchanged. A complex anchor
only removes the separate $x$ and $y$ reflection symmetries. The paired
weight still satisfies $W_0(r,-x,-y)=W_0(r,x,y)$, which separates the
even and odd polynomial blocks in $X$. The off-diagonal moment becomes
\[
 \Gamma=\mathbb E[W_0s\overline X^2]
 =\mathbb E[W_0s(x^2-y^2)]-2i\mathbb E[W_0sxy].
\]
The Hermite blocks have off-diagonal entries $\Gamma$ or its conjugate,
and $\Gamma/2$ or its conjugate. The directed comparison with $\lambda I$
checks the two positive diagonal gaps and
\[
 (\lambda-a)(\lambda-d)-(\Re\Gamma)^2-(\Im\Gamma)^2>0.
\]
Both signs of $y$ are integrated; the imaginary moment is retained.
No phase is discarded.

\paragraph{Numerical constants.}
At all eight anchors, the script certifies excesses over $p=2500/1891$
of $19/25$, $81/100$, $27/25$, $27/25$, $32/25$ on the cylinder spaces
$\mathcal H_{1,1}$, $\mathcal H_{2,0}$, $\mathcal H_{0,1}$,
$\mathcal H_{1,0}$, $\mathcal H_{0,0}$, respectively. It rebuilds the
exact Gaussian moments on the original 64-bin grid and verifies the
scalar and two-by-two Hermitian inequalities using outward arithmetic.
The check is at zero buffer; $K_g(u)\le K_0(u)$ handles the remaining
buffers wherever the core cover applies.

\paragraph{Scope.}
This gives an arbitrary-dimensional neighborhood of a certified
self-interacting core, including zero buffer. Analytic extensions of the
core theorem beyond its finite cover require their own complement
argument. It does not by itself remove the complement variance cost,
prove full K, or produce a new Grothendieck endpoint.

\paragraph{Consequences of the retained compact deficit.}
The new compact cover has $B\le S-3/5000$ for
$19/100\le c\le34/125$, $0\le g\le41/250$,
$|\alpha|\le2/5$, with every complex $\beta$. Thus
\[
 \|J\|_2\le\frac1{50}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \Lambda(Q+J+gU)\le S-\frac{11}{125000},
\]
and
\[
 \|J\|_2\le\frac1{44}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \Lambda(Q+J+gU)\le S+\frac{37}{605000}<S+\eta_{AB}.
\]
The rational inequalities are checked by
\texttt{check\_deductions.py}.

\autoref{ch:buffer-bootstrap}'s accepted complement theorem supplies, for $g\ge41/250$,
\[
 \Lambda(Q+J+gU)\le S-\frac{\min(c^4,c^2)}{32}
 -\frac{|\alpha|^2+|\beta|^2}{2000}
 +\frac{41}{10}\|J\|_2^2.
\]
Combining the two buffer intervals, a sufficient condition for exact K
throughout $19/100\le c\le34/125$ is
\[
 \boxed{\|J\|_2^2\le
 \min\left\{\frac3{6400},\frac{5c^4}{656}
                      +\frac{|\alpha|^2+|\beta|^2}{8200}\right\}.}
\]
The compact argument imposes $|\alpha|\le2/5$. If the separate universal
all-sector cutoff at $\|r_A\|_2\ge2/5$ is included, it removes this
restriction: conditional centering implies that the added A projection
is orthogonal to the core $\alpha X$, so the total A norm is at least
$|\alpha|$.

In particular, the simpler condition $\|J\|_2^2\le5c^4/656$ works for
every buffer. Indeed
\[
 \frac{32}{25}\frac5{656}\left(\frac{34}{125}\right)^4
 =\frac{2672672}{50048828125}
 <\frac3{5000}-\frac1{2000}.
\]
This also retains a deficit greater than $1/2000$ in the small-buffer
interval. None of these statements removes the need for a general
reduction to a core with a sufficiently small complement.

\paragraph{Removing the minimum by the universal profile plane.}
The new universal profile plane treats total A/B sector variance at least
$4/25$, even for arbitrary-dimensional coupled cubics. Conditional
centering makes the added A/B projection orthogonal to the core
projection, so its total squared norm is at least
$r^2=|\alpha|^2+|\beta|^2$.
For $r^2\ge4/25$ that theorem applies directly. For $r^2<4/25$ the
compact cover applies, and the exact checker verifies
\[
 \frac{32}{25}\left[
 \frac5{656}\left(\frac{34}{125}\right)^4+
 \frac{4/25}{8200}\right]
 <\frac3{5000}-\frac1{2000}.
\]
Thus, using that universal plane as an explicit additional input, the
all-buffer condition improves to
\[
 \boxed{19/100\le c\le34/125,\qquad
 \|J\|_2^2\le\frac{5c^4}{656}
             +\frac{|\alpha|^2+|\beta|^2}{8200}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad\Lambda(Q+J+gU)\le S.}
\]
This allows every complex $\alpha,\beta$ and every $g\ge0$. It retains
more than $1/2000$ in the small-buffer part with $r^2<4/25$; \autoref{ch:buffer-bootstrap}
supplies the large-buffer part.
