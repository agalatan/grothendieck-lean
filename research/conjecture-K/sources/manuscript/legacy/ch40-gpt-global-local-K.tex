\subsection{Scope, normalization, and audit}
Throughout,
\[
 \varepsilon=\frac{1891}{2500},\quad a=\frac{699}{1250},\quad
 b=\frac{179}{1250},\quad L=Z(a+b|Z|^2),
\]
where $Z$ is a standard proper complex Gaussian, $\mathbb E|Z|^2=1$.
Write
\[
 \Lambda(G)=\mathbb E|L+G|+\mathbb E|L-G|
                  -\|G\|_2^2/\varepsilon,
 \qquad
 S=\sup_{v\ge0}\{2\mathbb E|L+\sqrt vU|-v/\varepsilon\},
\]
where $U$ is independent and proper. Cubics belong to a finite-dimensional
$H_{2,1}$; constants are uniform in that dimension. General remote sectors
may share every Gaussian driver. Independence is imposed only where explicitly
stated, notably for the additional Gaussian buffer in the local theorems.

The original external Node checker passed an isolated replay. Its hyperplane
theorem and origin Hessian are valid, and already recorded in the main
handoff. We do not count their restatement as a new endpoint improvement.
The new standard-library Python/C++17 wrapper regenerates all scalar inputs,

The final reread of \autoref{ch:K-state} also found a later cold-start progress package
already recorded there. It proves directional stability at the Gaussian

\paragraph{A sharp refinement of the external origin block [P,C].}
Let $M$ be its normalized $A/B$ Hessian block, and $h_0=I_0/2$.
Then
\[
 M\preceq\left(h_0-\rho I_1\right)I,
 \qquad \rho=\frac{7-\sqrt{17}}{16}>\frac{179}{1000}.
\]
For completeness, under the external Stieltjes representation, the normalized
integrand block is
\[
 N(x)=\begin{pmatrix}
 1-x+3x^2/4&(x/2-3x^2/4)/\sqrt2\\
 (x/2-3x^2/4)/\sqrt2&3x^2/8
 \end{pmatrix},\quad 0<x\le1.
\]
Put $A(x)=(I-N(x))/x$. Direct differentiation gives
\[
 -A'(x)=\begin{pmatrix}3/4&-3/(4\sqrt2)\\
 -3/(4\sqrt2)&x^{-2}+3/8\end{pmatrix}\succ0,
 \qquad\det(-A'(x))=3/(4x^2).
\]
Thus $A(x)\succeq A(1)\succeq\rho I$. Integrating gives the claimed
bound because the Stieltjes integral of $x$ times the $h_0$ measure is $I_1$.
At $b=0$, $x=1$ identically, so the coefficient is sharp for this universal
comparison. This is an origin statement, not deletion of a coupled component
from a nonzero residual.

\paragraph{The visible marginal mixture [P].}
The external handoff also gives a short proof used below.
Let $f(t)=t(a+bt)^2$ and $\psi=f^{-1}$. The survival function of $|L|^2$
is $e^{-\psi(s)}$. The reciprocal derivative
\[
 h(t)=1/f'(t)=\frac1{(a+bt)(a+3bt)}
\]
is completely monotone. Starting with $q_0(t)=e^{-t}$ and
$q_{j+1}=-h q_j'$, induction shows that every $q_j$ is completely monotone
and nonnegative, and
$(-1)^j(d/ds)^j e^{-\psi(s)}=q_j(\psi(s))\ge0$.
Bernstein's Laplace representation therefore expresses this survival
function as a probability mixture of exponential survivals. Circularity
gives $L\stackrel d=\sqrt{V_{\rm mix}}\,U$ with $V_{\rm mix}>0$ independent of $U$.
This is a marginal representation only: it must never be used to replace
the joint law of $L$ and a coupled cubic. With a fresh independent buffer,
it does validly give
\[
 F(v)=\sqrt\pi\,\mathbb E\sqrt{V_{\rm mix}+v}-v/\varepsilon,
 \quad F''<0,\quad F'''>0.
\]
The mixture has mean $\mathbb EV_{\rm mix}=\mathbb E|L|^2
=1181331/1562500$.

\subsection{A global variance-profile bound for arbitrary coupled cubics}
\label{sec:global-trace-profile}
\paragraph{Status and scope [P,C].}
The results in this section hold for every finite-dimensional cubic in
$H_{2,1}$, with arbitrary sharing of the remote Gaussian drivers among all
sectors. They are global in amplitude, include the diagonal, and do not assume
conditional Gaussianity. They give exclusion regions for a possible
counterexample, not a proof of K or a new endpoint.

Set
\[
a=\frac{699}{1250},\quad b=\frac{179}{1250},\quad
p=\frac{2500}{1891},\quad h(t)=t(a+bt)^2,\quad
\Lambda_0=\sqrt\pi(a+3b/2)\le S.
\]
The orthogonal decomposition relative to the visible coordinate is
\[
G=q(y)+ZB(y)+\bar ZC(y)+(T-1)D(y)+Z^2E(y)+cZ(T-2),
\qquad T=|Z|^2,
\]
where the remote bidegrees of $q,B,C,D,E$ are respectively
$(2,1),(1,1),(2,0),(1,0),(0,1)$. Define
\[
u=\|q\|_2^2,\quad d=\|D\|_2^2,\quad
w=\|B\|_2^2+\|C\|_2^2,\quad j=2\|E\|_2^2,
\quad n=2|c|^2,\quad V=u+d+w+j+n.
\]
Visible phase orthogonality, together with
$\mathbb E(q\bar D)=0$ and $\mathbb EB=0$, gives the exact identity
\begin{equation}
\mathbb E(|G|^2\mid T=t)
=u+d(t-1)^2+wt+\frac j2t^2+\frac n2t(t-2)^2.
\label{eq:trace-conditional-variance}
\end{equation}
In particular, no independence between the five remote components is used.

For any complex $x,z$,
$|x+z|+|x-z|\le2\sqrt{|x|^2+|z|^2}$.
Applying this pointwise and then applying conditional Jensen to
\eqref{eq:trace-conditional-variance} proves
\begin{equation}
\Lambda(G)\le\Phi(u,d,w,j,n)
:=2\int_0^\infty e^{-t}
\sqrt{h(t)+u+d(t-1)^2+wt+jt^2/2+nt(t-2)^2/2}\,dt-pV.
\label{eq:trace-Phi}
\end{equation}
The function $\Phi$ is concave in its five nonnegative arguments.
It is only an upper bound: the true payoff has not been replaced by an
allegedly equivalent law.

\subsubsection{Simultaneous positive profile penalties [P]}
Let
\[
J(V)=2\int_0^\infty e^{-t}\sqrt{h(t)+V}\,dt-pV.
\]
For the profiles $f_A=(t-1)^2$, $f_e=t$, $f_B=t^2/2$,
and $f_\nu=t(t-2)^2/2$, define
\[
P_A=t^2,\qquad P_e=t,\qquad P_B=t+t^2/2,\qquad
P_\nu=\frac t2(t^2-t+2).
\]
Direct differentiation gives
\begin{equation}
\int_0^t e^{-s}(f_i(s)-1)\,ds=-e^{-t}P_i(t).
\label{eq:trace-profile-cumulative}
\end{equation}
Every $P_i$ is nonnegative. For the diagonal this follows from
$t^2-t+2=(t-1/2)^2+7/4$.

Taking the tangent of the square root in \eqref{eq:trace-Phi} at
$h(t)+V$, and using \eqref{eq:trace-profile-cumulative} to integrate by
parts, proves the global inequality
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\Lambda(G)\le J(V)-dD_A(V)-wD_e(V)-jD_B(V)-nD_\nu(V),}
\label{eq:trace-profile-penalty}
\end{equation}
where
\[
D_i(V)=\frac12\int_0^\infty
\frac{e^{-t}P_i(t)h'(t)}{(h(t)+V)^{3/2}}\,dt>0.
\]
For $V>0$ the integration by parts has vanishing boundary terms; $V=0$
is the zero polynomial. All four $D_i$ decrease with $V$.

This is a statement about simultaneous finite-amplitude interactions.
The penalties are relative to the constant-profile \emph{Jensen bound}
$J$, not relative to $S$; confusing these two baselines would give an
incorrect proof of K.

\subsubsection{A universal variance cutoff [P,C]}
Exact arithmetic proves
\[
J(13/20)<\Lambda_0-1/2000.
\]
Moreover $J'(V)\le V^{-1/2}-p<0$ for $V\ge13/20$, since
$(13/20)p^2>1$. Consequently
\begin{equation}
\boxed{V\ge\frac{13}{20}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\Lambda(G)<\Lambda_0-\frac1{2000}\le S-\frac1{2000}.}
\label{eq:trace-variance-cutoff}
\end{equation}
For $0<V\le13/20$, the same checker proves
\begin{equation}
D_A(V)>\frac{31}{100},\quad D_e(V)>\frac15,\quad
D_B(V)>\frac9{25},\quad D_\nu(V)>\frac25.
\label{eq:trace-explicit-penalties}
\end{equation}
All four inequalities can be used simultaneously in
\eqref{eq:trace-profile-penalty}.

\subsubsection{A trace-sensitive affine certificate [P,C]}
For a complementary tangent put $d_0=2/25$ and
$r(t)=h(t)+d_0(t-1)^2$. The tangent inequality in
\eqref{eq:trace-Phi} gives
\[
\Phi\le K_0+(\lambda_0-p)u+(\lambda_A-p)d+
(\lambda_e-p)w+(\lambda_B-p)j+(\lambda_\nu-p)n,
\]
where
\[
K_0=\int_0^\infty e^{-t}
\frac{2h(t)+d_0(t-1)^2}{\sqrt{r(t)}}\,dt,
\qquad
\lambda_i=\int_0^\infty e^{-t}\frac{f_i(t)}{\sqrt{r(t)}}\,dt,
\qquad f_0=1.
\]
The exact certificate gives
\[
K_0<\frac{347}{250},\qquad
\lambda_0-p<\frac{111}{200},\qquad
\lambda_i-p<-\frac15\quad(i=A,e,B,\nu).
\]
It follows that every cubic, with all interactions present, satisfies
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\Lambda(G)\le\frac{347}{250}+\frac{151}{200}u-\frac15V.}
\label{eq:trace-affine-cut}
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{V\ge\frac1{12}+\frac{19}{5}u\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\Lambda(G)<\Lambda_0-\frac1{2000}-\frac{u}{200}
\le S-\frac1{2000}-\frac{u}{200}.}
\label{eq:trace-affine-exclusion}
\end{equation}
Indeed \eqref{eq:trace-affine-cut} is then at most
$2057/1500-u/200$, and the checker proves
$2057/1500<\Lambda_0-1/2000$.
This includes every cubic with zero remote projection and variance at
least $1/12$, with unrestricted A/B/even/diagonal interactions. Unlike
the hyperplane-vanishing theorem, this statement permits a nonzero
centered A sector.

The standard-library scripts \texttt{check\_trace\_profile.py} and

outward dyadic rounding at denominator $2^{90}$; no floating-point

A degree-$30/31$ alternating Taylor bound for $e^{-\Delta}$ gives the

Cell masses and polynomial moments use their exact exponential

formula with rational alternating-series remainders.

For $J(13/20)$, Jensen is applied separately on $1{,}024$ cells of width
$1/64$ in $[0,16]$. On the tail,
$\sqrt{h+V}\le\sqrt h+\sqrt V$ and
$\sqrt t\le t/4$ for $t\ge16$ give an integrable polynomial majorant.
The four penalty bounds use positive lower sums on $6{,}144$ cells

For the affine certificate, $r$ has positive coefficients and hence

\paragraph{Remaining region [?].}
A counterexample must satisfy both
$0<V<13/20$ and $V<1/12+(19/5)u$, as well as the stronger functional
bound \eqref{eq:trace-profile-penalty}. The upper bound $J$ remains too
large near its maximum to prove K. In particular, these global cuts
must be combined with a bound retaining the true residual deficit;

\subsection{A sharper global two-sign inequality}
\label{sec:anisotropic-two-sign-global}
\paragraph{An elementary sharp inequality [P].}
For every real $x>0$ and $w=u+iv\in\mathbb C$,
\begin{equation}
 |x+w|+|x-w|\le2x+\frac{u^2}{2x}+\frac{v^2}{x}.
 \label{eq:anisotropic-two-sign}
\end{equation}
Both coefficients are sharp: $w=2x$ forces the real coefficient $1/2$,
and $w=iv$, $v\to0$, forces the imaginary coefficient $1$.
To prove it, scale to $x=1$ and dualize the two norms with unit vectors
$p=e^{i(\theta+\phi)}$, $q=e^{i(\theta-\phi)}$.
Maximizing the resulting expression over $u,v$, after subtracting
$u^2/2+v^2+2$, gives, with $c=\cos\phi$ and $z=\cos\theta$,
\[
 2cz-2c^2-(1-c^2)z^2=-(c-z)^2-c^2(1-z^2)\le0.
\]
This proves the inequality at every amplitude, with no probability assumption.

\paragraph{The full cubic bound [P,C].}
Use the normalized sector notation
\[
 G=q+ZY+\bar ZW+(T-1)A+Z^2B/\sqrt2+\nu Z(T-2)/\sqrt2,
\]
where the remote bidegrees of $q,Y,W,A,B$ are
$(2,1),(1,1),(2,0),(1,0),(0,1)$, respectively.
All sectors may share all their drivers. Put
$u=\|q\|_2^2$, $d=\|A\|_2^2$, $j=\|B\|_2^2$,
$I_A=I_2-2I_1+I_0$, and $h_f=(I_3-4I_2+4I_1)/2$, where
\[
 I_k=\int_0^\infty\frac{t^{k-1/2}e^{-t}}{a+bt}\,dt.
\]
Let $Q$ denote the exact origin Hessian from the audited external note.
Applying \eqref{eq:anisotropic-two-sign} after rotation by the visible phase gives
\[
 \Lambda(G)\le\Lambda_0+\frac12\mathbb E\frac{|G|^2}{|L|}
                  +\frac12Q(G)-p\|G\|_2^2.
\]
Hermite orthogonality and phase integration give the explicit bound
\begin{align*}
 \Lambda(G)-\Lambda_0\le{}&
 (3I_0/4-p)u+(3I_A/4-p)d+(3I_2/8-p)j\\
 &+(3I_1/4-p)\|W\|_2^2
 +(3I_1/4-p)\|Y\|_2^2-\tfrac14I_1\Re\mathbb E Y^2\\
 &-\frac{I_2-I_1}{2\sqrt2}\Re\mathbb E(AB)
 +(h_f/2-p)(\Re\nu)^2+(h_f-p)(\Im\nu)^2.
\end{align*}
In particular the $A/B$ interference is retained. At the peak, independent
rational Stieltjes enclosures give
\begin{align*}
 \Lambda(G)-\Lambda_0\le{}&
 .826u+.181d-.749j+.119\sqrt{dj}\\
 &-.128\|Y\|_2^2-.426\|W\|_2^2
 -.826(\Re\nu)^2-.330(\Im\nu)^2.
\end{align*}
All displayed decimals are exact terminating rationals rounded outward.
Completing the $A/B$ square yields
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\Lambda(G)\le\Lambda_0+.826u+.186d
 -.128\|Y\|_2^2-.426\|W\|_2^2-.826(\Re\nu)^2-.330(\Im\nu)^2.}
 \label{eq:anisotropic-simple}
\end{equation}
Consequently, using the separately replayed benchmark lower bound
$96683367193980/2^{46}\le S$,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{q=0,\quad \|G\|_2^2\le1/100
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \Lambda(G)<S-1/5000.}
 \label{eq:anisotropic-small-visible}
\end{equation}
This allows simultaneous $A/B$, even, and diagonal interactions in every
dimension and needs no Gaussian buffer. Together with the global trace
theorem, it proves the entire no-remote class outside the intermediate
variance window $(1/100,1/12)$. It does not close that entire window.
The positive remote coefficient in \eqref{eq:anisotropic-simple} is a real
limitation for a sizeable remote cubic with shared drivers.

The standard-library checker \texttt{check\_anisotropic.py} rebuilds the
$I_k$ bounds using exact polynomial majorants and minorants, verifies the
dual sum of squares, and checks every rational comparison. The separate
scalar replay certifies the benchmark used in
\eqref{eq:anisotropic-small-visible}. No endpoint is asserted.

\subsection{The full cubic tangent space at an independent Gaussian buffer}
\label{sec:buffered-full-tangent}
\paragraph{Status and scope.}
The results here are analytic \st{P}, using exact Hessian integrals regenerated by the standalone local
checker; the new constants pass the rational
checker \path{check_buffered_local.py} \st{C}. They give a
uniform local theorem, not a new endpoint or a finite-amplitude solution.
Fix the exact peak profile and put $p=1/\varepsilon$. Let $G(Z,y)$ be
an arbitrary finite-dimensional $H_{2,1}$ polynomial. Let $U$ be a fresh
proper Gaussian, independent of $Z$ and of \emph{every} coordinate used
by $G$. Define
\[
 F(v)=2\mathbb E|L+\sqrt vU|-pv,\quad q=\mathbb E_ZG,\quad H=G-q,
 \quad u=\|q\|_2^2,\quad h=\|H\|_2^2,\quad r^2=u+h.
\]
No independence between $q$ and the shared drivers of $H$ is assumed.
The buffer is a graded limit object; a Gaussian marginal already
coupled to those drivers cannot be substituted for it.

\subsubsection{The exact buffered quadratic form}
Write the orthogonal decomposition
\[
 G=q+ZY+\bar ZW+(T-1)A+Z^2B/\sqrt2+\nu H_3(Z),\quad
 H_3(Z)=Z(T-2)/\sqrt2,
\]
where $q,Y,W,A,B$ belong respectively to
$H_{2,1},H_{1,1},H_{2,0},H_{1,0},H_{0,1}$ on the same remote space.
Put $d(t)=\sqrt t(t-2)/\sqrt2$ and
$\psi_v(x)=\mathbb E|x+\sqrt vU|$. At the real point $\ell(t)$, set
\[
 w_v=\tfrac12(\psi_{xx}+\psi_{yy}),\qquad
 k_v=\tfrac12(\psi_{xx}-\psi_{yy})\le0.
\]
In terms of normalized averaging on $[0,\pi/2]$,
\[
 w_v(t)=\left\langle\frac{\sqrt\pi}{2\sqrt v}
 e^{-\ell(t)^2\cos^2\theta/v}\right\rangle,
 \quad k_v(t)=\left\langle\frac{\sqrt\pi}{2\sqrt v}
 e^{-\ell(t)^2\cos^2\theta/v}\cos2\theta\right\rangle.
\]
After the joint phase rotation,
$R_t=q+(t-1)A+tB/\sqrt2+\sqrt t(Y+W)+\nu d(t)$.
Hermite orthogonality and circle charges give exactly
\begin{align*}
 \mathbb E_y|R_t|^2={}&u+(t-1)^2\|A\|^2+t^2\|B\|^2/2
 +t(\|Y\|^2+\|W\|^2)+|\nu|^2d(t)^2,\\
 \mathbb E_yR_t^2={}&\sqrt2t(t-1)\mathbb E(AB)+t\mathbb E Y^2
 +\nu^2d(t)^2.
\end{align*}
Thus the exact quadratic coefficient in
$\Lambda(\sqrt vU+sG)$ is $Q_v(G)-p\|G\|^2$, where
\[
 Q_v(G)=\mathbb E_T\{w_v(T)\mathbb E_y|R_T|^2+
 k_v(T)\operatorname{Re}\mathbb E_yR_T^2\}.
\]
This calculation uses no conditional Gaussianity. The Gaussian
covariance computation is the exact Hessian of the entire cubic space.

On $7/200\le v\le9/200$, \autoref{ch:polarized-profile-certificate}'s
certified Hessian integrals bound the A/B price by
\[
 -\tfrac25\|A\|^2-\tfrac9{10}\|B\|^2
 +\tfrac{16}{25}\|A\|\|B\|,
\]
and the even price by $-(\|Y\|^2+\|W\|^2)/10$.
The displayed $2\times2$ matrix dominates $I/10$.
The standalone local checker bounds the diagonal quadratic norm
coefficient by $1$, giving price at least $(p-1)|\nu|^2>|\nu|^2/10$.
There are no cross terms with $\nu$: $\mathbb EY=0$, and all remaining
charges or Hermite degrees differ. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:full-buffer-hessian}
 \boxed{Q_v(G)-p\|G\|^2\le F'(v)u-h/10.}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Remote directions are exactly the top eigenspace.}
Let $j(v)=F'(v)+p=\mathbb E_Tw_v(T)$ and let $v_*$ maximize $F$.
The standalone scalar certificate gives
$7/200<v_*<9/200$. The visible marginal mixture, used only
with the independent buffer, gives (here $V$ denotes its mixing variance)
\[
 j(v)=\frac{\sqrt\pi}{2}\mathbb E(V+v)^{-1/2},\qquad j(v_*)=p.
\]
The scalar checker proves $j(9/200)>p-1/60$; monotonicity of $j$
extends this throughout the buffer window. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:full-buffer-spectral-gap}
 \boxed{Q_v(G)\le j(v)\|G\|^2-\|G-q\|^2/12.}
\end{equation}
Every remote cubic attains $j(v)$. This is a strict, dimension-uniform
visible-sector spectral gap throughout the Gaussian buffer window.
No universal claim for all profiles or all variances is made.

\subsubsection{A uniform full-coupling local theorem}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:full-buffer-local}
If $7/200\le v\le9/200$ and $\|G\|_2\le1/20000$, then
\[
 \boxed{\Lambda(\sqrt vU+G)
 \le F(v)+F'(v)u-u^2/8-h/25.}
\]
In particular, at $v=v_*$ this is exact K, with deficit
$\|q\|_2^4/8+\|G-q\|_2^2/25$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For every complex $(2,1)$ chaos $J$,
\[
 \mathbb E|J|^4\le44\|J\|_2^4,
 \qquad\mathbb E|J|^6\le11352\|J\|_2^6.
\]
For explicit constants write $J=I_{2,1}(f)$, so $\|J\|^2=2\|f\|^2$.
Wick multiplication of bidegrees $(p,q),(r,s)$ has coefficients
$\binom pi\binom si i!\binom qj\binom rj j!$ and output
$(p+r-i-j,q+s-i-j)$. Contraction and symmetrization are norm
contractions. Combining equal outputs by the triangle inequality gives
coefficient bounds $(1,4,4)\|f\|^2$ for $J^2$ in grades
$(4,2),(3,1),(2,0)$, and $(1,12,42,40)\|f\|^3$ for $J^3$ in
$(6,3),(5,2),(4,1),(3,0)$. The isometry factors $p!q!$ give the two
constants. The finite coefficient calculations are checked exactly.
Also $\mathbb E|q|^4\ge2u^2$, by nonnegativity of the cubic fourth
cumulant, already implied by the fluctuation theorem.

Gaussian smoothing of the planar norm gives global multilinear bounds
\[
 \|D^4\psi_v\|_{op}\le\frac{3\sqrt\pi}{4v^{3/2}}<204,
 \quad\|D^6\psi_v\|_{op}\le\frac{15\sqrt\pi}{4v^{5/2}}<30000.
\]
Fourier differentiation with multiplier $e^{-v|\xi|^2/4}$ proves these:
the planar norm has radial density $-(2\pi)^{-1}|\xi|^{-3}$, and the
fourth and sixth angular integrals are $3\pi/4,5\pi/8$. H\"older
handles distinct directions. All differentiated integrals converge
absolutely. The rational comparisons use $\pi<22/7$.

Circularity of the remote $q$ gives its exact fourth Taylor term
$\mathbb E|q|^4F''(v)/4$; indeed the circular fourth tensor contracts
to $3\mathbb E|q|^4\Delta^2\psi_v/8$, while
$F''(v)=\mathbb E\Delta^2\psi_v(L)/8$.
With $M=\mathbb EV=1181331/1562500$, Jensen gives
\[
 -F''(v)=\frac{\sqrt\pi}{4}\mathbb E(V+v)^{-3/2}
 \ge\frac{\sqrt\pi}{4(M+v)^{3/2}}>\frac12,
\]
since $3>4(M+9/200)^3$. The remote fourth term is therefore at most
$-u^2/4$. Taylor expansion, the exact Hessian, and H\"older give
\begin{align*}
 \Lambda(\sqrt vU+G)\le{}&F(v)+F'(v)u-h/10-u^2/4\\
 &+748(4u^{3/2}\sqrt h+6uh+4\sqrt u\,h^{3/2}+h^2)
 +946000(u+h)^3.
\end{align*}
Here $748=204\cdot44/12$ and $946000=30000\cdot11352/360$.
The symmetry removes every odd term. No independence between $q,H$
is used. Put $\rho=1/20000$ and use
\[
 2992u^{3/2}\sqrt h\le h/20+5\cdot2992^2u^3,
\]
\[
 4488uh+2992\sqrt u\,h^{3/2}+748h^2\le8228\rho^2h,
 \qquad (u+h)^3\le4(u^3+h^3).
\]
The added $u^2$ coefficient is
$(5\cdot2992^2+4\cdot946000)\rho^2=151701/1250000<1/8$.
The added $h$ coefficient is
$1/20+8228\rho^2+3784000\rho^4<3/50$.
These exact rational inequalities prove the theorem.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{The variance-compensated tube}
The fixed-buffer result alone does not address lowering the buffer
variance to keep the total remote variance at $v_*$. Retain instead
$\kappa=\mathbb E|q|^4-2u^2$. Since $F'''(v)>0$,
\[
 F(v)+F'(v)u+F''(v)u^2/2\le F(v+u).
\]
The same proof, keeping the remote fourth coefficient exactly, gives:
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:buffered-compensated}
Suppose $7/200\le v\le9/200$, $\|G\|_2\le1/20000$, and
\[
 \kappa\ge776709120u^3.
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{\Lambda(\sqrt vU+G)\le F(v+u)-\kappa/16-h/25
 \le S-\kappa/16-h/25.}
\]
This allows $v+u=v_*$. A convenient sufficient subcase is
$\kappa\ge(3/50)u^2$ and $\|G\|_2\le1/120000$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The remote quartic term and the Gaussian scalar Taylor terms combine
to at most $F(v+u)-\kappa/8$. The mixed fourth and sixth remainders
above cost at most $48544320u^3$ and
$(1/20+8228\rho^2+3784000\rho^4)h$. The hypothesis is precisely
$48544320u^3\le\kappa/16$. The remaining sector price is at least
$h/25$. Finally $776709120/120000^2<3/50$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Limitations.}
These local radii are tiny. The normalized excess in the last corollary
belongs to $q$, not to $q+\sqrt vU$; confusing them would exaggerate
its relevance to the application's kurtosis window. The theorems show
that arbitrary nonlinear shared cubic interactions are locally stable,
including variance compensation under an explicit cumulant condition.
They do not resolve general finite amplitudes, remove the fresh-buffer
independence, or supply the missing row cover. The endpoint is unchanged.

\subsubsection{All-order balanced visible profiles: an exact cumulative sum of squares [P]}
\label{sec:laguerre-profile-sos}
Let $L_n$ and $L_j^{(1)}$ be the ordinary and associated Laguerre polynomials,
normalized by
\[
L_n(t)=\sum_{k=0}^n\frac{(-1)^k}{k!}\binom nk t^k,
\qquad L_j^{(1)}(t)=\sum_{k=0}^j\frac{(-1)^k}{k!}\binom{j+1}{j-k}t^k.
\]
For every $n\ge1$,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\int_0^t e^{-s}(L_n(s)^2-1)\,ds
=-e^{-t}t^2\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{L_j^{(1)}(t)^2}{(j+1)^2}.}
\label{eq:laguerre-profile-sos}
\end{equation}
In fact, the difference between consecutive orders is
\[
\int_0^t e^{-s}(L_n(s)^2-L_{n-1}(s)^2)\,ds
=-e^{-t}\frac{t^2}{n^2}L_{n-1}^{(1)}(t)^2.
\]
Thus the probability densities $e^{-t}L_n(t)^2$ are stochastically
increasing with $n$ and each has at most the exponential mass in $[0,t]$.

\paragraph{Polynomial proof.}
The coefficient formulas give
\[
tL_n''+(1-t)L_n'+nL_n=0,\qquad
L_n-L_{n-1}=\frac tnL_n',\qquad L_n'=-L_{n-1}^{(1)}.
\]
The ODE coefficient identity is
$(k+1)^2a_{k+1}+(n-k)a_k=0$ for
$a_k=(-1)^k\binom nk/k!$; the difference identity follows from Pascal's rule.
For $Q_n=t^2(L_n')^2/n^2$, substitution gives
\[
Q_n-Q_n'=\frac{2t}{n}L_nL_n'-\frac{t^2}{n^2}(L_n')^2
=L_n^2-L_{n-1}^2.
\]
Integrating the derivative of $-e^{-t}Q_n$ and using $Q_n(0)=0$ proves
the adjacent identity. Summation proves \eqref{eq:laguerre-profile-sos}.

For increasing $h$, the penalty associated with the normalized profile
$L_n(t)^2$ is therefore
\[
D_n(V)=\frac12\int_0^\infty
\frac{e^{-t}P_n(t)h'(t)}{(h(t)+V)^{3/2}}\,dt,
\qquad
P_n=t^2\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{L_j^{(1)}(t)^2}{(j+1)^2}.
\]
Since $P_n\ge P_{n-1}\ge P_1=t^2$, these penalties are increasing in
$n$. In the peak setting they are all greater than $31/100$ when
$0<V\le13/20$, whenever the profiles enter a diagonal conditional-variance
sum as above. Balanced visible Wick factors satisfy
$H_{n,n}(Z)/n!=(-1)^nL_n(|Z|^2)$, so the identity applies to those higher
visible grades. It does not eliminate conditional cross terms between
different total chaos grades.

\paragraph{A limitation [P,C].}
The naive charge-one analogue against its gamma ground state is false:
\[
\int_0^t e^{-s}\left(\frac12s(2-s)^2-s\right)ds
=\frac12e^{-t}t^2(1-t)>0\quad(0<t<1).
\]
At $t=1/2$ the difference is exactly $e^{-1/2}/16$. This does not
contradict the cubic diagonal comparison against the exponential ground
state. The portable checker \path{check_laguerre_profile.py} audits
all polynomial identities for $1\le n\le32$ and this counterexample in
exact rational arithmetic. The proof for all orders is the coefficient
argument, not the finite audit.

\subsection{A finite Gram problem for all even interactions with no remote cubic}
\label{sec:even-gram-reduction}
\paragraph{Status [P] reduction; [?] matrix certificate.}
Assume that the remote cubic projection vanishes. Write, in joint-phase
coordinates,
\[
O_T=(T-1)\alpha^\top y+\frac{T}{\sqrt2}\beta^\top\bar y,
\qquad E_T=\sqrt T\,[Y(y)+W(y)]+\nu d(T),
\quad d(T)=\frac{\sqrt T(T-2)}{\sqrt2},
\]
where $Y\in H_{1,1}$ and $W\in H_{2,0}$. Every component may share the
same remote Gaussian drivers. The involution $y\mapsto-y$ changes the
sign of $O$ and fixes $E$, so
\[
\mathbb E(|\ell+O+E|+|\ell-O-E|)
=\mathbb E(|\ell+O+E|+|\ell+O-E|).
\]
The price cross term also vanishes by parity. The two-sign Young bound
therefore proves
\begin{equation}
\Lambda(O+E)\le\Lambda(O)+
\mathbb E\frac{|E_T|^2}{|\ell(T)+O_T|}-p\|E\|_2^2.
\label{eq:even-gram-young}
\end{equation}
An infinite nonnegative expectation is permitted on the right; the
reduction itself does not assume a finite inverse-radius bound.

A unitary transformation puts both linear forms in $O$ into an internal
proper complex Gaussian vector $x\in\mathbb C^k$, $k\le2$. The remaining
coordinates $z$ are independent of $(T,x)$. The weight
$F(T,x)=|\ell(T)+O_T(x)|^{-1}$ is independent of $z$, but all internal
and mixed parts of $Y,W$ are retained.

Let
\[
\mathcal Q_k=\{x_i\bar x_j-\delta_{ij}:1\le i,j\le k\}
\cup\{x_ix_j/\sqrt{1+\delta_{ij}}:1\le i\le j\le k\},
\]
\[
\phi_0=((\sqrt TQ)_{Q\in\mathcal Q_k},d(T)),\qquad
\phi_1=\sqrt T(x_1,\ldots,x_k,\bar x_1,\ldots,\bar x_k).
\]
Define
\[
(M_r)_{ij}=\mathbb E[F\overline{(\phi_r)_i}(\phi_r)_j]
\quad(r=0,1),\qquad M_2=\mathbb E[TF].
\]
The matrix sizes are at most eight and four. If finiteness is not yet
known, these definitions mean extended nonnegative quadratic forms
$v\mapsto\mathbb E[F|\sum_i v_i(\phi_r)_i|^2]$.

The external Hermite decomposition gives exactly three types of block.
External degree zero contains the internal quadratic terms and the
diagonal, hence $M_0$. For each external $z_a$, its coefficient is an
arbitrary linear combination of $x_i$ and $\bar x_i$, arising from mixed
$H_{2,0}$ and $H_{1,1}$ respectively; this gives $M_1$. The coefficient
of $\bar z_a$ uses only $x_i$ and gives a principal submatrix of $M_1$.
External second-chaos terms have constant internal coefficients and
give the scalar $M_2$. External Hermite orthogonality removes all
cross-block terms before an inequality is applied.

The unweighted price is the sum of squared normalized coefficients:
$\mathbb ET=1$, $\mathbb Ed(T)^2=1$, and internal quadratics have mean
zero. Consequently, with
\[
\lambda_* =\max\{\lambda_{\max}(M_0),\lambda_{\max}(M_1),M_2\},
\]
one has
\begin{equation}
\mathbb E[F|E_T|^2]\le\lambda_*\|E\|_2^2.
\label{eq:even-gram-operator}
\end{equation}
When enough external coordinates are allowed, all blocks are realizable,
so this maximum is the exact operator norm of the unrestricted even
weighted form. The dimension reduction is independent of the number of
remote coordinates.

For $A=\|\alpha\|>0$, a convenient four-real-parameter representation is
\[
\alpha^\top y=Ax_1,\qquad
\beta^\top\bar y=b_1\bar x_1+b_2\bar x_2,
\qquad A,b_2\ge0,\quad b_1\in\mathbb C,
\quad B^2=|b_1|^2+b_2^2.
\]
For $A=0$, one anticomplex driver suffices. The proved Gaussian
covariance theorem gives
$\Lambda(O)\le S-A^2/10-B^2/4$. Combining this with
\eqref{eq:even-gram-young}--\eqref{eq:even-gram-operator} yields
\[
\Lambda(O+E)\le S-\frac{A^2}{10}-\frac{B^2}{4}
+(\lambda_*-p)\|E\|_2^2.
\]
The trace-sensitive exclusion already handles every no-remote cubic of
total variance at least $1/12$. Thus the following finite condition
would suffice to settle all remaining no-remote cubics in every dimension:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{(\lambda_*(A,b_1,b_2)-p)_+
\left(\frac1{12}-A^2-|b_1|^2-b_2^2\right)
\le\frac{A^2}{10}+\frac{|b_1|^2+b_2^2}{4}}
\label{eq:even-gram-target}
\end{equation}
on $A,b_2\ge0$, $A^2+|b_1|^2+b_2^2<1/12$. The variance boundary is already handled by the trace theorem, so no matrix check or zero-times-infinity convention is needed there.
This criterion retains the true Gaussian odd-baseline deficit. It has
not been certified and could fail as a sufficient condition even if
the true comparison holds.

\paragraph{Singular fibres and scope.}
At isolated $T$ values an improper conditional Gaussian can lie on a
real line meeting $-\ell(T)$, and its fixed-$T$ inverse-radius moment
can diverge. A certificate must therefore control the joint $(T,x)$
integral, not assume uniform fixed-$T$ boundedness. With a nonlinear
remote cubic $q$, the weight depends on its nonlinear drivers and the
two-coordinate reduction fails; this is explicitly a no-remote result.
The portable \texttt{check\_even\_gram.py} audits Hermite orthogonality,
normalizations and all block counts in exact arithmetic, but certifies
no inverse-radius matrix bound.

\subsubsection{A positive two-integral formula for the Gram matrices [P]}
Write $x_j=(R_{2j-1}+iR_{2j})/\sqrt2$, where $R$ is real standard
Gaussian of dimension at most four. Let $M_t$ be the real two-row matrix
with $(\Re O_t,\Im O_t)^\top=M_tR$, and let
$c_t=(\ell(t),0)^\top$. For $s\ge0$, put
\[
A_s=I_2+2sM_tM_t^\top,\qquad
C_s=(I+2sM_t^\top M_t)^{-1}
=I-2sM_t^\top A_s^{-1}M_t,
\]
\[
m_s=-2sM_t^\top A_s^{-1}c_t,\qquad
Z_s=\frac{\exp(-s c_t^\top A_s^{-1}c_t)}{\sqrt{\det A_s}}.
\]
Completing the Gaussian square proves
\[
\mathbb E[e^{-s|c_t+M_tR|^2}f(R)]
=Z_s\,\mathbb E_{N(m_s,C_s)}f(R)
\]
for nonnegative $f$. Therefore, for every complex quadratic polynomial
$Q_t$,
\begin{equation}
\mathbb E\frac{|Q_T(R)|^2}{|\ell(T)+O_T|}
=\frac1{\sqrt\pi}\int_0^\infty e^{-t}dt
\int_0^\infty s^{-1/2}Z_s\,
\mathbb E_{N(m_s,C_s)}|Q_t(R)|^2\,ds.
\label{eq:even-gram-laplace}
\end{equation}
This follows from the positive Laplace integral for $1/r$ and Tonelli,
so it is valid even when the quadratic form is infinite. Signed matrix
entries can be recovered by polarization; bounding positive quadratic
forms avoids that cancellation. Only a two-by-two inverse is needed.

For an explicit posterior evaluation, write
$Q(R)=R^\top K R+b^\top R+c$ with complex symmetric $K$. Set
\[
q_m=c+b^\top m+m^\top Km+\operatorname{tr}(KC),\qquad b_m=b+2Km.
\]
Translation to a centered Gaussian and orthogonality of its constant,
linear and centered quadratic chaoses give
\begin{equation}
\mathbb E_{N(m,C)}|Q(R)|^2
=|q_m|^2+b_m^*Cb_m+2\operatorname{tr}(KC K^*C).
\label{eq:even-gram-positive-moment}
\end{equation}
All terms are nonnegative. Since every Gram basis polynomial has degree
at most two, \eqref{eq:even-gram-laplace}--\eqref{eq:even-gram-positive-moment}
reduce the matrix problem to two positive scalar integrals and elementary
matrix algebra. The tails and parameter cover are still unproved; this
is a deterministic reduction, not a numerical certificate.

The standard-library checker \texttt{check\_gaussian\_posterior.py} audits 96 exact complex quadratic moment identities and 12 Gaussian completion, determinant and Woodbury identities. Its passing log is \texttt{replay\_gaussian\_posterior.log}; no integration tail or parameter cover is certified by this algebra audit.

\subsubsection{Exploratory matrix evidence [F], not a certificate}
The exact Gram reduction has been tested numerically in the one-driver case
$O_T=(T-1)\alpha X+(T/\sqrt2)\beta\bar X$.
All three blocks were included. A Laplace calculation integrates the Gaussian
polynomial moments analytically and then numerically integrates in $T$ and
the Laplace parameter, splitting at the isolated degenerate Gaussian fibres.

The broad scan used 2,046 points with real $\beta$ (relative phases zero or
$\pi$), with $\alpha^2+\beta^2$ between $0.02$ and $0.08$. The twelve
largest sampled criterion ratios were refined. The largest refined ratio was
approximately $0.90308657$ at $\alpha=\beta=\sqrt{0.027}$, with
$\lambda_*\approx1.61298937$ and criterion left minus right approximately
$-0.000915832$. This is a sampled value, not a uniform margin or global optimum.

Direct tensor Gauss--Laguerre quadrature had produced apparent ratios above
one. They were unstable near the singular fibres and disappeared under the
split Laplace calculation. They are not counterexamples.
The numerical calculation truncates $T$ at 40 without an interval tail bound,
uses floating-point eigenvalues and quadrature, and does not cover arbitrary
relative phase or the two-driver case. No matrix inequality is certified by
these experiments. The portable proof replay does not depend on the optional
NumPy/SciPy exploration. Full data and replay instructions are in
\path{probe/FLOAT_ONLY.md}.
