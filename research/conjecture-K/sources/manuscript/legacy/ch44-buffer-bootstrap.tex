\begin{corollary}[A coupled complement paid by the deficit, \st{P}]
\label{cor:bootstrap-complement}
Let \(J\) be any square-integrable complex random variable on the
original Gaussian space such that
\(\E[J\mid Z,X]=0\). It may depend on \(Z,X\) and arbitrarily many
additional coordinates. The buffer is independent of \(J\). Then,
for \(g\ge41/250\),
\[
 \boxed{\Lambda(Q_0+J+gU)\le
 S-\frac{m(c)}{32}-\frac{r^2}{2000}
                         +\frac{41}{10}\|J\|_2^2.}
\]
In particular exact K follows whenever
\[
 \|J\|_2^2\le \frac5{656}m(c)+\frac{r^2}{8200}.
\]
On \(41/250\le g\le1/6\), the right side of the first bound has
the additional term \(-1/20000\).
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Set \(\rho_g(u)=\E|\sqrt u+gU|\) and
\[
 K_g(u)=2\rho_g'(u)
 =\frac1{g\sqrt\pi}\int_0^1e^{-ut/g^2}
                       \sqrt{\frac{1-t}{t}}\,dt .
\]
Concavity of \(\rho_g\) gives its global quadratic tangent at each
of \(L+Q_0\) and \(L-Q_0\). The linear terms in \(J\) vanish by
conditional centering. The variance cross term vanishes for the
same reason. The remaining cost is at most
\[
 \left(\frac{\sqrt\pi}{2g}-p\right)\|J\|_2^2
 <\frac{41}{10}\|J\|_2^2 .
\]
The last comparison follows at \(g=41/250\) by squaring positive
rational bounds and using \(\pi<22/7\); it persists at larger \(g\).
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:retained-buffer-bootstrap}.
This argument uses a proved deficit and does not discard its cost
when conditioning.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Geometric propagation of the remote baseline}
\paragraph{Status [P]; finite arithmetic audit [C].}
Use the established marginal Gaussian scale mixture
$L\overset d=\sqrt VZ_0$. Let $J$ be any remote square-integrable random
variable, independent of the visible variable, with $\mathbb E|J|^2=1$.
Let $U$ be a fresh proper complex Gaussian and fix $g>0$.
For $\Phi_g(r)=\Lambda(\sqrt rJ+gU)$, the positive Rice integral gives
\[
 k_g(r):=\Phi_g'(r)+p
 =\mathbb E_{V,R}\frac{R}{\sqrt{\pi(V+g^2)}}
  \int_0^1 e^{-rRt/(V+g^2)}t^{-1/2}(1-t)^{1/2}\,dt,\qquad R=|J|^2.
\]
This is a positive Laplace transform of a finite measure. Its total
mass is at most $\sqrt\pi/(2g)$, so only square integrability of $J$
is needed. H\"older's inequality proves log-convexity:
\[
 k_g((1-t)r_0+tr_1)\le k_g(r_0)^{1-t}k_g(r_1)^t.
\]
If $A,B>0$ certify the two endpoint upper bounds and
$\Delta=r_1-r_0>0$, integration yields
\[
 \Phi_g(r_1)\le\Phi_g(r_0)+
 \Delta\left(\int_0^1A^{1-t}B^t\,dt-p\right).
\]
The logarithmic mean in this expression satisfies
\[
 \int_0^1A^{1-t}B^t\,dt
 \le\frac{A+4\sqrt{AB}+B}{6}.
\]
Indeed, writing $A=me^x$, $B=me^{-x}$ reduces the inequality to
$\sinh(x)/x\le(\cosh(x)+2)/3$. For every $n\ge1$, comparison of the
$x^{2n}$ coefficients is exactly $2n+1\ge3$.
Thus the directly implementable improvement is
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\Phi_g(r_1)\le\Phi_g(r_0)+
 \Delta\left(\frac{A+4\sqrt{AB}+B}{6}-p\right).}
 \label{eq:geometric-baseline-propagation}
\end{equation}
It improves the arithmetic-trapezoid increment by precisely
$\Delta(\sqrt A-\sqrt B)^2/3$.
At $r_i=c_i^2>0$, the existing anchor convention gives
$A=p+\overline d_c(c_i)/c_i$; the price must be added before
taking geometric means. Uniform endpoint bounds over a buffer interval
remain valid. Since $k_g$ decreases, one may replace $B$ by $\min(A,B)$.

For any dyadic $N$, generate $K_j=A^{1-j/N}B^{j/N}$ by repeated
outward square roots. A sharper upper mean is
\[
 \frac1{6N}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}
   (K_j+4\sqrt{K_jK_{j+1}}+K_{j+1}).
\]
These finite expressions converge to the logarithmic mean. For compatible
exact endpoints $A\ge B$, the latter is optimal among positive Laplace
transforms with those endpoints, since a single exponential attains it.
This optimality concerns abstract endpoint information only.

If $\mathbb E|J|^2=m\ne1$, replace the price in the derivative and
propagation formulas by $pm$. The buffer price remains $pg^2$.
The remote-independence and fresh-buffer hypotheses remain essential;
this argument is not a reversed heat comparison.

\subsection{Radial contraction in every pure chaos order}
\paragraph{Status [P]; finite polynomial audit [C].}
The preceding cubic contraction is a special case of the following
all-degree statement. For any complex-valued element $Q$ of one fixed
real Gaussian chaos order, any standard complex Gaussian coordinate $Z$,
and every nonnegative decreasing measurable $k$,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\mathbb E[k(|Z|^2)|Q|^2]\le
 \mathbb E k(|Z|^2)\,\|Q\|_2^2.}
 \label{eq:all-degrees-radial}
\end{equation}
In particular this holds on every fixed $\mathcal H_{p,q}$.
It does not assert the same for sums of distinct chaos orders.

Write $H_{mn}$ for the complex Hermite polynomial of squared norm $m!n!$,
and $P_{mn}(t)=|H_{mn}(z,\bar z)|^2/(m!n!)$, $t=|z|^2$.
For $m\ge n$,
\[
 P_{mn}(t)=\frac{n!}{m!}t^{m-n}(L_n^{m-n}(t))^2.
\]
Put $M=m+n$ and
\[
 a_k=[x^k](1+x)^m(1-x)^n,\qquad
 w_{mn,k}=\frac{k!(M-k)!a_k^2}{2^Mm!n!}.
\]
There is an exact nonnegative-mixture identity
\begin{equation}
 P_{mn}(t)=\sum_{k=0}^M w_{mn,k}(-1)^kL_k(2t),\qquad
 w_{mn,k}\ge0,\quad\sum_k w_{mn,k}=1.
 \label{eq:positive-hermite-laguerre-mixture}
\end{equation}
Here is a proof that covers arbitrary indices. If $\zeta,\eta$ are
independent standard complex Gaussians, expansion of
$(\sqrt s\,\zeta+\eta)^m(\sqrt s\,\zeta-\eta)^n$ and holomorphic
orthogonality give
\[
 \sum_k w_{mn,k}s^k
 =\frac1{m!n!}\mathbb E
 \left|\frac{\sqrt s\,\zeta+\eta}{\sqrt2}\right|^{2m}
 \left|\frac{\sqrt s\,\zeta-\eta}{\sqrt2}\right|^{2n}.
\]
The Gaussian quadratic integral therefore yields
\[
 \sum_{m,n,k}w_{mn,k}u^mv^ns^k
 =\frac1{1-(u+v)(1+s)/2+uvs}.
\]
Set $s=1$ for normalization. Extract the coefficient in $s$, then use
the Laguerre generating function: the right side of
\eqref{eq:positive-hermite-laguerre-mixture}, summed against $u^mv^n$,
equals
\[
 \frac1{1-uv}\exp\left(\frac{u+v-2uv}{1-uv}\,t\right).
\]
This also generates $P_{mn}$. Indeed,
$H_{mn}(z,\bar z)=\mathbb E(z+iB)^m(\bar z+i\bar B)^n$.
Use two independent copies and sum the exponential series. The resulting
complex Gaussian integral has matrix
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&v\\u&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$
and linear terms $i\bar z(u,v)$, $iz(v,u)^{\mathsf T}$; its determinant
and completing-square exponent give exactly the displayed function.
All manipulations are justified near zero and then by coefficient
extraction.

Define $T_k$ by
\[
 \int_x^\infty e^{-t}(-1)^kL_k(2t)\,dt=e^{-x}T_k(x).
\]
Its generating function is
$(1-s)^{-1}\exp(2xs/(1+s))$, so $T_k(0)=1$ and
$T_k'(x)=2\sum_{\ell<k}(-1)^\ell L_\ell(2x)$.
The required positivity is the following explicit Hermite-square identity:
\[
 \sum_{\ell=0}^n(-1)^\ell L_\ell(2x)
 =\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}
 \frac{\binom{2j}{j}}{4^j}
 \frac{(H^{\rm phys}_{n-2j}(\sqrt x))^2}
      {2^{n-2j}(n-2j)!}\ge0\quad(x\ge0).
\]
To verify it in every degree, multiply the real Mehler generating
function by $(1-s^2)^{-1/2}$; both sides generate
$(1-s^2)^{-1}\exp(2xs/(1+s))$.
Thus $T_k(x)\ge1$. Equation
\eqref{eq:positive-hermite-laguerre-mixture} now proves
\[
 \int_x^\infty e^{-t}P_{mn}(t)\,dt\ge e^{-x}.
\]
Each normalized radial profile therefore stochastically dominates the
exponential law, giving the required inequality for decreasing $k$.

For fixed total chaos order $N$, expand $Q$ in
$H_{ij}(Z,\bar Z)/\sqrt{i!j!}$ with remote coefficient of order $N-i-j$.
Different $i+j$ are orthogonal remotely. With equal $i+j$, unequal
$(i,j)$ have unequal angular frequencies $i-j$, so their cross terms
vanish after visible phase integration. Apply the one-coordinate
inequality to each squared term to obtain \eqref{eq:all-degrees-radial}.
Finite-coordinate approximation and monotone truncation give the
corresponding infinite-dimensional statement.
The fixed-order hypothesis is essential: $Q=2-|Z|^2$ and $k(t)=e^{-t}$
satisfy $\|Q\|_2^2=2$, $\mathbb Ek=1/2$, but
$\mathbb E[k|Q|^2]=5/4>1$.

\paragraph{Buffered consequence.}
The two-sign Rice tangent now proves for every fixed pure chaos order
\[

\]
where $U$ is fresh and independent, and the visible profile and $\kappa_0$
are unchanged; here $F(v)=2\mathbb E|L+\sqrt vU|-pv\le S$.
Hence the already certified scalar thresholds
$g\ge477/1000$, $g\ge12/25$, and $g\ge1/2$ give penalties
$1/2000$, $1/200$, and $3/80$, respectively, in every pure order.
This is an analytic extension requiring no new scalar integration.
It does not remove the buffer or prove an endpoint.

\paragraph{Unsmoothed variance exclusion.}
For $V=\|Q\|_2^2+g^2$ and
$J(V)=2\mathbb E\sqrt{T(a+bT)^2+V}-pV$, one also has
\begin{equation}
 \Lambda(Q+gU)\le J(V).
 \label{eq:pure-order-trace}
\end{equation}
Indeed,
$|l+x|+|l-x|\le2\sqrt{|l|^2+|x|^2}$.
The square-root tangent at $T(a+bT)^2+V$ gives an error
\[
 \mathbb E
 \frac{|Q+gU|^2-V}{\sqrt{T(a+bT)^2+V}},
\]
which is nonpositive by \eqref{eq:all-degrees-radial} after integrating

At $V=0$ the conclusion is direct.
The existing exact scalar certificate
$J(13/20)<\Lambda(0)-1/2000$ and $J'<0$ for $V\ge13/20$

\[
 \boxed{V\ge13/20\ \Longrightarrow\

for every fixed pure order and every $g\ge0$.
