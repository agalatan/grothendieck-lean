\subsection{Exactly what still obstructs $1.402$}
\label{sec:obstruction-1402-audit}

\paragraph{A complete gain that does not close this gap.}
The contraction-Gram certificate of
\S\ref{sec:contraction-gram-20261022}, at $\epsilon=149/200$,
gives the current complete endpoint $1.401691989174\stale{23 Oct}{now $\KG>1.401695322458$}$, with
its Lean integration and analytic-input status in \autoref{ch:certified}.
It improves the preceding $1.401687463503$ joint refined spectral/covariance
envelope and is independent of raw K. It does not supply the missing shared-response
inequality at $\epsilon=1891/2500$ described below; the source-cap,
Gaussian reduction and transfer inputs retain their stated status.

\paragraph{The target and its quantifiers.}
Fix one witness $T=P_{1,0}-\epsilon P_{2,1}$, with
$\epsilon=1891/2500$. The sufficient scalar target is
\[
 \sup_{n\ge1}\ \sup_{|f_\pm|\le1}
 \Re\langle f_+,Tf_-\rangle\le B,\qquad B<500/701.
\]
The strict, uniform margin is necessary to assert $K_G^{\mathbb C}>1.402$;
a cap only equal to $500/701$ yields $K_G^{\mathbb C}\ge1.402$.
The vector witness in Proposition~\ref{prop:exact-vector-transfer-audit}
and the functional transfer then give $K_G^{\mathbb C}\ge1/B$.
One successful peak row, or strict inequalities without a uniform
margin over all dimensions and rows, do not establish this target.

\paragraph{The exact missing inequality is about actual responses.}
Put $h=(f_++f_-)/2$, $k=(f_+-f_-)/2$, rotate $Ph=tZ$, and write
$F=\epsilon Qk$. Necessarily $0\le t\le\sqrt\pi/2$. For fixed
dimension define the closed convex value function
\[
 J_n(t,F)=\inf\{\|Pk\|^2+\epsilon\|Qh\|^2:
        |h\pm k|\le1,\ Ph=tZ,\ Qk=F/\epsilon\}.
\]
The infimum is $+\infty$ for infeasible pairs. The exact norm row is
$t^2+\|F\|^2/\epsilon-J_n(t,F)$. Thus the precise sufficient target is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:obstruction-exact-response}
 \boxed{\quad J_n(t,F)\ge t^2+\|F\|^2/\epsilon-B
 \quad\text{for every }n,t,F,\qquad B<500/701.\quad}
\end{equation}
This is weaker than raw K: it needs no claim about cubics that cannot
arise from bounded responses. Its support-function dual is already
proved in \S\ref{sec:canonical-response-cap}; the missing part is a
uniformly successful family of dual certificates, not duality itself.

\paragraph{What is now available.}
The sharp influence estimate, fourth-moment cap $44$, sixth-moment
constant $40.45313$, and radius $0.95133$ are mathematical
\st{P,C} results for genuine $\mathcal W_{2,1}$ cubics.
In particular all actual responses with $t\le0.1694$ are covered by
Theorem~\ref{thm:real-stein-radius-20261021} and its row corollary.
Independently, the moment-free response ellipse and anisotropic
certificate cover
\[
 e+\frac{191}{500}t^2\le\frac9{40},\qquad\text{or}\qquad
 0\le t\le0.851,\quad e\le0.0148,
 \qquad e=\|\E[F\mid X]\|^2.
\]
The response-moment slab of \autoref{thm:response-moment-slab} additionally
covers a specified six-moment projection box on $0.84\le t\le0.851$
with full variance $\|F\|^2\le1/20$, giving a cap below $0.7124$.
It allows arbitrary imaginary coefficients and arbitrary extra coordinates.
Its full-variance budget must not be substituted for the remote variance $e$
in the preceding criteria. No theorem yet places every surviving tensor in
one of these projection boxes.
The weighted full-energy theorem
\ref{thm:weighted-full-energy-response} requires no such projection box:
it gives $\mathcal N\le0.71321135$ for $0\le t\le0.851$ and
$\mathcal V_*\le0.0325$, or $\mathcal N\le0.71325087$ at the peak
for $\mathcal V_*\le0.03744$. Its weighted full energy must not be
substituted for remote $e$ in the other criteria.
The larger adjusted-energy criterion and the tensor-specific support
criteria must also be retained. Restricted raw-K theorems give further
regions only when their buffer, influence, driver and sector hypotheses
hold. These are upper bounds to take in minimum, not deficits to add.

\paragraph{A quantitatively located central gap.}
On $0.84\le t\le0.851$, any response not covered by the anisotropic
theorem must satisfy
\[
 \mathcal E_{\rm adj}>0.0148+0.3(0.851-t),
\]
with $\mathcal E_{\rm adj}$ exactly as in
\eqref{eq:aniso-adjusted}. The retained phase and completion defects
impose the still stronger necessary condition in
\S\ref{sec:canonical-response-ellipse}.
The remote feasibility theorem gives $e<0.046266$ throughout this
central interval (and smaller bounds toward its right end).
At $t=0.8456$, for example, the uncovered range is contained in
\[
 0.0166059<\mathcal E_{\rm adj}\le e<0.041319.
\]
For the stricter design cap $B_*=0.713260$, the new full-energy
certificate imposes the additional peak condition
$\mathcal V_*>0.037447304$ on any response with $\mathcal N>B_*$;
see \eqref{eq:weighted-full-energy-survivor} for the full $t$ dependence.
This does not conflict with the older full-energy upper bound
$\|F\|^2<0.071981$, so those scalar constraints alone do not finish
the cover.
This is an outer description of the remaining possibilities, not a
construction of feasible responses and not a counterexample. Such a
response must additionally evade every applicable support, cumulant,
influence and restricted-family theorem.

The central remote anchor has
\[
 \ell=1057/1250,\quad\beta=179/1250,\quad
 C'=2747927/2000000,\quad
 B_0(t)=t^2-2\ell t+2\beta^2/\epsilon+C'.
\]
The exact remaining budget is
\[
 \min_{0.84\le t\le0.851}\{500/701-B_0(t)\}
 =\frac{240297199}{2651182000000}
 =0.00009063776044\ldots .
\]
It is room above the certified remote cap $C'$, not above the Gaussian
value $S$. A raw-K theorem must pay for general shared $A/B$ and their
simultaneous interactions within this budget; $A$-only statements
with error $10^{-4}$ do not do so automatically.

\paragraph{A concrete weaker replacement for raw K.}
The existing joint support theorem supplies another sufficient route.
Use its notation $D(v)$, $m(v)$, $a_1(v)$, $a_2(v)$, $E_0$ and
$\theta=250/517$ from \eqref{eq:crc-transfer}. Define
\[
\begin{split}
 \mathcal R_\mu(v)={}&\mu\{D(v)-\theta m(v)^2\}
 -\frac{\mu\theta}{1+\mu\theta}\|a_1(v)\|^2
 +\frac{\mu}{\mu/\epsilon-8}\|a_2(v)\|^2\\
 &-\left(\frac{\mu}{4\epsilon^2}-\frac1\epsilon\right)E_0,
 \qquad\mu>8\epsilon.
\end{split}
\]
For each actual response, the theorem gives
$\mathcal N<B_0(t)+\mathcal R_\mu(v)$.
Consequently it suffices to prove that every surviving central response
admits a remote cubic test $v$ and $\mu>8\epsilon$ with
\[
 \mathcal R_\mu(v)\le500/701-B_0(t)-\zeta
 \quad\text{for one uniform }\zeta>0.
\]
This existence assertion is \st{?}: it is a specific possible covering
lemma, not a necessary consequence of K or a theorem established here.
If it fails, the full canonical response dual still remains available.
Neither choosing $v$ in finitely many tested coordinate dimensions nor
optimizing it for selected tensors proves the required universal cover.
Indeed \S\ref{sec:proportional-support-obstruction-20261022} now proves
that all proportional tests $v=\lambda q$, even combined and with their
scalar parameters fully optimised, leave a gap in the diffuse
Gaussian-grade relaxation. At $e=1/25$ their minimum transfer cost is
$0.0007977136526\ldots$, above the peak allowance. This is not a
counterexample to the existence assertion with arbitrary $v$, nor to K;
it is a stop rule for that restricted search family.

\paragraph{The specific quadratic relaxation now identified.}
The weighted Gram minimizer $k_0$ has the prescribed cubic moments but
need not obey $|k_0|\le1$. The discarded cost is exactly
$\|k-k_0\|_{M+P}^2$, and therefore at least
$\E\operatorname{dist}_M(k_0,\{u:|u|\le1\})^2$.
\S\ref{sec:clipped-response-feasibility} gives its exact scalar-root
formula and a closed lower estimate within the sharp factor $8/9$.
The exact joint defect in \S\ref{sec:joint-response-defect} is stronger:
it retains both disks $|h\pm k|\le1$ and has an explicit rational
lower formula within the sharp factor $81/250$. It replaces, rather
than adds to, the prior clipping and $h$-phase corrections.
A projected-price refinement now excludes $\mathcal V_*\le0.03748$
at the peak and $\mathcal V_*\le0.03255$ for $t\le0.851$.
The rational hierarchy in \S\ref{sec:joint-dual-descent} now retains
more than $99.99\%$ of this defect with an explicit error, and an
averaged finite Gram matrix gives a guaranteed descent of the joint
dual. Thus the factor $81/250$ is no longer an intrinsic approximation
loss. A uniform bound for those joint-law expectations on the surviving
complement is still open. The dual is minimized over trial parameters;
none of its Gaussian-grade diagnostics proves this uniform statement.
Separately, the exact visible scalar response geometry still admits
formal objective above $0.7175$ if cubic projection energy is constrained
only by total response energy; see
\S\ref{sec:exact-visible-response-geometry}. Hence scalar precision is
not the only obstruction. The location of energy in the projection,
and its cost for the same bounded response, must be retained.

\paragraph{Why the scalar improvements have not closed the gap.}
The law of $|q|$ controls the remote payoff. Shared sectors also depend
on the joint law and relative phase of $(q,r_A,r_B,Y,W)$ and on the
negative projections $Pk,Qh$. The new scalar moments restrict the
first object without determining the others. Conversely, the quadratic
response certificates retain every sector but leave a positive remote
variance coefficient, $0.54e$ in \eqref{eq:aniso-rational}, or a positive
weighted full-energy cost in \eqref{eq:weighted-full-energy-cap}; they
remain insufficient on the intersection of their high-energy complements.
The needed improvement must couple these two kinds of information for
the same response, rather than combine separately optimized losses.

\paragraph{What is not being claimed.}
No failure of K or of the true norm bound is known here. The positive
curvatures and failed relaxations are failures of particular upper
bounds. The present sharper radius does not repair the central gap,
and the sharp influence theorem does not create a fresh independent
Gaussian buffer. Even a complete central theorem must be followed by
a certified cover of the other $t$ rows at this same $\epsilon$.
Separately, a kernel-checked endpoint requires the bidegree/tensor
construction and the scalar reduction to be connected to the existing
Lean transfer theorem. The mathematical coverage gap and those Lean
assembly obligations are distinct.

\paragraph{Why the stronger multiplication lemma does not finish the cover.}
Corollary~\ref{cor:quadratic-all-sector-refinement} controls every visible
sector on arbitrary coupled cubics when a fresh buffer $g\ge9/40$ is
already present, with $e\le1/25$ and $h\le e/10$.
An actual cubic need not have this independent component.
Inserting it by Jensen only gives
\[
 \Lambda(G)\le\Lambda(G+gU)+pg^2,
 \qquad p(9/40)^2=\frac{2025}{30256}>0.0669,
\]
where $U$ is independent proper Gaussian and centered.
Indeed the two norm expectations increase on adding centered noise,
whereas the variance penalty increases by $pg^2$.
This valid but crude transfer spends vastly more than the central
allowance $240297199/2651182000000$.
Thus the new quadratic multiplication estimates strengthen an actual
buffered theorem; they do not justify pretending that every cubic has
such a buffer. A sharper joint deficit/replacement or original-response
certificate is still needed on the complement above.

\paragraph{The dominant Wick-direction branch is now covered.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:contact-concentration-cone} now gives an additional,
dimension-free necessary condition on every central-row survivor:
\[
 \sup_{X\perp Z}|\langle H_X,F\rangle|^2<\frac45\|F\|^2.
\]
The complementary concentration branch has cap $0.71315$ with arbitrary
coupled remainder, so it requires neither a raw-K theorem nor an
independent Gaussian buffer. The proof retains the first/cubic moments
of the same response pair in a two-coordinate support inequality.
It does not force the surviving tensors to have small influence: the
unit mixed monomial $X_1^2\bar X_2/\sqrt2$ has maximal diagonal Wick
share $4/27$.

\paragraph{The mixed-square branch is now covered too.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:mixed-contact-concentration} removes the latter
concentration type with an arbitrary coupled remainder. Every remaining
central response must also satisfy
\[
 \sup_{\substack{X,Y\perp Z\\X\perp Y}}
 \left|\left\langle X^2\bar Y/\sqrt2,F\right\rangle\right|^2
       <\frac{179}{200}\|F\|^2.
\]
The complementary branch has cap $0.713225$; at share $9/10$ its cap is
$0.71295$. Two fixed support integrals and four rational comparisons
suffice, with no independence assumption on the remainder.
These individual tests did not exhaust concentrated tensors:
$X_1X_2\bar X_3$ has largest diagonal-Wick share $2/27$ and
mixed-square share $1/2$. That example is now covered by the following
whole-subspace branch.

\paragraph{The remaining obstruction after the whole-subspace tests.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:whole-slice-contact} removes two larger concentration
types, with arbitrary coupled remainders. Every central survivor must
also have
\[
 \sup_{X\perp Z}\|\Pi_{\mathcal S_X}F\|^2<\tfrac9{10}\|F\|^2,
 \qquad
 \sup_{Y\perp Z}\|\Pi_{\mathcal A_Y}F\|^2<\tfrac{197}{200}\|F\|^2,
\]
where $\mathcal S_X=\{{:}X^2\bar A{:}/\sqrt2:A\perp Z\}$ and
$\mathcal A_Y=\bar Y\mathcal H_{2,0}(\{Z,Y\}^\perp)$.
The two complementary branches have caps $0.71315$ and $0.713225$;
the latter allows unbounded quadratic rank. It includes the formerly
uncovered trilinear example and
$\bar Y\sum_{j=1}^NX_j^2/\sqrt{2N}$ for arbitrary $N$.

An explicit shape escaping both single-subspace tests is
$F_0=(X_1^2\bar X_2+X_2^2\bar X_1)/2$. It is unit, both largest
subspace shares are $1/2$, and its largest combined directional
influence is $3/2$. Theorem~\ref{thm:two-slice-contact} now covers its
whole two-slice concentration neighbourhood, and adds the necessary
survivor condition
\[
 \sup_{\substack{X_1,X_2\perp Z\\X_1\perp X_2}}
 \|\Pi_{\mathcal S_{X_1}\oplus\mathcal S_{X_2}}F\|^2
       <\frac{39}{40}\|F\|^2.
\]
The complementary branch has cap $0.7132$; at share $49/50$ it has
cap $0.7130$. There is no independence assumption on the remainder.

These tests still do not force small influence. The unit three-cycle
$(X_1^2\bar X_2+X_2^2\bar X_3+X_3^2\bar X_1)/\sqrt6$
has maximal two-slice share $2/3$, single-slice and orthogonal
antiholomorphic-block shares $1/3$, and combined directional influence
$1$. This is geometry of the uncovered concentration complement, not
a violation of K or an asserted high-payoff response.
Moreover the canonical many-slice Jensen majorant of
Lemma~\ref{lem:multislice-support-reduction} loses the diffuse phase
spread and tends to constant modulus. Increasing its rank cutoff is
not by itself a route to a sharp dimension-free theorem.
The phase-preserving theorem adds a different exclusion: for every
orthogonal remote input split $(\mathcal A,\mathcal B)$, a surviving
response must have
\[
 \|\Pi_{\mathcal H_{2,0}(\mathcal A)\mathcal H_{0,1}(\mathcal B)}F\|^2
 <\eta_m\|F\|^2,
 \quad (m,\eta_m)\in
 \{(2,191/200),(4,49/50),(8,99/100),(16,499/500),(24,1999/2000)\},
\]
whenever $\dim\mathcal A\le m$; the complementary branches have cap
$0.71325$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:phase-preserving-contact}.
The optimal support for this test class is explicitly attained and
has the correct Gaussian limit. This repairs the phase-averaging loss
on that class. It does not put every self-interacting or diffuse
survivor into one of the projection regions.
What remains is a joint-response certificate on the coupled complement,
or an exhaustive structural alternative with a quantitative response
cost, followed by the flank rows at the same witness parameter.

\paragraph{Updated obstruction after whole-core certification.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:spherical-core-contact} now covers every overlapping
cubic on a core of at most eight remote coordinates under its full-energy
share conditions. It supersedes the three-cycle as an example outside
all certified concentration regions: the entire three-coordinate core
is covered at share $481/500$, with arbitrary dependent remainder.
For a central survivor, necessarily
\[
 \sup_{\substack{\mathcal A\perp Z\\\dim\mathcal A\le n}}
 \|\Pi_{\mathcal H_{2,1}(\mathcal A)}F\|^2<\eta_n V,
 \quad (n,\eta_n)\in
 \{(2,187/200),(3,481/500),(4,122/125),(6,124/125),(8,9999/10000)\}.
\]
This does not justify a small-influence conclusion. The normalized
$Q_N=X_1(\sum_j|X_j|^2-N-1)/\sqrt{N+1}$ has maximal influence
$(N+5)/(N+1)\to1$, while its best $n$-coordinate core share is
$(n+1)/(N+1)\to0$. These are exact formulas, not a numerical
counterexample to K. A fixed rank cutoff or a maximal-influence cutoff
alone therefore does not give an exhaustive structural alternative.
The following new response branch removes $Q_N$ itself for large $N$.

\paragraph{Rank-free linear--quadratic factors: a complete regional cover.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:conditional-response-contact} covers all $t$ at
$\epsilon=1891/2500$ whenever a remote exactly-one-leg module carries
at least $499/500$ of the full energy. Its cap is $0.71325$, with
arbitrary quadratic ranks, phases, both conditional covariance channels,
and a fully dependent remainder. In particular it covers $F=cQ_N$
for $N\ge999$. Thus that family no longer witnesses an uncovered
branch, even though its coordinate-core obstruction remains valid.
Every response exceeding $0.71325$ must additionally satisfy
\[
 \sup_{\mathcal A\subset Z^\perp}
 \norm{\Pi_{\mathcal M(\mathcal A)}F}^2<\frac{499}{500}\norm F^2.
\]
The exact entrance statistic is
$I_1-I_2+I_3/2$ from \eqref{eq:one-leg-projection}; the full rank of
its quadratic factor does not enter the scalar certificate.
The paired support identity charges the remaining energy through
$p\mapsto p\eta$, without an independent-noise substitution.

The open problem is the joint response on the complement of this
condition and all the earlier tests. No proved alternative puts that
complement into small influence, independent blocks, or finite cores.
Cubics can distribute substantial energy among several leg-occupancy
sectors. This structural gap remains after the new branch's flank rows
are covered. The one-sided support shortcut is unavailable;
\S\ref{sec:gaussian-point-cone-reach} retains the paired norm required
for visible-dependent tests. No global endpoint follows from the new
regional theorem alone.
