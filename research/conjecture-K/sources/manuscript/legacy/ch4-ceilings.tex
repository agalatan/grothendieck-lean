\section{Witness ceilings: numerical and proved}\label{ch:ceilings}
\chapsummary{Restricted-adversary calculations give numerical ceilings \st{F}; the closed-form and chirp paragraphs below give separate proved obstructions \st{P,C}. None determines the unrestricted operator norm. Keep the degree, witness parameter, adversary class and proof status attached to each number.}

A certificate for a game $Q$ can never beat the true norm $N=\sup_n\|Q\|_{\infty\to1}$. We compute $N$ in a model that is attained in the
limit, so the model value is a \emph{lower} bound for $N$ and therefore a genuine upper limit on what any certificate with that witness can
reach. The numerical tables and stability searches below are \st{F} with accuracy $10^{-8}$ to $10^{-6}$ (grids stated); the analytic obstructions are labeled separately. Files: \pathref{truenorm/} (\code{results.txt},
\code{results5.txt}, \code{lib5x.py}, \code{lib7.py}, \code{epstable.log}), \pathref{pipeline/deg9/RESULTS.md} (\code{eng9.py}),
\pathref{/private/tmp/claude-501/\{wval,wtrue\}/}.

\subsection{The asymptotic model}
For $f=F(z_1,W)$ with $W=n^{-1/2}\sum_{j\ge2}H(z_j)$ the limit pairing of $T=\Pc10-\varepsilon\Pc21$ is given by the rank-3 operator
$T=P_{z_1}-\varepsilon(P_{e_2}+P_W)$ on $L^2(\C^2)$, $e_2=H(z_1)$ (cross terms vanish like $O(1/n)$; the sign flip $W\to-W$ between $f$ and $g$ is the
best response). Since $T$ has finite rank, every extremiser is $\ph(h)$ with $h$ in the range, so $N$ is a three-parameter radial quadrature; the
$W$-integrals have closed forms ($\E_w\ph(k+\gamma w)=\ph(k)A(|k|/|\gamma|)$, $A(\kappa)=\frac{\sqrt\pi}2\kappa e^{-\kappa^2/2}[I_0+I_1](\kappa^2/2)$).
Validation: plain phase gives $\pi/4-\varepsilon\pi/32$ exactly; $n=1$ alone has the plain phase as global extremiser; a second linear coordinate
or the $(1,1)$ Gaussian coordinate adds nothing beyond the $W$ model.

\paragraph{A closed-form ceiling for degrees $\le5$ \st{P} (GPT, 22 October; IDE-checked).}
The phase kernel is $h(t)=\frac\pi4\bigl(t+\frac{t^3}8+\frac{3t^5}{64}+\frac{25t^7}{1024}+\cdots\bigr)$. Put $\rho(t)=at-\frac{a^3}8t^3$ with
$a+\frac{a^3}8=1$, so $a=0.906795303\ldots$. Then $h(\rho(t))=\frac{\pi a}4t+\frac{\pi a^7}{4096}t^7+O(t^9)$: the cubic \emph{and} the quintic terms vanish. The quintic
cancellation is the identity $\frac3{64}=3(\frac18)^2$. The pair $F_N=\ph(\sqrt a\,Z_0+\sqrt{a^3/8}\,W_N)$, $G_N=\ph(\sqrt a\,Z_0-\sqrt{a^3/8}\,W_N)$, with
$W_N$ the normalized diffuse cubic, realizes the inner correlation $\rho(t)$ in the limit $N\to\infty$, because $W_N$ correlates as $t^3$ under the OU flow.
Hence every game $\sum_{k\le2}\lambda_kP_{k+1,k}$ with $\lambda_0=1$ has norm at least $\pi a/4=0.7121953656$, whatever $\lambda_1,\lambda_2$ are. Every degree-$\le5$
witness is therefore capped at $4/(\pi a)=1.4041091087$. This is weaker than the numerical degree-5 ceiling $1.403641$ above, but it is analytic.
Cancelling $t^7$ as well, with budget $a+\frac{a^3}8+\frac{a^7}{1024}=1$, gives only $1.4046904015$ for degree $\le7$. That is weaker than the numerical $1.404111$, so it is
\emph{not} evidence that degree~7 has that much room. The IDE checked the series and both ceilings numerically. The degree-7 game of the table scores only $0.7121016$ against this
adversary. Its numerical norm $0.7121946$ nevertheless lies within $8\cdot10^{-7}$ of the degree-$\le5$ barrier $\pi a/4$.

\paragraph{Stronger proved ceilings: chirp functions \st{P,C} (GPT, 22 October; IDE-checked independently).}
Let $U=u\cdot X$, $V=v\cdot X$ with $u,v$ orthonormal, and take $F=\ph(U)\exp(id\,\Im(\bar UV))$ with the quadratic phase factor. Integrating out $V$ gives the generating function
\[
\E\bigl[F e^{\xi\cdot\bar X+\eta\cdot X-\xi\cdot\eta}\bigr]=\frac{\sqrt\pi}{2S^{3/2}}e^{-\alpha\beta}\sum_k\frac{(3/2)_k}{k!(k+1)!S^k}(\alpha+d\gamma/2)^{k+1}(\beta-d\delta/2)^k,
\qquad S=1+d^2/4,
\]
where $\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta$ are $u\cdot\xi$, $u\cdot\eta$, $v\cdot\xi$, $v\cdot\eta$. The coordinates $(Z,W,R)$ have weights $(1,3,7)$ and are realized by diffuse Wick sums. Take rational rotations
$u=(sA,sB,C)$, $v=(B,-A,0)$ and $G=F(Z,-W,-R)$. Mix with the ordinary phase, then cancel the remaining low coefficients with the signed pure-degree kernels $\pm h(t^{2k+1})$. The resulting kernel has
coefficients $(B_5,0,0,M_7)$, respectively $(B_7,0,0,0)$, through degree seven. Hence, for every choice of the other coefficients,
\[
N(P_{1,0}+\alpha P_{2,1}+\beta P_{3,2})>0.7124179279,\qquad N(\cdots+\gamma P_{4,3})>0.7120024685,\qquad N\ge B_5+M_7\gamma,
\]
with $M_7=0.00158877311$. Consequences: degree-$\le5$ witnesses are capped at $1.4036705<1.404$, and degree-$\le7$ witnesses at $1.4044895$, which is below Haagerup's
conjectured $1.40457593$. A degree-7 witness certifying $1.404$ needs $\gamma<-0.10524$; the table's degree-7 game has $\gamma=-0.2425$. Coefficients larger than one
do not help, since $N\ge\frac\pi4|q_j|$. \emph{IDE check:} the IDE re-derived the generating function by hand ($\E_Ve^{pV+q\bar V}=e^{pq}$ and the radial moment $\Gamma(k+\frac32)/S^{k+3/2}$).
It then computed every kernel coefficient from it independently, by exact sympy expansion of the Wick generating function rather than through the paper's finite formula. It reproduced the $d=0$ composition formula exactly
and all certificate values ($\theta=0.1451901496$, $m_1=0$, $|m_2|<10^{-14}$, $B_5$, $B_7$, $M_7$, the $\gamma$ bound). Paper and checker are in
\pathref{gpt-reviews/chirp-obstructions-20261022/}. These are rigorous limits of the witness method. They agree with, and are weaker than, the numerical $1.403641$ and $1.404111$ above.

\subsection{Are the high-degree ceilings local maxima in richer classes?}\label{sec:wval}
\paragraph{Second variation (\code{wval}, 15 Oct 06:30) \st{F}.} For the extremal pair of each game, the Hessian of the pairing in the directions
of a ``shift'' $(a,b)$ (adding a coordinate of bidegree shifted by $(a,b)$) was computed; $\tau$ is the largest Rayleigh quotient relative to the
norm penalty, so $\tau>1$ means the pair is not a local maximum in that enlarged class. Degree 5 (W3 model): $\tau\le1$ on all shifts (max
$0.9886$ on $(0,1)/(1,0)$). Degree 7 (W3): $\tau\le0.9917$; degree 7 (Z2): $\tau=1.0009$ on shift $(1,2)$. Degree 9 (Z2): $\tau=1.028$ on shifts
$(0,1)/(1,0)$ and $1.002$ on $(1,2)/(2,1)$. So the degree-7 and degree-9 Z2 pairs are \emph{not} local maxima of the norm once a third ordinary
coordinate is allowed: the ceilings $1.4041$/$1.4044$ are optimistic.
\paragraph{Direct values in richer classes (\code{wtrue}, 15 Oct 07:04, run cut by the session) \st{F}.} Grid $48\times48\times32$: degree 9 Z2
$0.712060$ (best of 5 starts; the plain phase start stays at $0.711356$), W3 $0.712061$; degree 7 Z2 $0.711800$, W3 $0.712201$. Three ordinary
coordinates (Z3) and Z2+W3 at the coarse grid $32\times32\times24$ (546 and 147 basis functions, $N=1.9\cdot10^7$ nodes): the Z2 optimum on that grid
($0.712180$) embedded in the 3-variable engine is reproduced to $1.3\cdot10^{-10}$ and two starts from it return with excess $+1.3\cdot10^{-10}$ and
zero weight on the new variable. So on a coarse grid no gain from a third coordinate was found, while the second-variation test says a gain exists
at second order. The two are not inconsistent (the coarse grid has $4.5\cdot10^{-4}$ artefacts, and the alternating iteration can stall at the
embedded point); \textbf{the question is open}. A finer Z3 run or a direct line search along the $\tau>1$ eigenvector decides it.

\subsection{What this means for the third decimal}
The upper bound is already below $1.405$, so the third decimal is $4$ exactly when $\KG\ge1.404$. The upper side can change the answer only if
$K<1.404$, and the ceilings make a lower certificate the decisive object: a degree-7 game certified to within $5.7\cdot10^{-5}$ in norm (ceiling
$0.712194$ against $1/1.404=0.712251$), or a degree-9 game to within $\approx2\cdot10^{-4}$. The certificate loss at degree 3 was
$1.6$--$1.8\cdot10^{-4}$ in norm on 15 October (cert-0745b) and is $1.45\cdot10^{-4}$ for the current $B=0.7134326$ against $N(0.745)=0.713288$, and the present method fails at degree 5 (\autoref{ch:negatives}). The dated survey recorded here supplied no certificate for these complex Hermite games at this precision beyond degree 3; this is not an impossibility result for other techniques. The cited real degree-21 certificate had a gap of about $0.3\%$ to its heuristic value.
