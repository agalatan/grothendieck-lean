\subsection{Consequences of the complete one-driver comparison}
\label{sec:milestone-closures}

The assertions in this section are \textbf{[P]}. Their comparison input
is the complete one-driver theorem with retained deficit, denoted (OD):
\[
 \Lambda\!\left(cH(X)+\alpha(T-1)X+
       \beta Z^2\overline X/\sqrt2+gU\right)\le S-\Delta(c,\alpha,\beta)
 \quad(c,g\ge0,\ \alpha,\beta\in\mathbb C).
\]
Here
\[
\Delta(c,\alpha,\beta)=\min\left\{\frac3{100000},
\frac{\min(c^4,c^2)}{32}+\frac{|\alpha|^2+|\beta|^2}{2000}\right\}.
\]
All named standard complex Gaussians are proper and independent.
These consequences concern this class and its specified mixtures, not
an arbitrary cubic coupled to its visible coordinate.

\subsubsection{Independent latent coefficients and improper Gaussian buffers}
Let $\mathcal F$ be independent of $(Z,X)$. Suppose $C,A,B$ and a
positive semidefinite real $2$-by-$2$ matrix $\Sigma$ are
$\mathcal F$-measurable. Conditionally on $\mathcal F$, let $N$ be a
centered complex Gaussian with real covariance $\Sigma$, independent
of $(Z,X)$. Assume
\[
 \mathbb E\bigl(|C|^2+|A|^2+|B|^2+\operatorname{tr}\Sigma\bigr)<\infty.
\]
Then (OD) implies
\[
 \boxed{\Lambda\!\left(CH(X)+A(T-1)X+
                  BZ^2\overline X/\sqrt2+N\right)\le S-\mathbb E\Delta(|C|,A,B).}
\]
Neither the covariance nor the three coefficients need be mutually
independent. They must be independent of the visible and coherent
drivers in the stated conditional sense.

To prove the claim, condition on $\mathcal F$. A rotation of $X$
makes the Wick coefficient nonnegative; it changes only the unrestricted
phases of the other two coefficients. Each of
\[
 L\pm\left(CH(X)+A(T-1)X+BZ^2\overline X/\sqrt2\right)
\]
has a circular distribution, because a simultaneous rotation of $Z,X$
multiplies every displayed term by the same phase. Thus applying an
independent uniform phase to $N$ leaves both norm expectations
unchanged. If $\lambda_1,\lambda_2$ are the eigenvalues of $\Sigma$,
the characteristic function of this phase-randomized Gaussian equals
that of $\sqrt VU$, where
\[
 V=2(\lambda_1\cos^2\Theta+\lambda_2\sin^2\Theta),\qquad
 \Theta\ \hbox{uniform on }[0,2\pi].
\]
The mixing variable is independent of the core, and
$\mathbb E[V\mid\mathcal F]=\operatorname{tr}\Sigma$.
Conditioning on $V$ invokes (OD) and preserves the quadratic price
exactly. The deficit is independent of $V$, and making $C$
nonnegative preserves $|A|$ and $|B|$. It therefore survives as
$\mathbb E\Delta(|C|,A,B)$ after integrating over $\mathcal F$.
The deficit is bounded and measurable, so no further moment
hypothesis is required.

In particular, an arbitrary independent proper Gaussian variance mixture
is permitted as buffer. An actual cubic example is
\[
 cH(X)+\sum_j\bigl(C_j(V_0)W_j+D_j(V_0)\overline W_j\bigr)
       +\alpha(T-1)X+\beta Z^2\overline X/\sqrt2,
\]
where $V_0,W,Z,X$ are mutually independent Gaussian blocks,
$C_j\in H_{1,1}(V_0)$ and $D_j\in H_{2,0}(V_0)$.
Conditionally on $V_0$, the sum is a centered, possibly improper
Gaussian buffer. This does not allow the coefficient field to depend
on $X$, nor does it permit additional A/B terms on the buffer drivers.
The all-amplitude one-driver theorem supplies no such independence
replacement.

\subsubsection{Equality rigidity inside the conditional-mixture class}
Let $v_*$ be the unique maximizing Gaussian variance. The visible
Gaussian-mixture representation gives
\[
F(v)=2\mathbb E|L+\sqrt vU|-pv
=\sqrt\pi\,\mathbb E\sqrt{V_{\rm vis}+v}-pv.
\]
This is strictly concave and continuous on $[0,\infty)$ and tends
to minus infinity as $v\to\infty$, so its maximum is unique.
If equality holds in the conditional-mixture comparison with $S$,
then $\mathbb E\Delta(|C|,A,B)=0$. Thus $C=A=B=0$ almost surely.
After phase randomization the remaining comparison is
$\Lambda=\mathbb EF(V)\le S$, so uniqueness forces $V=v_*$
almost surely. In terms of the conditional covariance eigenvalues,
\[
V=\operatorname{tr}\Sigma+(\lambda_1-\lambda_2)\cos2\Theta,
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(V\mid\mathcal F)
=\frac12(\lambda_1-\lambda_2)^2.
\]
Consequently $\Sigma=(v_*/2)I_2$ almost surely. Conversely these
conditions produce the Gaussian optimizer and equality. This is an
equality characterization inside the stated conditional-mixture class;
it asserts no rigidity theorem for arbitrary cubic kernels.

\subsubsection{Profile transport}
Write $(a_0,b_0,\epsilon_0)=(699/1250,179/1250,1891/2500)$.
For a profile $L_{a,b}=Z(a+b|Z|^2)$ and any square-integrable $G$,
the reverse triangle inequality gives
\[
 |\Lambda_{L,\epsilon}(G)-\Lambda_{L_0,\epsilon}(G)|
 \le2\mathbb E|L-L_0|.
\]
The Gaussian benchmarks obey the same inequality, by taking the
supremum over Gaussian variances. The retained version is
\[
 \Lambda_{a,b,\epsilon_0}(G)\le S(a,b,\epsilon_0)
 -\mathcal D(G)+2\sqrt\pi\,|a-a_0|+3\sqrt\pi\,|b-b_0|,
\]
where $\mathcal D(G)=\Delta(c,\alpha,\beta)$ for a deterministic
one-driver model and $\mathbb E\Delta(|C|,A,B)$ for its stated
conditional-mixture extension. In particular, dropping this
nonnegative deficit gives
\[
 \Lambda_{a,b,\epsilon_0}(G)\le S(a,b,\epsilon_0)
        +2\sqrt\pi\,|a-a_0|+3\sqrt\pi\,|b-b_0|
\]
for every member of the one-driver class, and for the independent
conditional mixtures just described.

On the supplied profile rectangle
\[
 |a-a_0|\le10^{-5},\qquad |b-b_0|\le10^{-5},
\]
the comparison has error strictly below
\[
 \boxed{\frac{1773}{20000000}=0.00008865
 <\eta_{AB}=\frac{240297199}{2651182000000}.}
\]
This uses $\sqrt\pi<1773/1000$; all subsequent comparisons are rational.
It is profile uniformity for this restricted residual class.
It is not the full central-row envelope for arbitrary cubic residuals.

The uniform small-buffer deficit gives a larger profile square without
another numerical calculation. For the one-driver model with
$g\le1/6$, replacing $\mathcal D(G)$ above by $3/100000$ and taking
\[
 |a-a_0|,\ |b-b_0|\le1/75000
\]
gives a comparison error strictly below
\[
 \frac{5(1773/1000)}{75000}-\frac3{100000}
 =\frac{441}{5000000}=0.0000882<\eta_{AB}.
\]
This larger square retains the stated small-buffer restriction.

There is also an exact scaling identity. For $\lambda>0$ put
$\epsilon=\lambda\epsilon_0$. Since the class is closed under division
by $\lambda$,
\[
 \Lambda_{\lambda L_0,\lambda\epsilon_0}(G)
 =\lambda\Lambda_{L_0,\epsilon_0}(G/\lambda),\qquad
 S(\lambda a_0,\lambda b_0,\lambda\epsilon_0)=\lambda S_0 .
\]
Consequently, retaining the deficit also under scaling,
\[
 \boxed{\Lambda_{a,b,\epsilon}(G)\le S(a,b,\epsilon)
   -\lambda\mathcal D(G/\lambda)+2\sqrt\pi\,|a-\lambda a_0|
   +3\sqrt\pi\,|b-\lambda b_0|.}
\]
This is not a fixed-variance or fibrewise Gaussian domination assertion.
