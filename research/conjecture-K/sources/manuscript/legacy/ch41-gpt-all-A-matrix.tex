\subsection{A smaller sufficient matrix problem}
\label{sec:hybrid-matrix}
Here the remote cubic projection is zero. The centered A and B sectors are
linear in the remote Gaussian drivers; the even sectors and the diagonal
are arbitrary admissible quadratic sectors and may share all those drivers.
Let $d=\|A\|_2^2$, $j=\|B\|_2^2$, and let $e^2$ be the combined variance of
the even and diagonal sectors. Put $\Lambda_0=2\E|L|$ and
$\Delta=S-\Lambda_0$. The existing benchmark, independently replayed in
this package, gives $\Delta>2074/10^6$.

\begin{proposition}[Global exclusions, \st{P,C}]
For all amplitudes, phases, and dimensions in this no-remote class,
\begin{align}
 d\le1/100&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
       \Lambda<S-1/5000,\label{eq:small-a}\relax\\
 j\ge2d/7&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
       \Lambda<S-1/12500.\label{eq:cone}
\end{align}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The previously proved global anisotropic bound, with its exact constants
recomputed here, implies
\[
 \Lambda\le\Lambda_0+.181d-.749j+.119\sqrt{dj}-.128e^2.
 \tag{H1}
\]
Completing the A/B square bounds the excess by $.186d$, proving
\eqref{eq:small-a} without a restriction on any other amplitude.
For \eqref{eq:cone}, total variance at least $1/12$ is covered by the
existing affine trace bound $\Lambda<\Lambda_0-1/2000$.
Below that variance, use
\[
 \sqrt{dj}\le(hd+j/h)/2,\qquad h=107/200.
\]
Writing $a'=.181+.119h/2$ and $b'=.749-.119/(2h)>0$, the maximum
of $a'd-b'j$ under $j\ge2d/7$ and $d+j\le1/12$ equals
\[
 \frac{a'-2b'/7}{12(1+2/7)}
 =\frac{1018913}{513600000}<\Delta-\frac1{12500}.
\]
All the displayed decimals are exact terminating rationals.
\end{proof}

Consequently the finite Gram calculation is only needed on
\[
 \boxed{d>1/100,\qquad 0\le j<2d/7,\qquad d+j<1/12.}\tag{H2}
\]
In particular, the nearly equal-A/B configurations producing the largest
earlier floating matrix ratios are already settled by a different proof.
This does not claim that the original matrix criterion has been checked
there.

Let $\lambda_*$ be the exact inverse-radius Gram operator norm from
\autoref{ch:global-local-coupled-K}, and write
$b(d,j)=.181d-.749j+.119\sqrt{dj}$. The global bound and the certified
Gaussian odd-baseline comparison give the simultaneous inequalities
\[
 \Lambda-S\le\min\{b(d,j)-\Delta-.128e^2,
               -.1d-.25j+(\lambda_*-p)e^2\}.\tag{H3}
\]
Thus points with $b(d,j)\le\Delta$ need no Gram calculation. At the other
points it suffices to check
\[
 (\lambda_*-p)_+\min\left\{\frac1{12}-d-j,
             \frac{b(d,j)-\Delta}{.128}\right\}\le .1d+.25j.\tag{H4}
\]
A certified lower bound for $\Delta$ can replace $\Delta$ here. This
uses the first member of (H3) above its zero and the second below it;
it does not assume convexity of $\lambda_*$.

\subsection{Uniform tails for the Gram integrals}
\label{sec:uniform-gram-tail}
The four real parameters can be written
\[
 A\ge0,\quad b_2\ge0,\quad b_1\in\mathbb C,
 \qquad O_t=(t-1)A x_1+(t/\sqrt2)(b_1\bar x_1+b_2\bar x_2),
\]
with $B^2=|b_1|^2+b_2^2$. No alignment or phase restriction is imposed.
Let $M_0,M_1,M_2$ be the internal-quadratic/diagonal, mixed-linear, and
external-scalar Gram blocks. Their entries are defined by the joint
$(t,s)$ Laplace--Gaussian integral of \autoref{ch:global-local-coupled-K}.

\begin{theorem}[All-parameter tail bound, \st{P,C}]
If $1/100\le A^2+B^2\le1/12$, restrict those positive matrix integrals to
$0\le t\le64$ and $0\le s\le2^{72}$, obtaining $M_r^{\rm box}$. Then
\[
 \operatorname{tr}(M_0-M_0^{\rm box})+
 \operatorname{tr}(M_1-M_1^{\rm box})+(M_2-M_2^{\rm box})<10^{-5}.
\]
Each omitted matrix is positive semidefinite and is bounded by $10^{-5}I$.
In particular, all joint Gram entries are finite, including at parameters
with rank-one conditional Gaussian fibres.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $x_j=(R_{2j-1}+iR_{2j})/\sqrt2$, where $R$ is standard real
Gaussian of dimension at most four, and $z=|R|^2$. Summing the numerator
traces of all three blocks gives
\[
 t\left(\tfrac38z^2+k+1+\tfrac12(t-2)^2\right),\qquad k\le2.
\]
The density ratio to a real Gaussian of covariance $2I$ is
$2^k e^{-z/4}$. Since $24/e^2+3<7$, the combined integrand is bounded by
$tP(t)Z_2(s,t)$, where $P(t)=28+2(t-2)^2$ and $Z_2$ is the Gaussian
Laplace factor with that enlarged covariance.

Put $X=A|t-1|$, $Y=Bt/\sqrt2$, $D=X-Y$, and $r=X^2+Y^2$.
For the real covariance $\Sigma_t$ of $O_t$,
\[
 \det(I+4s\Sigma_t)\ge
 (1+2s(X+Y)^2)(1+2sD^2).
\]
The shift exponential is at most
$e^{-s\ell(t)^2/2}+e^{-\ell(t)^2/(8r)}$.
Maximizing $u^{-1}e^{-\ell^2/(8u^2)}$ proves
\[
 \frac{e^{-\ell^2/(8r)}}{\sqrt{1+2s(X+Y)^2}}
 \le\frac1{\sqrt s\,\ell(t)}.
\]
Moreover $t/\ell(t)<2$. Hence the second term contributes at most
$2J_s/\sqrt s$ after integration in $t$, where
\[
 J_s=\int_0^\infty
       \frac{e^{-t}P(t)}{\sqrt{1+2sD(t)^2}}\,dt.
\]
Writing $C=B/\sqrt2$ and $k_0=A+C$, one has
$\sqrt2k_0\ge1/10$ and $k_0\le1/(2\sqrt2)$.
On $[0,1]$, $D=A-k_0t$; direct integration bounds this contribution by
$72\log(1+\sqrt s)/(\sqrt{2s}k_0)$.
On $[1,\infty)$, $D=(A-C)t-A$. If $A\le C$, its absolute value is at
least $k_0/2$. Otherwise put $t_0=A/(A-C)$ and split at
$[t_0/2,3t_0/2]$. Outside this interval $|D|\ge k_0/4$.
Inside it use $P(t)e^{-t/2}\le41$ and $t_0e^{-t_0/4}<3/2$,
and integrate the inverse square root exactly. Together these give
\[
 J_s\le\frac{10}{\sqrt s}
        \{318\log(1+\sqrt s)+128\}.
\]
This bound is uniform even when the second root escapes to infinity.

For $S_0=2^{2m}$, the combined trace tail with $s>S_0$ is at most
\[
 8100\,2^{-3m}
 +2^{-m}\{12720[\tfrac7{10}(m+1)+1]+5120\}.\tag{H5}
\]
Here the first shift term uses $\ell(t)^2\ge a^2t$, and the second
uses the preceding bound and $\log2<7/10$.
For the remaining $t>T_0$, $s\le S_0$ tail, discard the original
Laplace exponential before enlarging the Gaussian. Its unweighted
expected trace numerator is at most $t^3/2+14t$. The resulting bound is
\[
 2^{m+1}(3/8)^{T_0}
       (T_0^3/2+3T_0^2/2+17T_0+17).\tag{H6}
\]
At $m=36,T_0=64$, the exact sum of (H5)--(H6) is less than
$5.054\cdot10^{-6}$, and hence less than $10^{-5}$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{What this does not certify.}
The finite rectangle and the remaining parameter domain (H2\stale{23 Oct}{now the hypothesis \code{NodeCaps}; rows 0--11, 16--18 proved in target \code{GrotheCaps} (\code{lower\_bound\_of\_capsRest}); only rows 12--15 (\code{CapsRest}) remain}) still need
a directed matrix cover. The proof deliberately integrates the singular
fibres jointly; a pointwise-in-$t$ inverse-radius bound would be false.
The uniform tails also imply continuity of the Gram matrices on the
compact parameter domain: truncated integrals are continuous, and their
tails converge uniformly. Neither continuity nor the floating scans
proves (H4).

\subsection{A positive five-integral envelope}
\st{P} There is a sufficient scalar envelope that avoids cancellation
between matrix orientations at different integration points. Under the
Gaussian posterior $N(m_s,C_s)$ of \autoref{ch:global-local-coupled-K}, let $Q$ be the orthonormal
internal basis of $H_{1,1}\oplus H_{2,0}$, and put
\[
 B_s=\E_{m_s,C_s}[\overline Q Q^\top],\quad
 b_s=\E_{m_s,C_s}Q,\quad c(t)=(t-2)/\sqrt2,
 \quad d\mu=\pi^{-1/2}te^{-t}s^{-1/2}Z_s\,dt\,ds.
\]
Define five nonnegative integrals
\[
 n_0=\int\lambda_{\max}(B_s)d\mu,\quad
 n_1=\int|c(t)|\|b_s\|d\mu,\quad n_2=\int c(t)^2d\mu,
\]
\[
 n_m=\int\lambda_{\max}(C_s+m_sm_s^\top)d\mu,\qquad
 n_e=\int d\mu.
\]
Then
\[
 \lambda_*\le\max\left\{
 \lambda_{\max}\begin{pmatrix}n_0&n_1\\n_1&n_2\end{pmatrix},
 n_m,n_e\right\}.\tag{H7}
\]
Indeed, test the internal quadratic block against a coefficient vector
of norm $u$ and the diagonal against a scalar of modulus $z$. Its
posterior form is bounded by
$\lambda_{\max}(B_s)u^2+2|c|\|b_s\|uz+c^2z^2$.
The mixed block follows because $(x,\bar x)$ is a unitary transform of
the normalized real Gaussian vector; the external block is scalar.
Tonelli justifies these positive integrals before finiteness is known.
For any omitted integration region,
$\|b_s\|^2\le\lambda_{\max}(B_s)$ implies
$n_1^2\le n_0n_2$ on that region. The omitted $2\times2$ matrix is
therefore positive semidefinite, with norm at most $n_0+n_2$, itself
at most the omitted trace of $M_0$. The other omitted terms are bounded
by the omitted trace of $M_1$ and by the omitted $M_2$, respectively.
Thus the preceding trace-tail certificate also bounds the error of
(H7) by $10^{-5}$.

The complex quadratic subspace should be retained. If $J$ is the real
complex structure and $Q_D(R)=R^\top D R-\operatorname{tr}D$ for real
symmetric $D$, then
\[
 \|\Pi_{H_{1,1}\oplus H_{2,0}}Q_D\|_2^2
 =\tfrac32\operatorname{tr}(D^2)-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(DJDJ).
\]
For $D=vv^\top$, this is exactly $3/4$ of $\|Q_D\|_2^2$, since
$v^\top Jv=0$. The identity follows by the orthogonal decomposition
$D_+=(D-JDJ)/2$, $D_-=(D+JDJ)/2$: the latter splits equally between
the conjugate holomorphic and antiholomorphic spaces. An exact checker
audits 994 rational instances; the decomposition proves all dimensions.
Replacing the allowed complex subspace by every real quadratic is a
strict relaxation, and numerical probes show it can lose too much.

\paragraph{Numerical scope, \st{F}.}

\subsection{A sharper uniform Gaussian-smoothed Hessian}
\label{sec:all-v-hessian}
Let $H\in H_{2,1}(\mathbb C^n)$ in any finite dimension and let $A_0$ be its conditional expectation
onto all remote coordinates. Let $Q_v$ denote the quadratic coefficient
in $\E[U_v(L+tH)+U_v(L-tH)]$, where
$U_v(z)=\E|z+\sqrt v U|$ and $U$ is independent of every original
coordinate. The external directional-stability proof and its exact
checkers were audited and replayed.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform refinement, \st{P,C}]
For every $v>0$, every finite dimension, and every such cubic,
\[
 \boxed{Q_v(H)\le h'(v)\|A_0\|_2^2+\frac{31}{25}\|H-A_0\|_2^2,}
 \qquad h(v)=2\E|L+\sqrt v U|.
\]
At the Gaussian optimizer, the negative quadratic coefficient in the
variance-preserving expansion is therefore at least
\[
 p-31/25=3879/47275>0.082.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof of the improvement]
Retain the cancellation in the mixed visible block. Define
\[
 J_- =\int_0^1\frac{\sqrt t(1-t)e^{-t}}{a+bt}\,dt,
 \qquad J_+ =\int_1^\infty\frac{\sqrt t(t-1)e^{-t}}{a+bt}\,dt.
\]
If $I_k=\int_0^\infty t^{k-1/2}e^{-t}/(a+bt)\,dt$, then
$J_+-J_-=I_2-I_1$. On $[0,1]$, the even degree-eight Taylor polynomial
of $e^{-t}$ and the even degree-eight geometric polynomial of
$(a+bt)^{-1}$ are positive upper bounds. Their product integrates exactly
against $\sqrt t(1-t)$, proving
\[
 J_-<.291809683301,\qquad\max(J_-,J_+)<.625532459808.
\]
The mixed block off-diagonal has absolute value at most
$\max(J_-,J_+)/(2\sqrt2)$.

The Bessel identity for the averaged radial Hessian gives
\[
 2rq_v(r)=\sqrt{2\pi x}e^{-x}I_0(x),\qquad x=r^2/(2v).
\]
On $0\le x\le1$, 4096 rational cells, an even degree-twelve exponential
Taylor bound, and a degree-ten $I_0$ series with its geometric remainder
prove this is at most $2351/2000$. On $x\ge1$, the standard Bessel
ratio differential equation and its one-sided contact argument prove
the expression decreases. Thus the bound covers every $r,v$.
The mixed diagonal entries are bounded by
\[
 \frac{2351}{2000}\frac{I_2-2I_1+I_0}{2},\qquad
 \frac{2351}{2000}\frac{I_2}{4}.
\]
Fresh rational Stieltjes majorants and minorants prove that subtracting
this mixed block from $(31/25)I$ leaves positive diagonal entries and
determinant greater than $.000259431061$. The other visible blocks
are below $31/25$ using the same intervals. The remote block remains
exactly $h'(v)$.
\end{proof}

This is a uniform second-variation coefficient. It does not furnish a
finite-amplitude neighborhood independent of the cubic direction, nor
does it replace the shared-driver comparison needed by full K.

\subsection{Replay and remaining scope}
The package-level \code{verify\_all.py} uses standard-library Python and
C++17 with 128-bit integers. It regenerates the mathematical tables,
rebuilds the A-cover, recomputes the analytic exclusion constants, and
checks the Hessian and joint-tail bounds. Floating matrix scans are
separate exploratory files and are not used for acceptance. The
SHA256 manifest and replay receipts identify the delivered sources.

The all-A theorem has a fixed nonzero Wick coefficient and Gaussian
buffer box; it is not a reduction of every cubic to that box. The
general-phase/two-driver finite matrix cover, nonlinear remote cores
with several influential drivers, and the complete row envelope remain
open. The established Grothendieck bounds therefore remain unchanged.
