\subsection{The exact weighted-complement constant \st{P}}
Let $X\in\C^d$ and $V$ be independent standard proper Gaussian vectors,
with at least one coordinate in $V$. Let $w(X)\ge0$ have finite weighted
Gram entries in the spaces below. The smallest constant $C_w$ satisfying
\[
 \E[w(X)|J(X,V)|^2]\le C_w\|J\|_2^2
 \quad\bigl(J\in H_{2,1}(X,V),\ \E[J\mid X]=0\bigr)
\]
is exactly the largest eigenvalue among the five weighted Gram matrices on
\[
 H_{1,1}(X),\quad H_{2,0}(X),\quad H_{0,1}(X),\quad
 H_{1,0}(X),\quad H_{0,0}(X).
\]
Their sizes are $d^2,d(d+1)/2,d,d,1$. In particular, visible $Z$ plus
two shared coordinates gives sizes $9,6,3,3,1$.

\begin{proof}
In an orthonormal complex Hermite basis of the external block write
\[
 J=\sum_{(\mu,\nu)\ne(0,0)}
 f_{\mu,\nu}(X)H_{\mu,\nu}(V),\qquad
 f_{\mu,\nu}\in H_{2-|\mu|,\,1-|\nu|}(X).
\]
The possible external bidegrees are $(1,0),(0,1),(2,0),(1,1),(2,1)$.
External orthogonality holds for both the weighted and the unweighted
norm because the weight is core-measurable. The quotient is therefore
bounded by the maximum of the five Rayleigh quotients. Conversely,
multiply an eigenvector of a maximizing block by one normalized
one-coordinate external Hermite polynomial of the complementary
bidegree. This attains the constant.
\end{proof}

For a conditionally centered complement and a fresh independent proper
Gaussian buffer, the two-sign Rice tangent gives
\[
 \Lambda(Q+gU)\le B
 \ \Longrightarrow\
 \Lambda(Q+J+gU)\le B+(C_w-p)\|J\|_2^2,
\]
with
\[
 w=\tfrac12\{K_g(|L+Q|^2)+K_g(|L-Q|^2)\}.
\]
Both linear terms vanish by conditional centering. At $g=0$, the
singular Gram entries require proof of integrability. A regularized
tangent avoids this problem and must retain its regularization credit.
The numerical realization is in the all-sector core and
weighted-complement packages, not in this abstract lemma.

\subsection{Two shared directions do not mean two-coordinate cubics \st{P}}
The span of the A driver and the conjugate of the B driver has complex
dimension at most two. Choose coordinates $X$ on this span and an
independent Gaussian vector $V$ on its orthogonal complement. A remote
cubic has the unique orthogonal expansion
\[
 q=\sum_{\substack{|\mu|\le2\\|\nu|\le1}}
 H_{\mu,\nu}(X)q_{\mu,\nu}(V),\qquad
 q_{\mu,\nu}\in H_{2-|\mu|,\,1-|\nu|}(V).
\]
Only its deterministic shared cubic survives conditioning on $X$.
Other terms include a shared linear factor times a remote quadratic
and a shared quadratic times a remote linear factor. These coefficient
fields can carry arbitrarily much variance. Thus the span reduction
neither makes the remainder independent nor bounds its variance.
The Gaussian-factor theorem applies only when its own coefficient/driver
independence assumptions hold.

\subsection{A dominant-coordinate reduction cannot be universal \st{P}}
For $q_N=N^{-1/2}\sum_{j=1}^N H(X_j)$, $\|q_N\|_2=1$. If
$\ell=\sum_jv_jX_j$ and $\sum_j|v_j|^2=1$, Hermite covariance gives
\[
 |\langle q_N,H(\ell)\rangle|
 =N^{-1/2}\left|\sum_j v_j|v_j|^2\right|
 \le N^{-1/2}\sum_j|v_j|^3\le N^{-1/2},
\]
up to conjugation of the whole sum, which does not change the modulus.
A coordinate vector attains equality. Every one-driver projection
therefore leaves at least $1-1/N$ of the variance. A global proof needs
a diffuse comparison as well as a finite influential core.

These statements are reductions and obstructions, not a proof of K.
The independent-Wick envelope, independent-block replacement, and
conditionally centered-complement lifting have different hypotheses;
none can be substituted for another without checking them.
