\subsection{The exact-profile route: the Laplace decomposition is lossless}\label{sec:ab-laplace}
GPT's representation $F_D(u)=-u\int_0^\infty e^{-s|u|^2}\mu_D(ds)$ (\S\ref{sec:wct-false}) was realised numerically: a nonnegative measure on $460$ points $s\in[0.02,200]$ fitted by
NNLS to $-P_D(\rho)=-c_D(\sqrt\rho)/\sqrt\rho$ from \code{prof.profiles} reproduces it to $1.8\cdot10^{-5}$ ($A$) and $1.0\cdot10^{-5}$ ($B$), total masses $0.8351=|\gamma_A|$,
$0.4220=|\gamma_B|$, $20$ support points each, dominant $s\in[1,7]$, mass $0.0022$ above $s=20$. Writing $e_s(u)=ue^{-s|u|^2}$, the second-order content of Theorem~\ref{thm:R},
$\mathcal C_F=\norm{\Pi_{1,1}F_u(q)}^2+\norm{\Pi_{2,0}F_{\bar u}(q)}^2$, satisfies by Minkowski $\sqrt{\mathcal C_{F_A}}\le\int\sqrt{\mathcal C_{e_s}}\,\mu_A(ds)$, and the loss is negligible:
on the junta ($a=0.3,0.6,1$, $\sigma=0.2$), the L2 maximiser $n=2$, $cX^2\bar Y$ and the skew residual the Minkowski bound exceeds $\mathcal C_{F_A}$ by $0$--$2\%$, and the actual
contents recombine exactly ($\sum_k|\E\bar w_kF_A(q)|^2=|\int\E\bar w_ke_s(q)\,d\mu|^2$ to all digits). So a uniform bound $\mathcal C_{e_s}(q)\le g(s)\kappa$ for the exponential
profiles would give $\mathcal C_{F_A}\le(\int\sqrt{g}\,d\mu_A)^2\kappa$ with no further loss. Measured $\mathcal C_{e_s}/\kappa$ at $\sigma=0.2$: $0.13$--$0.84$ for $s\in\{1.1,2.1\}$,
$0.26$--$1.4$ for $s\approx3.8$, $0.29$--$2.0$ for $s=7$ (largest on the skew residual), i.e.\ $g(s)\approx0.1s^2$ on these tensors.

\emph{Why this does not yet give a proof.} The sup-norm Stein bound for $e_s$ gives $g(s)\approx c\,s^2$ with $c$ of order $5$, fifty times the measured values: the factor-$100$
loss of \S\ref{sec:wct-remains} is simply redistributed over $s$. The natural exact tool, Gaussian linearisation $e^{-s|q|^2}=\E_\xi e^{2i\sqrt s\Re(\bar\xi q)}$, expresses
$\Pi_{1,1}$ of the exponential profile through $\E[e^{i\Re(\bar\zeta q)}D_kq\,\overline{D_lq}]$; splitting $e^{i\Re(\bar\zeta q)}$ into its mean $\phi_q(\zeta)$ (which multiplies the
influence matrix, $\kappa$-controlled) and its fluctuation leaves a covariance with the Gram fluctuation $D_kq\overline{D_lq}-\Gamma_{kl}$ whose Hilbert--Schmidt size is of order
$\sigma^4$, not $\kappa$: the same obstruction as for every second-order route (\S\ref{sec:wct-remains}). The lossless decomposition is therefore a correct \emph{format} for a
certificate (it keeps the actual tensor contraction and matches GPT's crossbound Gram problems, \autoref{ch:crossbound}), not a proof.

\subsection{What is left, precisely}\label{sec:ab-left}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The inequality to prove or certify} is \eqref{eq:T0rig}: for every $q\in\Hc21(\C^n)$, every $n$, the actual odd-sector contents against the \emph{relaxed} deficit. It is
true with margin $\ge9\%$ on all tested classes \st{F}; its left side is a third-order quantity with the tensor orientation kept (Theorem~\ref{thm:R}, the Laplace decomposition
of \S\ref{sec:ab-laplace}, GPT's zero-projection Gram format of \autoref{ch:crossbound}); its right side is now computable (\S\ref{sec:ab-dstar}).
\item \textbf{The deficit side must be sharpened} or the margin is too thin to survive any rigorous content bound: $d^*/d_q\approx0.3$--$0.5$ near $\sigma^*$. Candidates: the spectral
(AMMP) inequalities of \autoref{ch:spectral-review} as additional linear cuts (they exclude exactly the laws that make $d^*$ small), or the two-node structure with the sign information
of $\Phi_0$'s derivatives (\autoref{ch:rescub}) applied with the cuts as extra constraints (the extremal law then has at most one more atom per active cut).
\item \textbf{No uniform content bound with usable constants exists yet.} Every derivative-based route is $50$--$100\times$ too weak at the relevant amplitude
(\S\ref{sec:wct-remains}, \S\ref{sec:ab-laplace}); the exact-conditioning route (GPT's box covers) handles conditionally Gaussian residuals and one-coordinate stars but not a
general cubic with several influential coordinates. The reverse influence inequality (\S\ref{sec:wct-external}: $\kappa\le\frac{14}3\norm M^2_{HS}$) shows that a counterexample
in the variance window must have an influential coordinate, so a dichotomy ``few influential coordinates (finite machinery) / diffuse remainder (second order)'' is the natural
architecture, with the mixed terms along the influential coordinates as the open piece.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Verdict.} Not proved. With GPT's residual-cubics theorem the odd sectors have a budget of $\approx9\cdot10^{-5}$ at the peak; the rigorous deficit available to pay their
content is now tabulated; (T$_0$) against that deficit holds on all our residual classes but with a margin as low as $9\%$; the exact-profile (Laplace) decomposition is lossless
and is the right certificate format; the missing step is a uniform bound on the actual $A/B$ content of an arbitrary cubic, for which no derivative-based method can work and
which therefore requires either the exact-conditioning machinery extended to several influential coordinates or a genuinely new idea. Certified bound unchanged by this chapter
(the current Lean bound $1.401674103\stale{22 Oct}{now $\KG>1.401695322458$}$, \S\ref{sec:certcombinede}, came from better cap tables, not from K). \emph{Later:} \autoref{ch:shared-sector-refinements}
proves the $A$- and $B$-sectors under phase symmetry / conditional centring. The later coherent one-driver theorem (\autoref{ch:complete-coherent-driver}) includes the junta in this table. The outstanding task is general coupled cubics, or the weaker original-response cover; the list above records the earlier research programme, not a necessity theorem for either route.
