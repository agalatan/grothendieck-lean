\subsection{Main theorem and its global extension}
Put
\[
 \epsilon=\frac{1891}{2500},\quad p=\frac1\epsilon,\quad
 a=\frac{699}{1250},\quad b=\frac{179}{1250},\quad
 L=Z(a+b|Z|^2),\quad H(X)=X(|X|^2-2)/\sqrt2.
\]
All standard complex Gaussians are proper, with second moment one.
For independent $Z,X,U$, define
\[
 Q_0=cH(X)+\alpha(|Z|^2-1)X+\beta Z^2\bar X/\sqrt2,
\]
\[
 \Lambda(G)=\mathbb E|L+G|+\mathbb E|L-G|-p\mathbb E|G|^2,
 \qquad S=\sup_{v\ge0}\{2\mathbb E|L+\sqrt vU|-pv\}.
\]
The new uniform result is
\[
 \boxed{c\ge\frac{19}{100},\quad g\ge0,\quad
 \alpha,\beta\in\mathbb C
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \Lambda(Q_0+gU)<S-\frac1{25000}.}
\]
It removes every A/B amplitude and phase restriction above $c=0.19$.
The previously accepted unrestricted threshold was $c=34/125=0.272$.

The freshly computed compact part is
\[
 \boxed{\frac{19}{100}\le c\le\frac{34}{125},\quad
 0\le g\le\frac{41}{250},\quad|\alpha|\le\frac25,\quad
 \beta\in\mathbb C
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \Lambda(Q_0+gU)<S-\frac3{5000}.}
 \tag{U}
\]
The exact cover gives an absolute upper bound less than
$1.373118558$. Independently, a newly recomputed one-dimensional
Gaussian comparator proves $S>5495/4000=1.37375$.

\subsection{Complex anchors and the exact compact cover}
\paragraph{The new anchor set.}
Use
\[
 c_0=\frac6{25},\qquad
 \alpha_0\in
 \left\{0,\frac3{50},\frac9{50},-\frac9{50},
 \frac{9i}{50},\frac{17}{50},-\frac{17}{50},\frac{17i}{50}\right\},
 \qquad\beta_0=0.
\]
Conjugating every Gaussian yields the reflected bounds without further
integration. In particular the nonreal anchors retain, rather than
discard, the orientation of the A coupling.

After the common phase rotation set $Z=r$, $X=x+iy$, $t=x^2+y^2$, and
\[
 \ell=ar+br^3,\quad H=(x+iy)(t-2)/\sqrt2,\quad
 A=(r^2-1)(x+iy),\quad B=r^2(x-iy)/\sqrt2.
\]
The variables have density $2r e^{-r^2-x^2-y^2}/\pi$.
The relative angle is present in $(x,y)$. All four Cartesian quadrants
are integrated.

For $\rho_g(u)=\mathbb E|\sqrt u+gU|$ and $K_g=2\rho_g'$, concavity gives,
on every integration cell $j$,
\[
 2\mathbb E_j\rho_g(|\ell+cH+\alpha A+\beta B|^2)
 \le2\rho_g(q_j)+K_g(q_j)
 \{\mathbb E_j|\ell+cH+\alpha A+\beta B|^2-q_j\},
\]
where $q_j=\mathbb E_j|\ell+c_0H+\alpha_0A|^2>0$.
This is an inequality on the entire cell, not midpoint quadrature.
The three unknown fields are orthonormal in complex $L^2$.
Their exact variance price is therefore $p(c^2+|\alpha|^2+|\beta|^2)$.

Partition each positive coordinate interval $[0,6]$ into $64$ equal
cells. The complement of the resulting bounded domain $D$ is covered by
\[
 2\mathbb E[|\ell+cH+\alpha A+\beta B+gU|1_{D^c}]
 \le P+L_T+g^2P+(c^2+|\alpha|^2+|\beta|^2)T_D,
\]
where $P=\mathbb P(D^c)$, $L_T=\mathbb E[\ell^2 1_{D^c}]$, and
$T_D=\mathbb E[(|H|^2+|A|^2+|B|^2)1_{D^c}]$.
These follow from $2|w|\le1+|w|^2$, symmetry and Cauchy--Schwarz.
The quadratic dependence is essential when eliminating an unbounded
coupling.

The resulting global majorant is
\[
 C+2\Re(b_Hc+b_A\alpha+b_B\beta)
 +(c,\alpha,\beta)^*M(c,\alpha,\beta).
\]
Here $M$ is Hermitian; its off-diagonal entries and the linear
coefficients may be complex. The implementation encloses all of them.
The convention is
$b_i=\mathbb E[K\ell H_i]$, $M_{ij}=\mathbb E[K\bar H_iH_j]-p\delta_{ij}$,
with the specified cell weights and tail additions.

\paragraph{Complex B elimination.}
Every computed matrix has $k=-M_{BB}>0$. Its B-dependent part is
\[
 2\Re\{\beta(b_B+cM_{HB}+\bar\alpha M_{AB})\}-k|\beta|^2.
\]
Its supremum is
$|b_B+cM_{HB}+\bar\alpha M_{AB}|^2/k$.
In particular, the reduced coefficients are
\[
 b'_A=b_A+\frac{b_B\overline{M_{AB}}}{k},
 \qquad
 M'_{HA}=M_{HA}+\frac{M_{HB}\overline{M_{AB}}}{k},
 \qquad
 M'_{AA}=M_{AA}+\frac{|M_{AB}|^2}{k}.
\]
The real $c$-linear term and constant receive the corresponding real
cross term and $|b_B|^2/k$. No real-only maximization is substituted for
this complex square.

\paragraph{Continuum verification.}
The forty endpoint matrices use the five buffer values
$0,1/25,2/25,3/25,41/250$.
After elimination the variables are $c,\Re\alpha,\Im\alpha$.
Conjugation permits $\Im\alpha\ge0$. The exact checker covers the A disk
by $2590$ intersecting rectangles of side $1/100$, keeping the full
$c$ interval $[19/100,34/125]$ on each.
For a reduced quadratic with diagonal coefficients $q_{cc},q_{AA},q_{AA}$,
its maximum over such a box is at most its largest vertex value plus
\[
 \frac{\max(0,-q_{cc}^{\rm low})}{4}(\Delta c)^2+
 \frac{\max(0,-q_{AA}^{\rm low})}{4}
       \{(\Delta\Re\alpha)^2+(\Delta\Im\alpha)^2\}.
\]
This follows from tensor-product linear interpolation; all cross terms
are reproduced exactly. The checker rounds coefficient intervals
outward to multiples of $10^{-12}$ and performs subsequent comparisons
with integers.

For adjacent buffers $g_i,g_{i+1}$, convexity of the unpenalized norm
in the amplitude gives, for $g=(1-u)g_i+ug_{i+1}$,
\[
 \Lambda(g)\le(1-u)B_i+uB_{i+1}
                 +p\,u(1-u)(g_{i+1}-g_i)^2.
\]
The maximum of this quadratic in $u\in[0,1]$ is checked exactly.
Minimizing valid anchor bounds separately at each endpoint is legitimate
because each endpoint bound holds throughout the whole parameter box.
