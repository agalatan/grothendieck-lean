\subsection{Setting and the stronger relative bound}
Put
\[
 p=\frac{2500}{1891},\quad a=\frac{699}{1250},\quad
 b=\frac{179}{1250},\quad L=Z(a+b|Z|^2),\quad T=|Z|^2,
\]
\[
 \Lambda(G)=\E|L+G|+\E|L-G|-p\E|G|^2,\qquad
 S=\sup_{v\ge0}\{2\E|L+\sqrt vU|-pv\}.
\]
Every standard complex Gaussian is proper with second moment one.
Let \(V\) be a finite vector of such Gaussians, independent of the
visible variable and of all variables defining a square-integrable
core \(Q\). The core may depend on \(Z\).
Take
\[
 q\in H_{2,1}(V),\qquad A\in H_{1,0}(V),\qquad
 B\in H_{0,1}(V),\qquad
 J=q+(T-1)A+\frac{Z^2B}{\sqrt2}.
\]
The three fields may use exactly the same coordinates of \(V\);
no independence within this triple is assumed. Write
\[
 c^2=\E|q|^2,\quad u^2=\E|A|^2,\quad v^2=\E|B|^2,\quad
 r^2=u^2+v^2.
\]
The fresh buffer \(U\) is independent of all these variables.
For \(g>0\), define
\[
 \boxed{
 E_g^+(c,r)=\left(\frac8{9g}-p\right)r^2+
 \frac{\frac{303}{14}c^4+\frac{75}{7}c^2r^2+
              \frac{625}{112}cr^3+\frac{275}{28}r^4}{9g^3}.}
 \tag{52.1}
\]
Then the dimension-free relative theorem is
\[
 \boxed{\Lambda(Q+J+gU)
 \le\Lambda\!\left(Q+\sqrt{g^2+c^2}\,U\right)+E_g^+(c,r).}
 \tag{52.2}
\]
In particular, the added cubic need not be a diagonal Wick monomial.
Independence between the added Gaussian block and the core is essential
to the stated proof.

\subsection{The sign of the fourth derivative [P]}
For \(\Phi_g(w)=2\E|w+gU|\), angular averaging of one-dimensional
absolute values gives, for every real unit direction \(e\),
\[
 0\le D_e^2\Phi_g(w)\le\frac{\sqrt\pi}{g},\qquad
 -\frac{3\sqrt\pi}{2g^3}\le D_e^4\Phi_g(w)
 \le\vartheta\,\frac{3\sqrt\pi}{2g^3},
 \quad \vartheta=2e^{-3/2}<\frac{25}{56}.
 \tag{52.3}
\]
Indeed, if \(N\) has density \(e^{-x^2/g^2}/(g\sqrt\pi)\), then
\[
 \psi_g(x)=\E|x+N|,\quad
 \psi_g''(x)=\frac{2e^{-x^2/g^2}}{g\sqrt\pi},\quad
 \psi_g''''(x)=
 \frac{4(2x^2/g^2-1)e^{-x^2/g^2}}{g^3\sqrt\pi}.
\]
Use
\[
 |w|=\frac14\int_0^{2\pi}|\Re(e^{-i\theta}w)|\,d\theta,
 \qquad
 \int_0^{2\pi}\cos^2\theta\,d\theta=\pi,\quad
 \int_0^{2\pi}\cos^4\theta\,d\theta=\frac{3\pi}{4}.
\]
The scalar function \((2t-1)e^{-t}\) lies between \(-1\) and
\(2e^{-3/2}\).
The rational comparison follows from
\[
 \sum_{k=0}^{11}\frac{3^k}{k!}>\left(\frac{112}{25}\right)^2.
\]
Thus the upper Taylor remainder through degree three is at most
\(\vartheta\sqrt\pi|h|^4/(16g^3)\), whereas the lower remainder is at
least \(-\sqrt\pi|h|^4/(16g^3)\).

Condition on the core and \(Z\). Simultaneous negation of the remote
coordinates negates \(J\), so its first and third Taylor terms vanish.
The cubic \(q\) is proper, has variance \(c^2\), and is orthogonal
in real covariance to both \(A\) and \(B\). Its quadratic Taylor term
therefore agrees with that of a fresh \(cW\).
The remaining quadratic term is at most
\(\sqrt\pi r^2/(2g)\) after all variables are averaged.
Subtracting the Gaussian Taylor expansion uses its \emph{lower}
remainder, not its upper one. Consequently,
\[
 \Lambda(Q+J+gU)\le
 \Lambda(Q+\sqrt{g^2+c^2}U)
 +\left(\frac{\sqrt\pi}{2g}-p\right)r^2+
 \frac{\sqrt\pi}{16g^3}
       \left(\vartheta\,\E|J|^4+2c^4\right).
 \tag{52.4}
\]
Apply this to \(L+Q\) and \(L-Q\) and take the half-sum, since
\(\Phi_g\) already contains the factor two.
Conditional centering makes the extra quadratic price exactly \(pr^2\).
This argument handles improper conditional laws of the A/B sum;
it never drops their pseudocovariance.

The incoming bound charged \(\E|J|^4+2c^4\) instead.
Its leading rational numerator \(46c^4\) is replaced by
\((303/14)c^4\). This is a \(52.95\%\) reduction of that coefficient,
not a corresponding improvement of the Grothendieck constant.

\paragraph{An optional further improvement [P].}
The Gaussian comparator may be integrated in its variance rather than
estimated at the initial buffer. Since
\(\partial_v\Phi_{\sqrt v}=\Delta\Phi_{\sqrt v}/4\) and
\(\Delta^2\Phi_h\ge-4\sqrt\pi/h^3\), its contribution
\(\sqrt\pi c^4/(8g^3)\) in (52.4) can be replaced by
\[
 \frac{\sqrt\pi}{2g}
       \left(\sqrt{g^2+c^2}-g\right)^2.
 \tag{52.5}
\]
Twice integrating
\(\partial_v^2\Phi_{\sqrt v}\ge-\sqrt\pi/(4v^{3/2})\)
proves the assertion. The rational regions below use (52.1) only.

\subsection{Why the same constants hold for every cubic tensor [P]}
Averaging the visible phase and \(T\) first gives the exact identity
\begin{align*}
\E|J|^4={}&\E|q|^4+
4\E\bigl[|q|^2(|A|^2+|B|^2)\bigr]
+2\Re\E[q^2\bar A^2]\\
&+8\Re\E[q\bar A(|A|^2+2|B|^2)]\\
&+9\E|A|^4+28\E[|A|^2|B|^2]+6\E|B|^4.
\end{align*}
No factorization between \(q,A,B\) occurs here.
The following estimates are dimension-free:
\[
 \E|q|^4\le44c^4,\qquad
 \E|q|^2|\ell|^2\le4c^2\E|\ell|^2
 \quad(\ell\text{ a proper or conjugate linear form}).
 \tag{52.6}
\]
For the second estimate, rotate a unit linear form to the first
coordinate. Multiplication by that coordinate splits into its
creation and annihilation parts in distinct bidegrees.
Its squared norm is
\[
 c^2+\|D_1q\|_2^2+\|\bar D_1q\|_2^2\le(1+2+1)c^2.
\]

Here is an explicit tensor proof of the first estimate. Write
\(q=I_{2,1}(f)\), with \(\E|q|^2=2\|f\|^2\).
In the product \(q\bar q\), the contraction coefficients are
\[
 r!s!\binom2r^2\binom1s^2,\quad
 0\le r\le2,\quad0\le s\le1.
\]
Grouping by \(k=r+s\) gives coefficient sums \(1,5,6,2\).
Each symmetrized contraction has norm at most \(\|f\|^2\).
The output bidegree is \((3-k,3-k)\), so orthogonality yields
\[
 \E|q|^4\le
 \bigl(36\cdot1^2+4\cdot5^2+1\cdot6^2+1\cdot2^2\bigr)\|f\|^4
 =176\|f\|^4=44c^4.
\]
The product coefficients follow by multiplying the elementary Wick
generating functions
\[
 e^{\langle z,\xi\rangle+\langle\bar z,\eta\rangle-\langle\xi,\eta\rangle}.
\]
Cross-contractions pair a holomorphic slot of one factor with an
antiholomorphic slot of the other. Choosing and matching the \(r\)
and \(s\) slots gives exactly the displayed coefficient.
Thus this calculation applies to every finite index set; the exact
checker verifies the small coefficient table, while the generating
identity and contraction Cauchy--Schwarz provide the dimension argument.

Put \(\rho=\E[AB]\), so \(|\rho|\le uv\).
The projection norms needed for the cubic cross term are
\[
 \|\Pi_{2,1}(A|A|^2)\|_2=\sqrt2\,u^3,\qquad
 \|\Pi_{2,1}(A|B|^2)\|_2^2=u^2v^4+|\rho|^2v^2
 \le2u^2v^4.
 \tag{52.7}
\]
These follow by symmetrizing the two holomorphic slots; the removed
lower-chaos components are orthogonal to \(q\).
Cauchy--Schwarz therefore bounds the cubic cross term by
\[
 8\sqrt2\,cu(u^2+2v^2)\le\frac{25}{2}cr^3.
\]
The \(q^2\bar A^2\) term is bounded by
\(\E|q|^2|A|^2\), giving \(24c^2r^2\) for the quadratic terms.
Finally,
\[
 \E|A|^4=2u^4,\quad \E|B|^4=2v^4,\quad
 \E|A|^2|B|^2=u^2v^2+|\rho|^2,
\]
so the remaining terms are at most
\[
 18u^4+56u^2v^2+12v^4
 \le\frac{284}{13}r^4<22r^4.
\]
We obtain, for the entire internally coupled block,
\[
 \boxed{\E|J|^4\le44c^4+24c^2r^2+\frac{25}{2}cr^3+22r^4.}
 \tag{52.8}
\]
Together with \(\sqrt\pi<16/9\), (52.4) proves (52.1)--(52.2).
One may retain the actual \(\E|q|^4\) in place of \(44c^4\)
throughout (52.8).

\subsection{New finite-buffer regions [P,C]}
For a retained coherent one-driver core, the accepted deficit is
\[
 D_0=\min\left\{\frac3{100000},
 \frac{\min(c_0^4,c_0^2)}{32}+\frac{r_0^2}{2000}\right\}.
\]
It follows that \(\Lambda\le S-D_0+E_g^+(c,r)\).
If \(g^2+c^2\le1/36\), one instead has the strict deficit \(3/100000\)

Exact rational corner checks prove
\[
 \boxed{\frac18\le g\le\frac{41}{250},\quad
 c\le\frac1{100},\quad r\le\frac1{600}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \Lambda<S-10^{-6}.}
 \tag{52.9}
\]
This holds for arbitrary internally coupled remote \(q,A,B\)
with the displayed norms. The incoming threshold was \(g\ge3/20\).
The same conclusion holds for every \(g\ge1/8\) when
\(D_0=3/100000\), for example when \(c_0\ge9/50\).

For arbitrary retained coherent coefficients and \(r\le1/500\),
the following half-lines satisfy \(\Lambda<S+9\cdot10^{-5}\):

\begin{array}{c|c}
 \text{buffer}&\text{added cubic norm}\\ \hline
 g\ge1/10&c\le1/80\\
 g\ge1/8&c\le3/200\\
 g\ge41/250&c\le19/1000.
\end{array}
\tag{52.10}
\]
The costs decrease in \(g\); at each listed lower endpoint they

\subsection{Many blocks and a diffuse criterion [P,C]}
Keep one coherent block \(J_0\), and let the other \(J_i\) be internally
coupled cubic/A/B blocks on mutually independent Gaussian coordinate
sets, also independent of the core and visible coordinate.
Choose any order \(i_1,\ldots,i_N\), and put
\[
 g_j^2=g^2+\sum_{k<j}c_{i_k}^2,\qquad
 h^2=g^2+\sum_{i=1}^N c_i^2.
\]
Repeated application of (52.2) gives
\[
 \boxed{\Lambda\left(J_0+\sum_{i=1}^NJ_i+gU\right)
 \le\Lambda(J_0+hU)+\sum_{j=1}^NE^+_{g_j}(c_{i_j},r_{i_j}).}
 \tag{52.12}
\]
At each step all surviving blocks form part of the core. They remain
independent of the block being removed. No artificial independence
is imposed within a block.
For the final baseline one can use the maximum of the valid deficits
\[
 \min(3/100000,d_0),\qquad
 3/100000\quad(h\le1/6),\qquad
 d_0\quad(h\ge41/250),
\]
where \(d_0=\min(c_0^4,c_0^2)/32+r_0^2/2000\).
The last, uncapped branch is already available in \autoref{ch:complete-coherent-driver}.

If \(c_i^2\le\delta\), \(R^2=\sum_{i\ge1}r_i^2\), then
\[
 \sum_j\frac{c_{i_j}^4}{g_j^3}\le\frac{2\delta}{g}+
                                             \frac{\delta^2}{g^3}.
 \tag{52.13}
\]
To prove it, let \(a_j=c_{i_j}^2\), \(s_j=g_j^2\) and
\(f(s)=s^{-3/2}\). The left-endpoint quadrature error is at most
\[
 \sum_j a_j(f(s_j)-f(s_j+a_j))
 \le\delta(f(g^2)-f(h^2)).
\]
Use \(a_j^2\le\delta a_j\) and
\(\int_{g^2}^{h^2}s^{-3/2}\,ds=2/g-2/h\).
Also
\[
 \sum c_i^2r_i^2\le\delta R^2,\quad
 \sum c_ir_i^3\le\sqrt\delta R^3,\quad
 \sum r_i^4\le R^4.
\]
Thus the total cost in (52.12) is at most
\[
 \left(\frac8{9g}-p\right)R^2+
 \frac{101}{42}\left(\frac{2\delta}{g}+\frac{\delta^2}{g^3}\right)
 +\frac{\frac{75}{7}\delta R^2+
        \frac{625}{112}\sqrt\delta R^3+\frac{275}{28}R^4}{9g^3}.
 \tag{52.14}
\]
In particular, for any finite number and dimension of internally
coupled blocks,
\[
 \boxed{c_0\ge\frac9{50},\quad g\ge\frac15,\quad
 c_i\le\frac1{1000},\quad\sum_{i\ge1}r_i^2\le10^{-6}
 \ \Longrightarrow\ \Lambda<S-\frac1{400000}.}
 \tag{52.15}
\]
The exact retained margin exceeds \(2.8293\cdot10^{-6}\).
There is no restriction on the total removed cubic variance.

For pure removed cubics (\(r_i=0\)), the rational cost is minimized,
for a fixed retained block, by processing increasing \(c_i\).
For adjacent squared amplitudes \(a\ge b\), the excess over the
reversed order is
\[
 a^2[f(s)-f(s+b)]-b^2[f(s)-f(s+a)]\ge0.
\]
This follows because \(-f'\) is positive and decreasing. Later
cumulative buffers agree, so adjacent swaps prove the claim.
No optimal-order assertion is made for blocks with couplings.

\subsection{A separate zero-buffer improvement using the slab [P,C]}
This result uses the already certified
\[
 M_\kappa(Q)<S-\frac1{3500}\quad
 (Q\in H_{2,1}(\C^2)),\qquad\kappa=\frac1{2500},
\]
where
\[
 M_\kappa(Q)=\E\sqrt{|L+Q|^2+\kappa}
            +\E\sqrt{|L-Q|^2+\kappa}-p\E|Q|^2.
\]
Here \(Q\) may contain all six sectors on \((Z,X)\), with unrestricted
complex coefficients.
Let \(q,A,B,Y,W\) be centered, pairwise Hermitian-orthogonal fields,
jointly independent of \((Z,X,U)\); their joint internal law is arbitrary.
Put
\[
 J=q+(T-1)A+Z^2B/\sqrt2+ZY+\bar ZW+gU,\quad
 v_0=\E|q|^2+g^2,\quad
 R^2=\E(|A|^2+|B|^2+|Y|^2+|W|^2).
\]
Define the two deficits
\begin{align*}
 d_{\rm low}&=\frac{13}{21000}+(p-5/6)v_0-(50-p)R^2,\\
 d_{\rm high}&=\frac1{3500}+\frac1{50}-(50-p)(v_0+R^2).
\end{align*}
For \(v_0\le\kappa\) one has
\[
 \Lambda(Q+J)<S-\min\{d_{\rm low},1/2000\};
 \tag{52.16}
\]
for \(v_0\ge\kappa\), without a variance restriction,
\[
 \Lambda(Q+J)<S-d_{\rm high}.
 \tag{52.17}
\]
The formulas agree at \(v_0=\kappa\).

For clarity, set
\[
 w_\kappa=\frac12\left((|L+Q|^2+\kappa)^{-1/2}
                      +(|L-Q|^2+\kappa)^{-1/2}\right)\le50.
\]
Conditional centering and the square-root tangent give
\[
 \Lambda(Q+J)\le M_\kappa(Q)+
    \E[(w_\kappa-p)\E(|J|^2\mid Z,X)]-\kappa\E w_\kappa.
\]
The conditional variance equals
\[
 v_0+\E|A|^2(T-1)^2+\E|B|^2T^2/2+
                \E(|Y|^2+|W|^2)T.
\]
The nonconstant profiles are nonnegative and each has mean one.
Consequently
\[
 \Lambda(Q+J)<S-\frac1{3500}-pv_0+
                  (v_0-\kappa)\E w_\kappa+(50-p)R^2.
 \tag{52.18}
\]
For \(v_0\ge\kappa\), use \(\E w_\kappa\le50\).
For \(v_0\le\kappa\), if the total variance is at least \(13/20\),
the existing five-profile cutoff gives deficit \(1/2000\).
It applies here because its proof uses only that conditional radial
second-moment profile and the two-sign Jensen bound, not a
pure-chaos assertion about the buffer or the external fields.
Otherwise \(\E|Q|^2<13/20\), and
\[
 \E w_\kappa\ge
 \left(\E|L|^2+\E|Q|^2+\kappa\right)^{-1/2}>\frac56,
\]
since \(\E|L|^2=1181331/1562500\).
This proves (52.16)--(52.17).

Exact rational consequences include
\[
 \boxed{v_0\le\frac1{2475},\quad R\le\frac7{2000}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad\Lambda(Q+J)<S-\frac1{50000}.}
 \tag{52.19}
\]
Also \(7/20000\le v_0\le1/2500,\ R\le1/250\) gives deficit
\(1/100000\), and \(v_0\le1/2500,\ R\le19/5000\) gives
the application slack \(\eta_{AB}=240297199/2651182000000\).
The added cubic alone can therefore have norm \(1/50\), separately
from its small shared sectors; the older arbitrary-remainder ball
limited the norm of their sum to \(0.0035\).

For example, for a fresh standard \(V\), every full core \(Q\) satisfies
\[
 \boxed{\Lambda\left(Q+\frac{H(V)}{50}
    +\frac{(T-1)V}{400}+\frac{Z^2\bar V}{400\sqrt2}\right)
       <S-\frac1{5000}.}
 \tag{52.20}
\]
The low-variance retained deficit is exactly
\(21821/105896000>1/5000\).
All three added coefficients may have arbitrary phases.
This is a new zero-buffer comparison with a shared nonlinear driver,
simultaneous A/B, and unrestricted even and diagonal sectors in the core.

\subsection{What the audit establishes, and what remains}
The incoming fourth-order proof and its exact verifier pass.
Its two-angle correction is correct: after normalizing the visible
phase, two independent driver angles remain. A common driver angle
cannot simply be fixed while retaining a nonzero real visible shift.

The new algebraic computations are portable standard-library Python,
with the incoming dependency-free Node verifier included.
The archive has a \texttt{verify\_all.py}, replay logs, SHA256 hashes,
this ready-to-port chapter, and Markdown proofs.
Finite arithmetic checks support the analytic proof; they are not
presented as enumerations proving an arbitrary-dimensional identity.

The large unresolved region still has two or more sizable
self-interacting cubic blocks and little fresh Gaussian buffer.
Theorems (52.2) and (52.12) require independence between the core and
added coordinate sets; they do not cover cubic monomials mixing those
sets. The zero-buffer theorem allows arbitrary dependence within the
external block, but its variance region is small.
No counterexample to K and no improved global endpoint is claimed.
