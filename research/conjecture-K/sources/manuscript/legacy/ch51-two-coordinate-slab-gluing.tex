\subsection{A regularized statement of the slab certificate}
Let \(Z,X\) be independent proper standard complex Gaussians and put
\[
a=\frac{699}{1250},\quad b=\frac{179}{1250},\quad
p=\frac{2500}{1891},\quad L=Z(a+b|Z|^2),\quad
\kappa=\frac1{2500}.
\]
As before,
\[
\Lambda(G)=\mathbb E|L+G|+\mathbb E|L-G|-p\mathbb E|G|^2,
\qquad
S=\sup_{v\ge0}\{2\mathbb E|L+\sqrt v U|-pv\},
\]
where \(U\) is an independent proper standard Gaussian.
Define
\[
M_\kappa(Q)=
\mathbb E\sqrt{|L+Q|^2+\kappa}
+\mathbb E\sqrt{|L-Q|^2+\kappa}
-p\mathbb E|Q|^2.
\]
The numerical statement used in this chapter is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{M_\kappa(Q)<S-\frac1{3500}
\quad\text{for every }Q\in H_{2,1}(\mathbb C^2).}
\label{eq:slab-regularized}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{The full basis [P].}
With \(T=|Z|^2,\ t=|X|^2\), every such \(Q\) has the form
\begin{align*}
Q={}&cH(X)+(T-1)AX+\frac{Z^2}{\sqrt2}B\bar X\\
&+Zy(t-1)+\bar Z wX^2+\nu H(Z),
\qquad H(x)=\frac{x(|x|^2-2)}{\sqrt2}.
\end{align*}
All six coefficients may be complex. Rotation of \(X\) makes \(c\ge0\);
conjugation permits \(\operatorname{Im}A\ge0\).
The displayed basis is Hermitian-orthogonal and
\[
\|Q\|_2^2=|c|^2+|A|^2+|B|^2+|y|^2+2|w|^2+|\nu|^2.
\]
Thus the result includes all simultaneous even and diagonal
interactions on this one remote driver. It is stronger in this respect
than the coherent A/B-only family.

\subsection{Repair of the profile exclusion [P]}
The incoming report invokes the pure-cubic variance cutoff on an
arbitrary conditional noise variable. Instead, apply the profile
majorant to the regularized core itself.

Write \(\Phi(u,d,e,j,n)\) for the \autoref{ch:global-local-coupled-K} bound associated with the
profiles
\[
1,\quad (T-1)^2,\quad T,\quad T^2/2,\quad T(T-2)^2/2.
\]
The two-sign square-root inequality and conditional Jensen give
\begin{equation}
M_\kappa(Q)\le
\Phi(|c|^2+\kappa,|A|^2,|y|^2+2|w|^2,|B|^2,|\nu|^2)
+p\kappa.
\label{eq:slab-profile-repair}
\end{equation}
This is an algebraic profile bound; it makes no assertion that the
added constant profile is a particular cubic law.

Let \(\widetilde V=\|Q\|_2^2+\kappa\) and
\(\Lambda_0=\sqrt\pi\,387/500\).
The existing profile certificates imply that the right side of
\eqref{eq:slab-profile-repair} is bounded above by
\[
\Lambda_0-\frac1{2000}+p\kappa
\quad\text{if }\widetilde V\ge\frac{13}{20},
\]
and by
\[
\frac{2057}{1500}+p\kappa
\quad\text{if }\widetilde V\ge
\frac1{12}+\frac{19}{5}(|c|^2+\kappa).
\]
Using \(\pi<22/7\) gives \(\Lambda_0<1373/1000\).
Both displayed bounds are smaller than
\[
\frac{27479}{20000}-\frac1{3500}.
\]
An independent Gaussian benchmark certificate proves
\(S>27479/20000\).

On the remaining domain,
\begin{equation}
|B|^2+|y|^2+2|w|^2+|\nu|^2
<\frac1{12}+\frac{14}{5}(|c|^2+\kappa)-|A|^2.
\label{eq:slab-even-budget}
\end{equation}
This proves the needed budget for the core without applying a
pure-chaos theorem to a non-chaos remainder.

\subsection{What the finite certificate proves [P,C]}
The visible and driver radii are integrated after taking the exact
angular mean of
\[
\sqrt{|\ell+Xm|^2+\kappa},\qquad
\ell=\sqrt T(a+bT),\quad
m=c(t-2)/\sqrt2+(T-1)A.
\]
The angular mean is evaluated through the arithmetic--geometric mean.
The A sector remains inside this exact core.
The tangent majorant handles B and the even/diagonal sectors.
The substitution \(X\mapsto-X\) cancels the even linear terms and the
B--even quadratic cross terms. Completing the B square and bounding a
three-dimensional Hermitian Gram matrix gives a vertex upper bound
\[
\mathrm{Core}+\mathrm{Gain}_B+\lambda_- B_0.
\]
Here \(B_0\) is a fixed upper bound for
\eqref{eq:slab-even-budget} throughout the box.

This fixed choice is important. The supremum over the remaining
coefficients has a common feasible set. After adding
\(p(c^2+|A|^2)\), the regularized payoff is convex in
\((c,\operatorname{Re}A,\operatorname{Im}A)\).
Consequently a box of circumradius \(R\) has upper bound
\[
\max_{\text{vertices}}
\{\mathrm{Core}+\mathrm{Gain}_B+\lambda_- B_0\}+pR^2.

\subsection{A weighted conditional gluing lemma [P]}
Let \(\mathcal F\) determine \(L,Q\), and suppose
\(\mathbb E[J\mid\mathcal F]=0\). Set
\[
v=\mathbb E[|J|^2\mid\mathcal F],\qquad
w_\kappa=\frac12\left(
\frac1{\sqrt{|L+Q|^2+\kappa}}+
\frac1{\sqrt{|L-Q|^2+\kappa}}\right).
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\Lambda(Q+J)\le M_\kappa(Q)
+\mathbb E[(w_\kappa-p)v]-\kappa\mathbb Ew_\kappa.}
\label{eq:slab-weighted-gluing}
\end{equation}
Indeed, conditional Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
\mathbb E[|L\pm Q\pm J|\mid\mathcal F]
\le\sqrt{|L\pm Q|^2+v}.
\]
Use the tangent inequality for the square root at
\(|L\pm Q|^2+\kappa\), add the signs, and use
\(\|Q+J\|_2^2=\|Q\|_2^2+\|J\|_2^2\).
This proof requires neither independence nor Gaussianity of \(J\).
In particular, it is a finite-amplitude comparison rather than a
directional Taylor expansion.

\subsection{An arbitrary-dimensional coupled neighborhood [P,C]}
Let \(G\in H_{2,1}(\mathbb C^n)\), choose a remote Gaussian unit
direction \(X\) orthogonal to \(Z\), and define
\[
Q=\mathbb E[G\mid Z,X],\qquad J=G-Q,\qquad r=\|J\|_2^2.
\]
Conditional projection preserves the bidegree, so
\(Q\in H_{2,1}(\mathbb C^2)\).
If \(\|G\|_2^2\ge13/20\), the global variance cutoff already gives
\(\Lambda(G)<S-1/2000\).
In the complementary case \(\|Q\|_2^2<13/20\).
Convexity of \(x\mapsto x^{-1/2}\), averaged jointly over the sample
and the two norm signs, gives
\[
\mathbb Ew_\kappa
\ge\big(\mathbb E|L|^2+\|Q\|_2^2+\kappa\big)^{-1/2}
>\frac56.
\]
The constants follow from
\[
\mathbb E|L|^2=\frac{1181331}{1562500},
\qquad
\frac{1181331}{1562500}+\frac{13}{20}+\frac1{2500}
<\frac{36}{25}.
\]
Since \(w_\kappa\le50\), equations
\eqref{eq:slab-regularized} and \eqref{eq:slab-weighted-gluing} imply
\begin{equation}
\Lambda(G)<S-\frac{13}{21000}
+\left(50-\frac{2500}{1891}\right)r
\qquad(\|G\|_2^2<13/20).
\label{eq:slab-neighborhood-cost}
\end{equation}
Thus, without any independence assumption on the remainder,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\|G-\mathbb E[G\mid Z,X]\|_2\le\frac7{2000}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\Lambda(G)<S-\frac1{50000}.}
\label{eq:slab-coupled-neighborhood}
\end{equation}
The exact low-variance deficit is
\[
\frac{13}{21000}
-\left(50-\frac{2500}{1891}\right)\frac{49}{4\cdot10^6}
=\frac{72251}{3176880000}>\frac1{50000}.
\]
The larger radius \(19/5000\) gives the peak-profile slack
\[
\Lambda(G)<S+\frac{240297199}{2651182000000}.
\]
These are absolute norm neighborhoods, not relative variance shares.
Some \autoref{ch:uniform-shared-bootstrap} neighborhoods have larger radii for restricted coherent
A/B cores. The present scope is the full six-coefficient core, including
the even and diagonal terms simultaneously.

For example, with a third independent Gaussian coordinate \(V\),
\[
J=\frac{7}{2000\sqrt2}X^2\bar V
\]
has conditional mean zero and norm \(7/2000\), but unbounded
conditional variance \(49|X|^4/(8\cdot10^6)\).
It genuinely shares the core driver. All six coefficients of \(Q\)
remain unrestricted in \eqref{eq:slab-coupled-neighborhood}.

\subsection{Conditional noise and a rigidity limitation [P,C]}
Equation \eqref{eq:slab-weighted-gluing} also slightly enlarges the
incoming noise slab. If
\[
\mathbb E[J\mid Z,X]=0,\qquad
\mathbb E[|J|^2\mid Z,X]\le(1/49)^2,
\]
then \(\Lambda(Q+J)<S-1/100000\) for every two-coordinate cubic \(Q\).
Indeed the supremum of \((w-p)v-\kappa w\) on
\(0\le w\le50,\ 0\le v\le(1/49)^2\) is
\[
\max\{0,(50-p)(1/49)^2-50\kappa\}<1/3500-1/100000.
\]
Here \(J\) can be non-Gaussian and non-polynomial.

For pure cubic polynomials, however, a uniform conditional-variance
bound has a restrictive consequence. Expand a conditionally centered
remainder in an orthonormal remote Hermite basis:
\[
J(Z,X,V)=\sum_{(\mu,\nu)\ne(0,0)}
f_{\mu,\nu}(Z,X)H_{\mu,\nu}(V).
\]
Conditional orthogonality gives
\(\mathbb E[|J|^2\mid Z,X]=\sum|f_{\mu,\nu}(Z,X)|^2\).
A uniform bound makes each polynomial coefficient bounded almost
everywhere. Continuity and full Gaussian support make it bounded
everywhere, hence constant. Bidegree then forces \(J\) to be a
remote \(H_{2,1}(V)\) polynomial, independent of \((Z,X)\).
Thus the original uniform conditional-variance condition by itself
does not cover genuinely mixed cubic monomials.
The norm neighborhood above avoids that limitation.

\subsection{An eight-moment lift of the vertex calculation [P]}
This specializes the five-block and parity reductions already present
in \autoref{ch:uniform-shared-bootstrap} and \autoref{ch:milestone-map-20261020}. The additional point here is their explicit
connection to the slab's AGM integrals and regularization credit.
It is unnecessary to integrate the weight at a full six-coefficient
core in order to retain cubic remainders. At the A-core used by the
incoming certificate, put
\[
Q_A=cH(X)+A(T-1)X,\qquad
\omega=\frac12\sum_\pm(|L\pm Q_A|^2+\kappa)^{-1/2}.
\]
This weight is invariant under simultaneous rotation of \(Z,X\) and
under \(X\mapsto-X\). Define six real and one complex moments by
\[
M_{ij}=\mathbb E[\omega T^it^j]\quad(i+j\le2),
\qquad \zeta=\mathbb E[\omega\bar Z^2X^2].
\]

Expand a conditionally centered cubic \(J\) in an orthonormal remote
Hermite basis. The five possible local coefficient spaces are
\[
H_{1,1},\quad H_{2,0},\quad H_{1,0},\quad H_{0,1},\quad H_{0,0}.
\]
In the basis \(T-1,t-1,Z\bar X,\bar ZX\), the first weighted Gram
matrix splits as
\[
\begin{pmatrix}A_0&C_0\\C_0&B_0\end{pmatrix}
\ \oplus\
\begin{pmatrix}M_{11}&\zeta\\\bar\zeta&M_{11}\end{pmatrix},
\]
where
\[
A_0=M_{20}-2M_{10}+M_{00},\qquad
B_0=M_{02}-2M_{01}+M_{00},
\]
\[
C_0=M_{11}-M_{10}-M_{01}+M_{00}.
\]
For \(H_{2,0}\), the even block on \(Z^2/\sqrt2,X^2/\sqrt2\)
and the odd block on \(ZX\) are, respectively,
\[
\frac12\begin{pmatrix}M_{20}&\zeta\\\bar\zeta&M_{02}\end{pmatrix},
\qquad M_{11}.
\]
Both first-chaos spaces have diagonal matrix
\(\operatorname{diag}(M_{10},M_{01})\); the constant space has
entry \(M_{00}\). The vanished entries are consequences of parity,
not assumptions about the coefficients of \(J\).

Consequently the exact common operator bound is
\begin{align*}
\mathcal K=\max\bigg\{&
M_{00},M_{10},M_{01},M_{11}+|\zeta|,\\
&\lambda_{\max}
\begin{pmatrix}A_0&C_0\\C_0&B_0\end{pmatrix},
\ \frac12\lambda_{\max}
\begin{pmatrix}M_{20}&\zeta\\\bar\zeta&M_{02}\end{pmatrix}
\bigg\}.
\end{align*}
Remote Hermite orthogonality then gives, in arbitrary dimension,
\[
\mathbb E[\omega |J|^2]\le\mathcal K\|J\|_2^2.
\]
The exact polynomial checker verifies all \(34\) entries of the five
Gram matrices and their unweighted Gaussian normalizations.

With \(m=c(t-2)/\sqrt2+(T-1)A\), the incoming angular primitives give
\[
M_{ij}=\mathbb E_{T,t}[T^it^jJ_0],\qquad
\zeta=\mathbb E_{T,t}\left[
Tt\left(\frac{\bar m}{|m|}\right)^2J_2\right].
\]
The second integrand is zero at \(m=0\).
Thus eight real radial integrals suffice, and no new angular
functions are needed.

At every vertex with \(p-M_{BB}>0\), the same tangent and parity
calculation now yields the lifted inequality
\begin{align}
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\Lambda(Q+J)\le{}&
\mathrm{Core}+\mathrm{Gain}_B+\lambda_-E_{\rm even}\\
&+(\mathcal K-p)\|J\|_2^2-\kappa M_{00},
\end{aligned}}
\label{eq:slab-eight-moment-lift}
\end{align}
where \(Q\) is the full two-coordinate cubic and
\(E_{\rm even}=|y|^2+2|w|^2+|\nu|^2\).
Conditional centering cancels every cross term involving \(J\).
One obtains the negative credit directly by taking the tangent at
\(|L\pm Q_A|^2+\kappa\), before inserting the other sectors.

This is an analytic reduction, not a numerical bound for
\(\mathcal K\). To extend the finite cover one must certify the
eight additional integrals and retain a valid sector budget.
If \(\|J\|_2^2\le\kappa\), the incoming budget already overestimates
the remainder's possible remote variance; beyond that range the
budget must be enlarged explicitly.
