\subsection{A common-shift certificate for the diagonal channel}
\label{sec:diagonal-common-shift}
Let $\varepsilon=1891/2500$, $L=Z(a+b|Z|^2)$,
$H=Z(|Z|^2-2)/\sqrt2$, and
$\Lambda(G)=\mathbb E(|L+G|+|L-G|)-\varepsilon^{-1}\mathbb E|G|^2$.
Uniformly on
$a\in[55919/100000,55921/100000]$ and
$b\in[14319/100000,14321/100000]$, for every jointly distributed
square-integrable remote pair $(Q,\nu)$ independent of $Z$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:common-shift-diagonal}
 \boxed{\Lambda(Q+\nu H)\le\Lambda(Q)
 -\frac{1079859}{1891000}\mathbb E(\Re\nu)^2
 -\frac{501213}{1891000}\mathbb E(\Im\nu)^2.}
\end{equation}
The real penalty is $0.5710518244\ldots$, an increase of exactly
$109/1000$ over the preceding $0.4620518244\ldots$.
The imaginary penalty is unchanged. This does not improve a complete
Grothendieck certificate by itself.

\paragraph{Retain the common shift.}
For the positive quadratic majorant
\[
 \mathcal Q_x(y)=\frac{3|y|^2}{4|x|}
 -\frac{\Re(y^2\overline x^2)}{4|x|^3},
 \qquad |x+y|+|x-y|\le2|x|+\mathcal Q_x(y),
\]
the exact Gaussian real-channel identity is
\[
 \mathbb E\mathcal Q_{G+s}(G)=\frac{\sqrt\pi}{4}B(|s|^2),
 \quad B(x)=\mathbb E[(1+4xT)e^{-xT}],\quad
 T\sim\operatorname{Beta}(1/2,1/2).
\]
Use the same eleven-term rational positive density envelope as the
preceding section, with coefficients $c_j$ and rates $\lambda_j$.
Unlike a sum of separate suprema, its all-shift bound is
\[
 \mathbb E\mathcal Q_{L+s}(H)\le R(|s|^2)+\tau,
 \quad R(z)=\frac12\sum_j w_j B(\lambda_j z),
 \quad w_j=\frac{\sqrt\pi}{2}c_j\lambda_j^{-3/2},
\]
where the unchanged radial tail satisfies $\tau<0.000085223$.
The common-shift verifier proves
\[
 \sup_{z\ge0}R(z)+\tau
 <0.750662732667456<\frac{751}{1000}.
\]
The imaginary cost remains bounded by $1057/1000$. Conjugation symmetry
kills the real--imaginary cross term after rotating the shift, leaving the
multiplier $\nu$ unchanged. Conditioning on $(Q,\nu)$, using the visible
half-turn, and using
$\mathbb E|Q+\nu H|^2=\mathbb E|Q|^2+\mathbb E|\nu|^2$
proves \eqref{eq:common-shift-diagonal}.

\paragraph{Finite arithmetic and tails.}
The cancellation-free expansion
\[
 B(x)=e^{-x}\sum_{n=0}^\infty
 \frac{\binom{2n}{n}}{4^n n!}x^n
 \left(1+\frac{2x}{n+1}\right)
\]
follows by expanding $e^{x(1-T)}$. On $0\le x<10$ the verifier uses
terms through $n=64$. If $t_{64}=\binom{128}{64}x^{64}/(4^{64}64!)$
and $r=x/65$, the omitted sum before the last factor is at most
$t_{64}r/(1-r)$; multiplying by $1+2x$ bounds the full omitted part.
For $x\ge10$, splitting the beta integral at $1/2$ gives
\[
 B(x)\le3\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi x}}+
          \frac{1+4x}{2}e^{-x/2}.
\]
Both terms decrease on that range, and $B(x)\le2$ globally.
Furthermore $|B'(x)|\le3/2$, so
$|R'(z)|\le(3/4)\sum_j w_j\lambda_j<4.512$.
The verifier checks $10{,}001$ nodes $z=j/5000$ on $[0,2]$ and pays
$4.512/10000$ between nodes. For $z\ge2$ each term with

other terms use $B\le2$. The radial $t\ge16$ tail is recomputed from
its density and mass, independently of this unbounded-shift tail.
The program uses only directed Decimal arithmetic from Python's standard

\subsection{An all-amplitude A-sector addition theorem under conditional circularity}
\label{sec:circular-a-addition}

Set $\epsilon=1891/2500$ and let $Z$ be standard proper complex Gaussian,
$T=|Z|^2$ and $L=Z(a+bT)$, where
\[
 a\in[0.55919,0.55921],\qquad b\in[0.14319,0.14321].
\]
Let $(Q,A)$ be any square-integrable complex random pair independent of
$Z$, whose law is invariant under $(Q,A)\mapsto(Q,e^{i\theta}A)$ for
every real $\theta$. For
$\Lambda(R)=\mathbb E(|L+R|+|L-R|)-\mathbb E|R|^2/\epsilon$, we prove
\begin{equation}\label{eq:circular-a-theorem}
 \boxed{\Lambda(Q+(T-1)A)\le\Lambda(Q)
       -\frac{231481}{5909375}\mathbb E|A|^2.}
\end{equation}
The coefficient exceeds $0.03917$. No Gaussianity, small-amplitude
condition, or independence between $Q$ and $|A|$ is required.

\paragraph{A global ring estimate.}
For $u>0,v\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:circular-ring-bound}
 M(u,v):=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}|u+ve^{i\theta}|\,d\theta
 \le u+\frac{38}{125}\frac{v^2}{u}.
\end{equation}
For $0\le r\le1$, the positive binomial series
\[
 M(1,r)=\sum_{k\ge0}\binom{1/2}{k}^{\!2}r^{2k}
 \le1+(4/\pi-1)r^2\le1+\frac{137}{500}r^2
\]
uses $M(1,1)=4/\pi$ and $\pi>157/50$. For $r\ge1$,
$M(1,r)=rM(1,1/r)\le r+137/(500r)$. To complete the proof put
\[
 P(r)=\frac{38}{125}r^3-r^2+r-\frac{137}{500},\qquad x=r-7/5.
\]
For $x\ge0$ the exact positive identity is
\[
 P(r)=\frac{38}{125}x^3+
 \frac{173}{625}\left(x-\frac{39}{1730}\right)^2+
 \frac{191}{5406250}>0.
\]
For $-2/5\le x\le0$, its expanded form gives
\[
 P(r)=x^2\left(\frac{173}{625}+\frac{38}{125}x\right)
 -\frac{39}{3125}x+\frac{11}{62500}
 \ge\frac{97}{625}x^2+\frac{11}{62500}>0.
\]
Homogeneity proves \eqref{eq:circular-ring-bound}.

\paragraph{The uniform inverse-distance certificate.}
Throughout the profile box,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:circular-a-kernel}
 K_A:=\sup_{s\in\mathbb C}\mathbb E
 \frac{(T-1)^2}{|L+s|}<\frac{211}{100}.
\end{equation}
Here is the reduction to the supplied directed interval checker.
Put $p(t)=a+bt$, $D(t)=a+3bt$, and $r(t)=\sqrt t\,p(t)$; use subscript
$0$ for the lower profile endpoint and subscript $1$ for the upper one.
The weighted pushforward under $z\mapsto L(z)$ has planar density
\[
 \frac1\pi\rho_{a,b}(r(t)),\qquad
 \rho_{a,b}(r(t))=\frac{e^{-t}(t-1)^2}{p(t)D(t)}
 \le\rho_0(t):=\frac{e^{-t}(t-1)^2}{p_0(t)D_0(t)}.
\]
Let $l=16/25$, $r_0=5/2$ and $H=\rho_0(l)$. Replace $\rho_0$ by the
constant $H$ on $[l,r_0]$, leaving it unchanged elsewhere. The resulting
function of the physical radius $r(t)$ is decreasing: $\rho_0$ decreases
on $[0,1)$; 8,192 interval rectangles give
\[
 \sup_{1\le t\le5/2}\rho_0(t)<0.123883<1/8<H;
\]
and on $[5/2,3]$ its logarithmic derivative is at most
\[
 \frac13-\frac{b_0}{a_0+3b_0}-\frac{3b_0}{a_0+9b_0}<0.
\]
Beyond $3$ the logarithmic derivative is automatically negative.

The convolution of two nonnegative radial decreasing functions is largest
at the common center. This follows by writing their level sets as disks
and observing that the overlap of two disks is maximal when concentric;
Tonelli and truncation cover the kernel $1/|w|$. Thus the desired supremum
is at most $2\int_0^\infty\widehat\rho(u)\,du$. The inequality
$D(t)/D_0(t)\le b_1/b_0$, and the change of variable $x=\sqrt t$, bound this by
\begin{align*}
 &\frac{b_1}{b_0}\left[
 \int_0^{4/5}\frac{2e^{-x^2}(x^2-1)^2}{a_0+b_0x^2}\,dx+
 \int_{\sqrt{5/2}}^\infty\frac{2e^{-x^2}(x^2-1)^2}{a_0+b_0x^2}\,dx
 \right]\\
 &\qquad+2H\left[
 a_1(\sqrt{5/2}-4/5)+b_1((5/2)\sqrt{5/2}-64/125)
 \right].
\end{align*}
The checker bounds the two finite radial integrals on 98,304 rational
rectangles and bounds the tail beyond $x=4$ analytically by
\[
 \frac{e^{-16}(16^2+2\cdot16+2)}{4a_0}.
\]
The total directed upper bound is
\[
 2.106737818390345870252<2.11.
\]
Grid coordinates, their squares and centered subtractions are checked
against exact rational arithmetic. Products forming squared numerators
are rounded upward. Exponentials and square roots are rounded correctly
at fifty decimal digits and then moved outward once. The verifier is
self-contained and uses only the Python standard library.

\paragraph{Completion of the addition theorem.}
Conditional circularity permits averaging the phase of $A$ while holding
$(Q,|A|,Z)$ fixed. Equation \eqref{eq:circular-ring-bound} bounds the sum
of the two norm increases by
\[
 \frac{38}{125}\mathbb E\left[
 |A|^2(T-1)^2\left(\frac1{|L+Q|}+\frac1{|L-Q|}\right)\right]
 \le\frac{76}{125}\frac{211}{100}\mathbb E|A|^2.
\]
The variance increases by $\mathbb E|A|^2$, since
$\mathbb E(T-1)=0$ and $\mathbb E(T-1)^2=1$. Therefore
\[
 \frac1\epsilon-\frac{76}{125}\frac{211}{100}
 =\frac{231481}{5909375}>0,
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:circular-a-theorem}.

\paragraph{New shared-driver family and remaining gap.}
For independent proper Gaussians $X,Y$, the cubic
$Q=Y(|X|^2-1)$ with $A=\alpha X$ satisfies conditional circularity for
every $\alpha$. The residual shares the modulus of the sector driver,
and is not linear in that driver with an independent coefficient.
An independent circular A driver over any remote residual is covered as
well, at every amplitude. The coherent Wick junta
$Q=cX(|X|^2-2)$, $A=\alpha X$ fails the circularity hypothesis.
General simultaneous B interactions and the complete row envelope remain
separate obligations; no new global numerical endpoint is asserted here.


\subsection{Fourfold symmetry suffices for the A-sector}
\label{sec:c4-a-addition}

The conditional circularity assumption in the preceding A-sector theorem
can be weakened to the finite symmetry
\[
 (Q,A)\overset{\rm law}=(Q,iA).
\]
For the same visible profile box, the same $\epsilon=1891/2500$, and every
square-integrable remote pair with this symmetry, we have the all-amplitude
comparison
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c4-a-theorem}
 \boxed{\Lambda(Q+(|Z|^2-1)A)\le\Lambda(Q)
       -\frac{999}{302560}\mathbb E|A|^2.}
\end{equation}
The coefficient is positive, approximately $0.0033018244$. Under the
stronger circularity assumption the preceding larger deficit remains valid.

\paragraph{The exact four-point inequality.}
For every $z\ne0$ and $w\in\mathbb C$,
\[
 \frac14\sum_{k=0}^3|z+i^kw|
 \le |z|+\frac5{16}\frac{|w|^2}{|z|}.
\]
By scaling and rotation, take $z=1$, $w=re^{i\theta}$. Put $a=1+r^2$ and
$f(t)=\sqrt{2a+2\sqrt{a^2-t}}$ on $[0,a^2]$. The function $f$ is
concave, as a composition of increasing concave square root with the
concave function $2a+2\sqrt{a^2-t}$. Pairing opposite points and using
$4r^2\le a^2$, we obtain
\[
 \frac14\sum_{k=0}^3|1+i^kre^{i\theta}|
 =\frac{f(4r^2\cos^2\theta)+f(4r^2\sin^2\theta)}4
 \le\sqrt{\frac{1+r^2+\sqrt{1+r^4}}2}.
\]
The angular maximum is attained at $\theta=\pi/4$. With $y=r^2$, two
positive squarings reduce the desired upper bound $1+5y/16$ to
\[
 \left(1+\frac y4+\frac{25y^2}{128}\right)^2-(1+y^2)
 =\frac{y}{16384}P(y)\ge0,
 \quad P(y)=8192-8960y+1600y^2+625y^3.
\]
Indeed,
\[
 P'(y)=(75y-112)(25y+80),\qquad
 \min_{y\ge0}P(y)=P(112/75)=\frac{311296}{675}>0.
\]

\paragraph{Application to A.}
Average both norms over the four conditional phases of $A$. Their total
increase is at most
\[
 \frac58 K_A\mathbb E|A|^2
 \le\frac58\frac{211}{100}\mathbb E|A|^2,
\]
using the already certified all-shift inverse-distance bound. The variance
increases by exactly $\mathbb E|A|^2$. Hence
\[
 \frac1\epsilon-\frac58\frac{211}{100}
 =\frac{999}{302560}>0,
\]
proving \eqref{eq:c4-a-theorem}.

\paragraph{A genuinely new shared-driver cubic family.}
Let $X,U,V$ be independent proper standard complex Gaussians and set
\[
 Q=X^2\bar U+U^2\bar V+V(|V|^2-2),\qquad A=X.
\]
Every summand of $Q$ is in $\mathcal H_{2,1}$. The Gaussian unitary
$(X,U,V)\mapsto(iX,-U,V)$ fixes $Q$ and sends $A$ to $iA$.
This proves the symmetry required above, at every scalar amplitude of $A$.
The pair is not conditionally circular: exact Gaussian moments give
\[
 \mathbb E[Q^5\bar Q\bar A^4]=80640\ne0.
\]
Writing the three summands as $q_1,q_2,q_3$, the contribution with
$\bar q_3$ is
$30\cdot24\cdot2\cdot32=46080$; the contribution with $\bar q_2$ is
$30\cdot24\cdot24\cdot2=34560$; the contribution with $\bar q_1$ vanishes.
Here
\[
 \mathbb E[T^2(T-2)^3]=32,\qquad
 \mathbb E[T^2(T-2)]=2,\qquad
 \mathbb E[T(T-2)]=0
\]
for standard exponential $T$. Conditional circularity would force the
displayed mixed moment to vanish. A separate standard-library checker
expands all 189 nonzero polynomial terms and verifies the expectation
exactly, as well as the four-point polynomial inequality.

The coherent Wick junta and general simultaneous A/B couplings remain
outside the theorem. This enlarges a proved class of the conjecture but
does not itself supply a new complete numerical lower bound.

\subsection{A non-Gaussian shared $B$ case, including simultaneous even channels}
\label{sec:shared-b-symmetry}
Let $\epsilon=1891/2500$, $L=Z(a+b|Z|^2)$, and
\[
 a\in[55919/100000,55921/100000],\qquad
 b\in[14319/100000,14321/100000].
\]
The visible $Z$ is standard proper complex Gaussian. All other random
variables in this section are jointly independent of $Z$; arbitrary
mutual dependence is allowed. Write
\[
 \Lambda(G)=\mathbb E(|L+G|+|L-G|)-\epsilon^{-1}\mathbb E|G|^2.
\]

\paragraph{A certified all-shift cost.}
Uniformly throughout this profile box,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shared-b-inverse}
 \sup_{s\in\mathbb C}\mathbb E_Z\frac{|Z|^4}{2|L+s|}<1.
\end{equation}
The standalone, standard-library checker \texttt{check\_b\_channel.py}
proves an outward upper bound $0.999851769051099$ using $65\,536$ radial
cells and an explicit infinite tail.

Here is the dimension-free analytic reduction. Set $X=a+bt$, $D=a+3bt$
and $r(t)=\sqrt tX$. The pushforward under $w=L(Z)$ of
$(|Z|^4/2)d\gamma(Z)$ has density, relative to $dA(w)/\pi$,
\[
 \rho(r(t))=\frac{e^{-t}t^2}{2XD}.
\]
The sign of its derivative is the sign of
$P(t)=a^2(2-t)+4abt(1-t)-3b^2t^3$.
Exact rational comparisons and Descartes' rule place its unique positive
zero in $[l,u]=[1.26308,1.26315]$ throughout the box. Put
\[
 \rho_0=\frac{e^{-l}u^2}
 {2(a_{\min}+b_{\min}l)(a_{\min}+3b_{\min}l)}.
\]
Replacing the density inside $r(u)$ by $\rho_0$ gives a radial decreasing
majorant. Its convolution with $1/|w|$ is maximal at zero: by layer cake,
this follows from the fact that the intersection of two disks has largest
area when their centres coincide. Hence the left side of
\eqref{eq:shared-b-inverse} is bounded by
\[
 2\rho_0\sqrt u(a_{\max}+b_{\max}u)
 +\int_u^\infty\frac{e^{-t}t^{3/2}}{2(a_{\min}+b_{\min}t)}\,dt.
\]
The integrand is decreasing after $u$. Left rectangles certify its
integral through $16$; the remaining integral is at most
$17e^{-16}/(8b_{\min})$, using $\sqrt t\le t/4$ for $t\ge16$.

\paragraph{Conditional centering suffices for $B$ alone.}
For every square-integrable remote pair $(Q,B)$ satisfying
$\mathbb E[B\mid Q]=0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shared-b-centered}
 \boxed{\quad
 \Lambda(Q+Z^2B/\sqrt2)
 \le\Lambda(Q)-\frac{609}{1891}\mathbb E|B|^2.
 \quad}
\end{equation}
There is no Gaussianity, chaos, small-amplitude or kurtosis hypothesis.
Indeed, the visible half-turn identifies the two payoff signs. Apply
\[
 2|x+y|\le2|x|+2\operatorname{Re}(\bar xy)/|x|+|y|^2/|x|
\]
with $x=L+Q$, $y=Z^2B/\sqrt2$. Conditional centering removes the linear
term; \eqref{eq:shared-b-inverse} bounds the quadratic term by
$\mathbb E|B|^2$. The variance cross term vanishes since $\mathbb EZ^2=0$.

\paragraph{A simultaneous interaction theorem.}
Suppose instead that the full remote law obeys
\[
 (Q,B,Y,W)\overset{\rm law}=(Q,-B,Y,W).
\]
Using the established even-channel quadratic bound with constant $32/25$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shared-b-even}
 \boxed{\begin{aligned}
 &\Lambda(Q+Z^2B/\sqrt2+ZY+\bar ZW)\\
 &\qquad\le\Lambda(Q)-\frac{609}{1891}\mathbb E|B|^2
       -\frac{1988}{47275}(\mathbb E|Y|^2+\mathbb E|W|^2).
 \end{aligned}}
\end{equation}
This controls the finite-amplitude interaction directly. To see this,
put $x=L+Q$, $b_0=Z^2B/\sqrt2$, $e_0=ZY+\bar ZW$.
The visible half-turn and the remote symmetry express the payoff as
$\frac12\mathbb E\sum_{s,t\in\{-1,1\}}|x+sb_0+te_0|$.
For the established positive quadratic form
\[
 \mathcal Q_x(y)=\frac{3|y|^2}{4|x|}
     -\frac{\operatorname{Re}(y^2\bar x^2)}{4|x|^3},
\]
pair opposite terms, use
$|x+y|+|x-y|\le2|x|+\mathcal Q_x(y)$, and apply the parallelogram
identity. The result is bounded by
$2\mathbb E|L+Q|+\mathbb E\mathcal Q_{L+Q}(b_0)
+\mathbb E\mathcal Q_{L+Q}(e_0)$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:shared-b-inverse} bounds the first cost; the newly certified
$32/25$ even-channel lemma bounds the second. Visible orthogonality makes
all variance prices additive.

\paragraph{Compatibility with the improved diagonal theorem.}

additional deficit $(609/1891)\mathbb E|B|^2$. For example, let

 J=\mathbb E|Y|^2+\mathbb E|W|^2,\qquad
 J_{\rm pred}=\mathbb E|\mathbb E[Y\mid Q]|^2+
               \mathbb E|\mathbb E[W\mid Q]|^2.
\]
For real $\nu(Q)$, the common-shift sharpened diagonal theorem gives
\[
 \sup_\nu\Lambda(Q+Z^2B/\sqrt2+ZY+\bar ZW+\nu(Q)H)
 \le\Lambda(Q)-\frac{609}{1891}\mathbb E|B|^2
 -\frac{1988}{47275}J+\frac{45444512}{26996475}J_{\rm pred}.
\]
In particular $J_{\rm pred}\le J/41$ leaves a deficit at least $J/1100$.
For complex $\nu(Q)$ the threshold $J_{\rm pred}\le J/123$
leaves $J/2000$. These statements use this round's newly certified
$32/25$ even-channel bound and common-shift diagonal theorem. The combined
theorem is restricted to the smaller profile box certified above.

\paragraph{A shared-driver cubic family.}
With independent remote standard proper Gaussians $X,U$, take
\[
 Q=cX^2\bar U+dH(U)+q_{\rm even},\qquad B=\lambda\bar X,
 \qquad Y=\eta(|X|^2-1),\qquad W=\theta X^2,
\]
where $q_{\rm even}$ is any remote charge-one cubic invariant under
$X\mapsto-X$. That transformation fixes $Q,Y,W$ and negates $B$.
Thus \eqref{eq:shared-b-even} applies at every amplitude, including
self-interacting non-Gaussian $Q$. An independent buffer can be added.
For a different example, $Q=cX^2\bar U+dU^2\bar X$, $B=\lambda\bar X$

\subsection{A rotation gate using the remote variance and kurtosis}
\label{sec:remote-sensitive-rotation}

The universal estimate $\|D_pG\|_2^2\le9\|G\|_2^2$ discards the
visible Hermite degree of the remote cubic. Retaining that degree yields
a stronger denominator without adding a hypothesis on the residual law.
The result applies simultaneously to all visible sectors; it does not
prove Conjecture K or constitute a new full numerical envelope.

\paragraph{Theorem.}
Let $G\in\mathcal H_{2,1}$, let $q$ be its orthogonal projection onto the
cubics independent of the visible coordinate $Z$, and suppose
$\|G\|_2^2\le V$. Put
\[
 v_0=\|q\|_2^2,\qquad R=V-v_0,\qquad
 \kappa_q=\mathbb E|q|^4-2v_0^2,\qquad
 \mu=\min\{3v_0,\sqrt{3\kappa_q}/2\}.
\]
For every unit remote first-chaos direction $p$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:remote-rotation-omega}
 \|D_pG\|_2^2\le
 \Omega:=9R+3\mu-2(\sqrt\mu-\sqrt R)_+^2
 \le9R+3\mu\le9V.
\end{equation}
The only moment input is the established influence contraction bound
\[
 I_p(q):=\|\partial_pq\|_2^2+\|\bar\partial_pq\|_2^2
 \le\min\{3v_0,\sqrt{3\kappa_q}/2\}.
\]
In particular, this uses the full remote cubic cumulant, never the
cumulant of a conditional mixed slice.

\paragraph{Proof.}
Decompose $G=G_0+G_1+G_2+G_3$ by its total visible Hermite degree, where
\[
 G_0=q,\quad G_1=YZ+W\bar Z,\quad
 G_2=r_A(|Z|^2-1)+r_BZ^2/\sqrt2,\quad
 G_3=\nu Z(|Z|^2-2)/\sqrt2.
\]
The generator changes visible degree by one, so
$D_p(G_0+G_2)\perp D_p(G_1+G_3)$. Writing $s=\|G_2\|_2^2$, the required
finite tensor bounds are
\[
 \|D_pG_2\|_2^2\le7s,\qquad
 |\langle D_pq,D_pG_2\rangle|\le2\sqrt{I_p(q)s},\qquad
 \|D_p(G_1+G_3)\|_2^2\le9(\|G_1\|_2^2+\|G_3\|_2^2).
\]
To prove them in every dimension, embed the Wick tensor isometrically in
three labelled one-particle slots. Split each slot into the plane
$\operatorname{span}\{Z,p\}$ and its orthogonal complement. After a harmless
sign convention for the generator, on each summand with $m\le3$ active
slots it has the form $D=A-A^*$, where $A$ changes $p$ to $Z$.
For the basis indexed by visible subsets $S$ of these slots,
\[
 (Af)_T=\sum_{i\in T}f_{T\setminus\{i\}},\qquad |T|=k+1.
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz followed by counting the $m-k$ successors of each source
gives
\[
 \|A|_k\|^2\le(k+1)(m-k),\qquad
 \|A^*|_k\|^2\le k(m-k+1).
\]
The two output degrees are orthogonal. At $k=2$ the sum of these bounds
is at most $7$, and the three one-particle operator norms give
$\|D\|\le3$. Since $A^*q=0$, one has $Dq=Aq$ and
$\|Aq\|^2=I_p(q)$. Moreover
\[
 |\langle Dq,DG_2\rangle|
 =|\langle A^2q,G_2\rangle|
 \le2\|Aq\|\|G_2\|,
\]
because the degree-one to degree-two raising norm is at most $2$.
These estimates remain valid upon restricting to the symmetric Wick
tensor subspace.

Consequently, for $0\le s\le R$,
\[
 \|D_pG\|^2\le\mu+7s+4\sqrt{\mu s}+9(R-s)
 =9R+3\mu-2(\sqrt s-\sqrt\mu)^2.
\]
The maximum is at $s=\min\{\mu,R\}$, proving
\eqref{eq:remote-rotation-omega}. The last inequality follows from
$\mu\le3v_0$. This also covers $v_0=0$.

\paragraph{Sharp elementary form.}
If no cumulant information is used, set $\mu=3v_0$. The exact largest
rotation cost given only $V$ and $v_0$ is
\[
 \Omega_0(V,v_0)=
 \begin{cases}
 9V,&v_0\le V/4,\\
 7V-4v_0+4\sqrt{3v_0(V-v_0)},&v_0\ge V/4.
 \end{cases}
\]
Indeed, the fully symmetric three-slot block on visible degrees $0,2$
has compression, up to the sign of one basis vector,
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}3&\sqrt{12}\\\sqrt{12}&7\end{pmatrix}
\]
with eigenvalues $1,9$. Its top eigenvector has degree-zero mass fraction
$1/4$. The degree-$1,3$ block also has top eigenvalue $9$, and supplies
any remaining variance. The cumulant-dependent estimate above may have
additional slack in its separate influence input.

\paragraph{Exact scaling in the pinned certificate.}
The pinned remote coordinate is $p_0=P_{2,1}^{\rm rem}k$, whereas
$q=\epsilon p_0$. With
\[
 \tau=\|p_0\|_2,\quad
 x=\sqrt{\mathbb E|p_0|^4/\tau^4-2},\quad
 V=\epsilon^2\gamma(t),\quad
 \gamma(t)=\frac{272665900591}{10^{11}}-3t,
\]
we have $v_0=\epsilon^2\tau^2$ and
$\mu=\epsilon^2\tau^2\min\{3,\sqrt3x/2\}$. Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rotation-law-factor}
 \|J_G\|^2\le\epsilon^2
 \left[9\gamma(t)-\bigl(9-3\min\{3,\sqrt3x/2\}\bigr)\tau^2\right].
\end{equation}
The omitted nonnegative term from
\eqref{eq:remote-rotation-omega} can strengthen this further.

For a gate used only when $\tau\ge\tau_-$ and $0\le x\le x_+$, define
\[
 \bar c=\min\{3,7x_+/8\},\qquad
 F_{\rm box}(t)=9\gamma(t)-(9-3\bar c)\tau_-^2.
\]
Since $\sqrt3/2<7/8$, this is an all-rational upper bound for the
bracket in \eqref{eq:rotation-law-factor}. The positivity of
$F_{\rm box}$ is checked over the gate's $t$ interval. At
$(t,\tau,x)=(17/20,61/250,18/25)$ the direct rational version has ratio
\[
 \frac{F_+}{9\gamma(t)}=
 \frac{12962556591}{17665900591}<0.734.
\]
Thus the rotation coefficient grows by more than $36.2\%$ there.
This percentage concerns the gate coefficient, not the Grothendieck bound.

\paragraph{Gate insertion and square accounting.}
At a global maximizer the same Ward identity as before gives
\[
 \epsilon\|H\|^2\ge
 \frac{\|t x_0+2\beta x_A+\sqrt2\beta x_B\|^2}
      {\epsilon F_{\rm box}(t)}.
\]
For an allocation $0\le a\le1$, the new admissible first gate is
\[
 E\succeq\operatorname{diag}(-1,\epsilon,\epsilon)
 -\frac{a}{\epsilon F_{\rm box}(t)}
 v_\beta(t)v_\beta(t)^T,
 \qquad v_\beta(t)=(t,2\beta,\sqrt2\beta)^T.
\]
All other gates and the pinned price are unchanged. The seven principal
minor numerators remain quadratic over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$, because
$F_{\rm box}$ is affine in $t$. The retained $h$ square is spent only
once; no old two-square penalty or later mixture discount is appended.
Source profiles are combined before applying the new gate, as in the
previous rotation construction.

The complete cover checker must authenticate the additional
$(\tau,x)$ domain and verify that every selected cell and every node

\subsection{IDE reading note}\label{sec:sharedsector-reading}
\paragraph{What the symmetry hypotheses assume, and which of our test residuals satisfy them.} Both $A$ theorems are statements about the \emph{joint law} of the remote pair: a measure-preserving
map of the remote space must fix $Q$ and rotate $A$ by $i$ (or by every phase). Since a remote cubic is charge one, a global phase never fixes $Q$; the symmetry must come from a nontrivial stabiliser of $Q$
in the remote unitary group, which a \emph{generic} cubic does not have. Hence:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=1pt]
\item \emph{Coherent Wick junta} $q=cH(X)+sW$ with $A=\alpha X$ (the family of \autoref{ch:weighted}, \autoref{ch:abattack}, \autoref{ch:weakk} on which the measured $A/B$ gains live): \textbf{fails} (GPT says so).
Same for $B=\lambda\bar X$: $\E[B\mid Q]\ne0$; exactly, $\E[\bar X\,H|H|^2]=\E[T^2(T-2)^3]/(2\sqrt2)=32/(2\sqrt2)\ne0$, so $\E[\bar X\mid q]\ne0$ at $s=0$ \st{C}. This is the same obstruction as in the
reading note of \autoref{ch:remote-cubic-refinement}: conditional centring of coefficients that share drivers with $q$ is \emph{not} automatic, and here it fails on exactly the binding family.
\item \emph{The (T) counterexample} $q=(7/(20\sqrt2))X^2\bar Y$ of \autoref{ch:weighted}: the torus $(X,Y)\mapsto(e^{i\phi}X,e^{2i\phi}Y)$ fixes $q$, so $A=\alpha X$ (take $\phi=\pi/2$, $Y\to-Y$) and
$A=\alpha Y$ ($\phi=\pi/4$) each satisfy the fourfold symmetry, and $B=\lambda\bar X$ or $\lambda\bar Y$ is conditionally centred; a \emph{mixed} $A=\alpha X+\alpha'Y$ does not satisfy $(Q,A)\overset{d}=(Q,iA)$
(no single torus element multiplies both by $i$) although its first-chaos content still vanishes by the torus average. So the theorem covers it coordinate by coordinate, not in general position.
\item \emph{Independent drivers} ($A$, $B$ built on coordinates independent of $Q$): covered (already covered by the Gaussian-factor theorems of \autoref{ch:kjoint} when $Q$ is Gaussian; new when $Q$ is not).
\item \emph{L2 maximisers and the coupled kernels} of \autoref{ch:kjoint}, \autoref{ch:crossbound} (buffered coupled $n=2$ kernel, coupled L2 maximisers at $n=2$--$6$): generic, no stabiliser; \textbf{not covered}.
\end{itemize}
\paragraph{How much of the measured junta/L2 gains would they pay?} If the hypotheses held, \emph{all} of it: the symmetry kills the first-order content $\E[F_D(Q)\,\bar A]$ (phase average), which is
precisely the measured mixed gain of \autoref{ch:abattack} and \autoref{ch:weakk}, and leaves a negative quadratic. Read the other way: a family with a nonzero measured gain (the coherent junta, e.g.\ gain
$\approx10^{-3}$ at $(c,s)=(0.2,0)$, of which K$_{\rm rot}$ pays a quarter to a half) necessarily violates both hypotheses, so these theorems contribute nothing on the families that bind (T$_0$). They
consume none of the $9.06\cdot10^{-5}$ budget either: on the covered classes K$_\eta$ holds with the remote $\eta<10^{-5}$ alone. The certified quadratic penalties are moreover method-limited \st{F}
(\code{sharedsector\_own\_check.py}): the four-point constant $5/16$ is sharp to $0.4\%$ (true $0.3112$) and the ring constant $38/125$ to $2\%$ (true $0.2988$), but the all-shift kernels are
loose---$\sup_sE[(T-1)^2/|L+s|]\approx2.027$ (certified $<2.107$) and $\sup_sE[T^2/(2|L+s|)]\approx0.917$ at $|s|\approx1.26$ (certified $<0.99985$, via the radial-decreasing rearrangement)---so the true
penalties are about $0.06$ (fourfold $A$), $0.11$ (circular $A$) and $0.41$ ($B$) per unit variance, against the certified $0.0033$, $0.039$, $0.322$. The fourfold-$A$ margin
$1/\varepsilon-\tfrac58\cdot2.11=0.0033$ is thin; it is a proof of sign, not a quantitative resource.

\paragraph{The diagonal $0.462\to0.571$: what changed.} Nothing in the density envelope. In \S\ref{sec:gaussian-sharpening} the eleven Gaussian components of the envelope were each charged their own
worst shift (``one-channel lemma'', the method limit we recorded there: envelope ratio $\ge1.0017$, finer cells useless). Here the \emph{same} shift $s$ is kept across all eleven terms until after
summation, $\sup_z\sum_jw_jB(\lambda_jz)/2$ instead of $\sum_j\sup$; the real cost drops $0.860\to0.751$, penalty $1/\varepsilon-0.751=0.571$. Our directional quadrature (\autoref{ch:remote-cubic-refinement})
gave a true real cost $\approx0.715$, so $0.751$ is now within $0.036$ of the truth; the imaginary channel ($1.057$ certified vs.\ $0.992$ true) was not reworked. The common-shift identity
$\E\mathcal Q_{G+s}(G)=\frac{\sqrt\pi}4B(|s|^2)$ is GPT's; its series and tails are exact/directed and replayed.

\paragraph{The sharper rotation denominator.} \autoref{ch:weakk} had $\varepsilon^2t^2+9\varepsilon\|G\|^2$ (both squares), \autoref{ch:rotation-gate-refinement} $9\varepsilon\gamma(t)$ (retained $H$-square only,
$\|G\|^2\le\varepsilon^2\gamma(t)$). The new tensor bound $\|D_pG\|^2\le9R+3\mu-2(\sqrt\mu-\sqrt R)_+^2$, $R=V-v_0$, $\mu=\min\{3v_0,\sqrt{3\kappa_q}/2\}$, uses that the remote cubic $q$ (visible degree $0$)
is rotated only through its influence $I_p(q)\le\mu$, and degree two only with norm $\sqrt7$; in the pinned variables it is $\varepsilon[9\gamma(t)-(9-3\min\{3,\sqrt3x/2\})\tau^2]$. At $(t,\tau,x)=(0.85,0.244,0.72)$
the bracket is $0.733\cdot9\gamma$ (we recomputed: $v_0/V=\tau^2/\gamma=0.34>1/4$, so even the variance-only sharp form helps), i.e.\ $+36\%$ on the gate coefficient. Unlike ch.~\autoref{ch:weakk}'s
$\omega=\min\{9v,3\sqrt{3\kappa/2}\}$, which ``changed nothing'' on the junta data (tiny junta variances), this one bites at the certificate's binding law because the pinned $\tau$ is not small. It needs a
$(\tau,x)$-authenticated cover (the local gate demo checks one box; no cover checker yet). This is the part relevant to the certificate worker; the endpoint gain is unknown \st{?} (as of \code{20eb917} still untried: the combined certificates of
\S\ref{sec:certcombinede} use no rotation gates, which would need the extra hypothesis \code{RotShared}).

densities is maximal at the centre'' is standard). The symmetric $A/B$ classes enlarge the proved part of K but avoid exactly the coherent shared-driver content that (T$_0$) is about; the open crux
is unchanged. Most useful now: the rotation cost (iv) for the regeneration, and the diagonal $0.571$.
