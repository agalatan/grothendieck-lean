\subsection{Two sufficient routes, with distinct quantifiers}
At the peak set
\[
 a=699/1250,\quad b=179/1250,\quad p=1/\varepsilon=2500/1891,\quad L=Z(a+b|Z|^2),
\]
\[
 \Lambda(G)=\E|L+G|+\E|L-G|-p\|G\|_2^2,\qquad S=\sup_{v\ge0}\{2\E|L+\sqrt vU|-pv\}\quad(S>1.37395,\ v_*\approx0.0403).
\]
Standard complex Gaussians are proper with second moment one, and $U$ is fresh and independent whenever it is used as a buffer.
\textbf{K at the peak} asks for $\Lambda(G)\le S$ for every finite dimension and every $G\in\Hc21$; \textbf{K$_\eta$} replaces $S$ by $S+\eta$.
\textbf{Profile uniformity} additionally quantifies over the row parameters. \textbf{A new endpoint} additionally needs a complete row cover and the project
reduction/transfer chain. The gate-free row identity is Proposition~\ref{prop:row}; the general-profile conjecture is Conjecture~\ref{conj:K}.

\textbf{Target for $1.402$}: K$_\eta$ on the peak box and the central rows, with the visible shared sectors fitting in
$\eta_{AB}=240297199/2651182000000\approx9.06\cdot10^{-5}$ (\autoref{ch:remote-cubic-refinement}); then the row envelope and a regenerated certificate at
$\varepsilon\approx0.7564$. The degree-3 ceiling is $1.402258$, so K can deliver at most that. A theorem for a restricted family at the peak does not by itself spend that
budget for an arbitrary residual. \emph{Corner of the row box:} where $\E[1/(2|L|)]\le1/\varepsilon$ the optimal variance is $v_*=0$ and $S=2\E|L|$ (around
$\varepsilon\approx0.74$--$0.75$, $\ell\gtrsim0.85$, $\beta\approx0.12$--$0.13$); arguments assuming $v_*>0$ must treat it separately.

\paragraph{The weaker original-response route.}
The preceding K statements are sufficient, not necessary. For actual bounded
responses $h=(f_++f_-)/2$, $k=(f_+-f_-)/2$, $Ph=tZ$, and
$F=\varepsilon Qk$, the exact objective is
\[
 \mathcal N=t^2+\varepsilon\|Qk\|^2-\|Pk\|^2-\varepsilon\|Qh\|^2.
\]
It is enough to bound this on the convex response body, uniformly in dimension
and over a complete cover of responses at the same $\varepsilon$.
\autoref{ch:weakk} develops this route; it retains information discarded
in raw K. Its regional statements are recorded separately below.

\subsection{Decomposition and exact reductions}
With $T=|Z|^2$,
\[
 G=q+(T-1)r_A+\frac{Z^2r_B}{\sqrt2}+ZY+\bar ZW+\nu H(Z),\qquad H(z)=z(|z|^2-2)/\sqrt2 .
\]
The remote bidegrees are $(2,1),(1,0),(0,1),(1,1),(2,0)$, and all remote fields may share coordinates. After removing the visible phase, odd relative frequencies carry the
$A/B$ content; even frequencies have a different cancellation. Successful comparisons integrate the visible variable first.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{One visible variable} \st{P} (\autoref{ch:forest}): a joint phase rotation reduces $\Lambda$ to an average over $T\sim\mathrm{Exp}(1)$ of a remote problem
with shift $\ell(T)=\sqrt T(a+bT)$; the price identity is exact.
\item \emph{Gaussian scale mixture} \st{P} (Lemma~\ref{lem:gsm}, \autoref{ch:rescub}): $L\overset d=\sqrt VW$, so $v\mapsto\sqrt\pi\,\E\sqrt{V+v}$ is concave.
\item \emph{K for every $\varepsilon$} $\iff$ Gaussian domination on every variance sphere (\autoref{ch:stronger}).
\item \emph{The remote part} \st{P}: for residuals independent of $Z$, exact K for every profile is equivalent to PVD$_0$, $\E|q|\le\frac{\sqrt\pi}2\|q\|_2$ on $\Hc21$,
which follows from BNC (Proposition~\ref{prop:bnc}). K$_\eta$ for the remote part is proved with slack $<10^{-5}$, so PVD$_0$ is worth only exactness.
\item \emph{Gaussian layer plus non-Gaussian gap} \st{P} (\autoref{ch:polarized-profile-certificate}): $\Lambda\le S-y/10-z/4-e/25+D$, with $D$ the visibly
averaged gap to the matched Gaussian. $D$ is not small in general (a cold-start example reaches $D\approx1.2\cdot10^{-2}$, absorbed by the $A$ penalty with ratio
$0.29$; Appendix~\ref{app:K-register-archive}), so any proof must use the $A$ cost.
\item \emph{Finite shared spans} \st{P} (\autoref{ch:milestone-map-20261020}): two shared linear directions span at most two coordinates, but the cubic keeps
coefficient fields of arbitrary variance outside that span; the complement cost is exactly a five-block weighted Gram problem; no universal dominant-coordinate reduction
exists.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Why the bounds stall: the duality view (IDE, 22 October).}
For the real constant, Heilman--Jones--Malavolta prove the minimax
$1/K_G=\sup_\mu[c_1(\mu)-\sum_{m\ge3}|c_m(\mu)|]=\inf_Q\mathrm{OPT}(Q)$ over
rounding schemes $\mu$ (kernel coefficients $c_m$) and Hermite projection
games $Q=\sum_mq_m\Pi_m$ (arXiv:2610.10477, Thm.~2.2); the complex problem
has the same structure on charge-one bidegrees $(k+1,k)$. A game supported
on degrees $\{1,3\}$ only sees the projection of the convex set of
achievable kernels onto $(c_1,c_3)$: it bounds the best rounding that need
only cancel the \emph{cubic} coefficient. Under Gaussian extremality its
value is about $1/0.71313=1.40227$ at the best $\epsilon\approx0.756$, a ceiling
for every certificate of $P_{1,0}-\epsilon P_{2,1}$. Our upper bound
$1.404599424\stale{22 Oct}{now $\KG<1.404598161566$}$ is only $2.35\cdot10^{-5}$ above Haagerup's integral
$1/\gamma^\dagger=1.404575935$ (HJM's conjectured value), so the
$2.9\cdot10^{-3}$ gap is almost entirely the cost of cancelling degrees
$\ge5$ that the cubic witness cannot see. The real case shows the same
pattern: its certified game has nonzero coefficients at every odd degree
$1,\dots,21$, and the certified lower bound lags only because
$\mathrm{OPT}$ of that game is hard to certify. A degree-nine complex game
(\S\ref{sec:finite-shared-witness-20261021}) already has best challengers
$0.712056$, i.e.\ at most $1.40438$. A significant lower-bound move
therefore needs a higher-degree game with a multi-chaos certificate, and a
complex primal/dual search (HJM's alternating LP) would locate $K_G^{\C}$ to
$\sim10^{-4}$ and test $1/\gamma^\dagger$. Full note:
\pathref{WHY\_THE\_GAP.md}.
