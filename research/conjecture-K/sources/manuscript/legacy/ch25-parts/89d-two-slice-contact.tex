\subsection{Two interacting slices and a canonical many-slice reduction}
\label{sec:two-slice-contact}

\paragraph{Status and scope.}
This \st{P,C} result continues \S\ref{sec:whole-slice-contact} for
actual bounded responses at $\epsilon=1891/2500$, $p=2500/1891$,
$Ph=tZ$, $F=\epsilon Qk$, $V=\|F\|^2$ and
$\mathcal N=t^2+pV-\|Pk\|^2-\epsilon\|Qh\|^2$.
For orthonormal remote first-chaos directions $X_1,\ldots,X_r$, let
\[
 \mathcal W_r=\mathcal S_{X_1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal S_{X_r},
 \qquad \mathcal S_X=\{{:}X^2\bar A{:}/\sqrt2:A\perp Z\}.
\]
The sum is orthogonal: different repeated holomorphic directions
have orthogonal symmetric tensor factors. The antiholomorphic
directions are arbitrary, and may include all the $X_i$ and any
additional remote coordinates. This is not the class of all tensors
supported on $r$ holomorphic coordinates: mixed holomorphic products
are not silently added to the definition.

\begin{theorem}[\st{P,C}: two-slice concentration]
\label{thm:two-slice-contact}
For every finite dimension and $21/25\le t\le851/1000$,
\[
 \boxed{\begin{aligned}
 \|\Pi_{\mathcal W_2}F\|^2\ge\frac{39}{40}V
       &\ \Longrightarrow\ \mathcal N<\frac{1783}{2500}=0.7132,\\
 \|\Pi_{\mathcal W_2}F\|^2\ge\frac{49}{50}V
       &\ \Longrightarrow\ \mathcal N<\frac{713}{1000}=0.7130.
 \end{aligned}}
\]
All phases and arbitrary coupled remainders are allowed, including
every visible sector. There is no fresh-buffer, higher-moment or
separate amplitude assumption. In particular the theorem covers
concentration near any combination of $X_1^2\bar X_2$ and
$X_2^2\bar X_1$, as well as all other terms in $\mathcal W_2$.
These are regional caps, not a complete same-witness cover or a new
Grothendieck endpoint.
\end{theorem}

\subsubsection{A reduction valid for every number of slices}
The visibly averaged function
\[
 \Phi(v)=\E|Z(a+b|Z|^2)+\sqrt v|,\qquad a>0,\ b\ge0,
\]
is increasing and concave by the scale-mixture proof in
\S\ref{sec:whole-slice-contact}. This is the true visible average,
not the fixed-radius angular mean.

\begin{lemma}[\st{P}: symmetric three-profile majorant]
\label{lem:multislice-support-reduction}
Let $J\in\mathcal W_r$ have norm one, and set $\psi=\sqrt2J$.
For $r\ge2$ there are $\alpha,\beta,\gamma\ge0$ with
$\alpha+\beta+\gamma=1$ such that, for independent
$U,R\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(r,1)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:multislice-support}
 \E|Z(a+b|Z|^2)+d\psi|
 \le\E\Phi\bigl(d^2\{\alpha D_r(U)+\beta I_r(U)+\gamma E_r(U,R)\}\bigr),
\end{equation}
where
\begin{align*}
 D_r(u)&=\frac{6u^3}{r(r+1)(r+2)}
           -\frac{8u^2}{r(r+1)}+\frac{4u}{r},\\
 I_r(u)&=\frac{2u^3}{r(r+1)(r+2)},\qquad
 E_r(u,s)=\frac{2u^2s}{r^2(r+1)}.
\end{align*}
All profiles are nonnegative and have mean $2$.
The right side is a concave function of the three energy weights.
For $r=1$ the same formula applies with $\beta=0$ and reproduces the
earlier single-slice phase-Jensen majorant.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write, before the normalization by $\sqrt2$,
\[
 \psi=\sum_{i,j\le r}a_{ij}{:}X_i^2\bar X_j{:}
           +\sum_{i\le r}X_i^2\bar B_i,
\]
where the jointly proper Gaussian vector $(B_i)$ is orthogonal to
$(Z,X_1,\ldots,X_r)$. Its components need not be independent.
Orthogonality and the Wick norms give
\[
 \sum_{i,j}|a_{ij}|^2+\sum_i\|B_i\|^2=1.
\]
Define $\alpha=\sum_i|a_{ii}|^2$, $\beta=\sum_{i\ne j}|a_{ij}|^2$
and $\gamma=\sum_i\|B_i\|^2$.
Condition on $T_i=|X_i|^2$ and on $(B_i)$. The distinct phase
frequencies are $e_i$, $2e_i-e_j$ ($i\ne j$), and $2e_i$.
All cross terms vanish under the independent $X_i$ phases.
Jensen for $\Phi$ bounds the support by the expectation of
\[
 \Phi\!\left(d^2\left\{
 \sum_i|a_{ii}|^2T_i(T_i-2)^2
 +\sum_{i\ne j}|a_{ij}|^2T_i^2T_j
 +\sum_i T_i^2|B_i|^2\right\}\right).
\]

For fixed $T$, the last sum has the law
$\sum_{j=1}^r\lambda_j S_j$, where $S_j$ are independent unit
exponentials, $\lambda_j\ge0$ and
$\sum_j\lambda_j=\sum_iT_i^2\|B_i\|^2$.
Pad the eigenvalues with zeros if necessary. Average their
permutations and apply concavity: replacing all eigenvalues by their
mean increases the expectation. Thus this last sum can be replaced
in the upper bound by
$(R/r)\sum_iT_i^2\|B_i\|^2$, with $R\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(r,1)$
independent of $T$.

Now average all permutations of the $T_i$ and apply concavity again.
The squared radius becomes
\[
 \frac\alpha r\sum_iT_i(T_i-2)^2
 +\frac\beta{r(r-1)}\sum_{i\ne j}T_i^2T_j
 +\frac{\gamma R}{r^2}\sum_iT_i^2.
\]
Condition on $U=\sum_iT_i$. The proportions have the uniform
Dirichlet law and are independent of $U,R$; their moments give
\[
 \E[\sum T_i^2\mid U]=\frac{2U^2}{r+1},\qquad
 \E[\sum T_i^3\mid U]=\frac{6U^3}{(r+1)(r+2)}.
\]
One final conditional Jensen inequality proves the formulas.
Nonnegativity of the only centered profile is explicit:
\[
 3r(r+1)(r+2)D_r(u)
 =2u\{3u-2(r+2)\}^2+4(r-1)(r+2)u\ge0.
\]
Gamma moments give mean $2$ for all three profiles.
No step replaces the unknown $F$ by a different law: these are
upper comparisons only for the known support test $J$.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{The two-slice triangle is covered by five values per row}
For $r=2$ the profiles are
\[
 D_2(u)=\frac{u\{(3u-8)^2+8\}}{36},\qquad
 I_2(u)=\frac{u^3}{12},\qquad E_2(u,s)=\frac{u^2s}{6}.
\]
Let $B(\alpha,\beta)$ be the right side of
\eqref{eq:multislice-support}, with $\gamma=1-\alpha-\beta$.
It is concave on the coefficient triangle
$\alpha,\beta\ge0$, $\alpha+\beta\le1$.
Fresh directed integration at
\[
 (1/10,1/10),\ (1/5,1/10),\ (0,1/10),\
 (1/10,1/5),\ (1/10,0)
\]
proves the following uniform support bounds:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
 \ell&b&d&\sup B<H\\\hline
 1&0&1/4&93835/100000\\
 1&37/200&87/500&83898/100000
\end{array}
\]
Here $a=\ell-2b$, so both visible profiles have $a>0$.
The first fresh replay gives continuum upper bounds below
$0.938326630245538$ and $0.838964356881351$.

\paragraph{A finite concave certificate, without derivative estimates.}
At $x_0=(1/10,1/10)$ choose a supergradient $s$ of the concave $B$.
Such a vector exists because $x_0$ is interior and $B$ is finite and
continuous. Write $\delta=1/10$, and use $l,H$ for certified lower
and upper point values. Concavity forces
\[
 \frac{l(x_0+\delta e_j)-H(x_0)}\delta
 \le s_j\le
 \frac{H(x_0)-l(x_0-\delta e_j)}\delta.
\]
For any $x$ in the whole triangle,
$B(x)\le H(x_0)+s\cdot(x-x_0)$.
Maximize the right side over the certified interval for each $s_j$.
The resulting function of $x$ is convex, so its maximum is at one of
the three triangle vertices. These three comparisons are rational.
They certify every coefficient and phase, not a sampled grid.

\subsubsection{Retaining the response cost and certifying the row}
Choose the unit $J\in\mathcal W_2$ aligned with
$\Pi_{\mathcal W_2}F$, unless $V=0$, in which case use the visible
test below directly. The preceding support lemma gives
\[
 u=\sqrt2|\langle J,F\rangle|,\quad V\le u^2/(2\eta),\quad
 u\le U(t)=\frac{H_0-t}{p/4},
\]
and the retained cost satisfies
\[
 \|Pk\|^2+\epsilon\|Qh\|^2
   \ge\frac{[t+pdu-H]_+^2}{2pb^2}.
\]
Use $u_0=31/125$. For $u\le u_0$, the visible-only test
$Z(|Z|^2-1/10)$, with freshly checked
$I_0=124233393/10^8$, gives
\[
 \mathcal N\le t^2+\frac{pu_0^2}{2\eta}
                 -\frac{(1.9t-I_0)^2}{2p}.
\]
For $u\ge u_0$ use
\[
 C_\eta(t,u)=t^2+\frac{pu^2}{2\eta}
                 -\frac{(t+pdu-H)^2}{2pb^2}.
\]
The same four-endpoint argument as the preceding subsection applies;
all sign, monotonicity and convexity conditions are checked exactly.
The largest rational endpoint is below $0.713182679967563$ for
$\eta=39/40$, and below $0.712969934087370$ for $\eta=49/50$.
This proves Theorem~\ref{thm:two-slice-contact}. The retained square
is spent once; complete valid caps may be combined by minimum.

\subsubsection{What the reduction explains, and what remains}
The previous two-way tensor
$(X_1^2\bar X_2+X_2^2\bar X_1)/2$ belongs to $\mathcal W_2$.
Its entire concentration neighbourhood is now covered. The new theorem
is larger than a single test for that tensor: it allows every diagonal,
internal and external antiholomorphic coefficient in both slices.

The reduction also explains its own limitation. As $r\to\infty$,
$U/r,R/r\to1$ in every fixed $L^p$, and
$D_r(U),I_r(U),E_r(U,R)\to2$ in $L^1$.
Thus its right side tends, uniformly over the weight simplex, to
$\Phi(2d^2)$, the constant-modulus support, rather than the diffuse
Gaussian support. The norm's Lipschitz property bounds the difference
by $d\sqrt{\E|\alpha D_r+\beta I_r+\gamma E_r-2|}$.
Discarding all relative phases cannot supply a sharp diffuse theorem.
The many-slice reduction is valid, but it is not an unrestricted-K
proof and is not asserted to retain a useful margin for large $r$.

Even the concentration tests leave explicit coupled shapes. For
orthogonal remote $X_1,X_2,X_3$, let
\[
 F_3=(X_1^2\bar X_2+X_2^2\bar X_3+X_3^2\bar X_1)/\sqrt6.
\]
It is unit. Its maximal $\mathcal W_2$ projection share is $2/3$:
for orthogonal unit $x,y$ the share is
$\frac13\sum_i(|x_i|^4+|y_i|^4)\le2/3$, with equality at coordinate
directions. Its maximal single-slice and orthogonal antiholomorphic
block shares are $1/3$; its combined influence matrix is the identity
on these three coordinates. This is an obstruction to exhausting the
tensor space by these concentration tests, not a counterexample to K
or a constructed high-payoff bounded response.

The remaining obligation is a joint-response bound on the complement,
or an exhaustive quantitative structural alternative, followed by the
flank rows at the same witness parameter. The package
\code{grothendieck-two-slice-contact-20261022.zip} includes the standalone
\code{verify\_all.py}, proof, exact replays, source snapshots and SHA256
manifest. It supplies no new Lean theorem and no global endpoint.
