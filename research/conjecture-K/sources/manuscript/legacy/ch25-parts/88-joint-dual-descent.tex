\subsection{Recovering the full joint defect and descending the exact dual}
\label{sec:joint-dual-descent}
\paragraph{Scope.}
The finite phase-cut obstruction in
\S\ref{sec:phase-lift-screening-20261022} concerns its specified tests;
it does not obstruct the pointwise original-response dual.
The following two analytic improvements are \st{P}, with exact
polynomial checks \st{C}. They remove the fixed loss in approximating
the joint defect and give an explicit descent step for that dual.
They use no new moment bound, tensor independence, or fresh buffer.
There is no new complete row cover or endpoint.

\subsubsection{A rational hierarchy with an explicit relative error}
Use $H,B_0,P_0,R_{\rm joint},\Delta$ from
\S\ref{sec:joint-response-defect}, and put
\[
 A=|L+D|+|L-D|,\qquad
 b_0=\bigl||L+D|-|L-D|\bigr|,\qquad
 f(z)=z^4-4B_0z^2+16l^2x^2.
\]
Thus $f(z)=(z^2-A^2)(z^2-b_0^2)$ and $A\ge b_0\ge0$.
Start at $z_0=H-R_{\rm joint}\ge A$ and define
\[
 z_{j+1}=z_j-\frac{f(z_j)}{4z_j(z_j^2-2B_0)},\qquad
 D_j=H-z_j.
\]
If the denominator vanishes, then $z_j=A=b_0$ already; keep that
value fixed. Every iterate is rational in the original data once the
initial regularized weight is fixed. No evaluation of either norm is
needed in this recurrence.
\begin{theorem}[\st{P,C}: relative-error joint-defect hierarchy]
\label{thm:joint-defect-hierarchy}
The iterates satisfy
\[
 0\le R_{\rm joint}=D_0\le D_j\le D_{j+1}\le\Delta,
 \qquad
 \Delta-D_j\le\frac{169}{250}\left(\frac34\right)^j\Delta.
\]
In particular,
\[
 \boxed{\quad \frac{9999}{10000}\Delta\le D_{32}\le\Delta.\quad}
\]
Also $D_{40}\ge(1-10^{-5})\Delta$ and
$D_{48}\ge(1-10^{-6})\Delta$. If $\E H<\infty$, the same estimates
hold after expectation, and $\E D_j\uparrow\E\Delta$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $z\ge A\ge b_0\ge0$ with positive denominator, write
$d_f=2z(2z^2-A^2-b_0^2)=f'(z)$. The two identities
\begin{align*}
 d_f(z-A)-f(z)
   &=(z-A)^2(3z^2+2Az-b_0^2),\\
 4f(z)-d_f(z-A)
   &=2(z-A)\bigl((z+2A)(A^2-b_0^2)+2A(z^2-A^2)\bigr)
\end{align*}
give $0\le z-f(z)/d_f-A\le(3/4)(z-A)$.
Both right-hand sides are nonnegative on the stated domain.
If $d_f=0$, $z\ge A\ge b_0$ forces $z=A=b_0$, including zero.
Initially $z_0-A=\Delta-R_{\rm joint}\le(169/250)\Delta$;
induction proves the result. The numerical fractions follow from exact
integer comparisons. Domination by $H$ justifies expectation and the
limit. The $3/4$ one-step factor is sharp for this Newton map at
$A=b_0=0$; no optimality is claimed for the combined prefactor $169/250$.
\end{proof}
This improves the approximation of the existing defect, not the size of
the defect itself. Expanded rational degrees grow with the iteration;
evaluation should retain the recurrence rather than expand its final
numerator. Each $D_j$ replaces, rather than adds to, the earlier clipping
or $h$-phase corrections.

\paragraph{An absolute error budget for the fixed certified weight.}
For an actual response, weighted Bessel and $|h|^2+|k|^2\le1$ give
\[
 \E\Delta\le\E H
 \le\E C+y^T\mathsf G^{-1}y
 \le2\E C+1-t^2.
\]
Indeed $D=Mk_0$, so $\E D^TM^{-1}D\le y^T\mathsf G^{-1}y$;
and $y^T\mathsf G^{-1}y\le\|k\|_{M+P}^2\le\E C+1-t^2$.
For the fixed weight of \S\ref{sec:projected-remote-price}, the
already certified constant gives
\[
 \E C\le\frac{4253566841}{10^{10}}-2p(2787/20000)^2<\frac38.
\]
The omitted projected-price charge is nonnegative. Consequently
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 0\le\E\Delta-\E D_{48}
 \le\frac74\frac{169}{250}\left(\frac34\right)^{48}
 <1.2\cdot10^{-6}.
 \quad}
\]
This absolute approximation budget is uniform in dimension and in all
shared sectors. It is conditional only on the preceding fixed-weight
integral certificate, not on a new cubic moment hypothesis.

\subsubsection{An exact averaged quadratic update for the joint dual}
The actual-response dual is minimized over its trial parameters.
For any finite real parameter vector $x$, write a chosen family as
\[
 \mathcal B(x)=c+x^TRx-2m^Tx
       +\E\bigl(|V_+(x)|+|V_-(x)|\bigr),\qquad R\succeq0,
\]
where $V_\pm(x)$ are affine complex random functions and their
coefficient functions belong to $L^2$.
For example, with $L=\ell Z+B$, $B\in\operatorname{ran}Q$,
$D=U\xi-a_0$, $a_0\in\operatorname{ran}P$, take
\[
 \begin{split}
 \mathcal B={}&t^2+pV-2\ell t+p\|B\|^2+\|a_0\|^2
                  -2\xi^Ty+\E(|L+D|+|L-D|),\\
 &V_+=L+D,\qquad V_-=L-D.
 \end{split}
\]
Complex coordinates are written as real pairs. This is an upper bound
for $\mathcal N$ for every trial point; no stationarity is assumed.

For a chosen $\delta>0$, replace each norm by
$\sqrt{|V_\pm|^2+\delta^2}$, obtaining $\mathcal B_\delta$.
Then, uniformly in the law and the parameters,
\[
 \mathcal B\le\mathcal B_\delta\le\mathcal B+2\delta.
\]
This is deterministic regularization of the objective, not the addition
of random noise or an independent Gaussian component.
At a trial $x$, let
\[
 w_\pm=\sqrt{|V_\pm(x)|^2+\delta^2},\qquad
 A_{\pm i}=\partial_{x_i}V_\pm,
\]
and define the real vector and matrix
\begin{align*}
 r_i&=m_i-(Rx)_i-	frac12\Re\E\left[
       \frac{\overline{V_+}A_{+i}}{w_+}
       +\frac{\overline{V_-}A_{-i}}{w_-}\right],\\
 K_{ij}&=R_{ij}+\tfrac12\Re\E\left[
       \frac{\overline{A_{+i}}A_{+j}}{w_+}
       +\frac{\overline{A_{-i}}A_{-j}}{w_-}\right].
\end{align*}
All entries are finite since $w_\pm\ge\delta$.
The visible and remote averages are taken \emph{before} forming the
inverse matrix. Shared drivers and phases are retained.
\begin{theorem}[\st{P}: certified joint-dual descent]
\label{thm:joint-dual-descent}
For every real step $d$,
\[
 \mathcal B_\delta(x+d)\le
 \mathcal B_\delta(x)-2r^Td+d^TKd.
\]
For any $\tau>0$, choose $d=(K+\tau I)^{-1}r$. Then
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \mathcal B_\delta(x+d)\le\mathcal B_\delta(x)
                   -r^T(K+\tau I)^{-1}r.
 \quad}
\]
After $N$ such steps, every actual response with the fixed target
moments satisfies
\[
 \mathcal N\le\mathcal B_\delta(x_0)
       -\sum_{j<N}r_j^T(K_j+\tau_j I)^{-1}r_j.
\]
The same assertions hold at $\delta=0$ if the displayed inverse-weight
integrals exist and the zero-set conventions are justified.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Concavity of the scalar square root gives the global tangent inequality
\[
 \sqrt{|v+a|^2+\delta^2}
 \le\sqrt{|v|^2+\delta^2}
 +\frac{\Re(\bar va)}{\sqrt{|v|^2+\delta^2}}
 +\frac{|a|^2}{2\sqrt{|v|^2+\delta^2}}.
\]
It follows also by squaring: with $w^2=|v|^2+\delta^2$,
the squared upper side minus the radicand is
$(2\Re\bar va+|a|^2)^2/(4w^2)$, and the upper side is nonnegative.
Apply it to both affine functions, integrate, and expand $x^TRx$.
This gives the first inequality. Replacing $K$ by $K+\tau I$ preserves
the majorant; complete the finite quadratic and telescope.
\end{proof}
There is no asserted uniform convergence rate for this optimization.
For a fixed target tensor and affine trial family, $\mathcal B_\delta$
is convex; $r=0$ therefore certifies a global minimum within that family.
This does not optimize over the target tensor. The unresolved universal
quantifier lies in the coupled cubic, not in local minima of this dual.
For rational step verification no inverse need be trusted: enclose $r$
and $K$ and prove $2r^Td-d^TKd>0$ for a chosen rational $d$.
Only finite integral enclosures and rational arithmetic are then used in
the decision. For $\delta=10^{-8}$ the total regularization cost is at
most $2\cdot10^{-8}$, paid once, not once per iteration.

\paragraph{How the two improvements combine.}
Starting at the old Gram trial point gives
$\mathcal B(x_0)=B_{\rm quad}-\E\Delta$ after dropping the separate
completion errors. Hence a valid combined bound is
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \mathcal N\le B_{\rm quad}-\E D_{48}+2\delta
       -\sum_{j<N}r_j^T(K_j+\tau_jI)^{-1}r_j.
 \quad}
\]
If the $Qh$ trial $B$ changes, its old completion square must not be
retained independently. The formula above drops it and therefore has
no such double counting. The remaining obligation is to certify these
joint-law expectations or successful trial families uniformly on the
surviving response set; the algebra does not provide that cover.

\paragraph{Diagnostic only \st{F}.}
In the independent Gaussian-grade test at $t=0.8456$, $e=0.04$, the
explicit update with unregularized finite integrals lowers the same
joint-dual trial from approximately $0.7157025135$ to $0.7131752277$
after 1000 steps, below the design cap. All tested steps satisfy the
predicted decrease numerically. This uses one-dimensional Rice integrals
in ordinary floating point, not interval arithmetic. The Gaussian-grade
family was already covered by other arguments; this is a check of the
update mechanism, not a new theorem about general coupled cubics or a
new endpoint.
