\subsubsection{The result [P,C]}
Fix $\epsilon=1891/2500$ and $p=1/\epsilon$. Write
\[
 h=(f_++f_-)/2,\quad k=(f_+-f_-)/2,\quad |f_\pm|\le1,
 \qquad Ph=tZ,\quad F=\epsilon Qk,
\]
where $P=P_{1,0}$ and $Q=P_{2,1}$ are Gaussian Wick projections.
The standard proper complex Gaussian $Z$ is independent of the remote
Gaussian space $X$. Decompose
\[
 F=q+(T-1)r_A+\frac{Z^2}{\sqrt2}r_B+ZY+\bar ZW+\nu H(Z),
 \quad T=|Z|^2,\quad H(Z)=Z(T-2)/\sqrt2.
\]
The five remote fields have bidegrees $(2,1),(1,0),(0,1),(1,1),(2,0)$,
respectively; their dependence on one another is unrestricted. Put
\[
 e=\|q\|_2^2,\quad A_2=\|r_A\|_2^2,\quad B_2=\|r_B\|_2^2,
 \quad YW_2=\|Y\|_2^2+\|W\|_2^2.
\]
The original objective is
\[
 \mathcal N=t^2+p\|F\|_2^2-\|Pk\|_2^2-\epsilon\|Qh\|_2^2.
\]
For every such response, the following bound holds:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sio-rational}
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal N\le{}&t^2-\frac{4734}{3125}t+\frac{6350267}{5000000}
       +\frac{361}{500}e\\
 &-\frac9{250}A_2-\frac{777}{1000}B_2
       -\frac3{100000}YW_2-\frac{13}{20}|\nu|^2.
\end{aligned}}
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sio-region}
\boxed{\begin{gathered}
 \frac{83}{125}\le t\le\frac{851}{1000},\qquad e\le\frac{113}{10000}
 \\\Longrightarrow\quad
 \mathcal N\le\frac{17831253}{25000000}
       =0.71325012<\frac{500}{701}.
\end{gathered}}
\end{equation}
This includes the whole previously targeted central interval
$[21/25,851/1000]$.

There is a larger region retaining the shared-sector penalties. Define
\[
 \mathcal E_{\mathrm{adj}}=e-\frac{18}{361}A_2-\frac{777}{722}B_2
             -\frac3{72200}YW_2-\frac{325}{361}|\nu|^2.
\]
On the central interval, the same cap follows from
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sio-adjusted}
 \boxed{\quad
 \mathcal E_{\mathrm{adj}}\le\frac{113}{10000}
       +\frac9{40}\left(\frac{851}{1000}-t\right).
 \quad}
\end{equation}
Thus the theorem is not restricted to a small remote variance when the
retained sector penalties are substantial.

\subsubsection{Why the first-chaos penalty matters [P]}
For an arbitrary positive radial weight $\lambda(T)$ let
\[
 J_j=\mathbb E\frac{T^j}{\lambda(T)},\qquad 0\le j\le3,
\]
and assume these integrals are finite. Define
\begin{align*}
 K_A&=J_2-2J_1+J_0-\frac{(J_1-J_0)^2}{1+J_0},&
 K_B&=J_2/2,\\
 K_Y&=J_1,&
 K_H&=\frac{J_3-4J_2+4J_1}{2}
                  -\frac{(J_2-2J_1)^2}{2(1+J_1)}.
\end{align*}
Whenever the displayed quantities are positive, weighted Cauchy--Schwarz
and remote-chaos orthogonality give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sio-coercivity}
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
 \mathbb E\lambda(T)|k|^2+\|Pk\|_2^2\ge p^2\bigg\{&
 \frac{e}{J_0}+\frac{A_2}{K_A}+\frac{B_2}{K_B}
 +\frac{YW_2}{K_Y}+\frac{|\nu|^2}{K_H}\bigg\}.
\end{aligned}}
\end{equation}
This statement holds with arbitrary shared remote drivers.

Here are the details of the dimension-free decomposition. Expand $k$ in
an orthonormal remote Hermite basis, leaving each coefficient a function
of $Z$. Multiplication by $\lambda(T)$ preserves each remote basis block.
The selected cubic coefficient and available first-chaos penalty in the
five types of block are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
 \text{remote bidegree}&\text{visible cubic basis function}
     &\text{visible basis function charged by }\|Pk\|^2\\\hline
 (2,1)&1&\text{none}\\
 (1,0)&T-1&1\\
 (0,1)&Z^2/\sqrt2&\text{none}\\
 (1,1),(2,0)&Z,\ \bar Z&\text{none}\\
 (0,0)&Z(T-2)/\sqrt2&Z
\end{array}
\]
All unlisted remote components contribute a nonnegative weighted norm.
For a visible coefficient function $u$, the elementary Hilbert-space
identity behind the second and last rows is
\[
 |\langle a,u\rangle|^2\le
 \left(\langle a,\lambda^{-1}a\rangle
 -\frac{|\langle b,\lambda^{-1}a\rangle|^2}
             {1+\langle b,\lambda^{-1}b\rangle}\right)
 \left(\mathbb E\lambda|u|^2+|\langle b,u\rangle|^2\right).
\]
It follows by applying Cauchy--Schwarz in the inner product on the second
factor, or by one rank-one inverse. Substitute $a=T-1,b=1$ or
$a=Z(T-2)/\sqrt2,b=Z$. The other rows use ordinary weighted
Cauchy--Schwarz. Summing proves~\eqref{eq:sio-coercivity}.

This is the key difference from dropping $\|Pk\|^2$ early. At our
certified weight, the uncorrected A entry is approximately $1.934$,
which exceeds $p=1.32205\ldots$. Keeping the negative first-chaos square
reduces it to $K_A<1.287<p$. Consequently all visible sectors can be
removed simultaneously, with retained positive penalties.

\subsubsection{A general original-response certificate [P]}
Choose any real $a,b$ and let
\[
 L_0=Z(a+bT),\qquad \ell_0=a+2b,\qquad
 \mathcal B=bZ(T-2).
\]
These are auxiliary dual parameters; they do not change the witness
$P-\epsilon Q$ or the peak profile in the statement of Conjecture K.
Completing the existing $Qh$ square exactly gives
\[
 -\epsilon\|Qh\|^2
 =2\operatorname{Re}\langle\mathcal B,h\rangle+2pb^2
       -\epsilon\|Qh+p\mathcal B\|^2.
\]
The pointwise disk constraints imply $|h|^2+|k|^2\le1$. Therefore
\[
 2\operatorname{Re}\langle L_0,h\rangle
 \le\mathbb E\lambda|h|^2+\mathbb E|L_0|^2/\lambda.
\]
Combine these two facts with~\eqref{eq:sio-coercivity}. The result is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sio-general}
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal N\le{}&t^2-2\ell_0t+C_\lambda+
               (p-p^2/J_0)e-\epsilon\|Qh+p\mathcal B\|^2\\
 &-p^2(K_A^{-1}-\epsilon)A_2-p^2(K_B^{-1}-\epsilon)B_2\\
 &-p^2(K_Y^{-1}-\epsilon)YW_2-p^2(K_H^{-1}-\epsilon)|\nu|^2,
\end{aligned}}
\end{equation}
where
\[
 C_\lambda=2pb^2+\mathbb E\{\lambda+|L_0|^2/\lambda\}.
\]
No signs on $K_j^{-1}-\epsilon$ were required for the identity's
upper-bound direction. Our numerical certificate makes all four positive.
The extra negative square in~\eqref{eq:sio-general} is compatible with
all displayed sector penalties: it was never used in their derivation.
It must not be subtracted a second time from a different dual already
retaining the same square.

\subsubsection{Four integrals certify all dimensions [C]}
Use the exact rational parameters
\[
 a=\frac{4503}{10000},\quad b=\frac{15357}{100000},\quad
 s_0=\frac{291}{125000},\quad d=\frac{2759}{100000},
\]
and the strictly positive weight
\[
 \lambda(T)=\sqrt{T(a+bT)^2+s_0+dT}.
\]
Then $2\ell_0=4734/3125$, and only four integrals are needed, since
\[
 \mathbb E\{\lambda+|L_0|^2/\lambda\}
 =s_0J_0+(2a^2+d)J_1+4abJ_2+2b^2J_3.
\]
The independent replay certifies
\[
 \begin{array}{c|c}
 \text{quantity}&\text{rational conclusion}\\\hline
 C_\lambda&<6350267/5000000\\
 p-p^2/J_0&0<\cdot<361/500\\
 p^2(K_A^{-1}-\epsilon)&>9/250\\
 p^2(K_B^{-1}-\epsilon)&>777/1000\\
 p^2(K_Y^{-1}-\epsilon)&>3/100000\\
 p^2(K_H^{-1}-\epsilon)&>13/20
 \end{array}
\]
These prove~\eqref{eq:sio-rational}. Convexity in $t$ reduces
\eqref{eq:sio-region} to its two endpoints; the larger is $t=851/1000$.
For~\eqref{eq:sio-adjusted}, substitution gives a quadratic whose derivative
is positive on the whole central interval; its maximum is again the
upper endpoint. All these comparisons use rational arithmetic.

For reproducibility, the integrands are
$t^j e^{-t}/\sqrt{P(t)}$, where
\[
 P(t)=s_0+(a^2+d)t+2abt^2+b^2t^3.
\]
The verifier integrates on $[0,40]$ by adaptive Simpson panels with
interval bounds on the fourth derivative. If $P_j$ and $u_j$ are local
Taylor coefficients of $P$ and $u=P^{-1/2}$, the identity
$2Pu'+P'u=0$ yields
\[
 u_0=P_0^{-1/2},\qquad
 u_{n+1}=-\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{\min(3,n+1)}(2n-j+2)P_j u_{n+1-j}}
                   {2(n+1)P_0}.
\]
Interval evaluation of these coefficients over each entire panel bounds
the derivatives there. A panel of length $h$ has error at most
$h^5\sup|f^{(4)}|/2880$. On the unbounded tail,
$P(t)\ge b^2t^3$ and $t\ge40\ge1$ give
\[
 0\le\int_{40}^\infty\frac{t^je^{-t}}{\sqrt{P(t)}}\,dt
 \le\frac{j!e^{-40}}b\sum_{k=0}^j\frac{40^k}{k!}.
\]
Every arithmetic operation is outward-rounded using Python integers at
scale $10^{40}$. Exponentials use a positive Taylor series at $t/64$,
an explicit geometric remainder, inversion and repeated squaring.
Square roots use integer square roots. No floating quadrature, solver
output, or stored integral is trusted. A finer replay reduces the error
tolerance by a factor of $100$.

\subsubsection{What part of the complement is now closed [P,C]}
At $q=0$, every remote Rice test has
\[
 D_t(v;0)=\mathbb E\rho(|v|^2)-t\ge R-t>0.015,
 \qquad R=\sqrt\pi/2,
\]
throughout the central interval. Thus the preceding small-gap criterion
cannot handle these responses with any test. The present theorem covers
them, and a uniform neighborhood $\|q\|^2\le0.0113$, regardless of
normalized kurtosis or support gap.

The region is nonempty. For any central $t$, take
$h=(t/R)\operatorname{phase}(Z)$ and $k=0$. More generally take any
bounded $k$ with $\|k\|_\infty\le1/100$, leaving this $h$ unchanged.
The elementary bound $\pi>3$ implies $t/R+1/100<1$, so $f_\pm=h\pm k$
are admissible. Since $\|q\|^2\le\epsilon^2/10000$, these responses lie
inside the new region. For example $k=\operatorname{phase}(H(X))/100$
produces a nonzero pure-Wick remote cubic, with normalized fourth moment
$44$; there is no small-kurtosis premise.

There is also a direct interpretation in terms of the preceding intrinsic
support test $v=(p/t)q$. The global Rice tangent
$\rho(u)\le R+(R/2)u$ gives, for $t>R/2$,
\[
 D_t((p/t)q;q)\le R-t-\gamma(t)e,\qquad
 \gamma(t)=\frac{p^2(2t-R)}{2t^2}>0.
\]
Consequently the new certificate can be written
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal N\le{}&t^2-1.51488t+1.2700534
 +\frac{0.722}{\gamma(t)}\{R-t-D_t((p/t)q;q)\}\\
 &-0.036A_2-0.777B_2-0.00003YW_2-0.65|\nu|^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus a \emph{large} intrinsic support gap gives a negative deduction in
this certificate. In particular the central-row implication
\[
 D_t((p/t)q;q)\ge R-t-0.0113\,\gamma(t)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \mathcal N\le0.71325012
\]
is valid without a cumulant restriction. Together with the preceding
small-gap theorem, this controls opposite ends of the support-gap range;
it does not cover the intermediate range.

Conversely any actual central response violating $\mathcal N<500/701$
must now obey
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sio-survivor}
 \boxed{\quad
 \mathcal E_{\mathrm{adj}}\ge
 \frac{500/701-t^2+(4734/3125)t-6350267/5000000}{361/500}.
 \quad}
\end{equation}
(The rational relaxation alone gives $\ge$ for a putative $\mathcal N\ge
500/701$; the strict integral bounds can sharpen it.) The right side is
approximately $0.0140063$ at $t=0.84$, $0.0126822$ at $t=0.8456$, and
$0.0113230$ at $t=0.851$. The exact replay prints directed enclosures.
Survivors must \emph{also} evade every previous cumulant/support test.
The intermediate-variance support-interior region and the larger
cumulant/influence region remain unproved. This is a new complete
regional original-response certificate, not a complete central-row
certificate or a new endpoint.
