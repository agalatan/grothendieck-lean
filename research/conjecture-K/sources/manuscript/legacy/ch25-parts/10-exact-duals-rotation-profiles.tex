\subsection{A weaker row target retaining both squares}\label{sec:weakk-rowtarget}
The following identity retains an additional loss in the gate-free reduction.
Put $h=(f+g)/2$, $k=(f-g)/2$, $Ph=tZ$, $B\in\operatorname{ran}Q$,
$L=\ell Z+B$, and $G=\epsilon Qk$. Then
\begin{align*}
\operatorname{Re}\langle f,(P-\epsilon Q)g\rangle
={}&t^2-2\ell t+\frac{\|B\|_2^2}{\epsilon}
 +\operatorname{Re}\mathbb E[\overline{L+G}f+\overline{L-G}g]
 -\frac{\|G\|_2^2}{\epsilon}\\
&-\|Pk\|_2^2-\epsilon\|Qh+B/\epsilon\|_2^2.
\end{align*}
This is an equality. Let $R(G)$ be the supremum of the terms after the row
constant, over unimodular $f,g$ satisfying $Qk=G/\epsilon$, with the constraint
$Ph=tZ$ removed. A bound on $\sup_G R(G)$ is sufficient and weaker than the
previous $K'$ target, which discarded the last square.

For $a\in\operatorname{ran}P$ and $b,C\in\operatorname{ran}Q$, every trial gives
\begin{align*}
R(G)\le D_G(a,b,C):={}&\mathbb E|L+G-a-b-C|+
\mathbb E|L-G+a+b-C|+\|a\|_2^2+\frac{\|C\|_2^2}{\epsilon}\\
&-\frac{2\operatorname{Re}\langle C,B\rangle}{\epsilon}
+\frac{2\operatorname{Re}\langle b,G\rangle}{\epsilon}
-\frac{\|G\|_2^2}{\epsilon}.
\end{align*}
The infimum is equal to $R(G)$ in fixed finite Gaussian dimension, by finite
moment purification and convex duality. The upper-bound direction is simply
completion of squares, with no existence or regularity assumptions. The
common shift $C$ is essential: at a differentiable minimizing response it
satisfies $C=\epsilon Qh+B$.

Here is an explicit finite certificate allowing the responses to readjust.
Set $w_\pm=L\pm G$, $\rho_\pm=\max(|w_\pm|,\tau)$, for $\tau>0$, and
\[
 k_\tau=\tfrac12(w_+/\rho_+-w_-/\rho_-),\qquad
 h_\tau=\tfrac12(w_+/\rho_++w_-/\rho_-),\qquad
 B_\tau=\frac{\mathbb E[(\tau-|w_+|)_+^2+(\tau-|w_-|)_+^2]}{2\tau}.
\]
On arbitrary finite trial subspaces for $(a,b,C)$, define
\[
 H(a,b,C)=\|a\|_2^2+\|C\|_2^2/\epsilon+
 \tfrac12\mathbb E\left[
 \frac{|a+b+C|^2}{\rho_+}+\frac{|a+b-C|^2}{\rho_-}\right].
\]
Let $r$ be the coordinate vector of the pairings with
$Pk_\tau$, $Qk_\tau-G/\epsilon$, and $Qh_\tau+B/\epsilon$.
The positive definite Gram matrix $H$ gives
\[
 \boxed{R(G)\le\Lambda(G)+B_\tau-r^*H^{-1}r.}
\]
Indeed $|z-v|\le(\rho^2+|z-v|^2)/(2\rho)$, applied to the two moduli,
reduces the dual to $\Lambda(G)+B_\tau-2\operatorname{Re}(v^*r)+v^*Hv$.
A rational trial $v$ certifies the lower bound
$r^*H^{-1}r\ge2\operatorname{Re}(v^*r)-v^*Hv$, so the checker need not
invert a matrix. All entries are finite since $\rho_\pm\ge\tau$.

If $Q_0$ denotes the visible-independent part of $G$, the sufficient weaker
comparison is now
\[
 \Lambda(G)+B_\tau-r^*H^{-1}r\le\Lambda(Q_0)+\eta_{AB}.
\]
This comparison is \emph{open}. Nine sampled junta-plus-buffer tests with
optimized shared $A/B$ terms support it: the joint $h/Pk$ trial reduction
exceeds the mixed gain at all nine points. Including the cubic consistency
multiplier improves each tested trial further. These are numerical diagnostics,
not interval certificates. The exact finite-sum audit verifies 256 row
identities, 6,912 dual trials, 256 Gram bounds, and 1,875 complex Young
identities. No Grothendieck endpoint is improved by this note alone.


\subsection{Rotation stationarity pays for the main $A/B$ amplitude}\label{sec:weakk-rotation}
The following inequality applies at a global norm maximizer and retains every
simultaneous visible-sector interaction. It does not assert stationarity for a
fixed-$t$ constrained row maximizer.

Let $T=P-\epsilon Q$, $h=(f+g)/2$, $k=(f-g)/2$, $Ph=tZ$,
$G=\epsilon Qk$, $B=\beta Z(|Z|^2-2)$, and
\[
 H_{\mathrm{res}}=Qh+B/\epsilon,\qquad
 \mathcal F=\|Pk\|_2^2+\epsilon\|H_{\mathrm{res}}\|_2^2.
\]
Write $E_{\rm rem}$ for the inclusion of the remote first chaos in $P$,
and $P_{\rm rem}=E_{\rm rem}^*$ for its orthogonal projection. The dual
variable $a$ and $Pk$ may lie in the full first chaos.
For each remote first-chaos direction $p=\sum p_jX_j$, put
\[
 D_p=p\partial_Z+\bar p\partial_{\bar Z}
   -Z\sum_j\bar p_j\partial_{X_j}
   -\bar Z\sum_jp_j\partial_{\bar X_j}.
\]
This is the skew-adjoint generator of a unitary coordinate rotation. Rotating
$g$ alone at a global maximizing pair and expanding the derivative gives
\[
 t\operatorname{Re}\langle p,Pk\rangle+
 \operatorname{Re}\langle Qh,D_pG\rangle=0.
\]
A maximizer exists in fixed dimension: the weak-* compact unit disks have
compact images under the finite-rank map $(P,Q)$, the objective is continuous
on these images, and successive replacement by $f=\operatorname{phase}(Tg)$
and $g=\operatorname{phase}(Tf)$ gives a unimodular maximizing pair. The
rotation derivative only differentiates finite-degree projections, so no
regularity of $f,g$ is required.

Write the usual coordinate decomposition
\[
 G=q+r_A(|Z|^2-1)+r_BZ^2/\sqrt2+YZ+W\bar Z+\nu H(Z),
 \qquad d=2\beta r_A+\sqrt2\beta\overline{r_B}.
\]
Since $D_pB=2\beta(|Z|^2-1)p+\beta Z^2\bar p$, stationarity is exactly
\[
 \operatorname{Re}\langle p,d\rangle
 =-\epsilon t\operatorname{Re}\langle p,Pk\rangle
  -\epsilon\operatorname{Re}\langle H_{\mathrm{res}},D_pG\rangle.
\]
The map $J_G:p\mapsto D_pG$ is \emph{real linear}, generally not complex
linear. On the realification of the remote first chaos, least squares gives
\[
 \mathcal F\ge
 d^{\mathsf T}(\epsilon^2t^2I+\epsilon J_G^*J_G)^{-1}d.
\]
A Moore--Penrose inverse on the range handles $t=0$. In particular, if
$J_G^*J_G\preceq\omega I$, then
\[
 \mathcal F\ge\frac{\|d\|_2^2}{\epsilon^2t^2+\epsilon\omega}.
\]
The quotient is defined as zero when $t=\|G\|=0$, in which case $d=0$.
On a cubic Wick tensor, the rotation generator is the sum of three
one-particle generators, each of norm $\|p\|_2$. Thus
$\omega=9\|G\|_2^2$ is always valid. The exact gate-free row consequently gives
\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{Re}\langle f,Tg\rangle\le{}&
 t^2-2\ell t+2\beta^2/\epsilon+\Lambda(G)\\[-2pt]
 &-\frac{\|2\beta r_A+\sqrt2\beta\overline{r_B}\|_2^2}
 {\epsilon^2t^2+9\epsilon\|G\|_2^2}.
\end{aligned}}
\]
This holds at a norm maximizer with all sectors present. Therefore a uniform
cap on its right side bounds the norm. It is a strictly weaker target than
bounding $\Lambda(G)$ alone.
The sufficient supremum may impose $0\le t\le1$ and
$\|G\|_2^2\le\epsilon^2(1-t^2)$: unimodularity gives
$\|h\|_2^2+\|k\|_2^2=1$, and projection decreases the two norms.
This does not imply any smaller variance window used in the examples below.

\paragraph{Sharper denominator from the influence matrix.}
Let $v=\|G\|_2^2$, $\kappa_G=\mathbb E|G|^4-2v^2$, and let $M_G\succeq0$
be the influence matrix, so
\[
 I_p=\Big\|\sum_j\bar p_j\partial_jG\Big\|_2^2+
       \Big\|\sum_jp_j\bar\partial_jG\Big\|_2^2=p^*M_Gp.
\]
The existing contraction identity says
$\operatorname{Tr}M_G=3v$ and
$\operatorname{Tr}M_G^2=\|\Pi_{1,1}|G|^2\|_2^2=:A_G\le3\kappa_G/4$.
For a unit $p\perp Z$, one-particle tensor Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
 \|D_pG\|_2^2\le3(I_Z+I_p)
 \le3\min\{3v,\sqrt{2A_G}\}.
\]
Hence one can replace $9v$ by
\[
 \boxed{\omega=\min\{9v,\,3\sqrt{3\kappa_G/2}\}.}
\]
The cumulant here is that of the full cubic $G$, not a conditional slice.
When sector variances are known,
$I_Z=2(\|r_A\|^2+\|r_B\|^2)+\|Y\|^2+\|W\|^2+3|\nu|^2$ and
$I_p\le\min\{3v-I_Z,\sqrt{A_G-I_Z^2}\}$ give a further improvement.
For remote $q$, the exact identity
$\|D_pq\|^2=a_p+b_p$ instead gives, with $j=\|G-q\|^2$,
\[
 \omega\le\min\left\{9(v_q+j),
 \left(\sqrt{\min\{3v_q,\sqrt{3\kappa_q}/2\}}+3\sqrt j\right)^2\right\}.
\]
At the peak parameters and $t\le0.851$, put
$r_{\parallel}=(2r_A+\sqrt2\overline{r_B})/\sqrt6$.
The unconditional denominator yields penalties at least
$\|r_{\parallel}\|^2/7$ for $v\le1/16$ and
$\|r_{\parallel}\|^2/6$ for $v\le1/25$.
The remote version gives $6\|r_{\parallel}\|^2/25$ if
$v_q\le1/25$, $\kappa_q\le v_q^2$, and $j\le1/400$.
These rational comparisons pass exact arithmetic checks.

\paragraph{Combining with response adjustment without double counting.}
Write $\Theta_G$ for any certified lower bound on $\mathcal F$ above. Allocate
$\mu\mathcal F$ to the response dual and $(1-\mu)\mathcal F$ to rotation, for
$0<\mu\le1$. The common-shift dual changes its prices to
$\|a\|^2/\mu+\|C\|^2/(\mu\epsilon)$, keeping the linear terms
$-2\operatorname{Re}\langle C,B\rangle/\epsilon$
and $2\operatorname{Re}\langle b,G\rangle/\epsilon$ unchanged.
The clipped Gram matrix $H_\mu$ therefore has these two modified diagonal
prices and the same residual vector $r$. At the norm maximizer,
\[
 \text{row}\le\text{constant}+\Lambda(G)+B_\tau
      -r^*H_\mu^{-1}r-(1-\mu)\Theta_G.
\]
At $\mu=0$, use $\Lambda(G)-\Theta_G$. Finite minima over certified choices
are allowed. Subtracting both full corrections would spend the same squares
twice and is invalid.

\subsection{An exact profile identity linking $A/B$ content to the common response}
For the remote-only baseline, define
\[
 F_0(u)=\mathbb E_Z\operatorname{phase}(L+u),\quad
 F_A(u)=\mathbb E_Z(|Z|^2-1)\operatorname{phase}(L+u),\quad
 F_B(u)=\mathbb E_Z\frac{Z^2}{\sqrt2}
                         \overline{\operatorname{phase}(L+u)}.
\]
Let $h_u=(\operatorname{phase}(L+u)+\operatorname{phase}(L-u))/2$,
$H_1(u)=\mathbb E\bar Zh_u$, and $H_2(u)=\mathbb E Zh_u$.
The first is real radial; the second has phase charge two. Visible Gaussian
integration by parts gives
\[
 F_*(u):=\ell F_0(u)+2\beta F_A(u)+\sqrt2\beta F_B(u)
       =2\mathbb E_Z[Z|L+u|],\qquad
 (F_*)_u=H_1,\quad(F_*)_{\bar u}=H_2.
\]
For $a=\ell-2\beta>0$, regularize the norm and use its smooth radial
pushforward density to justify the identity and the locally integrable first
phase derivatives. No derivative supremum occurs.

For remote $q$, put $c_D=\Pi_{1,0}F_D(q)$, including $c_0$.
Let $h_{\mathrm{even}}$ project $h_q$ onto
$Z\mathcal H_{1,1}(X)\oplus\bar Z\mathcal H_{2,0}(X)$, and define the
\emph{complex-linear} map
\[
 T_qp=\sum_jp_j\bigl[Z\overline{\partial_jq}
                         +\bar Z\bar\partial_jq\bigr].
\]
Remote Gaussian integration by parts proves
\[
 \boxed{\ell c_0+2\beta c_A+\sqrt2\beta c_B=T_q^*h_{\mathrm{even}}.}
\]
For $I_q=T_q^*T_q$ and $r=\ell c_0+2\beta c_A+\sqrt2\beta c_B$, this yields
$r\in\operatorname{ran}I_q$ and
$r^*I_q^+r\le\|h_{\mathrm{even}}\|^2$.
Moreover $\|T_qp\|^2=a_p+b_p$, the directional influence. This is a matrix
constraint coupling the content, $Pk$, and the retained $Qh$ square.
It controls one combination; the transverse profile
$(F_A-\sqrt2F_B)/\sqrt3$ remains.

For a real polynomial $p$ and $M_p(u)=2\mathbb E[Zp(|Z|^2)|L+u|]$, the weighted
hierarchy is
\[
 M_{p-p'}(u)=\mathbb E\left[p(T)\{(a+2\beta T)\operatorname{phase}(L+u)
                  +\beta Z^2\overline{\operatorname{phase}(L+u)}\}\right].
\]
Its derivatives are the correspondingly weighted $H_1,H_2$ profiles.
Choosing $p=T-2$ introduces visible degree three; paired with remote
second-chaos derivatives of $q$, this requires degree-five projections of

The self-contained rational Gaussian-moment checker verifies the complex

\subsection{Stationarity inside the response dual, with no allocation loss}
The previous allocation rule is valid but can be strengthened. Put
$A=\epsilon^2t^2I+\epsilon J_G^*J_G$ on the realified remote space.
For every dual trial $(a,b,C)$ and every real vector $u$, stationarity gives
\[
 \text{row}\le\text{constant}+D_G(a,b,C)+u^{\mathsf T}Au
       -2\operatorname{Re}\langle u,d+\epsilon t P_{\rm rem}a+J_G^*C\rangle.
\]
Indeed, for $x=Pk$, $y=H_{\mathrm{res}}$, the difference in the square
completion is exactly
\[
 \|x-a+\epsilon t E_{\rm rem}u\|^2+
 \epsilon\|y-C/\epsilon+J_Gu\|^2\ge0.
\]
Thus minimizing over $u$ yields the stronger bound
\[
 \boxed{D_G(a,b,C)
 -(d+\epsilon t P_{\rm rem}a+J_G^*C)^{\mathsf T}A^{-1}
                  (d+\epsilon t P_{\rm rem}a+J_G^*C).}
\]
The same two squares are used only once. Setting $u=0$ recovers the ordinary
dual; setting $a=b=C=0$ recovers $\Lambda(G)-\Theta_G$.
The displayed inverse is ordinary for $t>0$. At $t=0$, use the
Moore--Penrose inverse on the range, or the preceding trial-$u$ bound:
$d+J_G^*C$ belongs to $\operatorname{ran}J_G^*$ by stationarity.
Skew-adjointness also gives the sign check $J_G^*B=-d$.
A certified bound $A\preceq\overline A I$ permits subtraction of
$\|d+\epsilon t P_{\rm rem}a+J_G^*C\|^2/\overline A$ instead; any explicit rational
$u$ also gives a verifiable trial without matrix inversion.

In the clipped implementation, let $H$ and $r$ be the preceding response
Gram matrix and residual vector, and set $K(a,b,C)=\epsilon t P_{\rm rem}a+J_G^*C$.
The single augmented real matrix and residual are
\[
 H_{\mathrm{aug}}=\begin{pmatrix}H&-K^*\\-K&A\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad r_{\mathrm{aug}}=(r,d).
\]
For $t>0$ the matrix is positive definite: its price part is
$\|a-\epsilon t E_{\rm rem}u\|^2+\|C-\epsilon J_Gu\|^2/\epsilon$, while the
reciprocal-modulus part controls $a+b$ and $C$; simultaneous vanishing,
using $P\perp Q$, forces $u=0$. Therefore
\[
 \text{row}\le\text{constant}+\Lambda(G)+B_\tau
       -r_{\mathrm{aug}}^{\mathsf T}H_{\mathrm{aug}}^{-1}r_{\mathrm{aug}}.
\]
This retains response readjustment, cubic consistency, and stationarity in
one finite certificate. Twenty-five exact rational square completions audit
the new formula.

For completeness, if a pair is within $\delta$ of the global norm, then a
unit-direction rotation has $|J''(\theta)|\le M=\max\{1,9\epsilon\}$ and
$|J'(0)|\le\sqrt{2M\delta}$. Hence its stationarity defect has norm at most
$\epsilon\sqrt{M\delta/2}$, and the valid approximate version is
\[
 \mathcal F\ge
 \frac{(\|d\|-\epsilon\sqrt{M\delta/2})_+^2}
 {\epsilon^2t^2+\epsilon\omega}.
\]
The exact zero-defect formulas above are used at actual norm maximizers.


\paragraph{Arbitrary finite families of unitary coordinate generators.}
Let $D_1,\dots,D_m$ be skew-adjoint generators induced by skew-Hermitian
coordinate transformations, and put $D_u=\sum_j u_jD_j$ for $u\in\mathbb R^m$.
Define $Su=D_u(Ph)$, $Ju=D_uG$, and $d_j=\operatorname{Re}\langle D_jB,G\rangle$.
Rotation stationarity gives
\[
 d+\epsilon S^*Pk+\epsilon J^*H_{\rm res}=0,
 \qquad
 \mathcal F\ge d^{\mathsf T}
       (\epsilon^2S^*S+\epsilon J^*J)^+d.
\]
The stationarity identity puts $d$ in the matrix range, so the
Moore--Penrose formula is valid. The joint response dual above also
extends with $\epsilon tP_{\rm rem}a$ replaced by $\epsilon S^*a$.
Appending generators cannot weaken the correction: it enlarges the set of
trial multipliers $u$. Remote-remote rotations have $d_j=0$ but can improve
the Schur complement. The visible phase rotation satisfies
\[
 D_\phi=iZ\partial_Z-i\bar Z\partial_{\bar Z},\quad
 D_\phi B=iB,\quad
 d_\phi=\sqrt2\beta\operatorname{Im}\nu,\quad S_\phi=itZ.
\]
Thus the full rotation family also constrains the imaginary diagonal
amplitude. No elimination of the remaining amplitude or numerical global
cap is claimed.

\subsubsection{The density certificate}
Write $p(t)=a+bt$, $D(t)=a+3bt$, and $U(t)=tp(t)^2$.

$\bar w^2e^{-t}/(\pi\sqrt2p^3D)$.

The JSON witness gives positive rational rates $\lambda_j$ and rational

\[
 \sum_jc_{0j}/\lambda_j=1,\qquad

\[
 \frac1\pi\sum_jc_{0j}e^{-\lambda_j|w|^2},\quad

\subsubsection{Closed formulas and the conjugation in the B profile}
For $s\ge0$, set $x_j=\lambda_js^2/2$. With modified Bessel functions
$I_0,I_1$, the formulas are
\begin{align*}
 \widetilde F_D(s)&=\sum_j\frac{c_{Dj}\sqrt\pi s}{2\sqrt{\lambda_j}}
       e^{-x_j}[I_0(x_j)+I_1(x_j)]\quad(D=0,A),\\
 \widetilde F_B(s)&=\sum_j\frac{c_{Bj}\sqrt\pi s}{4\lambda_j^{3/2}}
       e^{-x_j}[I_1(x_j)-I_0(x_j)],\\
 \widetilde\Phi_0(s)&=\sum_j\frac{c_{0j}\sqrt\pi}{\lambda_j^{3/2}}
       e^{-x_j}[(1+2x_j)I_0(x_j)+2x_jI_1(x_j)].
\end{align*}
Each profile at $q\ne0$ is $(q/|q|)\widetilde F_D(|q|)$; set it to
zero at $q=0$. The potential is radial.

For completeness, a proper Gaussian $W$ of variance $\gamma$ satisfies
\[
 \E\ph(q+W)=qh_\gamma(|q|^2),\quad
 h_\gamma(u)=\frac{\sqrt\pi}{2\sqrt\gamma}
 e^{-x}[I_0(x)+I_1(x)],\quad x=\frac{u}{2\gamma}.
\]
Gaussian integration by parts gives
$\E[\bar W^2\ph(q+W)]=\gamma^2\partial_q^2(qh_\gamma)$.
Now
\[
 \partial_q^2(qh(u))=\bar q(2h'(u)+uh''(u)),\quad
 2h_\gamma'(u)+uh_\gamma''(u)
 =\frac{\sqrt\pi}{4\gamma^{3/2}}e^{-x}[I_1(x)-I_0(x)].
\]
Conjugating this expression yields the charge-$+1$ $F_B$ above.
The conjugation is essential, although invisible for real positive $q$.

Both $e^{-x}(I_0+I_1)$ and $e^{-x}(I_0-I_1)$ are completely monotone:
\[
 e^{-x}[I_0(x)\pm I_1(x)]
 =\frac1\pi\int_0^\pi e^{-x(1-\cos\theta)}

Thus the first Gaussian kernel is positive and the $B$ kernel is
negative. A signed $A$ mixture does not automatically inherit a
derivative sign; none is required in the proof.
