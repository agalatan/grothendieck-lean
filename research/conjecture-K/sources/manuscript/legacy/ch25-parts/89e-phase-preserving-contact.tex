\subsection{A sharp tensor support that preserves the Gaussian phase}
\label{sec:phase-preserving-contact}

\paragraph{Scope and status.}
The \st{P,C} theorem below keeps the phase discarded by
Lemma~\ref{lem:multislice-support-reduction}, for a different whole
subspace. Let $\mathcal A,\mathcal B$ be mutually orthogonal complex
first-chaos spaces, both orthogonal to the visible coordinate $Z$, and
put
\[
 \mathcal D(\mathcal A,\mathcal B)
   =\mathcal H_{2,0}(\mathcal A)\mathcal H_{0,1}(\mathcal B).
\]
The holomorphic block includes mixed products $X_iX_j$; the
antiholomorphic rank is unrestricted. Orthogonality of the two blocks
is essential. It is a condition on the selected support-test subspace,
not an independence assumption on the unknown response remainder.

\begin{theorem}[\st{P}: sharp support and its extremizer]
\label{thm:sharp-decoupled-support}
Let $m=\dim\mathcal A\ge1$, $a>0$, $b,d\ge0$, and
$L=Z(a+b|Z|^2)$. For every unit $J\in\mathcal D(\mathcal A,\mathcal B)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sharp-decoupled-support}
 \E|L+\sqrt2dJ|
 \le I_m(a,b,d):=
 \E\left|L+d\sqrt{\frac{2}{m(m+1)}}R_mG\right|,
 \qquad R_m\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(m,1),
\end{equation}
where $R_m,G,Z$ are independent and $G$ is a standard proper Gaussian.
The bound is attained when the antiholomorphic block has dimension at
least $m(m+1)/2$. An extremizer, with independent standard $Y_{ij}$, is
\[
 J_{\rm iso}=
 \frac{\sum_iX_i^2\bar Y_{ii}
       +\sqrt2\sum_{i<j}X_iX_j\bar Y_{ij}}{\sqrt{m(m+1)}}.
\]
The numbers $I_m$ increase with $m$ and converge to
$I_\infty=\E|L+\sqrt2dG|$. For $d>0$ this increase is strict, and
\[
 0\le I_\infty-I_m\le
 d\sqrt{\frac\pi2}\left(1-\sqrt{\frac m{m+1}}\right).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $J=\sum_\nu Q_\nu(X)\bar Y_\nu$ in an orthonormal basis of
$\mathcal B$, with homogeneous holomorphic quadratics $Q_\nu$.
Conditionally on $X$, $J$ is a proper Gaussian with variance
$V(X)=\sum_\nu|Q_\nu(X)|^2$. By the visible scale-mixture proof in
\S\ref{sec:whole-slice-contact}, $L$ has the law $\sqrt M N$,
$M>0$, with $N$ proper standard Gaussian independent of $M$. Hence
\[
 K(v):=\E|L+\sqrt vG|=\frac{\sqrt\pi}{2}\E\sqrt{M+v}
\]
is increasing and strictly concave. The required integral is
$\E K(2d^2V(X))$. Put $R=\|X\|^2$. Gaussian polar decomposition
makes $X/\sqrt R$ independent of $R$, and homogeneity gives
\[
 \E[V(X)\mid R]=\frac{R^2}{m(m+1)},
\]
since $\E V=1$ and $\E R^2=m(m+1)$. Conditional Jensen proves
\eqref{eq:sharp-decoupled-support}. In the displayed extremizer the
conditional variance of the numerator is
$\sum_i|X_i|^4+2\sum_{i<j}|X_i|^2|X_j|^2=R^2$,
so equality holds and the norm is one.

Put $W_m=R_m^2/[m(m+1)]$. Couple
$R_{m+1}=R_m+E$, $E\sim\operatorname{Exp}(1)$ independent.
Conditionally on $R_{m+1}$, the fraction $R_m/R_{m+1}$ is
$\operatorname{Beta}(m,1)$ and its second moment is $m/(m+2)$.
Thus $\E[W_m\mid R_{m+1}]=W_{m+1}$; concavity proves monotonicity,
strictly for $d>0$. Also $\E W_m=1$ and
$\operatorname{Var}W_m=(4m+6)/[m(m+1)]$. Jensen gives $I_m\le I_\infty$,
and the sharper displayed error follows without inverse moments.
For $s=2d^2>0$ put
$j(x)=\sqrt{x+s}-\E\sqrt{x+sW_m}$. For $x>0$,
$j'(x)\le0$ by convexity of $y\mapsto y^{-1/2}$.
Thus $j(x)\le j(0)=\sqrt s(1-\E\sqrt{W_m})$.
Since $\E\sqrt{W_m}=\sqrt{m/(m+1)}$, average over $x=M$
and multiply by $\sqrt\pi/2$. The case $d=0$ is immediate.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Exact norms of the extremizer \st{P}.}
The conditional Gaussian law also gives
\[
 \E|J_{\rm iso}|=\frac{\sqrt\pi}{2}\sqrt{\frac m{m+1}},
 \qquad
 \E|J_{\rm iso}|^4=\frac{2(m+2)(m+3)}{m(m+1)}.
\]
The first expression is the sharp $L^1/L^2$ norm on this subspace,
by the same spherical Jensen proof with zero visible term.
The support convergence estimate above is $O(m^{-1})$.

\paragraph{The same formula at other degrees \st{P}.}
For a homogeneous holomorphic degree-$k$ block times an orthogonal
antiholomorphic linear block, replace $R_m^2/[m(m+1)]$ by
$R_m^k/(m)_k$. The extremizer has coefficients
$\sqrt{k!/\alpha!}$ on $X^\alpha\bar Y_\alpha$, divided by
$\sqrt{(m)_k}$. The multinomial theorem gives conditional variance
$R_m^k/(m)_k$. The conditional Beta moment proves the same dimension
monotonicity. Only degree two is used in the following certificate.

\begin{theorem}[\st{P,C}: response concentration through 24 inputs]
\label{thm:phase-preserving-contact}
Use $\epsilon=1891/2500$, $p=2500/1891$, $Ph=tZ$,
$F=\epsilon Qk$, $V=\|F\|^2$, and
$\mathcal N=t^2+pV-\|Pk\|^2-\epsilon\|Qh\|^2$ as above.
For $21/25\le t\le851/1000$, each row below is sufficient for
\[
 \boxed{\mathcal N<\frac{2853}{4000}=0.71325<\frac{500}{701}.}
\]
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
 \dim\mathcal A\le m&2&4&8&16&24\\\hline
 \|\Pi_{\mathcal D(\mathcal A,\mathcal B)}F\|^2/V\ge\eta
 &191/200&49/50&99/100&499/500&1999/2000
\end{array}
\]
The ambient dimension and $\dim\mathcal B$ are arbitrary. Every
coefficient phase and every coupled remainder, including all visible
sectors, is allowed. There is no fresh-buffer or higher-moment input.
For $V=0$ the conclusion follows from the visible-only test below.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Choose a unit $J$ aligned with the indicated projection. Put
$u=\sqrt2|\langle J,F\rangle|$, so $V\le u^2/(2\eta)$.
Dimension monotonicity permits replacing $\dim\mathcal A$ by $m$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:sharp-decoupled-support} and the directed integrator
prove these rational support ceilings:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
 m&I_m(1,0,6/25)<H_0&I_m(317/500,183/1000,81/500)<H\\\hline
 2&0.933570&0.834773\\
 4&0.934677&0.835589\\
 8&0.935277&0.836055\\
 16&0.935586&0.836305\\
 24&0.935690&0.836390
\end{array}
\]
All terminating decimals in this table denote exact rationals.
Set $b=183/1000$, $d=81/500$, $d_0=6/25$. The two-disk support
inequality, followed by Cauchy--Schwarz against the norm-$\sqrt2$
visible Wick cubic, gives
\[
 u\le U(t)=\frac{H_0-t}{pd_0},\qquad
 \|Pk\|^2+\epsilon\|Qh\|^2
    \ge\frac{[t+pdu-H]_+^2}{2pb^2}.
\]
This is the same-response cost, spent once.

Use $u_0=49/200$. The visible test $Z(|Z|^2-1/10)$ has norm integral

$u\le u_0$ use
\[
 L_\eta(t)=t^2+\frac{pu_0^2}{2\eta}
                    -\frac{(1.9t-I_0)^2}{2p}.
\]
For $u\ge u_0$ use
\[
 C_\eta(t,u)=t^2+\frac{pu^2}{2\eta}
                  -\frac{(t+pdu-H)^2}{2pb^2}.
\]
The checker proves exactly that the bracket is positive, $L_\eta$
increases on the interval, $C_\eta$ is convex in $u$,
$C_\eta(t,u_0)$ increases in $t$, and $C_\eta(t,U(t))$ is convex in
$t$. Only four endpoints remain. Their maxima, rounded upward for
presentation, are respectively
\[
 0.712981,\quad0.712948,\quad0.713191,\quad0.713160,\quad0.713225.
\]
Every exact fraction is below $2853/4000$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[\st{P}: a spectral entrance test]
\label{cor:phase-contact-spectral}
Let $q$ be the remote projection of $F$, $e=\|q\|^2$, and let
$H_q,A_q$ be the derivative Gram operators defined by
\[
 \operatorname{tr}(P_{\mathcal A}H_q)
 =\sum_{X\in\operatorname{ONB}(\mathcal A)}\|\partial_Xq\|^2,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{tr}(P_{\mathcal A}A_q)
 =\sum_{X\in\operatorname{ONB}(\mathcal A)}\|\partial_{\bar X}q\|^2.
\]
Both act on the same remote first-chaos space; these basis-independent
trace formulas fix the antiholomorphic coordinate convention.
Put $K_q=H_q-A_q$, so $\operatorname{tr}K_q=e$.
Let $s_m(K)$ be the sum of its largest at most $m$ positive eigenvalues.
Each row $(m,\eta)$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:phase-preserving-contact} is
implied by the dimension-free invariant test
\[
 \boxed{s_m(K_q)\ge e+\eta\|F\|^2.}
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For a remote $\mathcal A$ put $\mathcal B=\mathcal A^\perp$ within
remote first chaos. Decompose $q$ into six orthogonal sectors
$q_{ij}$ having $i\in\{0,1,2\}$ holomorphic and $j\in\{0,1\}$
antiholomorphic legs in $\mathcal A$, and put $e_{ij}=\|q_{ij}\|^2$.
The Hermite derivative count gives the exact identity
\[
 \|\Pi_{\mathcal D(\mathcal A,\mathcal B)}q\|^2
 =\operatorname{tr}(P_{\mathcal A}K_q)-e
      +e_{00}+2e_{01}+e_{11}
 \ge\operatorname{tr}(P_{\mathcal A}K_q)-e.
\]
Indeed the trace is $\sum(i-j)e_{ij}$; after subtracting $e$ the
three displayed additions leave precisely $e_{20}$.
Choose $\mathcal A$ from the largest positive eigenvalues of $K_q$
(Ky Fan's variational principle). Its dimension is at most $m$.
The projection of $F$ equals that of $q$, so the theorem applies.
\end{proof}
This is a sufficient condition, not an equivalence. On the separated
class the identity is tight. If $K_q\succeq0$, however,
$s_m(K_q)\le e$, so the spectral test cannot trigger for nonzero $F$.
More generally it requires
$\operatorname{tr}(K_q)_-\ge\eta\|F\|^2$.
This locates the influence overlap that this method still leaves
uncontrolled; failure of this test does not imply failure of all
geometric projection tests.

\paragraph{Rank-free continuation.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:conditional-response-contact} below covers this entire
separated class at arbitrary ranks with the uniform energy share
$499/500$, and also allows the holomorphic linear times centered
$(1,1)$-quadratic channel. It covers all visible rows. The present
finite-rank theorem retains its better concentration thresholds at
small ranks and its sharp extremizer formulas.

\paragraph{What this does and does not cover.}
The sharp comparison reaches the Gaussian limit instead of a
constant-modulus limit: the conditional Gaussian phase survives.
It supplies whole-subspace response certificates well beyond two
repeated-holomorphic slices. Cubics reusing a direction on both the
holomorphic and antiholomorphic sides need not have a large projection
into this class. Nor must a diffuse tensor concentrate on finitely many
holomorphic inputs. An exhaustive alternative for that coupled
complement, and the same-$\epsilon$ flank rows, remain open. These
regional caps do not change the complete Grothendieck endpoint.
