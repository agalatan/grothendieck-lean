\subsection{The exact joint-response defect}
\label{sec:joint-response-defect}
\paragraph{Scope and purpose.}
The next step after the disk-distance correction is to retain
$|h\pm k|\le1$ together. The following identity is \st{P}; its
polynomial formulas have standalone exact coefficient checks \st{C}.
It concerns actual responses, all dimensions and all shared sectors.
It neither assumes nor proves raw K. The new defect \emph{replaces}
the earlier clipping and $h$-phase estimates; these must not be added
to it a second time.

Use the real Hilbert inner product, $Ph=tZ$, $F=\epsilon Qk$,
$p=1/\epsilon$, and $V=\|F\|^2$. Choose
\[
 L=Z(a+bT),\quad \ell=a+2b,\quad B=bZ(T-2),\quad T=|Z|^2,
\]
with real $a,b$, and a regularized radial weight $\lambda\ge |L|$
bounded below by a positive constant. Write $l=|L|$ and let $M$ have
pointwise eigenvalues $C=\lambda+l^2/\lambda$ parallel to $L$, and
$\lambda$ perpendicular to $L$. Let $U$ embed a finite real orthonormal
set of cubic moments (all cubic moments in finite Gaussian dimension),
and put
\[
 y=U^*k,\quad \mathsf G=U^*(M+P)^{-1}U,\quad
 k_0=(M+P)^{-1}U\mathsf G^{-1}y,
 \quad D=U\mathsf G^{-1}y-Pk_0=Mk_0.
\]
Assume the finite weighted integrals and positive Gram, as in the
preceding regularized certificates. Define
\[
 H=C+D^TM^{-1}D,\qquad
 \mathcal S(L,D)=|L+D|+|L-D|,\qquad
 \Delta(L,D)=H-\mathcal S(L,D),
\]
and the old quadratic upper value
\[
 B_{\rm quad}=t^2-2\ell t+2pb^2+pV+\E C-y^T\mathsf G^{-1}y.
\]
\begin{theorem}[\st{P}: exact joint defect]
\label{thm:exact-joint-response-defect}
Every actual response satisfies the identity
\begin{align}
 \mathcal N={}&B_{\rm quad}-\E\Delta(L,D)
 -\|Pk-Pk_0\|^2-\epsilon\|Qh+pB\|^2\nonumber\\
 &-\E\bigl[\mathcal S(L,D)-2\Re(\bar Lh+\bar Dk)\bigr].
 \label{eq:exact-joint-response-defect}
\end{align}
Every subtracted quantity is nonnegative. Moreover,
\[
 \Delta(L,D)\ge
 \operatorname{dist}_M(M^{-1}D,\{u:|u|\le1\})^2.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Complete the $Qh$ square using $\|B\|^2=2b^2$, and the $Pk$
square around $a_0=Pk_0$. For $\xi=\mathsf G^{-1}y$,
$D=U\xi-a_0$ and $U^*k=y$, while
\[
 \|a_0\|^2+\E D^TM^{-1}D
 =\|k_0\|_{M+P}^2=y^T\mathsf G^{-1}y.
\]
Substituting gives the identity. The exact support of the two disks is
$\mathcal S$, so its displayed support gap is nonnegative.
The old quadratic inequality is
$2\Re(\bar Lh)\le C-k^TMk$.
Taking the joint support gives
\[
 \mathcal S(L,D)\le C+
 \sup_{|u|\le1}(2D\cdot u-u^TMu)
 \le C+D^TM^{-1}D.
\]
Completing this last quadratic also proves the disk-distance comparison.
\end{proof}
The matching error $\|Pk-Pk_0\|^2$ is a remainder of the same
completion, not a second use of $\|Pk\|^2$. The $Qh$ completion is
compatible. For arbitrary $\xi$ and $a_0\in\operatorname{ran}P$, the
same proof gives the exact-support dual
\[
 \mathcal N\le t^2-2\ell t+2pb^2+pV-2\xi^Ty+\|a_0\|^2
 +\E\mathcal S(L,U\xi-a_0)-\epsilon\|Qh+pB\|^2.
\]
Thus the old Gram trial point need not be optimal in this stronger dual.

\subsubsection{A rational formula retaining a sharp fraction of the defect}
Rotate $L$ to $l\ge0$ and write $D=x+iy$. Set
\[
 u=\frac{x^2}{C},\quad w=\frac{y^2}{\lambda},\quad s=u+w,
 \quad d=\lambda-\frac{l^2}{\lambda},\quad H=C+s,
 \quad B_0=l^2+x^2+y^2.
\]
The numerator
\[
 P_0=H^4-4H^2B_0+16l^2x^2
\]
has the exact nonnegative decomposition, for $s>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:joint-defect-polynomial}
 P_0=\frac{u}{s}(s-C)^2\bigl((s+C)^2-4l^2\bigr)
       +\frac{w}{s}(s-d)^2(s+C)^2.
\end{equation}
At $s=0$, $P_0=C^2d^2$; the remaining factor in the first summand is
$s^2+2Cs+d^2\ge0$.
This also proves $H\ge\mathcal S$ directly: the identity
\[
 H^2-2B_0=\lambda^2+(l^2/\lambda)^2+s^2+2(l^2/\lambda)w\ge0
\]
selects the upper square-root branch in
$\mathcal S^2=2B_0+2\sqrt{B_0^2-4l^2x^2}$.
\begin{theorem}[\st{P,C}: rational joint correction]
\label{thm:rational-joint-correction}
For $R_{\rm joint}=P_0/(2H^3)$,
\[
 \boxed{\qquad
 \frac{81}{250}\Delta(L,D)\le R_{\rm joint}\le\Delta(L,D).
 \qquad}
\]
The lower factor is sharp, attained at $\lambda=l>0$, $D=L$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put $A_0=|L+D|+|L-D|$ and $b_0=\bigl||L+D|-|L-D|\bigr|$.
Twice rationalizing gives
\[
 P_0=(H-A_0)(H+A_0)(H^2-b_0^2),\qquad
 \frac{R_{\rm joint}}\Delta
 =\frac{(1+A_0/H)(1-b_0^2/H^2)}2
\]
when $\Delta>0$. The upper bound follows from $H\ge A_0$.
For $l>0$ use $b_0\le2\min(l,|D|)$ and
$H\ge C+|D|^2/C$, $C\ge2l$. Minimizing the latter expression over
$C\ge2l$ and splitting $|D|/l$ into $[0,1]$, $[1,2]$, and
$[2,\infty)$ proves $v=b_0/H\le4/5$.
Since $A_0/H\ge v$,
\[
 \frac{R_{\rm joint}}\Delta\ge\frac{(1-v)(1+v)^2}{2}
 \ge\frac{81}{250},\qquad
 (1-v)(1+v)^2-\frac{81}{125}
 =\left(\frac45-v\right)
 \left(v^2+\frac95v+\frac{11}{25}\right).
\]
If $l=0$, $v=0$ directly; zero gaps follow by continuity.
At $\lambda=l$, $D=L$, the gap is $l/2$ and the rational correction
is $81l/500$. Expanding finite polynomials checks
\eqref{eq:joint-defect-polynomial} and the displayed factorizations
in exact rational arithmetic, without a symbolic-algebra dependency.
\end{proof}
For $l>0$ the exact zero set of $\Delta$ consists of
$D=\pm C$ in the parallel direction and
$D=\pm i\sqrt{\lambda^2-l^2}$ in the perpendicular direction.
Thus even interpolants inside the unit disk can incur a positive joint
defect. A small average requires specific visibly varying directions and
amplitudes, not merely small modulus. The maximum of $R_{\rm joint}$
and the earlier harmonic disk-distance lower bound is valid; their sum
is not justified.

\subsubsection{A modest certified enlargement from a projected price}
\label{sec:projected-remote-price}
Let $q$ be the remote part of $F$, $e=\|q\|^2>0$, and
$\widehat q=q/\sqrt e$. Its bidegree makes it circular. Put
$\rho_q=\E|\widehat q|\le1$ and
$S_q=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C}(\widehat q,\overline{\widehat q})$;
the two spanning functions are orthonormal. For each fixed $Z$,
\[
 \|\Pi_{S_q}k(Z,\cdot)\|^2\le\rho_q^2.
\]
Indeed, by circularity and angular Cauchy--Schwarz,
\[
 \E|a\widehat q+b\overline{\widehat q}|
 \le\rho_q\sqrt{|a|^2+|b|^2};
\]
duality and $|k|\le1$ prove the claim.
Adding a nonnegative radial multiplier $r(T)$ on this subspace changes
only the remote Gram in the direction $q$:
\[
 C_r=\frac12\E\left[
 \frac1{\lambda+r}+\frac1{\lambda+l^2/\lambda+r}\right].
\]
The subspace is conjugation-invariant, so it commutes with the
real-linear radial weight. It is orthogonal to $P$ and to the remote
linear, quadratic and constant groups; dependence among their underlying
drivers does not change this chaos orthogonality. Weighted Bessel gives
\[
 \mathfrak m(k)+\|Pk\|^2\ge
 \frac{p^2e}{C_r}+\text{the old nonremote costs}-\rho_q^2\E r.
\]
For $e=0$ omit this subspace; retaining the nonnegative charge in the
resulting upper bound is still valid.

\begin{theorem}[\st{P,C}: projected-price response region]
At $\epsilon=1891/2500$, every actual response satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:projected-price-cap}
 \mathcal N\le t^2-\frac{1401}{2500}t
 +\frac{4253566841}{10^{10}}
 +\frac{1558765869}{1250000000}\mathcal V_*.
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal V_*$ is exactly \eqref{eq:weighted-full-energy-definition}.
Consequently the following all-sector regions have cap below $0.713260$:
\[
 \begin{array}{c|c}
 0\le t\le0.8456,\quad\mathcal V_*\le0.03748
     &\mathcal N\le0.713259839916096\\
 0\le t\le0.851,\quad\mathcal V_*\le0.03255
     &\mathcal N\le0.713247547328760.
 \end{array}
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use the preceding eight-integral weight with
$a=3/2000$, $b=2787/20000$,
$\lambda^2=T(a+bT)^2+47/500000$, and take
$r=11/10000$ on $[0,1/15]$, zero elsewhere. Use only $\rho_q\le1$.
The exact checker freshly recomputes all eight integrals and infinite
tails, then subtracts from the remote Gram the integral
\[
 \int_0^{1/15}\frac{r e^{-T}}2
 \left[\frac1{\lambda(\lambda+r)}+
 \frac1{C(C+r)}\right]dT.
\]
Its integrand decreases: both $\lambda$ and
$C=2\lambda-(47/500000)/\lambda$ increase.
Left and right rectangular sums give directed enclosures with no
derivative estimate. The charge is $r(1-e^{-1/15})$.
The checker verifies every scalar nonremote cost and the full A/B matrix
against the same weighted-energy coefficient, rounding the two displayed
constants upward. Positivity of that coefficient and convexity in $t$
reduce each rectangle to its two $t$ endpoints.
The default replay uses 8192 extra cells and the finer replay uses 32768.
At remote energy $e=0.03748$, the projected multiplier improves the
unrounded old weight by more than $0.0000056098$ in the cap.
\end{proof}
This slightly increases the sufficient peak energy cutoff from $0.03744$
to $0.03748$. It is not a complete cover. The previous weighted bound
is still useful at lower energies; take the minimum of the two bounds.

\paragraph{The remaining mathematical obligation.}
A lower bound for $\E R_{\rm joint}$ on the still-surviving response
set, or a uniformly stronger optimized exact-support dual, remains open.
The formula retains the full joint law, but no universal rate sufficient
on that complement has been proved. A scalar multiplier on all of
$|k|^2$ alone cannot supply new geometry: its coefficients
$A=\lambda+r$, $B=\lambda+l^2/\lambda+r$ are dominated by the
existing quadratic envelope with perpendicular coefficient
$(B+\sqrt{B^2-4l^2})/2\ge A$ and the same constant $B$.
The projected price above and the joint defect avoid that particular
redundancy. Neither result establishes a new Grothendieck endpoint.
