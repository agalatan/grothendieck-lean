\subsection{Joint drivers, cubic-phase tails, and a common certificate}
\label{sec:joint-structure-progress-20261022}

\paragraph{Status.}
The results below are analytic \st{P}, with exact finite supporting
audits \st{C}. They do not change the numerical Grothendieck endpoints.
They strengthen the interpretation of the radial family already used in
\autoref{ch:zero-buffer-bootstrap} and the certificate interface in
\autoref{ch:crossbound}. The uniform claims concern every finite Gaussian
dimension; no independent-buffer assumption is made.

\paragraph{Shared drivers survive phase truncation.}
For independent proper standard complex Gaussians $X,Y$, put
$H(X)=X(|X|^2-2)/\sqrt2$. The pairs $(H(X),X)$ and $(H(Y),X)$ have
identical marginal laws and zero cross covariances and pseudocovariances.
Their nonlinear contents are $8\sqrt2$ and zero, respectively:
\[
 \E[\bar XH(X)|H(X)|^2]
 =\frac{\E[T^2(T-2)^3]}{2\sqrt2}=8\sqrt2,
 \qquad T=|X|^2.
\]
For their bounded phase responses the pairings are, respectively,
$2e^{-2}-1$ and zero. Even their separate Hermite energy sequences agree;
the mixed coefficients need not agree. Thus separate laws and separate
noise spectra do not encode the joint response profile.

\begin{theorem}[An exact coordinate-core obstruction, \st{P}]
\label{thm:joint-radial-core}
For $X\in\mathbb C^N$ standard proper Gaussian, set
\[
 Q_N=\frac{X_1(\norm X^2-N-1)}{\sqrt{N+1}}\in\mathcal H_{2,1},
 \qquad \norm{Q_N}_2=1.
\]
For any complex orthogonal projection $P$ of rank $m$,
\[
 \E[Q_N\mid PX]
 =\frac{(PX)_1(\norm{PX}^2-m-1)}{\sqrt{N+1}},\qquad
 \norm{\E[Q_N\mid PX]}_2^2
 =\frac{m+1}{N+1}\norm{Pe_1}^2.
\]
Consequently, for $1\le m\le N$,
\[
 \inf_{\operatorname{rank}P=m}\inf_{F\in L^2(\sigma(PX))}
 \norm{Q_N-F}_2^2=1-\frac{m+1}{N+1}.
\]
Nevertheless the first-coordinate Wirtinger influence is
$\norm{\partial_{z_1}Q_N}_2^2+\norm{\partial_{\bar z_1}Q_N}_2^2
=(N+5)/(N+1)$, tending to one; the total influence is three.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $X=PX+(I-P)X$ as independent proper Gaussians. Conditional
expectation of the cubic gives the displayed formula. Rotation inside
the range of $P$ reduces its norm to the same radial Wick calculation
in dimension $m$, with the extra factor $\norm{Pe_1}^2$.
Conditional expectation is the best approximation by every
square-integrable function of $PX$. The maximum of $\norm{Pe_1}^2$ is
one. Direct differentiation gives the influence identities.
\end{proof}

In fact $(X_1,Q_N)\Rightarrow(X,XG)$, where $G$ is independent real
standard Gaussian. Its conditional variance is $|X|^2$ and its
conditional pseudovariance is $X^2$. The limiting Gaussian driver has
grade two. A small conditional mean does not license a fresh proper
Gaussian replacement. The example still has a small description in
terms of polynomial features: a linear driver and a radial quadratic.

Theorem~\ref{thm:conditional-response-contact} now covers actual responses
with $F=cQ_N$ for $N\ge999$: their exactly-one-leg share relative to
$X_1$ is $(N-1)/(N+1)$. This does not alter the fixed-coordinate-core
obstruction just proved. It replaces the core by the entire quadratic
factor in the selected response test. The mixed families below are
not asserted to satisfy that $499/500$ share condition.

The same phenomenon occurs at smaller kurtosis excess. On an independent
block let $R_M=M^{-1/2}\sum_{j=1}^M H(Y_j)$ and put
$q=\sqrt a\,Q_N+\sqrt{1-a}\,R_M$. Then
\[
 \E|q|^4-2
 =a^2\left(4+\frac{52}{N+1}+\frac{48}{(N+1)^2}\right)
 +(1-a)^2\frac{42}{M}\longrightarrow4a^2.
\]
The first-coordinate influence tends to $a$. For every rank-$m$
complex projection of the full space,
\[
 \norm{\E[q\mid P(X,Y)]}_2
 \le\sqrt{\frac{a(m+1)}{N+1}}+\sqrt{\frac{(1-a)m}{M}}.
\]
For the second term, projecting the normalized diagonal cubic tensor
gives squared norm at most
$M^{-1}\sum_{i,j}|\langle Pe_i,Pe_j\rangle|^2\le m/M$;
the first term follows from the preceding theorem and independent-noise
contraction. At $a=17/50$, the limiting kurtosis excess is $0.68^2$
while the persistent influence is $0.34$. This does not assert
near-optimal payoff for this family.
For the explicit finite choice $N=M=100\,000$,
$0.4626<\E|q|^4-2<0.4627$ and the first-coordinate influence exceeds
$0.34$, while every 20-dimensional complex-linear observation captures
less than $1/2500$ of the squared norm, by an exact rational
square-root comparison.

\begin{theorem}[Exact limiting cubic-phase spectrum, \st{P,C}]
\label{thm:joint-radial-spectrum}
For $f_N=\operatorname{phase}(Q_N)$ and each fixed $k$,
$\norm{P_{k+1,k}f_N}_2^2\to e_k$, where $e_0=0$ and
\[
 e_k=\frac12\sum_{j+2\ell=k-1}
 \frac{(1/2)_j^2}{j!(j+1)!}
 \frac{\binom{2\ell}{\ell}}{4^\ell(2\ell+1)}\quad(k\ge1).
\]
The energies sum to one, and their first nonzero entries are
$1/2,1/16,41/384,139/6144,8009/163840$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Couple $X,X'$ by real noise correlation $\rho\in[0,1)$.
The remote radial fluctuations converge jointly to real Gaussians with
correlation $\rho^2$, independent of $(X_1,X_1')$.
Their sign boundaries have probability zero. Bounded convergence in
distribution therefore gives the limiting kernel
\[
 C(\rho)\frac2\pi\arcsin(\rho^2),\qquad
 C(\rho)=\frac\pi4\sum_{j\ge0}
 \frac{(1/2)_j^2}{j!(j+1)!}\rho^{2j+1}.
\]
Here $C$ is the complex Gaussian phase kernel: expand the phase in
$zL_j^1(|z|^2)/\sqrt{j+1}$ and integrate the Laguerre generating
function. Multiplication by the arcsine series gives the formula.
Coefficient convergence follows by diagonal subsequences: nonnegative
coefficient tails at $\rho<1$ are uniformly at most
$\rho^{2K+3}$, and uniqueness of power series identifies every limit.
The limiting kernel tends to one at $\rho=1$, proving normalization.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[A necessary uniform tail cutoff, \st{P,C}]
\label{cor:joint-cubic-tail-obstruction}
For every integer $D\ge1$,
\[
 \sup_N\sum_{k>D}\norm{P_{k+1,k}\operatorname{phase}(Q_N)}_2^2
 \ge\frac1{6\sqrt{\lceil D/2\rceil}}.
\]
There is no dimension-uniform $o(D^{-1/2})$ estimate for phases of all
genuine complex Wick cubics. A uniform tail allowance $10^{-4}$
necessarily requires $D\ge5\,555\,555$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Keep $j=0$ and $\ell\ge L:=\lceil D/2\rceil$ in the preceding theorem.
The elementary bound $\binom{2\ell}{\ell}/4^\ell\ge1/(2\sqrt\ell)$
follows by induction from $(2\ell+1)^2\ge4\ell(\ell+1)$.
Thus the tail is at least
$\frac1{12}\sum_{\ell\ge L}\ell^{-3/2}\ge1/(6\sqrt L)$.
Finite heads converge and every total energy is one. The numerical
cutoff follows from $36\cdot2\,777\,777<10^8$.
\end{proof}

This is an obstruction to a general individual-energy estimate, not
to a signed mixture or a theorem restricted to near-maximizers. The
limiting self-pair has cubic-witness payoff $-\varepsilon/2$; reversing
one response gives $\varepsilon/2$, far from the current norm cap.
One-sided best-response form alone also does not remove the obstruction.
For every nonzero $q\in\mathcal H_{2,1}$, the Gram matrix of
$\mathcal H_{1,0}\oplus\mathcal H_{2,1}$ restricted to a Gaussian ball
is positive definite. Inverting it and scaling a polynomial truncated
to that ball produces a bounded charge-one $g$ with
$(P_{1,0}-\varepsilon P_{2,1})g=aq$, $a>0$.
Thus $\operatorname{phase}(q)$ is a one-sided best response, although
its payoff need not be near the global norm.

\begin{proposition}[Common certificate with priced smoothing, \st{P}]
\label{prop:joint-common-certificate}
Let $c_k$ be the real coefficients of an actual mixture of bounded
charge-one response pairs, so $\sum_k|c_k|\le1$. Suppose a witness
has a global norm certificate $N(\lambda)\le B$.
If $R\ge\sum_{k>D}|c_k|$ and $0<\rho\le1$, put
\[
 A_\rho=\rho c_0-\sum_{k=1}^D\rho^{2k+1}|c_k|
                  -R\rho^{2D+3}.
\]
Whenever $A_\rho>0$, the inherited transfer theorems give
\[
 \frac1B\le\KG\le\frac1{A_\rho}.
\]
With only certified bounds $c_0\ge a_0>0$, $|c_k|\le u_k$, a valid
replacement for $A_\rho$ is
\[
 (\rho+\rho^{2D+3})a_0
 -\sum_{k=1}^D(\rho^{2k+1}-\rho^{2D+3})u_k-\rho^{2D+3}.
\]
In particular, a truncated score at least $v>0$ gives the score
$\rho v-(1-v)\rho^{2D+3}$ without a separate tail hypothesis.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Apply the Ornstein--Uhlenbeck operator of parameter $\rho$ to the left
response. It preserves the unit disk and multiplies the mixed
coefficient by $\rho^{2k+1}$. Bound the omitted coefficients and use
the Chapter~17 transfer. For the interval version first substitute the
actual Bessel budget $1-c_0-\sum_{1\le k\le D}|c_k|$, and then use
the signs of the coefficients. For the final statement the retained
score is at least $\rho v$ and the omitted absolute sum at most $1-v$.
\end{proof}

For $m=2D+3$ and $0<v<1$, the last score is maximized at
\[
 \rho_* =\min\{1,[v/(m(1-v))]^{1/(m-1)}\}.
\]
It supplies an explicit $O((\log D)/D)$ cost by changing the responses,
and does not contradict the tail obstruction for unchanged responses.
For an exact pool value $v_D$ and a certified global cap $v_D+\delta$,
the reciprocal interval width is
$\delta+(1-\rho)v_D+(1-v_D)\rho^{2D+3}$.
Exact LP optimality is unnecessary; universal norm certification is
still necessary.
