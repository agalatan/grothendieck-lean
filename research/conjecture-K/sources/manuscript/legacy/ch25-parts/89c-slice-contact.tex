\subsection{From individual monomials to whole tensor subspaces}
\label{sec:whole-slice-contact}

\paragraph{Status and scope.}
The following \st{P,C} results concern actual bounded responses, with
$\epsilon=1891/2500$, $p=2500/1891$, $Ph=tZ$, $F=\epsilon Qk$,
$V=\|F\|^2$ and $\mathcal N=t^2+pV-\|Pk\|^2-\epsilon\|Qh\|^2$.
They allow arbitrary coupled remainders, every visible sector, all
phases and every finite dimension. There is no fresh-buffer or
independent-remainder assumption. These are new regions, not a full
same-witness cover or a new endpoint.

Let $\mathcal H$ be the remote first Gaussian chaos, orthogonal to $Z$.
For unit $X,Y\in\mathcal H$, define the cubic subspaces
\[
 \mathcal S_X=\{\,{:}X^2\bar A{:}/\sqrt2:A\in\mathcal H\,\},
 \qquad
 \mathcal A_Y=\bar Y\,\mathcal H_{2,0}(\mathcal H\cap Y^\perp).
\]
Here ${:}X^2\bar A{:}=X^2\bar A-2\E[X\bar A]X$.
Write $\Pi_{\mathcal S_X},\Pi_{\mathcal A_Y}$ for the orthogonal
projections. The first space keeps one repeated holomorphic direction
and all antiholomorphic directions; the second keeps one antiholomorphic
direction and an arbitrary holomorphic quadratic on its orthogonal
complement. Their dimensions need not be bounded.

\begin{theorem}[\st{P,C}: whole-subspace concentration]
\label{thm:whole-slice-contact}
Throughout $21/25\le t\le851/1000$, in every finite dimension,
\[
 \boxed{\begin{aligned}
 \|\Pi_{\mathcal S_X}F\|^2\ge\frac9{10}V
   &\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\mathcal N<\frac{14263}{20000}=0.71315,\\
 \|\Pi_{\mathcal A_Y}F\|^2\ge\frac{99}{100}V
   &\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\mathcal N<\frac{14261}{20000}=0.71305,\\
 \|\Pi_{\mathcal A_Y}F\|^2\ge\frac{197}{200}V
   &\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\mathcal N<\frac{28529}{40000}=0.713225.
 \end{aligned}}
\]
Each conclusion is below $35663/50000<500/701$.
The remainder orthogonal to the indicated subspace may share all its
coordinates and all visible sectors. There is no separate amplitude
restriction. The latter two statements allow holomorphic quadratic
rank tending to infinity.
\end{theorem}

\subsubsection{The support lemma separates the test from the remainder}
Let $J$ be a unit remote cubic test and choose its phase so that
$u=\langle\sqrt2J,F\rangle=\sqrt2|\langle J,F\rangle|\ge0$.
For
\[
 L=Z\{\ell+b(|Z|^2-2)\},\quad D=\sqrt2dJ,\quad
 m=\Re\langle Z(|Z|^2-2),h\rangle,
\]
the circular test law gives $\E|L+D|=\E|L-D|$.
The two-disk support lemma therefore gives
\[
 \ell t+bm+pdu\le\E|L+D|.
\]
If the right side is at most $H$ and $b>0$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:slice-support-penalty}
 \|Pk\|^2+\epsilon\|Qh\|^2
 \ge\frac{[\ell t+pdu-H]_+^2}{2pb^2}.
\end{equation}
For $b=0$ it gives a feasibility cut. This spends the existing penalty
once; it is not an additional charge to combine with another bound on
the same square. Only the known test law is integrated. No law of the
unknown remainder is replaced or assumed independent.

\subsubsection{Visible averaging supplies the needed concavity}
For $a>0$, $b\ge0$, set
\[
 \Phi(v)=\E|Z(a+b|Z|^2)+\sqrt v|,\qquad v\ge0.
\]
This is increasing and concave \st{P}. Here is a self-contained route
to the analytic input, also used in Lemma~\ref{lem:gsm}.
The inverse $\tau$ of $t\mapsto t(a+bt)^2$ satisfies
$\tau'=G\circ\tau$, where
$G(t)=1/((a+bt)(a+3bt))$ is completely monotone.
Induction in the chain-rule formula gives
$(-1)^{j-1}\tau^{(j)}\ge0$ for all $j\ge1$.
The density $e^{-\tau(v)}\tau'(v)$ is consequently completely
monotone. Bernstein's representation theorem, normalized by its
integral, identifies the circular visible variable as $\sqrt R\,N$
in law, with $R>0$ independent of a proper standard Gaussian $N$.
For a fixed $g>0$, the Rice mean $\rho_g(v)=\E|\sqrt v+gN|$ has
\[
 2\rho_g'(v)=\frac1{g\sqrt\pi}
   \int_0^1e^{-vt/g^2}\sqrt{\frac{1-t}{t}}\,dt,
\]
so it is increasing and concave. Mixtures preserve these properties.
This argument concerns the \emph{visibly averaged} potential.
The analogous fixed-radius angular mean need not be concave in squared
radius, and is not used as if it were.

\subsubsection{The whole holomorphic slice has one mixing parameter}
The map $A\mapsto{:}X^2\bar A{:}/\sqrt2$ is an antilinear isometry.
Choose its unit vector aligned with $\Pi_{\mathcal S_X}F$.
If $V=0$, use the visible test below directly; otherwise the
concentration hypothesis makes the selected projection nonzero.
Gaussian phase changes reduce its law to that of
\[
 \psi_w=\sqrt w\,X(|X|^2-2)+\sqrt{1-w}\,X^2\bar Y,
 \qquad 0\le w\le1,\quad X\perp Y,Z,
\]
before division by $\sqrt2$. Its squared norm is $2$.
Write $T=|X|^2$, $S=|Y|^2$; these are independent exponentials.
First integrate the visible variable, and then average the relative
phase of $X,Y$. Concavity gives the uniform test majorant
\begin{equation}\label{eq:slice-concave-relaxation}
 \E|L+d\psi_w|\le B(w):=
 \E_{T,S}\Phi\!\left(d^2\{wT(T-2)^2+(1-w)T^2S\}\right).
\end{equation}
The cross term has zero phase mean. Moreover $B$ is concave in $w$,
since its argument is affine in $w$. No monotonicity in $w$ is asserted.
The computer certificate proves
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
 \ell&b&d&\sup_{0\le w\le1}B(w)<H\\\hline
 1&0&1/4&93533/100000\\
 1&37/200&87/500&8367/10000
\end{array}
\]
using directed lower and upper values at only
$w\in\{0,1/20,1/10,1/5,1\}$.

\paragraph{How five enclosures cover the continuum.}
For concave $B$, a secant through $x_{i-1},x_i$ extrapolated to the
right is an upper bound, and a secant through $x_{i+1},x_{i+2}$
extrapolated to the left is an upper bound. If $l_i\le B(x_i)\le h_i$,
the corresponding certified lines are
\[
 h_i+\frac{h_i-l_{i-1}}{x_i-x_{i-1}}(x-x_i),\qquad
 h_{i+1}+\frac{l_{i+2}-h_{i+1}}{x_{i+2}-x_{i+1}}(x-x_{i+1}).
\]
On each interval use whichever lines are available and take their
minimum. Its maximum occurs at an endpoint or the intersection of the
two lines. All those comparisons are rational. This proves the
continuum claim from the ten fresh integral enclosures.

\subsubsection{An arbitrary holomorphic quadratic needs no rank cutoff}
\begin{lemma}[\st{P}: a quadratic-block support comparison]
\label{lem:quadratic-block-support}
Let $Q_2\in\mathcal H_{2,0}$ have norm $1$ and use only remote
coordinates orthogonal to $Y$. Let $G_1,G_2$ be independent proper
standard complex Gaussians. For every increasing concave $\Phi$ for
which these expectations exist,
\[
 \E\Phi(s|\bar YQ_2|^2)
       \le\E\Phi(s|G_1G_2|^2),\qquad s\ge0.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Takagi diagonalization writes $Q_2=\sum_j\lambda_j X_j^2$ in law,
with $\lambda_j\ge0$, independent standard $X_j$, and
$2\sum_j\lambda_j^2=1$. For complex $z$ its characteristic function is
\[
 \E e^{i\Re(\bar zQ_2)}
       =\prod_j(1+\lambda_j^2|z|^2)^{-1/2}.
\]
Hence $Q_2\overset d=\sqrt W\,G_1$, where
$W=4\sum_j\lambda_j^2\Gamma_j$, the $\Gamma_j$ are independent
$\operatorname{Gamma}(1/2,1)$ variables, and $\E W=1$.
Take this representation independent of $Y$ and condition on $G_1,Y$.
Jensen in $W$ proves the inequality. This is a comparison for the
test polynomial's law, not an insertion of a fresh buffer in $F$.
\end{proof}

Apply the lemma to the visibly averaged potential with $s=2d^2$.
Every unit test $J=\bar YQ_2\in\mathcal A_Y$ satisfies
\[
 \E|L+\sqrt2dJ|\le\E|L+\sqrt2dG_1\bar G_2|.
\]
This product comparison is sharp over unbounded quadratic rank \st{P}.
Indeed $Q_N=(2N)^{-1/2}\sum_{j=1}^NX_j^2$ has mixing variable
$W_N=(2/N)\sum_{j=1}^N\Gamma_j$, with
$\E W_N=1$ and $\operatorname{Var}(W_N)=2/N$. Coupling
$Q_N=\sqrt{W_N}G_1$ in law gives, by the norm's Lipschitz property,
\[
 0\le \E|L+\sqrt2dG_1\bar Y|-\E|L+\sqrt2dQ_N\bar Y|
 \le\frac{2d}{\sqrt N}.
\]
Here $|\sqrt w-1|\le|w-1|$ and $\E|G_1\bar Y|\le1$.
Thus the product integral equals the supremum over all such unit
quadratics. This is sharpness of the test-law comparison only, not of
the resulting response cap or of the global Grothendieck bound.
Two directed product-integral certificates give
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
 \ell&b&d&\E|L+\sqrt2dG_1\bar G_2|<H\\\hline
 1&0&1/4&93868/100000\\
 1&37/200&87/500&83925/100000
\end{array}
\]
The default upper values are below $0.938672025190$ and
$0.839242280558$. Their proofs use the convex product-amplitude
cubature from \S\ref{sec:mixed-contact-concentration}, now for
$\sqrt2|G_1||G_2|$.

\subsubsection{The actual-response cap remains a four-endpoint proof}
For either pair of integral bounds, put $d_0=1/4$,
$b=37/200$, $d=87/500$, and let $H_0,H$ be its first and second bounds.
With $\eta$ the asserted projection share, set
\[
 U(t)=\frac{H_0-t}{pd_0},\qquad
 C_\eta(t,u)=t^2+\frac{pu^2}{2\eta}
                 -\frac{(t+pdu-H)^2}{2pb^2}.
\]
Use $u_0=47/200$ for $\mathcal S_X$ and $u_0=49/200$ for
$\mathcal A_Y$. Projection onto the chosen unit test gives
$V\le u^2/(2\eta)$, while the first support gives $u\le U(t)$.
For $u\le u_0$ use the visible test $Z(|Z|^2-1/10)$ with its
independently recomputed integral bound $I_0=124233393/10^8$.
For $u\ge u_0$, the bracket in $C_\eta$ is positive.
Exactly as in \eqref{eq:mixed-contact-canonical-cap}, convexity in $u$
reduces to $u_0,U(t)$, the first endpoint increases in $t$, and the
second is convex in $t$. The checker verifies every sign and the four
endpoint comparisons with fractions. This proves
Theorem~\ref{thm:whole-slice-contact}.

\paragraph{Directed integration, including lower values and tails.}
To enclose $B(w)$, partition the two exponential variables into radial
cells up to radius $6$. Cell masses and the first three exponential
moments are exact incomplete-gamma polynomials times $e^{-t}$.
The mean of the squared-radius argument is
$w\E[T(T-2)^2]+(1-w)\E[T^2]\E[S]$ on each product cell.
For upper bounds, group cells by approximate mean radius and apply
concavity to each group's \emph{exact} mass and first moment.
Any grouping is valid; no correctness assertion depends on a floating
classification. For lower bounds, the chord between the minimum and
maximum squared radius gives two endpoint atoms; compress these on a
rational squared-radius grid by another concave chord bound.
Then enclose the visibly averaged $\Phi$ using convex amplitude
cubature, with its lower bound supplied by Jensen on each visible
radial cell. The positive angular series and directed Gaussian
primitives are the same as in the preceding subsection.

This order matters: concavity is used for the true \emph{already
visibly averaged} $\Phi$, before substituting numerical upper or lower
enclosures. No false pointwise squared-radius concavity is invoked.
All arithmetic decisions are integer/rational. The explicit union-tail
bound uses
$\sqrt{wT(T-2)^2+(1-w)T^2S}\le\sqrt T|T-2|+T\sqrt S$;
the package gives the full formula, including all three unbounded tails.

\subsubsection{What this removes, and the new explicit obstruction}
The antiholomorphic-block theorem is not a finite-rank result.
For example
\[
 F_N=\bar Y\,\frac1{\sqrt{2N}}\sum_{j=1}^NX_j^2
\]
is unit, lies entirely in $\mathcal A_Y$, and has largest
$\mathcal S_X$ projection share $1/N$. Thus an arbitrarily spread
holomorphic quadratic block is covered, with a coupled remainder up
to the stated energy fraction. The former example
$X_1X_2\bar X_3$ also lies in $\mathcal A_{X_3}$ and is now covered
as a concentrated direction.

The cone union is still not exhaustive. A useful geometric obstruction is
\[
 F_0=\tfrac12(X_1^2\bar X_2+X_2^2\bar X_1),\qquad\|F_0\|=1.
\]
Its largest $\mathcal S_X$ projection share is $1/2$:
the contraction norm is
$\tfrac12(|x_1|^4+|x_2|^4)\le1/2$.
Its largest $\mathcal A_Y$ share is also $1/2$: before restricting
the holomorphic variables to $Y^\perp$, the contraction has norm
squared $\tfrac12(|y_1|^2+|y_2|^2)\le1/2$, and equality is attained
at $Y=X_1$. Its maximal directional influence, with the combined
holomorphic and antiholomorphic convention, is $3/2$.
These facts concern tensor geometry; $F_0$ is not asserted to be a
counterexample or a high-payoff actual response.

Every central survivor must now escape both entire-subspace cones,
in addition to the stronger earlier individual tests where applicable.
The missing step is a proved dimension-free alternative controlling
the genuinely coupled complement, together with the remaining flank
rows at the same witness parameter. Small influence or an independent
buffer does not follow from these projection exclusions.
