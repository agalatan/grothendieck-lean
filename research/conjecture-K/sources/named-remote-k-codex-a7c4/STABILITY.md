# Local stability in profile and price

Write $\theta=(a,b,p)$ with $a,b,p>0$. Around each named row there is an open neighbourhood in $\theta$ on which the exact remote equality of suprema continues to hold and every finite cubic satisfies the strict inequality. The central energy and excess intervals and the two-node sets stay as in the named-row proof. This argument gives no numerical radius in $a,b$.

On a central near-Gaussian rectangle, the normalized deficit estimate is strictly positive. The analytic KSLIV estimate can be made continuous in $a,b$ by fixing the interpolation polynomials, integration cells and choice of upper envelope used on each cell at the named row. Each branch is a valid upper bound at every profile, independent of which one gives the smaller numerical value. For $a,b$ in a positive compact neighbourhood, the finitely many inverse moments entering those bounds are continuous. The integer moments have the displayed positive Lagrange formula. The half-integer moments are finite linear combinations of single-pole integrals, continuous by domination by a multiple of $t^{-1/2}e^{-t}$ with $a$ bounded below. For the contraction remainder the unrounded upper polynomial is coefficientwise dominated at the named row by the exact rational polynomial used in the certificate; this follows from positivity and outward rounding. The strict degree-ten inequality for all $y\in[0,7/8]$ therefore persists by compactness. The final strict lower bound for $D/\delta$ on each energy interval persists by the same continuity, since each slab uses upper or lower values at its endpoints. These comparisons permit $\chi\downarrow0$.

For the exterior, $B_\theta(v)$ at the fixed reference variance is continuous in $a,b$ by the reverse triangle inequality. The radial values $H_\theta(x),H_\theta'(x)$ at the finitely many positive nodes and at $3/20$ are continuous in $a,b$. For the derivative, differentiate the angularly averaged norm under the integral: its absolute derivative in the positive squared shift $x$ is at most $1/\sqrt x$, and the norm is differentiable except on the null event $L=-\sqrt x$. For the radial values the integrable envelope follows from $2(|Z|(a+b|Z|^2)+\sqrt x)$. Consequently every true Hermite coefficient varies continuously. For each named-row dyadic upper polynomial add an arbitrarily small positive amount to its four coefficients. The finite endpoint Bernstein tests and the strict signs of the cubic coefficients still hold by their positive margins. Now these fixed, perturbed coefficients strictly majorize the four true interpolation coefficients, and continue to do so for nearby profiles. The universal sign $H^{(4)}\le0$ gives global radial domination throughout the neighbourhood. Pricing with $B_\theta(v)-pv$, all finite endpoint inequalities continue to hold. On the infinite Jensen ray both the tangent's slope inequality and its strict intercept comparison at $3/20$ persist by the same continuity. Intersect the finitely many open neighbourhoods just obtained. This proves the claimed local stability; the Gaussian-block limiting argument works at every positive $a,b,p$.


At fixed $a,b$ one can give explicit price intervals with the same witnesses. Flank admits every $1.34201\le p\le1.34239$, and upper admits every $1.3214\le p\le1.3225$. If $c_j$ is the $j$th certified Bernstein reserve on the cell $[l,r]$ at the original price $p_0$, replacing $p_0$ by $p$ changes that reserve to $c_j+(p-p_0)(l+j(r-l)/3-v)$, where $v$ is the fixed witness variance. Compute each linear inequality exactly, include the tangent reserve at $3/20$ and the positive tail slope, and intersect them. The central normalized deficit is independent of the price. The exterior verifier recomputes the finite and tail inequalities at both price endpoints. Each reserve is affine in the price for the fixed competitor, so its positivity at both endpoints covers the intervening interval.
