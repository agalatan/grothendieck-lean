Rigorous local result near the Gaussian (no explicit numerical threshold)

Fix an odd d >= 3. For F in the pure real Wiener chaos H_d with E F^2=1,
write delta=kappa_4(F). Uniformly over these F as delta tends to zero,

  E|F| = sqrt(2/pi) (1 - delta/24) + O_d(delta^(5/4)).

Thus the desired strict inequality holds for all sufficiently small positive
delta, but the argument here does not supply an explicit numerical threshold.
This is a local theorem, not a proof of global Gaussian maximality.

Proof and sources.

1. Mansanarez, Poly, Swan, Edgeworth expansion on Wiener chaos,
   https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14002v2 , Theorem 1.2 with m=2,
   gives total variation error O_d(delta^(3/2)) against the signed measure

     dnu(x)=phi(x)[1 + delta H_4(x)/24 + kappa_6(F) H_6(x)/720] dx.

   Indeed, odd chaos is symmetric, so the odd Hermite moments vanish;
   E H_4(F)=delta and E H_6(F)=kappa_6(F).
   The variance of the (appropriately normalized) carre-du-champ is
   bounded by a constant depending only on d times delta.

2. Schulte, Thaele, Cumulants on Wiener chaos: moderate deviations and the
   fourth moment theorem, https://arxiv.org/html/1410.7964v1 ,
   Lemmas 13--17, equations (23)--(25), yield

     |kappa_6(F)| <= C_d delta^(3/2).

   To see the exponent, their matching bound L(d,6) is 3. Each of the
   three matched pairs contributes a nontrivial contraction of the kernel;
   every such contraction norm is O_d(sqrt(delta)), by the fourth-cumulant
   identity. Constants depend only on the fixed chaos order, not dimension.

3. Total variation initially controls only bounded tests. Put
   R=delta^(-1/4) and use min(|x|,R). Its error is O_d(R delta^(3/2))
   = O_d(delta^(5/4)). Hypercontractivity uniformly bounds E|F|^6
   (for d=3, E|F|^6 <= 5^9). Hence

     E[|F| 1_{|F|>R}] <= R^(-5) E|F|^6 = O_d(delta^(5/4)).

   The signed measure nu has uniformly bounded absolute sixth moments
   for delta <= 1 as well, so its tail satisfies the same estimate.

4. Writing c=sqrt(2/pi), direct Gaussian integration gives

     E|G| H_4(G) = -c,       E|G| H_6(G) = 3c.

   Consequently int |x| dnu = c(1 - delta/24 + kappa_6(F)/240).
   Step 2 absorbs the sixth-cumulant term into the stated remainder.

All four steps are rigorous deductions from the cited theorems. Constants
have not been tracked numerically; no quantitative neighborhood is claimed.
