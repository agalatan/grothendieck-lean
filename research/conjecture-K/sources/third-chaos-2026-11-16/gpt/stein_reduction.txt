Sharp L1 comparison for cubic Wiener chaos: rigorous Stein reductions

All assertions below are rigorous except the explicitly identified missing sign
conditions. No proof of the sharp bound for a general cubic is claimed.

1. Exact Mills-ratio covariance identity

Let X be a real element of the d-th Gaussian Wiener chaos, d >= 1, with
E[X^2] = 1. Put

    c = sqrt(2/pi),        Gamma = ||DX||^2/d.

The Gaussian integration-by-parts identity gives

    E[X a(X)] = E[Gamma a'(X)],       E[Gamma] = 1.

For x >= 0 define the standard-normal Mills ratio and its auxiliary function

    r(x) = (1-Phi(x))/phi(x)
         = integral_0^infinity exp(-x t - t^2/2) dt,
    w(x) = 1-x r(x)
         = integral_0^infinity t exp(-x t - t^2/2) dt.

The last equality follows by integrating the derivative of
exp(-x t-t^2/2). In particular 0 < w(x) <= 1, and w is decreasing.

The bounded odd solution of the normal Stein equation

    a'(x)-x a(x) = |x|-c

is, for x >= 0,

    a(x) = -1+c r(x),

and is extended oddly to negative x. The identity c r(0)=1 makes a(0)=0.
Because r'(x)=x r(x)-1, its derivative is

    a'(x) = -c w(|x|).

Consequently integration by parts yields the exact identity

    E|X|-c
      = E[(1-Gamma)a'(X)]
      = c E[(Gamma-1) w(|X|)].                         (1)

Thus the desired bound is equivalent to the sign condition

    E[(Gamma-1) w(|X|)] <= 0.                          (2)

Condition (2) is a reformulation, not an established inequality for arbitrary
cubic chaos. The Malliavin identity supplies (1) but does not by itself supply
the sign. Replacing Gamma by the one-dimensional Stein kernel
tau_X(X)=E[Gamma | X] gives the same formula.

No pointwise expression p_X(0) E[Gamma | X=0] is needed or asserted here.
Such an expression can be ill-defined when the density is singular at zero,
as happens for products of independent Gaussian variables. Distributional
delta identities at zero must instead be interpreted using a mollifier or
the density of the Gamma-weighted pushforward measure. Formulas (1) and
(3)-(5) use bounded smooth kernels (or a bounded C^1 Stein solution), so
they avoid that issue entirely.

Normalization check: for X=G standard normal (d=1), Gamma=1 identically.
Both sides of (1) are zero.

2. Exact Gaussian-smoothing reduction

Let G be an independent standard Gaussian, s>0, and set

    W_s = X+sqrt(s)G,
    m(s) = E|W_s|,
    phi_s(x) = (2 pi s)^(-1/2) exp(-x^2/(2s)).

Gaussian smoothing gives

    m'(s) = E[phi_s(X)] = p_{W_s}(0).

Moreover, conditional expectation and Gaussian integration by parts give

    E[X sign(W_s)]
      = E[X (2 Phi(X/sqrt(s))-1)]
      = 2 E[Gamma phi_s(X)],

    E[sqrt(s)G sign(W_s)] = 2s E[phi_s(X)].

Adding these identities,

    m(s) = 2 E[(Gamma+s) phi_s(X)].                    (3)

Hence

    d/ds [m(s)/sqrt(1+s)]
      = E[(1-Gamma) phi_s(X)]/(1+s)^(3/2).             (4)

Thus the stronger sufficient condition for monotonicity under normalized
Gaussian smoothing is

    E[(Gamma-1) exp(-X^2/(2s))] <= 0  for every s>0.   (5)

Equivalently,

    2(1+s) p_{W_s}(0) >= E|W_s|.

If (5) held for every cubic X, then letting s increase to infinity in (4)
would prove E|X|<=c. Indeed m(s)/sqrt(1+s) tends to E|G|=c, while its limit
as s decreases to zero is E|X|. Condition (5) remains unproved here.

Gaussian check: Gamma=1 makes the derivative in (4) exactly zero, as required.

3. A tempting stronger argument fails already for He_3

It is not legitimate to assume that tau_X(x) is increasing with |x|.
Take

    X=H(G)=(G^3-3G)/sqrt(6),
    Gamma=(G^2-1)^2/2.

For a regular value x with real preimages u satisfying H(u)=x, the
change-of-variables formula gives

    p_X(x) = sum_u phi(u)/|H'(u)|,
    tau_X(x) = [sum_u phi(u)|H'(u)|/3]
               /[sum_u phi(u)/|H'(u)|].               (6)

At x=0 the three preimages are 0 and +/-sqrt(3), and (6) gives

    tau_X(0) = (1+4 exp(-3/2))/(2(1+exp(-3/2))) > 0.

Now put x_c=2/sqrt(6)=sqrt(2/3). At x=x_c the equation H(u)=x has a
double root u=-1 and a simple root u=2, because

    u^3-3u-2=(u+1)^2(u-2).

As x increases to x_c from below, two preimages approach u=-1. Their
contributions to the denominator in (6) diverge like (x_c-x)^(-1/2),
whereas their numerator contributions tend to zero like (x_c-x)^(1/2).
The remaining preimage approaches 2 and contributes a finite quantity to
both numerator and denominator. Therefore

    lim_{x -> x_c from below} tau_X(x) = 0.

This disproves monotonicity of tau_X(x) in |x|. The other simple branch is
included in this argument and does not prevent the zero limit, because the
denominator diverges. This does not refute either (2) or (5).

For reference, direct one-dimensional integration gives

    E|He_3(G)/sqrt(6)|
      = c (1+4 exp(-3/2))/sqrt(6) < c.

4. A broad cubic class with an elementary proof and exact deficit

Let (G_1,...,G_m) be a standard Gaussian vector independent of another Gaussian
vector Y. Let Q_1(Y),...,Q_m(Y) be real pure quadratic-chaos polynomials, and
set

    X = sum_i G_i Q_i(Y),        V(Y)=sum_i Q_i(Y)^2.

Then X belongs to the third Wiener chaos. Conditional on Y it is centered
Gaussian with variance V(Y), so

    E|X| = c E[sqrt(V)],         E[X^2]=E[V].

For E[X^2]=1, Cauchy-Schwarz immediately yields E|X|<=c. In fact there is
the exact nonnegative deficit formula

    c-E|X| = (c/2) E[(sqrt(V)-1)^2].                 (7)

This includes every decoupled trilinear Gaussian form

    sum_{i,j,k} a_{ijk} G_i Y_j Z_k

with the three Gaussian blocks independent; Q_i(Y,Z)=sum_{j,k}a_{ijk}Y_jZ_k
belongs to the second chaos. It also includes sums of independent such forms.

For a finite-dimensional, nonzero example of this type the inequality is
strict. Equality in (7) would force sum_i Q_i(y)^2=1 almost everywhere,
hence everywhere by the polynomial identity theorem. Each real polynomial
Q_i would then be bounded on all Euclidean space and therefore constant.
Being in pure second chaos, each would be zero, a contradiction.

The constant remains sharp within this subclass: use independent blocks and

    X_N = (1/sqrt(N)) sum_{i=1}^N G_i (Y_i^2-1)/sqrt(2).

Conditional variance V_N=N^(-1)sum_i (Y_i^2-1)^2/2 converges to 1; formula
(7), or the law of large numbers with uniform integrability, gives
E|X_N| -> c.

Checks: a single product G_1 G_2 G_3 has E|X|=c^3 and variance one, so it
satisfies the bound strictly. The analogous conditional-Gaussian proof also
applies to quadratic products G_1 G_2, with E|X|=c^2. It is not a proof for
all quadratic forms, nor for general cubic tensors with self-interactions.
