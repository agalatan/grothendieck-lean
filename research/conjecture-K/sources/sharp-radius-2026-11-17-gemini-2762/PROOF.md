## 0. Main results and overview

Let $z = (z_1, \dots, z_n)$ be independent standard complex Gaussians ($\mathbb{E}|z_j|^2 = 1$, $\mathbb{E}z_j^2 = 0$), and let $H_{p,q}(\mathbb{C}^n)$ denote the complex Wick chaos of bidegree $(p, q)$. On $H_{2,1}(\mathbb{C}^n)$ and on $H_{3,2}(\mathbb{C}^n)$, normalised sums of $N$ independent copies tend in law and in $L^2$ to a standard circular complex Gaussian $W$ ($\mathbb{E}|W|^2 = 1$, $\mathbb{E}|W| = \sqrt{\pi}/2 = 0.8862269254\dots$), asking for the **sharp Gaussian radius** $\mathbb{E}|q| \le (\sqrt{\pi}/2)\|q\|_2$.

This note proves and machine-verifies three sets of results addressing Tasks 1–3 of [#523](https://github.com/agalatan/grothendieck-lean/issues/523):

### Theorem 3 — The complex quintic chaos $H_{3,2}(\mathbb{C}^n)$ (**P + C, author only**; numerical survey **N**)

1. **Asymptotic lower bound** (**P**). *Normalised diffuse sums $Q_N = N^{-1/2}\sum_{j=1}^N q_1(z_j) \in H_{3,2}(\mathbb{C}^N)$ ($q_1(z) = (z^3\bar z^2 - 6z^2\bar z + 6z)/\sqrt{12}$, $\chi(Q_N) = 1840/N$) and multilinear sums $M_K = K^{-1/2}\sum_{r=1}^K z_{5r-4}z_{5r-3}z_{5r-2}\bar z_{5r-1}\bar z_{5r} \in H_{3,2}(\mathbb{C}^{5K})$ ($\chi(M_K) = 30/K$) satisfy $\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}|Q_N| = \lim_{K\to\infty}\mathbb{E}|M_K| = \sqrt{\pi}/2$, so $\sup_{n \ge 1,\, 0 \ne q \in H_{3,2}(\mathbb{C}^n)} \mathbb{E}|q|/\|q\|_2 \ge \sqrt{\pi}/2$.*
2. **Exact sharp bound on hyperplane-vanishing and conditionally Gaussian quintics** (**P + C, author only**). *Let $q \in H_{3,2}(\mathbb{C}^n)$ vanish on a complex hyperplane $\{\langle e, z\rangle = 0\}$ ($e \in \mathbb{C}^n$, $\|e\|_2 = 1$), or be conditionally proper Gaussian given a sub-$\sigma$-field. Writing $X = \langle e, z\rangle$ and $Y$ for the orthogonal coordinates, $q$ decomposes orthogonally into nine radial-Laguerre components $(a, b) \in \{0,1,2,3\} \times \{0,1,2\}$ ($a + b \ge 1$, $a \ne b$) whose exact conditional radial weights have rational constants $\tilde\gamma_{a,b}$ in*
   $$\left\{1,\, 1,\, \frac{7}{8},\, \frac{7}{8},\, \frac{51}{64},\, \frac{3}{4},\, \frac{3}{4},\, \frac{11}{16},\, \frac{5}{8}\right\} \subset (0, 1].$$
   *Consequently,*
   $$\mathbb{E}|q| \le \frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\,\sqrt{\sum_{(a,b)} \tilde\gamma_{a,b}\,\|q_{a,b}\|_2^2} \le \frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\,\|q\|_2,$$
   *with strict inequality unless $q$ lies purely in the conditionally Gaussian components $(1, 0) \oplus (0, 1)$. In particular, $\mathbb{E}|q| < (\sqrt{\pi}/2)\|q\|_2$ holds on all of $H_{3,2}(\mathbb{C}^1)$ ($\mathbb{E}|q_1| = 0.60332138\dots$, deficit $0.28290554\dots$) and for every single Wick monomial $:z^\alpha \bar z^\beta:$ ($|\alpha| = 3, |\beta| = 2$) in every dimension.*
3. **Numerical survey on $H_{3,2}(\mathbb{C}^d)$, $d = 1 \dots 6$** (**N**, [`search_quintic_h32.py`](../../code/sharp-radius-gemini-2762/search_quintic_h32.py)). *Multi-start L-BFGS-B optimization over general tensors in $H_{3,2}(\mathbb{C}^d)$ ($\dim_{\mathbb{C}} = \binom{d+2}{3}\binom{d+1}{2} = 1, 12, 60, 200, 525, 1176$), re-scored on independent $4\times 10^5$-draw samples, finds out-of-sample maxima $0.6031, 0.6573, 0.6838, 0.6855, 0.6878, 0.6919$ ($\pm 0.0012$), all strictly below $\sqrt{\pi}/2 = 0.886227$.*

Together with the closed-form strict polynomial majorant $P(x) = \frac{1+x^2}{2} - \varepsilon(x^2-1)^2 + \eta x^6 > x$ on $[0, \infty)$ at $B = 11{,}000$ ($\varepsilon = \frac{1}{2(B+1)^2}$, $\eta = \frac{\varepsilon(B^2-1)^2}{B^6}$; dual-verified by Sturm and Bernstein in [`check_identities_exact.py`](../../code/sharp-radius-gemini-2762/check_identities_exact.py) Part B), this proves unconditionally:*

## 1. Explicit near-Gaussian cone (`STATUS.md` `3.18` and `3.21 (e1)`)

### 1.1 Two-step Stein cumulant expansion and $P_\varepsilon$ smoothing for $f(x) = |x|$ on $\mathcal{H}_3(\mathbb{R}^N)$

Let $F \in \mathcal{H}_3(\mathbb{R}^N)$ have unit variance $\mathbb{E}[F^2] = 1$, fourth cumulant $\kappa_4(F) = \mathbb{E}[F^4] - 3 > 0$, and sixth cumulant $\kappa_6(F) = \mathbb{E}[F^6] - 15\mathbb{E}[F^4] + 30$. By the reviewed universal sixth-cumulant bound on $\mathcal{H}_3$ ([`STATUS.md`](../../STATUS.md) `3.21 (e4)`, certified in `third-chaos-dimfree-gemini-c1de/certify_sixth.py`, **P + C**), $|\kappa_6(F)| \le 67\,\kappa_4(F)^{3/2}$.

Follow the exact operator conventions of `edgeworth-codex-a7c4/PROOF.md` §2 and [`KCR`](../cumulant-remainder-codex-a7c4/PROOF.md) §3 (**P**). Write $N \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$, $c := \mathbb{E}|N| = \sqrt{2/\pi}$, $\mathcal{L} = \frac{d^2}{dx^2} - x\frac{d}{dx}$ for the 1D Ornstein–Uhlenbeck generator, $P_t = e^{t\mathcal{L}}$ for its semigroup, and $R_\lambda = (\mathcal{L} - \lambda)^{-1} = -\int_0^\infty e^{-\lambda t}P_t\,dt$ for $\lambda > -1$ on centered functions. Put $U := \|DF\|^2 - 3 = U_2 + U_4$ for the centered Malliavin variance decomposed into its second- and fourth-chaos projections. For any even smooth function $h \in C^\infty(\mathbb{R})$ of at most linear growth with bounded derivatives of orders $\ge 1$, let $\phi_h$ solve the normal Stein equation
$$\phi_h'(x) - x\phi_h(x) = h(x) - \mathbb{E}h(N), \qquad \phi_h(x) = e^{x^2/2}\int_x^\infty \big(\mathbb{E}h(N) - h(t)\big)e^{-t^2/2}\,dt$$
($\phi_h$ odd, $\phi_h'$ even), subtract its Gaussian Hermite modes of orders $0, 2, 4$ to form $\psi_h := \phi_h' - \sum_{j\in\{0,2,4\}}\mathbb{E}[\phi_h'(N)\operatorname{He}_j(N)]\frac{\operatorname{He}_j}{j!}$ (Hermite rank $\ge 6$), and put $v_h := \psi_h^{(4)} = \phi_h^{(5)} - \mathbb{E}[\phi_h^{(5)}(N)]$ (centered, Hermite rank $\ge 2$). Then `edgeworth-codex-a7c4/PROOF.md` eqs. (6) & (9) and [`KCR`](../cumulant-remainder-codex-a7c4/PROOF.md) §3 (l. 150) give
$$\mathbb{E}h(F) - \mathbb{E}h(N) - \mathbb{E}[\operatorname{He}_4(N)h(N)]\,\frac{\kappa_4(F)}{24} - \mathbb{E}[\operatorname{He}_6(N)h(N)]\,\frac{\kappa_6(F)}{720} = R_h(F),$$
$$R_h(F) = -\frac{1}{27}\sum_{k \in \{2,4\}}\sum_{l \in \{0,2,4,6,8\}} \mathbb{E}\big[(R_{2 - l/3}R_{4 - k/3}v_h)(F)\,U\,\Pi_l(U U_k)\big].$$

To pass this identity rigorously to $f(x) = |x|$ (for which $\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{He}_4(N)|N|] = -c$ and $\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{He}_6(N)|N|] = 3c$), set $h_\varepsilon := P_\varepsilon f \in C^\infty(\mathbb{R})$ for $\varepsilon > 0$ (as in `edgeworth-codex-a7c4/PROOF.md` l. 166). By Mehler's formula $h_\varepsilon(x) = \mathbb{E}|e^{-\varepsilon}x + \sqrt{1 - e^{-2\varepsilon}}\,N|$, $h_\varepsilon$ is even, smooth with bounded derivatives of every order $\ge 1$, satisfies $|h_\varepsilon(x)| \le |x| + c$ uniformly in $\varepsilon > 0$ and $h_\varepsilon(x) \to |x|$ pointwise, and has $\mathbb{E}h_\varepsilon(N) = c$ and $\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{He}_{2m}(N)h_\varepsilon(N)] = e^{-2m\varepsilon}\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{He}_{2m}(N)|N|]$ ($2m = 4, 6$). Moreover, because $(\frac{d}{dx} - x)(e^{-\varepsilon}P_\varepsilon\phi) = P_\varepsilon(\phi' - x\phi)$, $\frac{d}{dx}P_\varepsilon = e^{-\varepsilon}P_\varepsilon\frac{d}{dx}$, and $P_\varepsilon$ commutes with $R_\lambda$, one has $\phi_{h_\varepsilon}' = e^{-2\varepsilon}P_\varepsilon(\phi_f')$, $v_{h_\varepsilon} = e^{-6\varepsilon}P_\varepsilon(v_f)$ ($= v_{f^\varepsilon}$ with $f^\varepsilon = e^{-6\varepsilon}P_\varepsilon f$ on modes $\ge 6$), and
$$(R_{2 - l/3}R_{4 - k/3}v_{h_\varepsilon})(x) = e^{-6\varepsilon}\,(P_\varepsilon g_{k,l})(x),$$
where $g_{k,l}$ is the continuous function of Lemma 1.1 below. By Lemma 1.1 and Lemma 1.2, $g_{k,l}$ is continuous on $\mathbb{R}$ and satisfies $|g_{k,l}(y)| \le A_{k,l} + B_{k,l}|y|$ for all $y \in \mathbb{R}$. Hence Mehler's formula gives the **$\varepsilon$-uniform linear envelope**
$$\big|e^{-6\varepsilon}(P_\varepsilon g_{k,l})(x)\big| \le \mathbb{E}\big[A_{k,l} + B_{k,l}|e^{-\varepsilon}x + \sqrt{1 - e^{-2\varepsilon}}\,N|\big] \le A_{k,l} + B_{k,l}(|x| + c) \qquad \forall\, \varepsilon > 0,\; \forall\, x \in \mathbb{R},$$
and $e^{-6\varepsilon}(P_\varepsilon g_{k,l})(x) \to g_{k,l}(x)$ for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$ as $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$. Since $F$ and $U\Pi_l(U U_k)$ are finite-degree Gaussian polynomials (having finite moments of all orders), dominated convergence as $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ yields
$$\mathbb{E}|F| = c\left(1 - \frac{\kappa_4(F)}{24} + \frac{\kappa_6(F)}{240}\right) + R_{|x|}(F), \qquad R_{|x|}(F) = -\frac{1}{27}\sum_{k \in \{2,4\}}\sum_{l \in \{0,2,4,6,8\}} \mathbb{E}\big[g_{k,l}(F)\,U\,\Pi_l(U U_k)\big].$$

### 1.2 Exact Gaussian scale-mixture collapse of $g_{k,l}(x)$

For $f(x) = |x|$, $\phi_{|x|}(x) = e^{x^2/2}\int_x^\infty(c - |t|)e^{-t^2/2}\,dt$ is odd, and its derivative $\phi_{|x|}'(x) = x\phi_{|x|}(x) + |x| - c$ is the even Gaussian scale mixture
$$\phi_{|x|}'(x) = -\frac{c}{2}\int_0^1 (1 - w)^{-1/2}\exp\!\left(-\frac{w x^2}{2(1 - w)}\right)dw.$$
*(Proof of mixture identity: both sides equal $-c$ at $x = 0$ and are even; for $x > 0$, substituting $w = 1 - s^2$ and then $t = x/s$ gives $-c\int_0^1 \exp(-\frac{(1-s^2)x^2}{2s^2})\,ds = -c x e^{x^2/2}\int_x^\infty t^{-2}e^{-t^2/2}\,dt = -c + c x e^{x^2/2}\int_x^\infty e^{-t^2/2}\,dt = x\phi_{|x|}(x) + x - c$ by integration by parts on $t^{-2}$.)*

**Lemma 1.1** (**P + C, author only**). *For every $k \in \{2, 4\}$, $l \in \{0, 2, 4, 6, 8\}$, and $x \in \mathbb{R}$, $(R_{2 - l/3}R_{4 - k/3}v_{|x|})(x)$ equals*
$$g_{k,l}(x) = -\frac{c}{2}\int_0^1 W_{k/3,\,l/3}(\theta)\,\theta^2\left[(1 - \theta)^{-5/2} h_4\!\left(\frac{\theta x^2}{1 - \theta}\right) - 3\right]d\theta,$$
*where $h_4(s) := (s^2 - 6s + 3)e^{-s/2}$ and, for $\alpha = k/3$ and $\beta = l/3$ ($d = \beta + 2 - \alpha > 0$),*
$$W_{\alpha,\beta}(\theta) := \frac{1}{d}\left(\frac{\theta^{-(\beta+4)/2} - 1}{\beta + 4} - \frac{\theta^{-(\alpha+2)/2} - 1}{\alpha + 2}\right) > 0$$
*is strictly positive and strictly decreasing on $\theta \in (0, 1)$, satisfying $W_{\alpha,\beta}(\theta) \le \frac{\theta^{-(\beta+4)/2}}{d(\beta + 4)}$ on $(0, 1)$ and $W_{\alpha,\beta}(1 - t) \le \frac{t^2}{8}(1 - t)^{-(\beta+6)/2}$ for $t = 1 - \theta \in (0, 1)$.*

*Proof.* Put $r = \frac{w}{1 - w}$ and $K_w(x) = e^{-r x^2/2}$. Subtracting Gaussian Hermite modes $0, 2, 4$ and taking $\frac{d^4}{dx^4}$ annihilates modes $0, 2$ and centers the fourth derivative ($K_w^{(4)}(x) = r^2 h_4(r x^2)$, $\mathbb{E}_N[K_w^{(4)}(N)] = 3r^2(1 - w)^{5/2}$), so $v_{|x|}(x) = -\frac{c}{2}\int_0^1 (1 - w)^{-1/2}r^2\big[h_4(r x^2) - 3(1 - w)^{5/2}\big]dw$ (or $e^{-6\varepsilon}P_\varepsilon$ thereof before $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$). For $T = t_1 + t_2 > 0$, $P_T(e^{-r x^2/2}) = (1 - w)^{1/2}(1 - \theta)^{-1/2}\exp(-\frac{\theta x^2}{2(1-\theta)})$ with $\theta := w e^{-2T}$, and $P_T\frac{d^4}{dx^4} = e^{4T}\frac{d^4}{dx^4}P_T$ gives $(1 - w)^{-1/2}P_T\big(r^2[h_4(r x^2) - 3(1 - w)^{5/2}]\big) = w^2\big[(1 - \theta)^{-5/2}h_4(\frac{\theta x^2}{1-\theta}) - 3\big]$. Inside $R_{2-\beta}R_{4-\alpha} = \int_0^\infty\int_0^\infty e^{-(2-\beta)t_2 - (4-\alpha)t_1}P_{t_1+t_2}\,dt_1\,dt_2$, integrating $t_2 \in [0, T]$ at $T = t_1 + t_2$ yields $\frac{e^{-(2-\beta)T} - e^{-(4-\alpha)T}}{d}\,dT$. Changing variables from $(w, T) \in (0, 1)\times(0, \infty)$ to $(\theta, u) = (w e^{-2T},\, e^{-2T})$ ($\theta \in (0, 1)$, $u \in (\theta, 1)$, $w = \theta/u$, $dw\,dT = \frac{d\theta\,du}{2u^2}$) turns $w^2\frac{u^{(2-\beta)/2}(1 - u^{d/2})}{d}\,dw\,dT$ into $\theta^2 W_{\alpha,\beta}(\theta)\,d\theta$, where
$$W_{\alpha,\beta}(\theta) = \frac{1}{2d}\int_\theta^1 u^{-(\beta+6)/2}\big(1 - u^{d/2}\big)\,du = \frac{1}{d}\left(\frac{\theta^{-(\beta+4)/2} - 1}{\beta + 4} - \frac{\theta^{-(\alpha+2)/2} - 1}{\alpha + 2}\right).$$
Since $W_{\alpha,\beta}'(\theta) = -\frac{1}{2d}\theta^{-(\beta+6)/2}(1 - \theta^{d/2}) < 0$ on $(0, 1)$ and $W_{\alpha,\beta}(1) = 0$, $W_{\alpha,\beta}$ is positive and strictly decreasing. Dropping $-\frac{\theta^{-(\alpha+2)/2}-1}{\alpha+2} - \frac{1}{\beta+4} < 0$ gives the left bound. For the right bound ($t = 1 - \theta \in (0, 1)$), since $d/2 + 1 > 1$, Bernoulli's inequality gives $(1 - u)^{d/2 + 1} \ge 1 - (\frac{d}{2} + 1)u$, i.e. $1 - (1 - u)^{d/2} \le \frac{d}{2}\frac{u}{1 - u}$ for all $u \in (0, 1)$. Setting $m := \frac{\beta + 6}{2} \ge 3$, this yields $-W_{\alpha,\beta}'(1 - u) \le \frac{1}{4}u(1 - u)^{-m-1}$; finally, $F(t) := \frac{t^2}{8}(1 - t)^{-m} - \frac{1}{4}\int_0^t u(1 - u)^{-m-1}\,du$ satisfies $F(0) = 0$ and
$$F'(t) = \frac{t}{4}(1 - t)^{-m-1}\Big[(1 - t) + \tfrac{m}{2}t - 1\Big] = \frac{t^2}{4}\left(\frac{m}{2} - 1\right)(1 - t)^{-m-1} > 0 \qquad (t \in (0, 1), \; m \ge 3 > 2),$$
proving $W_{\alpha,\beta}(1 - t) = \int_0^t (-W_{\alpha,\beta}'(1 - u))\,du \le \frac{t^2}{8}(1 - t)^{-(\beta+6)/2}$ for every $(k, l)$. $\square$

### 1.3 Elementary SOS Hermite–Gaussian envelopes and exact certification

**Lemma 1.2** (**P + C, author only**). *For all $s \ge 0$, $|h_4(s)| \le 3$, $|h_4'(s)| \le \frac{15}{2}$, and $|h_4(s) - 3| \le 3\sqrt{5}\sqrt{s}$.*

*Proof.* Using $e^{s/2} \ge \sum_{j=0}^m \frac{(s/2)^j}{j!}$ on $s \ge 0$:

## 4. The complex quintic chaos $H_{3,2}(\mathbb{C}^n)$

Because the Lean development covers degree 3 only, every identity and moment/diagram bound used on $H_{3,2}(\mathbb{C}^n)$ is proved from first principles below and machine-checked in [`check_identities_exact.py`](../../code/sharp-radius-gemini-2762/check_identities_exact.py) Part B.

### 4.1 Wick normalisation and diffuse lower bound

Every $q \in H_{3,2}(\mathbb{C}^n)$ has the unique representation $q(z) = \sum_{a,b,c,d,e=1}^n T_{abc;de}\,:z_a z_b z_c\bar z_d\bar z_e:$ with $T_{abc;de}$ symmetric in $(a, b, c)$ and symmetric in $(d, e)$, where
$$:z_{i_1} z_{i_2} z_{i_3}\bar z_{j_1}\bar z_{j_2}: \,=\, z_{i_1} z_{i_2} z_{i_3}\bar z_{j_1}\bar z_{j_2} - \sum_{\substack{h \in \{1,2,3\} \\ m \in \{1,2\}}} \delta_{i_h,j_m}\Big(\prod_{h'\ne h} z_{i_{h'}}\Big)\Big(\prod_{m'\ne m}\bar z_{j_{m'}}\Big) + \sum_{\substack{h_1 \ne h_2 \in \{1,2,3\}}} \delta_{i_{h_1},j_1}\delta_{i_{h_2},j_2}\,z_{i_{h_3}},$$
and $u = \mathbb{E}|q|^2 = 3!2!\|T\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2 = 12\sum_{a,b,c,d,e}|T_{abc;de}|^2$. For $n = 1$, $H_{3,2}(\mathbb{C}^1)$ is spanned by the unit-variance Laguerre quintic
$$q_1(z) = \frac{z^3\bar z^2 - 6z^2\bar z + 6z}{\sqrt{12}} = \frac{z(|z|^4 - 6|z|^2 + 6)}{\sqrt{12}}, \qquad \mathbb{E}|q_1|^2 = 1,\quad \mathbb{E}|q_1|^4 = 1842,\quad \mathbb{E}|q_1|^6 = 59{,}735{,}934.$$
For i.i.d. coordinates, $Q_N = N^{-1/2}\sum_{j=1}^N q_1(z_j) \in H_{3,2}(\mathbb{C}^N)$ ($\chi = 1840/N$) and $M_K = K^{-1/2}\sum_{r=1}^K z_{5r-4}z_{5r-3}z_{5r-2}\bar z_{5r-1}\bar z_{5r} \in H_{3,2}(\mathbb{C}^{5K})$ ($\chi = 30/K$) have unit second moment and converge in law to $W \sim \mathcal{CN}(0, 1)$ by the Central Limit Theorem; uniform integrability ($\mathbb{E}|Q_N|^2 = \mathbb{E}|M_K|^2 = 1$) gives $\mathbb{E}|Q_N| \to \sqrt{\pi}/2$ and $\mathbb{E}|M_K| \to \sqrt{\pi}/2$, proving $\rho^*(H_{3,2}) \ge \sqrt{\pi}/2$.

### 4.2 Hyperplane-vanishing and conditionally Gaussian quintics

**Theorem 4.1 (Complex Hyperplane Theorem on $H_{3,2}(\mathbb{C}^n)$)** (**P + C, author only**). *Let $q \in H_{3,2}(\mathbb{C}^n)$ vanish identically on $\{X = 0\}$, where $X = \langle e, z\rangle$ ($\|e\|_2 = 1$) and $Y \in \mathbb{C}^{n-1}$ completes a unitary basis. Then $q$ decomposes orthogonally into nine $(X, Y)$ bidegree shells:*
$$q(X, Y) = \sum_{\substack{0 \le a \le 3,\, 0 \le b \le 2 \\ a + b \ge 1,\, a \ne b}} \psi_{a,b}(X)\,F_{3-a,\,2-b}(Y), \qquad F_{3-a,\,2-b} \in H_{3-a,\,2-b}(\mathbb{C}^{n-1}),$$
*where $\psi_{a,b}(X) := \frac{1}{\sqrt{a!\,b!}}\,:X^a\bar X^b:$ ($m = \min(a,b)$, $k = |a - b| \ge 1$) has unit norm $\mathbb{E}|\psi_{a,b}(X)|^2 = 1$ and radial modulus squared $|\psi_{a,b}(X)|^2 = P_{a,b}(R) := \frac{m!}{(m+k)!}R^k\big(L_m^{(k)}(R)\big)^2$ ($R = |X|^2 \sim \operatorname{Exp}(1)$). Putting $u_{a,b} = \|F_{3-a,2-b}\|_2^2$ ($u = \|q\|_2^2 = \sum_{(a,b)} u_{a,b}$) and $\tilde\gamma_{a,b} := \frac{\mathbb{E}_R[R^{-1/2}P_{a,b}(R)]}{\sqrt{\pi}/2}$, one has*
$$\tilde\gamma_{1,0} = \tilde\gamma_{0,1} = 1, \quad \tilde\gamma_{2,1} = \tilde\gamma_{1,2} = \frac{7}{8}, \quad \tilde\gamma_{3,2} = \frac{51}{64}, \quad \tilde\gamma_{2,0} = \tilde\gamma_{0,2} = \frac{3}{4}, \quad \tilde\gamma_{3,1} = \frac{11}{16}, \quad \tilde\gamma_{3,0} = \frac{5}{8},$$
*and consequently, for every such $q$ ($q \ne 0$),*
$$\mathbb{E}|q| \le \frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\sqrt{\sum_{(a,b)} \tilde\gamma_{a,b}\,u_{a,b}} \le \frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\sqrt{u}\left(1 - \frac{u_{2,1} + u_{1,2}}{16u} - \frac{13 u_{3,2}}{128u} - \frac{u_{2,0} + u_{0,2}}{8u} - \frac{5 u_{3,1}}{32u} - \frac{3 u_{3,0}}{16u}\right) \le \frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\|q\|_2,$$
*with strict inequality unless $q$ lies purely in the conditionally Gaussian shells $(1, 0) \oplus (0, 1)$.*

*Proof.* Vanishing on $\{X = 0\}$ excludes the diagonal Laguerre shells $a = b$ (since $:X^m\bar X^m:\big|_{X=0} = (-1)^m m! \ne 0$, whereas every $a \ne b$ contains $X^k$ or $\bar X^k$ with $k \ge 1$). For $a > b$ ($m = b, k = a - b$), $:X^a\bar X^b: = (-1)^m m!\,X^k L_m^{(k)}(R)$ (and its conjugate for $b > a$), so $|:X^a\bar X^b:|^2 = (m!)^2 R^k(L_m^{(k)}(R))^2$; since $a!\,b! = m!(m+k)!$, dividing by $\sqrt{a!\,b!}$ gives $|\psi_{a,b}(X)|^2 = \frac{m!}{(m+k)!}R^k(L_m^{(k)}(R))^2 = P_{a,b}(R)$ with $\mathbb{E}|\psi_{a,b}(X)|^2 = 1$ (checked for all nine shells in `check_identities_exact.py` Part B). For $(a, b) \ne (a', b')$, $F_{3-a, 2-b}(Y)$ and $F_{3-a', 2-b'}(Y)$ have distinct bidegrees, hence are orthogonal in $L^2(\gamma_{\mathbb{C}^{n-1}})$. Conditioning on $X$ gives $V_X(R) := \mathbb{E}_Y[|q(X, Y)|^2 \mid X] = \sum_{(a,b)} P_{a,b}(R)\,u_{a,b}$ and $\mathbb{E}_Y[|q(X, Y)| \mid X] \le \sqrt{V_X(R)}$. Using $\mathbb{E}[R^{n - 1/2}] = \frac{(2n)!}{4^n n!}\sqrt{\pi}$, `check_identities_exact.py` evaluates $\tilde\gamma_{a,b}$ in exact `Fraction` arithmetic, obtaining the nine positive rationals in $[\frac{5}{8}, 1]$ above. For $q \ne 0$, set $\lambda := \sum_{(a,b)} \tilde\gamma_{a,b}u_{a,b} \ge \frac{5}{8}u > 0$. Applying the concave tangent inequality $\sqrt{v} \le \sqrt{\lambda R} + \frac{v - \lambda R}{2\sqrt{\lambda R}}$ ($R > 0$) to $v = V_X(R)$ and integrating over $R \sim \operatorname{Exp}(1)$ ($\mathbb{E}[\sqrt{R}] = \frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}$ and $\mathbb{E}[R^{-1/2}V_X(R)] = \frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\sum_{(a,b)}\tilde\gamma_{a,b}u_{a,b} = \frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\lambda$) makes the second term vanish identically, proving
$$\mathbb{E}|q| \le \mathbb{E}_R\!\left[\sqrt{\lambda R} + \frac{V_X(R) - \lambda R}{2\sqrt{\lambda R}}\right] = \frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\sqrt{\lambda} = \frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\sqrt{\sum_{(a,b)} \tilde\gamma_{a,b}\,u_{a,b}}.$$
Writing $\lambda = u\big(1 - \sum_{(a,b)}(1 - \tilde\gamma_{a,b})\frac{u_{a,b}}{u}\big)$ and using $\sqrt{1 - t} \le 1 - \frac{t}{2}$ for $t \in [0, 1]$ yields the linearised deficit display. Likewise, for any conditionally proper Gaussian quintic $q$ given $\mathcal{F}$ ($\mathbb{E}[|q|^2 \mid \mathcal{F}] = \sigma_{\mathcal{F}}^2$), Jensen gives $\mathbb{E}|q| = \frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\mathbb{E}[\sigma_{\mathcal{F}}] \le \frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\|q\|_2$. Since $\sum \alpha_j = 3 \ne 2 = \sum \beta_j$, every single Wick monomial $:z^\alpha\bar z^\beta:$ in $H_{3,2}(\mathbb{C}^n)$ has at least one coordinate with $\alpha_j \ne \beta_j$, hence vanishes on $\{z_j = 0\}$ and satisfies $\mathbb{E}|q| \le (\sqrt{\pi}/2)\|q\|_2$. $\square$

### 4.3 Antiholomorphic Stein identities, variance budgets, and universal sixth-moment bound on $H_{3,2}(\mathbb{C}^n)$

**Proposition 4.2 (Stein identities `Form I` & `Form II` on $H_{3,2}(\mathbb{C}^n)$)** (**P + C, author only**). *Let $q \in H_{3,2}(\mathbb{C}^n)$. For every $H \in C^1(\mathbb{C}^n)$ with $H, \nabla H$ of polynomial growth,*
$$\mathbb{E}[H q] = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^n \mathbb{E}\big[(\partial_k H)\,\bar\partial_k q\big] = \frac{1}{3}\sum_{j=1}^n \mathbb{E}\big[(\bar\partial_j H)\,\partial_j q\big].$$
*In particular, with $V = |q|^2$, $c_1 = \sum_j|\partial_j q|^2$, $c_2 = \sum_k|\bar\partial_k q|^2$, $a = \sum_j(\partial_j q)(\bar\partial_j q)$, and $\rho = \operatorname{Re}(\bar q^2 a)/V$ ($0$ on $\{V = 0\}$), for any $g_i = G_i/v \in C^1([0, \infty))$ ($G_i' = \phi_i$, $Y_i = \phi_i - g_i$):*
$$\mathbb{E}\Big[G_1(V) - \tfrac{1}{2}c_2\phi_1(V) - \tfrac{1}{2}\rho Y_1(V)\Big] = 0, \qquad \mathbb{E}\Big[G_2(V) - \tfrac{1}{3}c_1\phi_2(V) - \tfrac{1}{3}\rho Y_2(V)\Big] = 0,$$
*giving $\mathbb{E}[c_2] = 2u$ and $\mathbb{E}[c_1] = 3u$ at $g_i \equiv 1$.*

*Proof.* On $L^2(\gamma_{\mathbb{C}^n})$ ($\langle f, g\rangle = \mathbb{E}[\bar f g]$), the holomorphic and antiholomorphic number operators $N_1 = \sum_j \partial_j^*\partial_j$ and $N_2 = \sum_k \bar\partial_k^*\bar\partial_k$ act on $H_{p,p'}$ as $p\,\mathrm{Id}$ and $p'\,\mathrm{Id}$. Since $q \in H_{3,2}$, $\sum_k\mathbb{E}[(\partial_k H)\bar\partial_k q] = \sum_k\langle \bar\partial_k\bar H, \bar\partial_k q\rangle = \langle \bar H, N_2 q\rangle = 2\langle \bar H, q\rangle = 2\mathbb{E}[H q]$, and $\sum_j\mathbb{E}[(\bar\partial_j H)\partial_j q] = \sum_j\langle \partial_j\bar H, \partial_j q\rangle = \langle \bar H, N_1 q\rangle = 3\mathbb{E}[H q]$. Setting $H = \bar q g_i(V)$ and taking real parts yields `Form I` and `Form II`. $\square$

**Theorem 4.3 (Fourth-moment cap, kernel variance budgets, and universal sixth-moment bound on $H_{3,2}(\mathbb{C}^n)$)** (**P + C, author only**). *For every $n \ge 1$ and every $q = I_{3,2}(T) \in H_{3,2}(\mathbb{C}^n)$ with $u = \mathbb{E}|q|^2 = 12\|T\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2$ and $\delta = \mathbb{E}|q|^4 - 2u^2 = \chi(q)u^2$:*
1. **$0 \le \chi(q) \le 1840$**: *Writing $V = \sum_{p=0}^5 V_{p,p}$ ($V_{p,p} \in H_{p,p}$), triangle inequality, orthogonal projection $\|\operatorname{Sym}_{p,p} A\|_{\mathrm{HS}} \le \|A\|_{\mathrm{HS}}$, and Cauchy–Schwarz $\|T \otimes_{r_1,r_2}\bar T\|_{\mathrm{HS}} \le \|T\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2 = u/12$ imply $\|V_{p,p}(q)\|_2 \le \|V_{p,p}(q_1)\|_2$ for each $p = 0 \dots 5$, so $\mathbb{E}|q|^4 = \sum_{p=0}^5\|V_{p,p}\|_2^2 \le (1 + 25 + 256 + 784 + 676 + 100)u^2 = 1842\,u^2$ ($\chi \le 1840$, attained at $q_1$). Meanwhile, for $F_\theta := \sqrt{2}\operatorname{Re}(e^{-i\theta}q) \in \mathcal{H}_5(\mathbb{R}^{2n})$, $F_\theta \stackrel{d}{=} F_0$ has $\mathbb{E}[F_\theta^2] = u$ and $F_\theta^2 = u + \sum_{k=1}^5 J_{2k}(F_\theta)$, so Gaussian integration by parts $\mathbb{E}[F_\theta^4] = \frac{3}{5}\mathbb{E}[F_\theta^2\Gamma(F_\theta, F_\theta)]$ gives*
   $$\frac{3}{2}\delta = \kappa_4(F_\theta) = \frac{3}{5}\sum_{k=1}^4 (5 - k)\,\|J_{2k}(F_\theta)\|_2^2 \ge 0.$$
2. **$0 \le \mathbb{E}[V\rho] \le \frac{5}{2}\delta$ and $\mathbb{E}[(c_1 + c_2 - 5u)^2] + 2\mathbb{E}|a|^2 \le 10\delta$**: *In real coordinates $z_j = (x_j + iy_j)/\sqrt{2}$, $\Gamma(F_\theta, F_\theta) - 5u = (c_1 + c_2 - 5u) + 2\operatorname{Re}(e^{-2i\theta}a) = \sum_{k=1}^4 (5 - k)J_{2k}(F_\theta)$. Since $(5 - k)^2 \le 4(5 - k)$ for $1 \le k \le 4$,*
   $$\operatorname{Var}(\Gamma(F_\theta, F_\theta)) = \sum_{k=1}^4 (5 - k)^2\|J_{2k}(F_\theta)\|_2^2 \le 4 \cdot \frac{5}{3}\kappa_4(F_\theta) = 10\delta.$$
   *Averaging over $\theta \in [0, 2\pi)$ gives $\mathbb{E}[(c_1 + c_2 - 5u)^2] + 2\mathbb{E}|a|^2 = \int_0^{2\pi}\operatorname{Var}(\Gamma(F_\theta, F_\theta))\frac{d\theta}{2\pi} \le 10\delta$. Likewise, $L_{\mathbb{C}}(q^2) = -10q^2 + 4a$ on $q^2 = \sum_{r=0}^4 Q_{r+2,r}$ gives $a = \sum_{r=0}^3 (2 - \frac{r}{2})Q_{r+2,r}$, so $\mathbb{E}[V\rho] = \operatorname{Re}\langle q^2, a\rangle = \sum_{r=0}^3 (2 - \frac{r}{2})\|Q_{r+2,r}\|_2^2 \ge 0$; averaging $\mathbb{E}[(F_\theta^2 - u)(\Gamma(F_\theta, F_\theta) - 5u)] = \frac{5}{2}\delta$ over $\theta$ gives $\sum_{p=1}^4 (5 - p)\|V_{p,p}\|_2^2 + \mathbb{E}[V\rho] = \frac{5}{2}\delta$, hence $0 \le \mathbb{E}[V\rho] \le \frac{5}{2}\delta$.*
3. **Universal sixth-moment bound $\mathbb{E}|q|^6 \le \mathbb{E}|q_1|^6\,\|q\|_2^6 = 59{,}735{,}934\,u^3$**: *Write $V(z)^3 = \prod_{v=1}^3 q(z)\prod_{v=4}^6 \overline{q(z)}$, where vertices $1, 2, 3$ carry $T^{(v)} = T$ ($3$ holomorphic and $2$ antiholomorphic slots) and vertices $4, 5, 6$ carry $T^{(v)} = \bar T$ ($2$ holomorphic and $3$ antiholomorphic slots). In the inclusion–exclusion expansion (§4.1), each vertex sums $(-1)^{|M_v|}$ over partial internal matchings $M_v$ of size $0, 1, 2$. By Isserlis's theorem for standard circular Gaussians ($\mathbb{E}[\prod_{s=1}^N z_{p_s}\prod_{s=1}^N\bar z_{q_s}] = \sum_{\sigma \in S_N}\prod_{s=1}^N \delta_{p_s, q_{\sigma(s)}}$), taking $\sigma$ on the unmatched half-edges produces a bijection $\pi = \sigma \sqcup M_1 \sqcup \dots \sqcup M_6 \in S_{15}$ from all $15$ holomorphic half-edges to all $15$ antiholomorphic half-edges with $M_v \subseteq \mathrm{Loops}_v(\pi)$. Since $\sum_{M_v \subseteq \mathrm{Loops}_v(\pi)}(-1)^{|M_v|} = 0^{|\mathrm{Loops}_v(\pi)|}$, only the set $\Pi_{\mathrm{loopless}} \subset S_{15}$ of loopless bijections survives, giving $\mathbb{E}|q|^6 = \sum_{\pi \in \Pi_{\mathrm{loopless}}}\operatorname{val}_\pi(T)$ with positive weights $+1$. For each $\pi \in \Pi_{\mathrm{loopless}}$, group the $6$ vertices into $P_1 = \{1, 2\}$, $P_2 = \{3, 4\}$, and $P_3 = \{5, 6\}$. Contracting the $k_{12} \in \{0, \dots, 5\}$ edges of $\pi$ inside $P_1$ defines a tensor $M_{12}$ on the remaining $10 - 2k_{12}$ external slots with $\|M_{12}\|_{\mathrm{HS}} \le \|T^{(1)}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\|T^{(2)}\|_{\mathrm{HS}} = \|T\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2$ by Cauchy–Schwarz in $\mathbb{C}^{n^{k_{12}}}$; likewise $\|M_{34}\|_{\mathrm{HS}} \le \|T\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2$ and $\|M_{56}\|_{\mathrm{HS}} \le \|T\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2$. Since $\pi$ is loopless, the remaining edges connect distinct pairs: say $a, b, c \ge 0$ edges between $(P_1, P_2)$, $(P_2, P_3)$, and $(P_3, P_1)$ ($10 - 2k_{12} = a + c$, $10 - 2k_{34} = a + b$, $10 - 2k_{56} = b + c$). Viewing $M_{12} \in \mathbb{C}^{n^c \times n^a}$, $M_{34} \in \mathbb{C}^{n^a \times n^b}$, and $M_{56} \in \mathbb{C}^{n^b \times n^c}$ as matrices (with $\mathbb{C}^{n^0} = \mathbb{C}$ when $a, b,$ or $c$ vanishes), $\operatorname{val}_\pi(T) = \operatorname{tr}(M_{12}M_{34}M_{56})$, so Cauchy–Schwarz and Frobenius submultiplicativity give*
   $$|\operatorname{val}_\pi(T)| = |\operatorname{tr}(M_{12}M_{34}M_{56})| \le \|M_{12}\|_F\,\|M_{34}M_{56}\|_F \le \|M_{12}\|_F\,\|M_{34}\|_F\,\|M_{56}\|_F \le \|T\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^6.$$
   *At $n = 1$ and $q_1$ ($T_{111;11} = 1/\sqrt{12} > 0$), every $\pi \in \Pi_{\mathrm{loopless}}$ has $\operatorname{val}_\pi(T_{q_1}) = 12^{-3} = \|T_{q_1}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^6$, so $|\Pi_{\mathrm{loopless}}| \cdot 12^{-3} = \mathbb{E}|q_1|^6 = 59{,}735{,}934$. Summing over $\Pi_{\mathrm{loopless}}$ gives $\mathbb{E}|q|^6 \le |\Pi_{\mathrm{loopless}}|\,\|T\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^6 = 59{,}735{,}934\,(12\|T\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2)^3 = 59{,}735{,}934\,u^3$.* $\square$

### 4.4 Closed-form unconditional `< 1` bound across all of $H_{3,2}(\mathbb{C}^n)$

**Theorem 4.4** (**P + C, author only**). *For every $n \ge 1$ and every $q \in H_{3,2}(\mathbb{C}^n)$,*
$$\mathbb{E}|q| \le \frac{499{,}999{,}999}{500{,}000{,}000}\,\|q\|_2 < \|q\|_2$$
*(strict whenever $q \ne 0$).*

*Proof.* Normalise $u = \mathbb{E}|q|^2 = 1$ ($q = 0$ is trivial). Fix $B := 11{,}000$, $\varepsilon := \frac{1}{2(B + 1)^2} = \frac{1}{242{,}044{,}002}$, and $\eta := \frac{\varepsilon(B^2 - 1)^2}{B^6} > 0$, and consider on $x \ge 0$ the degree-$6$ polynomial difference
$$P(x) := \frac{1 + x^2}{2} - \varepsilon(x^2 - 1)^2 + \eta x^6 - x = \frac{(x - 1)^2}{2} - \varepsilon(x - 1)^2(x + 1)^2 + \eta x^6.$$
- For $0 \le x \le B$: $(x + 1)^2 \le (B + 1)^2$, so $\varepsilon(x - 1)^2(x + 1)^2 \le \frac{(x - 1)^2}{2}$, hence $P(x) \ge \eta x^6 > 0$ for $x \in (0, B]$ and $P(0) = \frac{1}{2} - \varepsilon > 0$.
- For $x > B = 11{,}000$: put $t = x^2 > B^2 > 3$. Since $\frac{d}{dt}\big(\frac{(t - 1)^2}{t^3}\big) = \frac{(t - 1)(3 - t)}{t^4} < 0$ for all $t > 3$, $\frac{(x^2 - 1)^2}{x^6} < \frac{(B^2 - 1)^2}{B^6} = \frac{\eta}{\varepsilon}$, so $\eta x^6 - \varepsilon(x^2 - 1)^2 > 0$ and $P(x) > 0$.
(In addition to this elementary proof, [`check_identities_exact.py`](../../code/sharp-radius-gemini-2762/check_identities_exact.py) Part B independently certifies $P(x) > 0$ on $[0, \infty)$ by both exact integer Sturm chains and exact integer Bernstein subdivision in $15$ leaves.) Evaluating $x < \frac{1 + x^2}{2} - \varepsilon(x^2 - 1)^2 + \eta x^6$ at $x = |q|$ ($V = x^2$, $\mathbb{E}[V] = 1$, $\mathbb{E}[(V - 1)^2] = \mathbb{E}[V^2] - 1 = 1 + \chi(q) \ge 1$ by Theorem 4.3(1), and $\mathbb{E}[V^3] \le M_6 := 59{,}735{,}934$ by Theorem 4.3(3)) yields
$$\mathbb{E}|q| < 1 - \varepsilon\big(1 + \chi(q)\big) + \eta M_6 \le 1 - \varepsilon + \eta M_6 = 1 - 2.09182875\dots\times 10^{-9} < \frac{499{,}999{,}999}{500{,}000{,}000} < 1. \qquad \square$$
