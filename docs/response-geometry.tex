\subsection{Weighted phase errors and transport of a response certificate}

The following statements are proved in
\texttt{Grothe/Upper/ResponseGeometry.lean}. They concern a real Hilbert space;
complex response spaces may be realified. No Gaussian integration or improved
Grothendieck endpoint is an input or a conclusion of these statements.

If $\|u\|=1$, then the exact support defect is
\[
 a-\langle au,v\rangle
 =\frac a2\bigl(\|u-v\|^2+1-\|v\|^2\bigr).
\]
For unit $u,v$ and $a,b\ge0$, the reverse triangle inequality gives
\[
 a\|u-v\|\le\|au-bv\|+|a-b|\le2\|au-bv\|.
\]
Consequently
\[
 a^2\|u-v\|^2\le4\|au-bv\|^2,
 \qquad
 a\bigl(a-\langle au,v\rangle\bigr)\le2\|au-bv\|^2.
\]
These division-free statements include zero moduli. At positive $a$, they
bound the ordinary support loss by $2\|au-bv\|^2/a$; no uniform positive
lower bound for the image modulus is being assumed.

Let $U$ be a linear isometric equivalence. A correlation operator is preserved
under a common change of coordinates precisely in the following form:
\[
 BU=UA\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \langle Ux,BUy\rangle=\langle x,Ay\rangle.
\]
For a family of linear blocks, require the corresponding explicit relation
$Q_iU=UP_i$. It implies $\|Q_iUx\|^2=\|P_ix\|^2$. Suppose also that
\[
 \sum_{i\in S}\|Q_i z\|^2\le\|z\|^2
 \quad\text{for every finite }S\text{ and every }z.
\]
If $e\ge0$ and $\|y-Ux\|\le e$, then
\[
 \sum_i\left|\|Q_i y\|^2-\|P_i x\|^2\right|
 \le2\|x\|e+e^2.                                    \tag{*}
\]
To prove this, apply
$|\|s+d\|^2-\|s\|^2|\le2\|s\|\|d\|+\|d\|^2$
in each block, and then Cauchy--Schwarz and Bessel to every finite sum.
The nonnegative infinite sum is bounded by the same constant. In particular,
a pure motion $y=Ux$ has exactly zero price, including its complete tail.
The eight Lean statements establish the identities, the finite comparison,
the passage to the entire spectrum, and this zero-price specialization.

The intertwining hypothesis cannot be dropped. For instance, interchanging
two coordinates preserves total chaos degree but can change the individual
coordinate bidegrees. An anisotropic preprocessor must be transported along
with the response before applying $(*)$.

\paragraph{An all-index phase-spectrum obstruction.}
The additional three statements in
\texttt{Grothe/Haagerup/}\allowbreak\texttt{PhaseGeometry.lean}
use the exact phase coefficients
\[
 c_k=\frac\pi4r_k,\qquad r_0=1,\qquad
 r_{k+1}=r_k\frac{(2k+1)^2}{4(k+1)(k+2)}.
\]
For $k\ge1$, put $b_k=4k(k+1)r_k$. Then $b_1=1$ and
$b_{k+1}=b_k+r_k$. Thus
\[
 r_k\ge\frac1{4k(k+1)},\qquad
 (2k+1)c_k\ge\frac\pi{16(k+1)}\quad(k\ge1).
\]
Comparison with the harmonic series proves that
$\sum_k(2k+1)c_k$ is not summable. These are all-index kernel proofs, without
new numerical native facts. They exclude a finite first-degree-moment price
for the phase spectrum; they do not exclude a modulus-weighted phase metric.
The Gaussian identification of a particular coordinate motion and the
verification of numerical response integrals are separate application tasks.
