\begin{abstract}
Grothendieck's inequality bounds how much a semidefinite (vector) relaxation of a bilinear optimisation problem can exceed the
true optimum. The best constant in the complex case, $\KG$, has been known since 1987 to lie roughly in $[1.338,1.405]$; its
first two decimals were settled only in September 2026. We improve both bounds, to an interval of width $0.0025346452$, and verify
the arguments in Lean. This note explains the problem, the two constructions, exactly what the computer checks, the new inequalities and
obstructions found along the way, and why the remaining gap is hard: the two sides are dual optimisation problems, and the obstruction on both is the nonlinear (cubic and
higher) structure of bounded functions of Gaussian vectors.
\end{abstract}

\section{The question}
Let $A=(a_{ij})$ be an $m\times n$ complex matrix. Compare
\[
 C(A)=\sup_{|\varepsilon_i|=|\delta_j|=1}\Bigl|\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}\,\overline{\varepsilon_i}\,\delta_j\Bigr|,
 \qquad
 V(A)=\sup_{\|x_i\|=\|y_j\|=1}\Bigl|\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}\,\langle x_i,y_j\rangle\Bigr|,
\]
the first over unimodular scalars, the second over unit vectors in any complex Hilbert space. Clearly $C(A)\le V(A)$.
Grothendieck proved in 1953 that $V(A)\le K\,C(A)$ for a constant $K$ independent of $m$, $n$ and the dimension. The smallest
such $K$ is the \emph{complex Grothendieck constant} $\KG$.

Further general results about these optima and comparison budgets (a transfer
of Grothendieck bounds to unit vectors of any complex inner product space,
attained optima in dimension $\min(m,n)$, a quadratic dual with overlapping
error budgets, a polarized Gram bound, and a rational support-defect correction
with a quartic Newton contraction) are archived in \path{abandonware/} and are
not built.

\paragraph{Why it matters.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Optimisation.} $V(A)$ is a semidefinite program; $C(A)$ is a hard combinatorial problem. $\KG$ is the best
 approximation ratio of the semidefinite relaxation, and rounding algorithms for it are exactly upper-bound proofs.
\item \emph{Functional analysis.} $K_G$ is the norm of the identity between the injective and projective tensor
 norms on $\ell_\infty\otimes\ell_\infty$, and controls absolutely summing operators (Grothendieck's original setting).
\item \emph{Quantum information.} The real constant $K_G^{\R}$ is the largest ratio between quantum and classical values of
 two-player XOR games (Tsirelson); the finite-order constants $K_G(d)$ govern the nonlocality of qubit states.
\end{itemize}

\section{Two dual constructions}
Both bounds use the same Gaussian picture. Let $Z=(z_1,\dots,z_n)$ be a standard complex Gaussian vector and decompose
$L^2(\C^n,\gamma)$ into the \emph{Wick chaoses} $H_{p,q}$ (polynomials of bidegree $(p,q)$ in $z,\bar z$, orthogonalised).
Under the rotation $z\mapsto e^{i\theta}z$ every relevant object has \emph{charge one}, so only the bidegrees
$(1,0),(2,1),(3,2),\dots$ (degrees $1,3,5,\dots$) appear.
The standard complex Gaussian measure, its rotation invariance and radial-moment reduction
form a shared Lean foundation. The upper rounding proof depends on this foundation
independently of the lower row certificates.

\subsection{Upper bounds by rounding}

The six original channels, their data and their checker are in
\path{Grothe/UpperSix/}, and their certificate is in \path{Grothe/UpperSix/Certificate/}. Cell-map energies for the three smoothed channels
and the two reflected channels feed interval coefficients. The phase channel
uses its analytic Hermite coefficients. The finite check takes absolute values
after mixing the signed coefficients. Each infinite tail is controlled by a
mean on its own circle. The seventh channel, in \path{Grothe/UpperSelector/}, adds a Gaussian angular selector.
A finite Fourier sum and the scalar phase expansion give its signed head and its full Wiener allowance.
The advertised rational bound follows from the reciprocal
gap in the transfer theorem. The production theorem applies to every finite
complex matrix and uses the scalar and vector optima in \path{Grothe/Statement.lean}.
The article in \path{haagerup-article/} is frozen; it has its own Lean package under
\path{haagerup-article/anc} and states a bound of its own.

An earlier proof of $\KG<1.404599395362$ by a single Gaussian rounding channel, with a certified

\subsection{Lower bounds: Hermite projection games}
A lower bound needs an operator $T$ whose \emph{vector} value is close to $1$ while its \emph{scalar} value is small. We use
\[
 T=P_{1,0}-e\,P_{2,1}+\nu\,(\mathrm{Id}-P_{1,0}-P_{2,1}),\qquad (e,\nu)=\bigl(\tfrac{191}{250},\tfrac{47}{2000}\bigr),
\]
the projection onto linear functions minus a multiple of the projection onto the cubic chaos, plus a small multiple of
the identity on all the other chaoses (an \emph{identity tail}).
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Vector value.} With $u_\ell=z_\ell/\|z\|$, $\sum_\ell\langle u_\ell,Tu_\ell\rangle\ge1-(1+e)/(n+1)\to1$.
\item \emph{Scalar value.} For all $|f|,|g|\le1$, $|\langle f,Tg\rangle|\le B=(1-\nu)\hat B+\nu$, with
 $\hat B=8829435313/12500000000$.
\end{itemize}
The finite-partition transfer gives $\KG\ge 1/B=25000000000000/17831387166289$.
The lower half of \lean{main\_bounds} rounds this exact value strictly down.
The two central rows use the same circular cubic, fourth excess and residual scale.
Dilating eleven plain Stein polynomials to four target scales gives fourteen new
bounds on their expectations. Within each of nineteen common shape intervals, Lean
checks nine mixture prices on the closed intersections of the two scale chords, globally and up to scale $21/100$.
The Schur complement has curvature $-g''/2\ge2019911/10^{10}$, giving a quadratic saving at
intermediate weights. A second pair combines two quadratic majorants with its shared Gaussian gate.
The ordinary rows 13 and 14 interpolate across their crossover on amplitude cell 34.
The 38 amplitude intervals then cover the full range of the witness.

The scalar cap is the hard part. For unimodular $f,g$ write $h=\tfrac12(f+g)$, $k=\tfrac12(f-g)$ and $t=\|P_{1,0}h\|$.
The tail acts pointwise, $\Re\langle f,g\rangle=1-2\|k\|^2$, so in the \emph{hat units} $\hat\epsilon=(e+\nu)/(1-\nu)=25/31$
and $\hat\nu=\nu/(1-\nu)=47/1953$ one has $\Re\langle f,Tg\rangle=(1-\nu)\hat V+\nu$ with
\[
 \hat V=t^2-\hat\epsilon\|P_{2,1}h\|^2-\|P_{1,0}k\|^2+\hat\epsilon\|P_{2,1}k\|^2-2\hat\nu\|k\|^2,
\]
and it suffices to show $\hat V\le\hat B$.
Only the term $\hat\epsilon\|P_{2,1}k\|^2$ is positive: we must show that a \emph{bounded} function cannot have too large a
cubic part without paying elsewhere. After a rotation $P_{1,0}h=t\,z_1$, the cubic part of $k$ splits into pieces that
involve $z_1$ (\emph{visible} sectors) and a \emph{remote} residual $q\in H_{2,1}$ in the other coordinates. The proof then
has four layers.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Row reduction.} For each of 14 rows, with nominal $t$ from $0.4$ to $0.8875$, the contribution of the
 visible sectors is the supremum of an explicit finite-dimensional Gaussian integral, a convex one-variable
 \emph{potential} $\Psi_r$, evaluated at the scale of the remote residual. The tail stays pointwise inside the integrand:
 $|A|+|B|$ becomes the pair potential $\Psi_{\hat\nu}(A,B)=\sup_{|f|=|g|=1}\Re(\bar fA)+\Re(\bar gB)+\hat\nu\Re(\bar fg)$.
 One gate variable is shared across rows, and each row gives
 $\hat V\le t^2-2\ell t+C-\hat\epsilon\|P_{2,1}h+(\beta/\hat\epsilon)v\|^2$, with the square retained.
\item \textbf{The law of the remote cubic.} What remains is a statement about the distribution of $U=|q|^2/\E|q|^2$ for an
 arbitrary cubic Wick polynomial in arbitrarily many variables. We use exact identities and inequalities valid for
 \emph{every} such cubic: the sharp fourth-moment cap $\E U^2\le44$; the sixth-moment bound
 $\E U^3\le9m-12+40.45313\,(m-2)^{3/2}$, $m=\E U^2$, which rests on thirteen tensor-contraction facts including the Wick
 expansion of the sixth cumulant; a first-moment bound; and families of \emph{Stein cuts}
 $\E\,G(U)\ge0$, obtained by Gaussian integration by parts and completing squares against spectral budgets, a covariance
 order and \emph{contraction-Gram budgets} (positive semidefinite Gram matrices of the coefficient tensor).
\item \textbf{A finite certificate.} Each row certifies its constant $C$: on cells covering the range $x\le31/4$ of
 $x^2=\E U^2-2$, moment rectangles and polynomial majorants at finitely many nodes, combined with Stein cuts (twelve
 rows) or spectral cuts (the rows $0.4$ and $0.6$) and checked in exact rational arithmetic, bound
 $\E\Psi_r(\sigma\sqrt U)-\kappa\sigma^2$ for every admissible law. For small $t$ a \emph{mass row} with $\lambda=1/2$
 needs no fibre reduction. With $q=P_{2,1}k$, $c=2\hat\nu+\lambda$ and $\varphi_c(r)=\max_{0\le y\le1}(2\hat\epsilon ry-cy^2)$,
 it combines $\|k\|^2\le1-t^2$, the bound $2\hat\epsilon\|q\|^2-c\|k\|^2\le\E\varphi_c(|q|)$ (from
 $\|q\|^2=\Re\langle k,q\rangle$ and $|k|\le1$) and a law bound
 $\E\varphi_c(|q|)-\hat\epsilon\|q\|^2\le S$ into
 $\hat V\le(1-\lambda)t^2+\lambda+S-\hat\epsilon\|P_{2,1}h\|^2$, $S=0.165606335315\ldots$; its tangents
 $(\ell,\beta,C)=(\lambda m,0,\lambda+S+\lambda m^2)$ are rows. At each witness scale $\tau$, the majorant bounds the sum of the separate spectral cut and
 the potential. Lean checks this on the quadratic and affine branches of $\varphi_c(\tau v/\delta)$ in $v=\sqrt U$, with $\delta=3/4$. Convexity gives the bounds
 between witness scales. The kernel checks these polynomial inequalities and the cover of $0\le x\le31/4$.
 The amplitude cover has 38 base intervals. In the active intervals the mixing weight is
 a clipped affine function of $t$. The discount is bounded below by the chord between its
 endpoint weights. Subtracting it leaves a quadratic in the segment parameter, whose
 maximum is checked at the endpoints and at an interior vertex when one is present.
 The other intervals use the tightened scalar rows or a mass tangent.
\item \textbf{The node caps.} The rows use each potential only through upper bounds at the nodes of its table,
 $\Psi_r(s_i)-10^{-11}\le c_i$ (\lean{TailNodeCaps}). Lean proves them. By convexity it suffices to treat the vertex
 nodes; $238$ of the $2{,}012$ table entries are chord nodes. At a vertex node a pointwise majorant of the pair potential
 with prices on a polar grid (a rank-one correction of the anchored-pair majorant, needed for the tail) bounds the
 integral by a quadratic form in the twelve real parameters; its coefficients are enclosed by verified interval tables,
 and an exact $LDL^{\mathsf T}$ factorisation certifies the cap for all parameters. Localised nodes need a finer
 argument: a cover of the parameter space by pieces cut out by one to three linear constraints, up to two symmetries of
 the potential, with one S-lemma certificate per piece.
\end{enumerate}
\lean{SparseStein.profile} combines the rows and the mass bound for $0\le t\le9/10$.
The cubic first-moment inequality bounds the residual scale $\tau$ by $93/100$.
Rows $0.835$ and $0.845$ share that scale, their fourth excess and the Gaussian gate.
The resulting cap passes through \lean{witnessCap\_of\_circular\_profile} to the matrix transfer
\lean{SparseStein.lower\_of\_nodeCaps}. The certified node caps \lean{tailNodeCaps\_cert}
then give $25000000000000/17831387166289\le\KG$ independently of Conjecture~K.

\subsection{The primal/dual viewpoint}
For the real constant, Heilman, Jones and Malavolta proved an exact minimax: over rounding schemes $\mu$ with kernel
coefficients $c_m(\mu)$ and Hermite projection games $Q=\sum_m q_m\Pi_m$,
\[
 \frac1{K_G}=\sup_\mu\Bigl[c_1(\mu)-\sum_{m\ge3}|c_m(\mu)|\Bigr]=\inf_Q\ \mathrm{OPT}(Q),
\]
The complex programme uses charge-one bidegrees. A global complex minimax identity is not established here;
the two endpoint proofs use separate one-sided transfer theorems. An upper bound comes from a realizable kernel,
and a lower bound from a game with a uniform scalar norm cap. Finite-pool duality alone does not certify either global claim.

For a convex mixture with weights $w_p$ and coefficient sequences $h_p(n)$,
each finite interval list may have its own length $m_p$.
The signed sum uses the coefficients with $n<m_p$ and pads each list with zeros.
The true mixture differs from this polynomial by at most
$\sum_p w_p\sum_{n\ge m_p}|h_p(n)|$ in $\ell^1$.
The resulting finite-matrix bound is \lean{convex\_transfer\_variable\_ival}.

Separate paper results in the Conjecture K monograph strengthen the common-response
energy ellipsoid and reduce a joint-sector estimate to a specified pointwise remainder
inequality. An exact rational checker verifies the conditional error budget, but
the full pointwise inequality remains open. For the peak research profile, new
paper and interval results cover every coefficient direction when $|w|\geq5/4$
or $\sqrt{|A|^2+|B|^2}\leq1/512$, together with a directional exterior.
They also give an unconditional retained joint estimate with error below
$19\cdot10^{-6}$ on the restricted family $r_B=-2r_A$, under the monograph's
stated energy caps, at the explicit remote trial with zero visible correction.
These results were independently re-derived and replayed in review \#444 and are
not premises of the Lean lower bound above.

A separate scalar Edgeworth argument gives the same-variance remote deficit
$D(q)\geq(7/50)\delta$ at the peak profile for $0<u\leq0.0463$
and $\chi=\delta/u^2\leq10^{-10}$, and $D(q)\geq(11/100)\delta$ for every
cubic shape at $u\leq10^{-5}$. Its explicit remainder and cutoffs have
exact arithmetic checks; the analytic proof is author-only, pending independent
review. On $0.03\leq u\leq0.0463$, $\chi\leq10^{-10}$ it also gives an exact
retained A-core bound through the reviewed tilted remainder, with arbitrary
sharing. With no A/B shells, the reviewed remote-core odd cost separately gives
$\Lambda(q+O)\leq S-(7/50)\delta-(1988/47275)\|O\|^2$ on the small-$\chi$
class, at every odd amplitude and sharing. These are separate subclasses;
odd sectors over A/B cores remain open. Neither result is a premise of the Lean endpoint.

The Lean modules that serve neither bound have been moved to \path{miscellaneous/research/}. They were part of the

\subsection{Common responses, common tails, and an architecture separator}
\textbf{[P; supporting algebra in Lean, archived]} A symmetry-closed family of actual cubic responses has an exact
five-mode first/cubic image and a fixed-size original-noise replica representation. It preserves the shared
quadratic drivers: a scalar Gaussian used to describe a cubic's law must retain third-chaos noise correlation
$\rho^3$, rather than acquire first-chaos correlation $\rho$.

\textbf{[Lean, archived]} All correlation samples of a response pair use the same signed Hermite coefficients. Prices
$a_i$ with $|\sum_i a_i\rho_i^{2k+1}|\le1$ for all $k\ge2$ bound the complete omitted absolute tail. A finite
front and one geometric remainder budget certify every index. The resulting separator is linear in response
columns, so it bounds whole convex pools, with explicit integration-error costs. Numerical pool screens
still require certified input integrals; a favorable relaxation value is not an achievable rounding score.

\textbf{[Lean, archived]} An exchange can retain the incumbent's signed coefficients with one error allowance.
If $\sum_k|c_k-\widehat c_k|\le\xi$, then every admissible spectral price has error at most $\xi$,
and the full reciprocal score satisfies $D(c)\ge D(\widehat c)-\xi$.
The same coefficient error must not be charged independently at every sampled correlation.
The current bounded phase-perturbation searches use this interface for discovery; their Gaussian
integrals still require certification before they can improve either endpoint.

\textbf{[P,C; polynomial certificate in Lean, archived]} The entire Gaussian-phase cubic-preprocessor family
$H_{\rm phys}(A\rho+B\rho^3)$, $|A|+|B|\le1$, including arbitrary complex phases and convex mixtures,
has full reciprocal score at most $0.71186$. Even the single-channel bound $1.404599395362$
has reciprocal $0.7119464829\ldots$, above this ceiling.
The separator is $\Re(c_0-\frac9{25}c_1-c_2-c_3)$; positive polynomial certificates handle all complex phases.
Thus optimizing this class further cannot improve the current upper bound. General non-Gaussian cubic
responses and nonlinear shared image fields are not excluded. The Gaussian-family coefficient extraction
is a written application of the Gaussian phase identity; it is not connected end to end in Lean.

\paragraph{Phase errors and exact symmetries [Lean, archived].}
Phase alignment has an exact modulus-weighted quadratic defect. An unweighted
degree-moment budget is unavailable: the phase coefficients satisfy
$r_k\ge1/(4k(k+1))$ for $k\ge1$, so $\sum_k(2k+1)c_k=\infty$.
Certificate comparison can instead align an anchor by a common isometry:
if the measured blocks are transported with it and the remaining error is at
most $e$, the complete energy-spectrum discrepancy is at most
$2\|x\|e+e^2$. A pure symmetry has zero price, including its entire tail.
The kernel is preserved only when its correlation operator is transported
too; rotating only the response can change the anisotropic kernel used here.
An additional polarized certificate prices the whole omitted energy change
by $AB$, where $A,B$ bound the approximation errors of the response difference
and sum by retained polynomials. These may be certified with positive
squared-error integrals, avoiding subtraction of nearly equal moments in a
residual Gram matrix. Two overlapping coordinate tests either keep the phase
argument away from zero or resolve it by an affine polar chart. Lean checks
the exact transformed Gaussian density and a Bernstein range enclosure.
A symmetry-invariant cutoff preserves admissibility and costs at most its
omitted mass in squared $L^2$ distance. This lets an upper construction use
compact chart domains with an explicit full-spectrum allowance.
An exact rational phase update has unit modulus for every real field, with
a cancellation-free increment formula. A finite radius jet retains the radius
and angle as independent polynomial variables until cancellations finish.
Lean checks carry-free coefficient indexing, exact translation, and the
finite remainder bound on complex patches. A norm-only homogeneous transport
certificate also has a proved ceiling: its certified score cannot exceed
$b/N^2$ when the anchor has norm $N>0$ and score at least $0\le b\le N^2$.
This limits that certificate formula, not the corrected response's true score.
The update assembly keeps the signed retained changes, passes the positive
error price through contractive smoothing and the bivariate kernel, and
connects the entire score to the transfer theorem. The reference error is
the inherited anchor error plus finite weighted retained-data errors,
charged once. These forty-seven Lean results change neither endpoint.
The compact Gaussian integrals are not certified.

\subsection{Inequalities for cubic Gaussian chaos}
Throughout, $q$ is a circular cubic Wick polynomial (bidegree $(2,1)$) in any number of complex Gaussian variables,
normalised by $\E|q|^2=1$, and $U=|q|^2$.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Sharp fourth moment [Lean; the real case archived].} Every real third-chaos variable satisfies $\E F^4\le93\,(\E F^2)^2$, with equality
 for $x^3-3x$. For the cubic kernel, write $N=\sum f^2$, $s=\|f\star f\|^2$ and
 $h=\|\operatorname{Sym}(f\star f)\|^2$. The Wick identity
 $\E F^4-3(\E F^2)^2=1296(s+3h/2)$, together with $\E F^2=6N$ and
 $h\le s\le N^2$, proves the bound directly, without a separate positivity premise
 on the Gaussian expectation. For a circular cubic, the complex contraction identities and Hilbert--Schmidt
 estimates instead give $\E U^2\le44$ at any dimension. Equality is attained by
 $Z(|Z|^2-2)/\sqrt2$ for a standard complex Gaussian $Z$; the equality calculation
 is analytic. The Lean theorem for the circular bound is \lean{Circular.fourth\_moment\_le\_variance}, in the build;
 the real bound is \lean{Chaos3.fourth\_le} in the archived module \lean{FourthMoment}.
 The circular bound implies $x\le\sqrt{42}$, while the certificate uses the larger range $x\le31/4$.
\item \textbf{Sixth moment [Lean].} $\E U^3\le9m-12+C_6(m-2)^{3/2}$ with $m=\E U^2$ and $C_6=40.45313$. The Wick monomial mixed
 with Gaussian noise attains $40.295$, so the constant is sharp to $0.4\%$. The inequality is \emph{false} without circularity:
 two real cubics $He_3(x_0)/\sqrt{12}$, $He_3(x_1)/\sqrt{12}$ give $m=47$ and $\E U^3=13506>12622.55$.
\item \textbf{The sixth cumulant as a sum over graphs [Lean].} The Wick identity for $\kappa_6$ of a cubic chaos is proved by
 expanding $\E F^2,\E F^4,\E F^6$ over closed cubic multigraphs, one Gaussian integration by parts per edge ($69+15+3$
 certified steps). The disconnected graphs cancel, and the six connected cubic multigraphs on six vertices give the tensor
 contractions.
\item \textbf{Contraction-Gram budgets [Lean].} A new family of universal inequalities between the chaos components of $U$
 and of $q^2$. With $X=\Gamma(q,\bar q)/3-1$, $c=\Gamma(q,q)/3$, $r=\Re(c\bar q^2)/U$, $P=\E[XU]$, $Q=\E[rU]$, for example
 \[
  \E X^2+\tfrac1{10}\E r^2\le\tfrac{34}{45}P-\tfrac1{18}Q .
 \]
 Each budget is certified by two positive semidefinite $2\times2$ Gram matrices of contractions of the coefficient tensor.
\item \textbf{First moment [P,C].} $\E|q|\le0.93$, by exact dual certificates, one for each range of $\E U^2$: pointwise
 majorants of $|q|$ built from the two radial forms of the antiholomorphic Stein identity, second moments of the
 contraction fields and the moment inequalities above. An independent exact verifier accepts every row
 (\path{research/conjecture-K/}, ledger row~3.7); none of this is in Lean. With Lean theorems as the only inputs the
 same method gives $0.9387$, and the Lean proof of the lower bound uses the constant $0.973612157$. A different
 argument, a two-plane small-ball estimate (a winding argument on $S^3$ and a horizontal Lipschitz bound), gives
 $0.97073$. The sharp value $\sqrt\pi/2=0.8862$, which the Gaussian attains, is open.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{A common budget for the same tensor.}
The two joint sixth-moment bounds share one tensor and scalar argument in
\path{JointSixthCore.lean}: Hilbert--Schmidt and cubic-trace bounds, a completed
square, and Young's inequality. The production cuts and parameterized refinements
specialize that argument through the same real-to-complex normalization identities.
The sixth moment and the Stein fluctuations cannot be optimized independently:
both depend on the same contraction invariants. This connection is formalized
in Lean, including the real/complex normalization and a finite checker that reserves
part of the contraction budget before accepting a Stein cut. Signed reserve
coefficients are retained. Ordinary and joint Gram budgets specialize one signed-reserve
soundness theorem. Likewise, ordinary spectral cuts specialize the general shifted-cut
theorem, and Stein and spectral nodes use the same polynomial-majorant integration lemma for an
arbitrary integrable cut with nonnegative expectation. The spectral row checker accepts such nodes, each restricted
to its proved kurtosis range, and the full checker and analytic bridge are in Lean; the spectral nodes of the rows of
the lower bound cite only the separate budgets.
Retaining the upper bound on the negative cumulant contraction yields a stronger
parameterized moment family. An exact transport identity then upgrades a node's
majorant when its budget permits a larger moment-cut weight, without a new
half-line search. These two refinements were also formalized in Lean
(archived modules \lean{JointSixth}, \lean{MomentLift}); the endpoint does not use them.
The moment costs also admit a convex formulation with two $2\times2$ PSD blocks
and linear constraints. An exact rational checker connects each accepted recipe
to the actual cubic moment inequality, replacing the outer parameter grid.
The checker and its exact scalar boundary were proved in Lean (archived modules
\lean{MomentCone}, \lean{MomentConeBoundary}). For moment weight $w>0$ and
$s_0$ cost $A>0$, the minimum $h_0$ cost is
\[
 B_{\min}(w,A)=
 \begin{cases}
 11781w/32+18225w^2/(4A),&11A\le360w,\\
 198w+22500w^2/(A+40w),&11A\ge360w.
 \end{cases}
\]
Both branches have explicit feasible recipes. A three-rational checker for
$(w,A,B)$ therefore replaces the auxiliary moment-cost variables, and the
archived module connects its acceptance to the actual cubic on the checked node range. The
contraction reserve and pointwise majorant still need their own exact checks.
This is optimality within the certificate family; it does not identify all
attainable response laws.


\subsection{The remote functional}
Let $L=z_1(a+b|z_1|^2)$ with the constants of the witness, and for a cubic $G$ put
$\Lambda(G)=\E|L+G|+\E|L-G|-\E|G|^2/\epsilon$ and $S=\sup_v\{2\E|L+\sqrt vU|-v/\epsilon\}$, $U$ a complex Gaussian. The
conjecture $\Lambda(G)\le S$ for all cubics (``Conjecture~K'') is the analytic heart of the lower bound.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A one-function reformulation [P].} Circular symmetry, $\E|W|=(\pi/2)\E|\Re W|$, and the identity $|a+b|+|a-b|=2\max(|a|,|b|)$ for real $a,b$ give exactly
 \[
  \Lambda(G)=2\E|L|+\pi\,\E\bigl(|\Re G|-|\Re L|\bigr)_+-\E|G|^2/\epsilon,
 \]
 so $\sup_G\Lambda-2\E|L|=\sup_t\bigl[\tfrac{\pi^2\epsilon}{4}\|P_{2,1}t\|^2-\pi\E(|t|\,|\Re L|)\bigr]$ over real functions
 $|t|\le1$. All of the gain sits near the hyperplane $\Re z_1=0$.
\item \textbf{The Gaussian is a strict local maximum [P,C].} At the Gaussian point every direction that involves $z_1$ has
 negative second variation, certified in interval arithmetic for seven sector coefficients. The Hessian's kernel is the
 entire $z_1$-independent cubic sector; a negative fourth-order term controls those genuine cubic directions.
\item \textbf{The independent sector [P,C].} For every cubic $q$ not involving $z_1$, $\Lambda(q)<S+3.6\cdot10^{-7}$. Moment
 inequalities alone stall about $10^{-5}$ above $S$, because $h(x)=2\E|L+\sqrt x|$ is a Bernstein function and the
 three-moment relaxation is maximised by a two-point law [P]. Going below that floor needs the Stein and spectral cuts.
\item \textbf{Two natural shortcuts are false [P,C].} $|q|^2$ does not dominate $\E|q|^2\cdot\mathrm{Exp}(1)$ in convex order,
 and there is no Gaussian Laplace domination: $Q=X(4(|X|^2-2)+3i(|Y|^2-1))/\sqrt{41}$ has $\E e^{-20|Q|^2}<0.0453<1/21$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Actual responses}
Instead of bounding $\Lambda$ for all cubics, one can use that the cubic comes from a bounded response. Write $F$ for the
cubic part of the response and $\mathcal N$ for the value of the witness on it.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Exact support [P].} The paired support $\frac12(\E|A+B|+\E|A-B|)$ is exactly the pointwise support function
 of the complex response set $\{(h,k)=(e^{i\alpha}\cos\theta,\,ie^{i\alpha}\sin\theta)\}$, because
 $(|A+B|+|A-B|)^2=4\max_\theta|A\cos\theta-iB\sin\theta|^2$.
\item \textbf{Concentrated cubics are harmless [P,C].} At $\epsilon=1891/2500$ on the central rows $0.84\le t\le0.851$,
 $\mathcal N<0.71325<1/1.402$ whenever the cubic part of the response concentrates enough energy on a structured subspace:
 \begin{itemize}
 \item $80\%$ on one diagonal Wick direction;
 \item $90\%$ on one mixed monomial $X^2\bar Y$, or on a whole slice $\{X^2\bar A\}$;
 \item $93.5\%$, $96.2\%$, $97.6\%$, $99.2\%$ on any cubic in $2,3,4,6$ remote coordinates;
 \item $95.5\%$ to $99.95\%$ on $H_{2,0}(A)H_{0,1}(B)$ with $\dim A\le24$, via a sharp support theorem whose extremiser has law
  $R_mG/\sqrt{m(m+1)}$, $R_m\sim\Gamma(m,1)$.
 \end{itemize}
 The remainder of the cubic may be arbitrary and fully coupled.
\item \textbf{The obstruction to covering [P].} $Q_N=X_1(\sum_{j\le N}|X_j|^2-N-1)/\sqrt{N+1}$ keeps an influential direction
 while its projection onto every fixed-dimensional core tends to zero. So ``small core or Gaussian'' is not an exhaustive
 dichotomy.
\item \textbf{How far from the benchmark structured cubics sit [F].} Remote-only payoffs relative to $S$:
 \begin{center}\small
 \begin{tabular}{@{}lccccccc@{}}\toprule
 sum of $N$ slices, $N=$ & 1 & 2 & 4 & 8 & 16 & 24 & $\infty$ (Gaussian)\\\midrule
 $S-\sup\Lambda$ ($10^{-3}$) & 1.68 & 1.42 & 1.08 & 0.73 & 0.43 & 0.32 & 0\\\bottomrule
 \end{tabular}
 \end{center}
 The tested concentrated cubics leave room of order $10^{-3}$; these diffuse sums approach the benchmark.
 This evidence does not classify all near-maximizers.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Response-penalty interface.}
The weighted response Gram argument gives a price for the squares discarded by
the raw lower row. Its Hilbert-space square completions and a consumer that
allocates each square exactly once are formalized in
\texttt{Lower/ResponsePenalty.lean};
\texttt{VOf\_frame\_with\_penalty} connects the consumer to the witness identity.
At the research peak profile the scalar h-only cut is greater than $10^{-4}$
throughout $t\geq0.8456$, $D\geq0.04$, where $D$ is the weighted six-sector energy.
The Gaussian Gram instantiation and a certificate that tracks $D$ are still
required: the rows and the envelope of the lower bound do not, and these lemmas
leave its endpoint unchanged.

\subsection{Limits of the witness method}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Ceilings [P,C] and [Lean, archived].} Actual finite Gaussian responses give the scalar cubic family a
 ratio ceiling $<1.4022581233$ when taking suprema over Gaussian dimensions, for every complex multiplier; its generic cancellation arithmetic
 is in Lean (archived module \lean{ScalarCubicFamilyCeiling}), its Gaussian construction and integral bounds are external. At $\epsilon=149/200$ a genuine response
 gives norm $>0.71329$, excluding $1.402$ for that witness. Explicit ``chirp'' responses prove that games of
 degree $\le5$ cannot exceed $1.4036705$, and of degree $\le7$ cannot exceed $1.4044895$ [P,C].
\item \textbf{Extending the pattern fails [P,C].} The full alternating game $\sum_{k\ge0}(-1)^kP_{k+1,k}$ has
 $\sup_n\|\cdot\|_{\infty\to1}=1$, attained by explicit unimodular responses (one in complex dimension $308{,}835$ is
 replayed). So better high-degree games must be re-optimised against new responses, not extrapolated.
\item \textbf{Certified finite exchange [P].} For a finite response pool truncated at level $D$ with value $v_D$, a
 global witness norm cap $v_D+\delta$ and a realizable mixture with omitted coefficient tail at most $\tau<v_D$
 give $1/(v_D+\delta)\le\KG\le1/(v_D-\tau)$ by the two one-sided transfers. The reciprocal interval has width $\delta+\tau$;
 both the uniform witness cap and the complete mixture tail must actually be certified.
\item \textbf{A global spherical form, and why it does not certify cheaply [P] and [Lean, archived].} Finite Gaussian Hermite games
 transfer, in every Gaussian dimension, to compact operators on the sphere with the same bounded responses and the same
 shared drivers; the numerical bounds are unchanged. The dual operator can then be truncated with a rigorous error: the
 cubic multiplier tail and the signed cap assembly are proved in Lean (\lean{SphericalTransfer.cubic\_tail\_32\_12},
 \lean{global\_cap\_from\_truncation}; archived module); the analytic bridge is a written proof
 (\path{miscellaneous/research/docs/SPHERICAL_TRANSFER.md}). But
 the operator has no square-integrable kernel: its multiplicity-weighted squared coefficients grow like $r^7$. So no
 Hilbert--Schmidt-type certificate can control it, and direct coefficient optimisation is impractical (about
 $8\cdot10^{20}$ coefficients in the tested truncation). A useful norm cap must bound the signed action on bounded
 responses, exploiting the unitary symmetry.
\end{itemize}

\section{Why the gap is hard}
\paragraph{The witness class caps the lower bound.} A game supported on degrees $\{1,3\}$ sees only the cubic constraint on
roundings. Even a perfect evaluation of the norm of $P_{1,0}-\epsilon P_{2,1}$ cannot certify more than $1.4022581233$
(the certified family ceiling above). An identity tail, as in the witness of the Lean lower bound, acts pointwise and
raises this ceiling only to about $1.40248$ in the model [F]. Proved obstructions are known for higher degrees: every
game of degree $\le5$ is capped at $1.4036705$, and every game of degree $\le7$ at $1.4044895$, both below Haagerup's
value. Reaching the upper
end of the interval therefore needs games of degree $\ge9$; in the real case the certified game uses all odd degrees up to
$21$.

\paragraph{Certifying higher degree is the real bottleneck.} Two different certificate architectures, gated rows and
support functions, both lose about $2\cdot10^{-2}$ on the best degree-5 game. The reason is structural: a bounded function
with a large cubic part is forced to have a degree-five part too. Through the visible coordinate, the cubic part sees the
average of the remote coefficient profile and the quintic part its variation, and boundedness makes the profile vary. A
certificate that treats the chaos degrees as independent cannot see this coupling.
The coupling is \emph{driver sharing}: in the dimension-limit model the best degree-5 game has norm $0.7124294$
($1/N=1.403648$) when its degree-5 mixed sectors $(|z_1|^2-1)W$ and $z_1^2\overline W$ are driven by the same remote cubic $W$ as
the cubic sector, and $0.724753$ when they are driven by independent copies; the optimum then switches the degree-5 part off.
So a higher-degree certificate must let one remote residual feed several degrees through the visible coordinate. The cubic
rows already do this for their degree-3 sectors; extending them to the degree-5 companions of the same residual is the
next construction [F]. For one degree-5 game with a capped cubic weight such rows are computed
(\path{research/higher-degree/}, ledger rows~4.11--4.18; exact, by one author): they give $1.402$ if a sector inequality
holds on each row, and that inequality is open in several parts.

\paragraph{Why the complex case is harder than the real one.} For real $\pm1$ responses, $h=\frac12(f+g)$ and
$k=\frac12(f-g)$ have disjoint supports, so the two sides of a game decouple at every degree. For complex unimodular
responses $|h|^2+|k|^2=1$ and $\Re(h\bar k)=0$ force $f=e^{i\alpha}e^{i\theta}$, $g=e^{i\alpha}e^{-i\theta}$: one shared
phase field $\alpha$ drives both sides, and they never separate. The same shared phase is why marginal laws do not
determine the relevant nonlinear correlations: $(H(X),X)$ and $(H(Y),X)$, with $H(X)=X(|X|^2-2)/\sqrt2$ and $X,Y$
independent, have identical marginals and covariances, but $\E[\bar r\,q|q|^2]$ equals $8\sqrt2$ in the first case and $0$
in the second.

\section{Open problems}
Work is tracked as GitHub issues of the repository: the open epics are \#22 (a better lower bound) and \#34 (research
plans). Three epics are closed: \#12 (unconditional lower bound: the first part of \lean{main\_bounds}), \#17 (inputs of the upper bound:
a bound below the earlier threshold $1.404599395362$, by the single-channel proof that has since been removed) and \#29 (build infrastructure).
\begin{enumerate}
\item Certify a game of degree $\ge5$ with a \emph{multi-grade fibre}: one bounded function of the visible coordinate and one
 remote direction per degree, so that the cross-degree coupling is kept. This is the route to a lower bound above $1.402$,
 then $1.403$. A design (\path{research/higher-degree/}, [N]) finds that, for the game of degree $5$ that it studies, a lossy
 all-sector inequality with error up to about $6\cdot10^{-4}$ per row suffices for $1.402$. That game needs a radius
 bound $0.916$ on the cubic chaos, and $0.93$ is what is proved; the game with a smaller cubic weight that has a
 conditional certificate leaves $3.7\cdot10^{-4}$ at its binding rows. The current gated rows lose several times that.
\item Run a complex primal/dual search, alternating linear programs over games of degree $\le D$ and pools of roundings, to
 locate $\KG$ to about $10^{-4}$ using higher-degree games. A first search
 (\path{research/higher-degree/}, [N]) gives model values $1.403648$, $1.404113$ and $1.40430$ for the best games of
 degree $5$, $7$ and $9$, so $1.4043\lesssim\KG\le1.404556392$. No rounding built from the binding responses
 beats Haagerup's $\gamma_H$ within that family. The independent seven-channel comparison is stated above.
\item Prove the sharp first-moment bound $\E|q|\le\frac{\sqrt\pi}{2}\|q\|_2$ for cubic Wick polynomials. It is natural, it
 appears to be open, and it is the largest single lever on the law side. The best proved constant is $0.93$; a constant
 $0.916$ would already remove the cap that the first moment puts on the cubic weight of a degree-5 game (item~2).
 Equivalently, $P_{2,1}$ is extremal for the complex
 little Grothendieck inequality; the real analogue for every single Hermite projection is asserted without proof in
 Heilman--Jones--Malavolta (arXiv:2610.10477, Example~3.1).
\end{enumerate}

\section*{How this was made}
The constructions and certificates were found by several AI research systems working on a shared manuscript, cross-checking
each other's packages by independent replay. The endpoint theorems have no hypotheses; what their proofs rest on is
listed in Section~\ref{sec:trust}, and their axioms are audited in CI. The discoveries labelled [P] and [C] also include paper proofs
and external exact or interval certificates; they are not all formalized in Lean. Selected written proofs and reproducible certificate records accompany the repository; the larger working manuscript is maintained separately.
