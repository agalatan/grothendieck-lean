\begin{abstract}
Grothendieck's inequality bounds how much a semidefinite (vector) relaxation of a bilinear optimisation problem can exceed the
true optimum. The best constant in the complex case, $\KG$, has been known since 1987 to lie roughly in $[1.338,1.405]$; its
first two decimals were settled only in September 2026. We improve both bounds, to an interval of width $0.0029$, and verify
the arguments in Lean. This note explains the problem, the two constructions, exactly what the computer checks, the new inequalities and
obstructions found along the way, and why the remaining gap is hard: the two sides are dual optimisation problems, and the obstruction on both is the nonlinear (cubic and
higher) structure of bounded functions of Gaussian vectors.
\end{abstract}

\section{The question}
Let $A=(a_{ij})$ be an $m\times n$ complex matrix. Compare
\[
 C(A)=\sup_{|\varepsilon_i|=|\delta_j|=1}\Bigl|\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}\,\overline{\varepsilon_i}\,\delta_j\Bigr|,
 \qquad
 V(A)=\sup_{\|x_i\|=\|y_j\|=1}\Bigl|\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}\,\langle x_i,y_j\rangle\Bigr|,
\]
the first over unimodular scalars, the second over unit vectors in any complex Hilbert space. Clearly $C(A)\le V(A)$.
Grothendieck proved in 1953 that $V(A)\le K\,C(A)$ for a constant $K$ independent of $m$, $n$ and the dimension. The smallest
such $K$ is the \emph{complex Grothendieck constant} $\KG$.

\paragraph{Why it matters.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Optimisation.} $V(A)$ is a semidefinite program; $C(A)$ is a hard combinatorial problem. $\KG$ is the best
 approximation ratio of the semidefinite relaxation, and rounding algorithms for it are exactly upper-bound proofs.
\item \emph{Functional analysis.} $K_G$ is the norm of the identity between the injective and projective tensor
 norms on $\ell_\infty\otimes\ell_\infty$, and controls absolutely summing operators (Grothendieck's original setting).
\item \emph{Quantum information.} The real constant $K_G^{\R}$ is the largest ratio between quantum and classical values of
 two-player XOR games (Tsirelson); the finite-order constants $K_G(d)$ govern the nonlocality of qubit states.
\end{itemize}

\section{Two dual constructions}
Both bounds use the same Gaussian picture. Let $Z=(z_1,\dots,z_n)$ be a standard complex Gaussian vector and decompose
$L^2(\C^n,\gamma)$ into the \emph{Wick chaoses} $H_{p,q}$ (polynomials of bidegree $(p,q)$ in $z,\bar z$, orthogonalised).
Under the rotation $z\mapsto e^{i\theta}z$ every relevant object has \emph{charge one}, so only the bidegrees
$(1,0),(2,1),(3,2),\dots$ (degrees $1,3,5,\dots$) appear.
The standard complex Gaussian measure, its rotation invariance and radial-moment reduction
form a shared Lean foundation. The upper rounding proof depends on this foundation
independently of the lower row certificates.

Given unit vectors, project a Gaussian onto them and round: $\varepsilon_i=f(\langle G,x_i\rangle)$ with $|f|\le1$. The
correlation of two rounded values depends only on $t=\langle x_i,y_j\rangle$,
\[
 \E\bigl[\overline{f(\cdot)}\,g(\cdot)\bigr]=H(t)=\sum_{k\ge0}c_k\,t|t|^{2k}.
\]
If $H(t)=c\,t+r(t)$ with $\|r\|_A=\sum_k|c_k-c\,\delta_{k0}|<c$, a tensor-power argument (Krivine's trick) gives
$\KG\le1/(c-\|r\|_A)$. The shared Gaussian phase identity is proved from the Hermite basis and Mehler formula. Our sharp rounding is a smoothed two-coordinate phase rounding,
$F_0=\ph\bigl(z+\sqrt{1+a^2}\,v(|z|^2)\,w\bigr)$ with a twelve-term Laguerre profile $v$, multiplied by the cubic Taylor
polynomial of a damped polynomial phase $\varphi$: $T=F_0P_3(\varphi)$, $P_3(x)=1+ix-x^2/2-ix^3/6$. The identity
$|P_3(x)|^2=1-x^4(3-x^2)/36$ and the proved bound $\varphi^2\le99/100$ (hence $|\varphi|<1$) give $|T|\le1$, and a disk-valued rounding is as good as a unimodular one.
The phase certificate checks 119,077 leaves in 512 cubes through six native range facts, with an analytic far field.
Its kernel is $H(t)=\sum E[n,m]\,p(t)^n(-\tau(t))^m$ with an odd polynomial $p$ of degree $41$ and
$\tau$ of degree $15$ with zero linear coefficient (any odd preprocessors of Wiener norm $\le1$ are realised by tensor
powers). Compared with $c\,t$, $c=7119483103461/10^{13}$, the residual is at most
$\delta=455212621809/(5\cdot10^{17})\approx9.10425\cdot10^{-7}$:
\[
 \KG\le\frac1{c-\delta}=\frac{500000000000000000}{355973699960428191}<1.404598148840 .
\]

\section{Discoveries along the way}\label{sec:discoveries}
Status labels: \textbf{[Lean]} machine-checked in this repository; \textbf{[P]} written proof; \textbf{[P,C]} proof with an
exact or interval-arithmetic certificate replayed independently; \textbf{[F]} floating-point evidence only.

Other upper-bound variants and their verification records are preserved outside the active build
in \path{miscellaneous/other-bounds/}. Shared Gaussian phase identities and Laplace

\subsection{Common responses, common tails, and an architecture separator}
\textbf{[P; supporting algebra in Lean]} A symmetry-closed family of actual cubic responses has an exact
five-mode first/cubic image and a fixed-size original-noise replica representation. It preserves the shared
quadratic drivers: a scalar Gaussian used to describe a cubic's law must retain third-chaos noise correlation
$\rho^3$, rather than acquire first-chaos correlation $\rho$.

\textbf{[Lean]} All correlation samples of a response pair use the same signed Hermite coefficients. Prices
$a_i$ with $|\sum_i a_i\rho_i^{2k+1}|\le1$ for all $k\ge2$ bound the complete omitted absolute tail. A finite
front and one geometric remainder budget certify every index. The resulting separator is linear in response
columns, so it bounds whole convex pools, with explicit integration-error costs. Numerical pool screens
still require certified input integrals; a favorable relaxation value is not an achievable rounding score.

\textbf{[Lean]} An exchange can retain the incumbent's signed coefficients with one error allowance.
If $\sum_k|c_k-\widehat c_k|\le\xi$, then every admissible spectral price has error at most $\xi$,
and the full reciprocal score satisfies $D(c)\ge D(\widehat c)-\xi$.
The same coefficient error must not be charged independently at every sampled correlation.
The current bounded phase-perturbation searches use this interface for discovery; their Gaussian
integrals still require certification before they can improve either endpoint.

\textbf{[P,C; polynomial certificate in Lean]} The entire Gaussian-phase cubic-preprocessor family
$H_{\rm phys}(A\rho+B\rho^3)$, $|A|+|B|\le1$, including arbitrary complex phases and convex mixtures,
has full reciprocal score at most $0.71186$. This is below the incumbent $0.7119473997\ldots$.
The separator is $\Re(c_0-\frac9{25}c_1-c_2-c_3)$; positive polynomial certificates handle all complex phases.
Thus optimizing this class further cannot improve the current upper bound. General non-Gaussian cubic
responses and nonlinear shared image fields are not excluded. The Gaussian-family coefficient extraction
is a written application of the Gaussian phase identity; it is not yet connected end to end in Lean.

\paragraph{Phase errors and exact symmetries [Lean].}
Phase alignment has an exact modulus-weighted quadratic defect. An unweighted
degree-moment budget is unavailable: the phase coefficients satisfy
$r_k\ge1/(4k(k+1))$ for $k\ge1$, so $\sum_k(2k+1)c_k=\infty$.
Certificate comparison can instead align an anchor by a common isometry:
if the measured blocks are transported with it and the remaining error is at
most $e$, the complete energy-spectrum discrepancy is at most
$2\|x\|e+e^2$. A pure symmetry has zero price, including its entire tail.
The kernel is preserved only when its correlation operator is transported
too; rotating only the response can change the anisotropic kernel used here.
An additional polarized certificate prices the whole omitted energy change
by $AB$, where $A,B$ bound the approximation errors of the response difference
and sum by retained polynomials. These may be certified with positive
squared-error integrals, avoiding subtraction of nearly equal moments in a
residual Gram matrix. Two overlapping coordinate tests either keep the phase
argument away from zero or resolve it by an affine polar chart. Lean checks
the exact transformed Gaussian density and a Bernstein range enclosure.
A symmetry-invariant cutoff preserves admissibility and costs at most its
omitted mass in squared $L^2$ distance. This lets an upper construction use
compact chart domains with an explicit full-spectrum allowance.
An exact rational phase update has unit modulus for every real field, with
a cancellation-free increment formula. A finite radius jet retains the radius
and angle as independent polynomial variables until cancellations finish.
Lean checks carry-free coefficient indexing, exact translation, and the
finite remainder bound on complex patches. A norm-only homogeneous transport
certificate also has a proved ceiling: its certified score cannot exceed
$b/N^2$ when the anchor has norm $N>0$ and score at least $0\le b\le N^2$.
This limits that certificate formula, not the corrected response's true score.
The update assembly keeps the signed retained changes, passes the positive
error price through contractive smoothing and the bivariate kernel, and
connects the entire score to the transfer theorem. The reference error is
the inherited anchor error plus finite weighted retained-data errors,
charged once. These forty-seven Lean results change neither endpoint.
The compact Gaussian integrals still require certification.

\subsection{Inequalities for cubic Gaussian chaos}
Throughout, $q$ is a circular cubic Wick polynomial (bidegree $(2,1)$) in any number of complex Gaussian variables,
normalised by $\E|q|^2=1$, and $U=|q|^2$.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Sharp fourth moment [Lean].} Every real third-chaos variable satisfies $\E F^4\le93\,(\E F^2)^2$, with equality
 for $x^3-3x$. For the cubic kernel, write $N=\sum f^2$, $s=\|f\star f\|^2$ and
 $h=\|\operatorname{Sym}(f\star f)\|^2$. The Wick identity
 $\E F^4-3(\E F^2)^2=1296(s+3h/2)$, together with $\E F^2=6N$ and
 $h\le s\le N^2$, proves the bound directly, without a separate positivity premise
 on the Gaussian expectation. For a circular cubic, the complex contraction identities and Hilbert--Schmidt
 estimates instead give $\E U^2\le44$ at any dimension. Equality is attained by
 $Z(|Z|^2-2)/\sqrt2$ for a standard complex Gaussian $Z$; the equality calculation
 is analytic. The Lean theorem is \lean{Circular.fourth\_moment\_le\_variance}.
 It implies $x\le\sqrt{42}$, while the existing certificate still uses $x\le31/4$.
\item \textbf{Sixth moment [Lean].} $\E U^3\le9m-12+C_6(m-2)^{3/2}$ with $m=\E U^2$ and $C_6=40.45313$. The Wick monomial mixed
 with Gaussian noise attains $40.295$, so the constant is sharp to $0.4\%$. The inequality is \emph{false} without circularity:
 two real cubics $He_3(x_0)/\sqrt{12}$, $He_3(x_1)/\sqrt{12}$ give $m=47$ and $\E U^3=13506>12622.55$.
\item \textbf{The sixth cumulant as a sum over graphs [Lean].} The Wick identity for $\kappa_6$ of a cubic chaos is proved by
 expanding $\E F^2,\E F^4,\E F^6$ over closed cubic multigraphs, one Gaussian integration by parts per edge ($69+15+3$
 certified steps). The disconnected graphs cancel, and the six connected cubic multigraphs on six vertices give the tensor
 contractions.
\item \textbf{Contraction-Gram budgets [Lean].} A new family of universal inequalities between the chaos components of $U$
 and of $q^2$. With $X=\Gamma(q,\bar q)/3-1$, $c=\Gamma(q,q)/3$, $r=\Re(c\bar q^2)/U$, $P=\E[XU]$, $Q=\E[rU]$, for example
 \[
  \E X^2+\tfrac1{10}\E r^2\le\tfrac{34}{45}P-\tfrac1{18}Q .
 \]
 Each budget is certified by two positive semidefinite $2\times2$ Gram matrices of contractions of the coefficient tensor.
\item \textbf{First moment [P,C].} $\E|q|\le0.97073$, from a two-plane small-ball estimate (a winding argument on $S^3$ and a
 horizontal Lipschitz bound). The sharp value $\sqrt\pi/2=0.8862$, which the Gaussian attains, is open.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{A common budget for the same tensor.}
The two joint sixth-moment bounds share one tensor and scalar argument in
\path{JointSixthCore.lean}: Hilbert--Schmidt and cubic-trace bounds, a completed
square, and Young's inequality. The production cuts and parameterized refinements
specialize that argument through the same real-to-complex normalization identities.
The sixth moment and the Stein fluctuations cannot be optimized independently:
both depend on the same contraction invariants. We now formalize this connection
in Lean, including the real/complex normalization and a finite checker that reserves
part of the contraction budget before accepting a Stein cut. Signed reserve
coefficients are retained. Ordinary and joint Gram budgets specialize one signed-reserve
soundness theorem. Likewise, ordinary spectral cuts specialize the general shifted-cut
theorem, and Stein and spectral nodes use the same polynomial-majorant integration lemma for an
arbitrary integrable cut with nonnegative expectation. The endpoint uses 41 such nodes, with each node restricted
to its proved kurtosis range; the full checker and analytic bridge are in Lean.
Retaining the upper bound on the negative cumulant contraction yields a stronger
parameterized moment family. An exact transport identity then upgrades a node's
majorant when its budget permits a larger moment-cut weight, without a new
half-line search. These two refinements are also formalized in Lean.
The moment costs also admit a convex formulation with two $2\times2$ PSD blocks
and linear constraints. An exact rational checker connects each accepted recipe
to the actual cubic moment inequality, replacing the outer parameter grid.
Its exact scalar boundary is also proved in Lean. For moment weight $w>0$ and
$s_0$ cost $A>0$, the minimum $h_0$ cost is
\[
 B_{\min}(w,A)=
 \begin{cases}
 11781w/32+18225w^2/(4A),&11A\le360w,\\
 198w+22500w^2/(A+40w),&11A\ge360w.
 \end{cases}
\]
Both branches have explicit feasible recipes. A three-rational checker for
$(w,A,B)$ therefore replaces the auxiliary moment-cost variables, and Lean
connects its acceptance to the actual cubic on the checked node range. The
contraction reserve and pointwise majorant still need their own exact checks.
This is optimality within the certificate family; it does not identify all
attainable response laws.


\subsection{The remote functional}
Let $L=z_1(a+b|z_1|^2)$ with the constants of the witness, and for a cubic $G$ put
$\Lambda(G)=\E|L+G|+\E|L-G|-\E|G|^2/\epsilon$ and $S=\sup_v\{2\E|L+\sqrt vU|-v/\epsilon\}$, $U$ a complex Gaussian. The
conjecture $\Lambda(G)\le S$ for all cubics (``Conjecture~K'') is the analytic heart of the lower bound.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A one-function reformulation [P].} Circular symmetry, $\E|W|=(\pi/2)\E|\Re W|$, and the identity $|a+b|+|a-b|=2\max(|a|,|b|)$ for real $a,b$ give exactly
 \[
  \Lambda(G)=2\E|L|+\pi\,\E\bigl(|\Re G|-|\Re L|\bigr)_+-\E|G|^2/\epsilon,
 \]
 so $\sup_G\Lambda-2\E|L|=\sup_t\bigl[\tfrac{\pi^2\epsilon}{4}\|P_{2,1}t\|^2-\pi\E(|t|\,|\Re L|)\bigr]$ over real functions
 $|t|\le1$. All of the gain sits near the hyperplane $\Re z_1=0$.
\item \textbf{The Gaussian is a strict local maximum [P,C].} At the Gaussian point every direction that involves $z_1$ has
 negative second variation, certified in interval arithmetic for seven sector coefficients. The Hessian's kernel is the
 entire $z_1$-independent cubic sector; a negative fourth-order term controls those genuine cubic directions.
\item \textbf{The independent sector [P,C].} For every cubic $q$ not involving $z_1$, $\Lambda(q)<S+3.6\cdot10^{-7}$. Moment
 inequalities alone stall about $10^{-5}$ above $S$, because $h(x)=2\E|L+\sqrt x|$ is a Bernstein function and the
 three-moment relaxation is maximised by a two-point law [P]. Going below that floor needs the Stein and spectral cuts.
\item \textbf{Two natural shortcuts are false [P,C].} $|q|^2$ does not dominate $\E|q|^2\cdot\mathrm{Exp}(1)$ in convex order,
 and there is no Gaussian Laplace domination: $Q=X(4(|X|^2-2)+3i(|Y|^2-1))/\sqrt{41}$ has $\E e^{-20|Q|^2}<0.0453<1/21$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Actual responses}

\subsection{Limits of the witness method}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Ceilings [P,C] and [Lean].} Actual finite Gaussian responses give the scalar cubic family a
 ratio ceiling $<1.4022581233$ when taking suprema over Gaussian dimensions, for every complex multiplier; its generic cancellation arithmetic
 is in Lean, its Gaussian construction and integral bounds are external. At $\epsilon=149/200$ a genuine response
 gives norm $>0.71329$, excluding $1.402$ for that witness. Explicit ``chirp'' responses prove that games of
 degree $\le5$ cannot exceed $1.4036705$, and of degree $\le7$ cannot exceed $1.4044895$ [P,C].
\item \textbf{Extending the pattern fails [P,C].} The full alternating game $\sum_{k\ge0}(-1)^kP_{k+1,k}$ has
 $\sup_n\|\cdot\|_{\infty\to1}=1$, attained by explicit unimodular responses (one in complex dimension $308{,}835$ is
 replayed). So better high-degree games must be re-optimised against new responses, not extrapolated.
\item \textbf{Certified finite exchange [P].} For a finite response pool truncated at level $D$ with value $v_D$, a
 global witness norm cap $v_D+\delta$ and a realizable mixture with omitted coefficient tail at most $\tau<v_D$
 give $1/(v_D+\delta)\le\KG\le1/(v_D-\tau)$ by the two one-sided transfers. The reciprocal interval has width $\delta+\tau$;
 both the uniform witness cap and the complete mixture tail must actually be certified.
\item \textbf{A global spherical form, and why it does not certify cheaply [P] and [Lean].} Finite Gaussian Hermite games
 transfer, in every Gaussian dimension, to compact operators on the sphere with the same bounded responses and the same
 shared drivers; the numerical bounds are unchanged. The dual operator can then be truncated with a rigorous error: the
 cubic multiplier tail and the signed cap assembly are proved in Lean (\lean{SphericalTransfer.cubic\_tail\_32\_12},
 \lean{global\_cap\_from\_truncation}); the analytic bridge is a written proof (\texttt{docs/SPHERICAL\_TRANSFER.md}). But
 the operator has no square-integrable kernel: its multiplicity-weighted squared coefficients grow like $r^7$. So no
 Hilbert--Schmidt-type certificate can control it, and direct coefficient optimisation is impractical (about
 $8\cdot10^{20}$ coefficients in the tested truncation). A useful norm cap must bound the signed action on bounded
 responses, exploiting the unitary symmetry.
\end{itemize}

\section{Why the gap is hard}
\paragraph{The witness class caps the lower bound.} A game supported on degrees $\{1,3\}$ sees only the cubic constraint on
roundings. Even a perfect evaluation of the norm of $P_{1,0}-\epsilon P_{2,1}$ cannot certify more than $1.4022581233$
(the certified family ceiling above). Proved obstructions are known for higher degrees: every game of degree $\le5$ is
capped at $1.4036705$, and every game of degree $\le7$ at $1.4044895$, both below Haagerup's value. Reaching the upper
end of the interval therefore needs games of degree $\ge9$; in the real case the certified game uses all odd degrees up to
$21$.

\paragraph{Certifying higher degree is the real bottleneck.} Two different certificate architectures, gated rows and
support functions, both lose about $2\cdot10^{-2}$ on the best degree-5 game. The reason is structural: a bounded function
with a large cubic part is forced to have a degree-five part too. Through the visible coordinate, the cubic part sees the
average of the remote coefficient profile and the quintic part its variation, and boundedness makes the profile vary. A
certificate that treats the chaos degrees as independent cannot see this coupling.
The coupling is \emph{driver sharing}: in the dimension-limit model the best degree-5 game has norm $0.712464$
($1/N=1.40358$) when its degree-5 mixed sectors $(|z_1|^2-1)W$ and $z_1^2\overline W$ are driven by the same remote cubic $W$ as
the cubic sector, and $0.724753$ when they are driven by independent copies; the optimum then switches the degree-5 part off.
So a higher-degree certificate must let one remote residual feed several degrees through the visible coordinate. The cubic
rows already do this for their degree-3 sectors; extending them to the degree-5 companions of the same residual is the
next construction [F].

\paragraph{Why the complex case is harder than the real one.} For real $\pm1$ responses, $h=\frac12(f+g)$ and
$k=\frac12(f-g)$ have disjoint supports, so the two sides of a game decouple at every degree. For complex unimodular
responses $|h|^2+|k|^2=1$ and $\Re(h\bar k)=0$ force $f=e^{i\alpha}e^{i\theta}$, $g=e^{i\alpha}e^{-i\theta}$: one shared
phase field $\alpha$ drives both sides, and they never separate. The same shared phase is why marginal laws do not
determine the relevant nonlinear correlations: $(H(X),X)$ and $(H(Y),X)$, with $H(X)=X(|X|^2-2)/\sqrt2$ and $X,Y$
independent, have identical marginals and covariances, but $\E[\bar r\,q|q|^2]$ equals $8\sqrt2$ in the first case and $0$
in the second.

\section{Open problems}
Planned work is tracked as GitHub issues of the repository: epics \#12 (unconditional lower bound), \#17 (upper inputs),
\#22 (better lower bound), \#29 (build infrastructure) and \#34 (research plans).
\begin{enumerate}
\item Discharge the sharp upper rounding's numerical comparison and energy-table error; its Hermite expansion, structural analytic bridge and Hardy tail bound are formalized.
\item Certify a game of degree $\ge5$ with a \emph{multi-grade fibre}: one bounded function of the visible coordinate and one
 remote direction per degree, so that the cross-degree coupling is kept. This is the route to a lower bound above $1.403$.
\item Run a complex primal/dual search, alternating linear programs over games of degree $\le D$ and pools of roundings, to
 locate $\KG$ to about $10^{-4}$ and to test whether Haagerup's value $1/\gamma^\dagger$ is the answer.
\item Prove the sharp first-moment bound $\E|q|\le\frac{\sqrt\pi}{2}\|q\|_2$ for cubic Wick polynomials. It is natural, it
 appears to be open, and it is the largest single lever on the law side.
\end{enumerate}

each other's packages by independent replay. The endpoint theorems have the explicit Lean hypothesis lists in
Section~\ref{sec:trust}, and their axioms are audited in CI. The discoveries labelled [P] and [C] also include paper proofs
and external exact or interval certificates; they are not all formalized in Lean. Selected written proofs and reproducible certificate records accompany the repository; the larger working manuscript is maintained separately.

\paragraph{Production source error propagation.}
A supplied integer leaf check bounds the production phase on that leaf;
no concrete leaf check is supplied here. The existing native cover already
bounds the phase globally. A shared Gram-coordinate identity supplies the
domain constraint for both routes. On $[-1,1]$ the cubic phase factor is
$5/2$-Lipschitz. For positive radial parameter, the rounding-slice error is
bounded by the quadratic Gaussian-base remainder plus the phase approximation
error. Its phase bound remains an explicit premise to preserve independence
from the native cover. Numerical source comparisons and integral certificates
still have to instantiate these analytic bounds.

The conditional Gaussian $L^2$ triangle inequality on one unsmoothed radial
slice charges the base remainder once, giving $5\rho^2$ plus $5/2$ times the
phase-model error and the model-to-comparison error. The actual phase bound
follows from the existing native cover; it remains a premise to keep these
analytic theorems independent of that native computation. Choosing the actual
phase as the bounded comparison phase makes its phase error zero. The remaining
Gaussian model-to-comparison integral and the comparison function's relation
to stored coefficients still need certificates. Radial integration, smoothing
and band projection are separate steps toward the endpoint.

Keeping the actual phase inside the Gaussian model removes the phase-error
term exactly. The remaining saved-model discrepancy is one normalized
conditional Gaussian integral of model energy, saved energy and their cross
term. Separate energy upper bounds $U,V$ and a real cross lower bound $L$
give the squared-discrepancy budget $U+V-2L$. A complex cross enclosure
with center $c$ and radius $r$ gives $U+V-2\operatorname{Re}(c)+2r$.
These numerical receipts, the saved-source binding and outer radial weights
remain separate obligations.

Numerical source bounds and the final
endpoint remain open; no archived estimate is accepted as a proof.


\paragraph{Finite charge decomposition.}
The rational moment balance test makes unequal signed source charges
orthogonal in the actual conditional Gram evaluator. Thus its full rational
source norm equals the sum with cross-charge entries removed. Polynomial
insertion is a rational linear map, so the actual source can also be split
using the finite charge set of the original multiplier before expanding its
eight source variables. The same insertion reverses physical charge, including
when its rational rate is zero. Bilinearity and charge orthogonality therefore
give the exact identity
\[
 N_g=\sum_{k\in\operatorname{chargeSet}(P_g)}
       B_g\bigl(I_{r_g}(P_{g,k}),I_{r_g}(P_{g,k})\bigr),
\]
where $N_g$ is the actual rational source norm expression, $P_{g,k}$ its
physical multiplier's charge component, $I_{r_g}$ the existing coherent
insertion, and $B_g$ the existing rational tensor pair kernel with both source
coefficient factors. No charge count or square formula is assumed here.
Laguerre coefficient binding, block diagonalization into squares, and numerical
source enclosures remain separate obligations.


\paragraph{Zero and degree-one covariance contractions.}
The actual rational gate evaluator has exact zero and degree-one contraction
formulas under its original checked-gate premise. Its zero contraction is
the pair mass $n^2/\delta$; the outer and inner degree-one squared contractions
are this mass times $\chi_+$ and $\chi_-$. The ground-versus-quadratic
contraction is the mass times $\lambda$, retaining the intrinsic inserted
constant $n$, the conditional variance $R+P$, and the Jacobian $1/(RQ)$.
This is not an equality between opposite-charge Hermitian Gram entries.
Higher-degree diagonalization, the complete 18-block/1140-square identity,
numerical source and energy enclosures, and the final endpoint remain open.
