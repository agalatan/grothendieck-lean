\subsection{Weighted phase errors and transport of a response certificate}

The following statements are proved in
\texttt{Grothe/Upper/ResponseGeometry.lean}. They concern a real Hilbert space;
complex response spaces may be realified. No Gaussian integration or improved
Grothendieck endpoint is an input or a conclusion of these statements.

If $\|u\|=1$, then the exact support defect is
\[
 a-\langle au,v\rangle
 =\frac a2\bigl(\|u-v\|^2+1-\|v\|^2\bigr).
\]
For unit $u,v$ and $a,b\ge0$, the reverse triangle inequality gives
\[
 a\|u-v\|\le\|au-bv\|+|a-b|\le2\|au-bv\|.
\]
Consequently
\[
 a^2\|u-v\|^2\le4\|au-bv\|^2,
 \qquad
 a\bigl(a-\langle au,v\rangle\bigr)\le2\|au-bv\|^2.
\]
These division-free statements include zero moduli. At positive $a$, they
bound the ordinary support loss by $2\|au-bv\|^2/a$; no uniform positive
lower bound for the image modulus is being assumed.

Let $U$ be a linear isometric equivalence. A correlation operator is preserved
under a common change of coordinates precisely in the following form:
\[
 BU=UA\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \langle Ux,BUy\rangle=\langle x,Ay\rangle.
\]
For a family of linear blocks, require the corresponding explicit relation
$Q_iU=UP_i$. It implies $\|Q_iUx\|^2=\|P_ix\|^2$. Suppose also that
\[
 \sum_{i\in S}\|Q_i z\|^2\le\|z\|^2
 \quad\text{for every finite }S\text{ and every }z.
\]
If $e\ge0$ and $\|y-Ux\|\le e$, then
\[
 \sum_i\left|\|Q_i y\|^2-\|P_i x\|^2\right|
 \le2\|x\|e+e^2.                                    \tag{*}
\]
To prove this, apply
$|\|s+d\|^2-\|s\|^2|\le2\|s\|\|d\|+\|d\|^2$
in each block, and then Cauchy--Schwarz and Bessel to every finite sum.
The nonnegative infinite sum is bounded by the same constant. In particular,
a pure motion $y=Ux$ has exactly zero price, including its complete tail.
The eight Lean statements establish the identities, the finite comparison,
the passage to the entire spectrum, and this zero-price specialization.

The intertwining hypothesis cannot be dropped. For instance, interchanging
two coordinates preserves total chaos degree but can change the individual
coordinate bidegrees. An anisotropic preprocessor must be transported along
with the response before applying $(*)$.

\paragraph{An all-index phase-spectrum obstruction.}
The additional three statements in
\texttt{Grothe/Haagerup/}\allowbreak\texttt{PhaseGeometry.lean}
use the exact phase coefficients
\[
 c_k=\frac\pi4r_k,\qquad r_0=1,\qquad
 r_{k+1}=r_k\frac{(2k+1)^2}{4(k+1)(k+2)}.
\]
For $k\ge1$, put $b_k=4k(k+1)r_k$. Then $b_1=1$ and
$b_{k+1}=b_k+r_k$. Thus
\[
 r_k\ge\frac1{4k(k+1)},\qquad
 (2k+1)c_k\ge\frac\pi{16(k+1)}\quad(k\ge1).
\]
Comparison with the harmonic series proves that
$\sum_k(2k+1)c_k$ is not summable. These are all-index kernel proofs, without
new numerical native facts. They exclude a finite first-degree-moment price
for the phase spectrum; they do not exclude a modulus-weighted phase metric.
The Gaussian identification of a particular coordinate motion and the
verification of numerical response integrals are separate application tasks.

\paragraph{A positive-integral certificate for an omitted response increment.}
Let $P_i$ be linear blocks satisfying the same finite Bessel inequality, and
let $x,y$ be the old and new responses. Polarization gives the exact identity
\[
 \|P_i y\|^2-\|P_i x\|^2
 =\langle P_i(y-x),P_i(y+x)\rangle.
\]
If $p,q$ have zero projection in every measured block and
\[
 \|(y-x)-p\|\le A,\qquad \|(y+x)-q\|\le B,\qquad A,B\ge0,
\]
then Cauchy--Schwarz and Bessel, first on every finite set, prove
\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \sum_i\big|\|P_i y\|^2-\|P_i x\|^2\big|\le AB.}
\]
The approximants need not be exact orthogonal projections. For Gaussian
responses, $A^2$ and $B^2$ may therefore be certified by directly integrating
the nonnegative squared errors of frozen finite Hermite polynomials. A
polynomial supported in the retained total grades has zero projection on
the omitted grades, also after a degree-preserving contraction. A downstream
coefficient map of column $\ell^1$ norm at most one preserves this allowance.

This is the same price as the common two-response Gram computation when
the exact residual Gram is
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\b&c\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ for $x,y-x$:
\[
 (2b+c)^2+4(ac-b^2)=c(4a+4b+c).
\]
The right side is the product of the squared residual norms of $y-x$ and
$y+x$. Its direct-integral formulation avoids subtracting almost equal
moments or repairing a numerical Gram matrix. Four further Lean statements
prove polarization, the finite bound, the complete-tail certificate, and the
scalar identity. A positive quadrature sum alone is still not an enclosure
of its Gaussian integral, and these results change neither endpoint.

\paragraph{Two conditioned charts for an actual shared Gaussian phase.}
Write the phase argument as $s+cw$, where $s\ge0$ and $c\in\mathbb R$.
On $|w|^2\le R$, the test $4Rc^2\le s^2$ implies
$|s+cw|\ge s/2$. Otherwise $c\ne0$, and for any $A\ge4R$ one has
$s^2<Ac^2$. Thus the ordinary and centered tests cover every point,
including zeros of $c$, without separately isolating these zeros.
In the centered chart put
\[
 w=\frac{re^{i\chi}-s}{c},\quad r\ge0.
\]
For $r>0$ the normalized phase is exactly $e^{i\chi}$, and
\[
 |w|^2=\frac{r^2+s^2-2rs\cos\chi}{c^2},\qquad
 \frac{e^{-|w|^2}}\pi\,d\Re w\,d\Im w
 =\frac r{\pi c^2}e^{-\frac{r^2+s^2-2rs\cos\chi}{c^2}}\,dr\,d\chi.
\]
The real scale may have either sign. These are identities for the original
shared coordinates; no Gaussian replacement or independence is introduced.
The regularizers become $r^2+\kappa$, removing the variable square-root
branch of the phase from the centered integrand.

The module \texttt{Grothe/Upper/PhaseChart.lean} proves these identities,
their integral form, the noncentrality bound $(s/c)^2\le A$, and a Bernstein
range enclosure. If $\beta_i\in[L,U]$ are the Bernstein coefficients on a
real interval, positivity and partition of unity imply the same range for
the polynomial. This can be much tighter than interval Horner evaluation.
The finite numerical choice of chart is distinct from certification of the
integrals inside each chart.

\paragraph{An admissible cutoff rather than a discontinuous integration boundary.}
For a measurable set $\Omega$ and measurable $|T|\le1$, let
$T^\Omega=1_\Omega T$. Lean proves integrability and
\[
 \|T-T^\Omega\|_2^2\le\mathbb P(\Omega^c).
\]
It also proves that a symmetry-invariant $\Omega$ preserves the disk bound
and the response's equivariance. Consequently a charge-one cutoff remains
an actual admissible rounding. If $p=\mathbb P(\Omega^c)$, the entire energy
spectrum changes by at most $2\sqrt p+p$ by the Bessel comparison above.
A downstream contraction of coefficient $\ell^1$ norm at most one preserves
this allowance. Thus the complete reciprocal score has the same error bound.

For $U=|z|^2,V=|w|^2$ and $R>0$, a finite chart union can contain
$\{U+V\le R\}$. On ordinary cells use $|w|\le\sqrt R$; on centered
cells use $0\le r\le s+|c|\sqrt R$, where $s=\sqrt U$. The triangle
inequality proves containment, so $p\le(R+1)e^{-R}$.
The cutoff response is zero outside that union, hence a polynomial residual
has only its polynomial tail there. Inside, each integral is over its full
chart domain, with no discontinuous $U+V\le R$ indicator.
This construction uses the freedom to choose an upper rounding. It is not
a restriction on the responses in a universal lower-bound problem.
The cutoff theorem and coordinate identities do not certify any compact
numerical integral or change either endpoint.

\paragraph{A rational phase update.}
For an arbitrary real phase field $\delta$, put
\[
 C(\delta)=\frac{1+i\delta/2}{1-i\delta/2},\qquad
 C(\delta)-1=\frac{i\delta}{1-i\delta/2}.
\]
Lean proves $|C(\delta)|=1$ and the displayed increment identity at every
real $\delta$. Thus $TC(\delta)$ remains disk-valued whenever $T$ is,
including unbounded fields. This update agrees with $e^{i\delta}$ through
quadratic order but has a rational continuation in the complicated field,
rather than its exponential. It changes the actual response; its full score
and polynomial approximation errors require fresh integration enclosures.

\paragraph{Exact circular profiles before interval evaluation.}
At fixed $s,c,r$, write $w=ax+b+iay$ with $a=r/c$, $b=-s/c$ and
$x=\cos\chi,y=\sin\chi$. The identity $x^2+y^2=1$ gives the recurrence
\[
 R_{n+1}=(ax+b)R_n-a(1-x^2)I_n,\qquad
 I_{n+1}=aR_n+(ax+b)I_n,
\]
starting at $R_0=1,I_0=0$. Thus $w^n=R_n(x)+iyI_n(x)$.
The multiplication step is proved in Lean for complex components as well,
which justifies the same identity under holomorphic continuation.
Since $|w|^2$ and $s\Re w$ are affine in $x$, a frozen real-coefficient
Hermite field reduces to two univariate profiles. Performing these exact
polynomial cancellations before interval evaluation avoids artificial
imaginary errors from independently enclosing reflected expressions.
This algebraic identity supplies no numerical integration bound on its own.

\paragraph{Retaining the radius until polynomial cancellation is complete.}
The same dependency issue occurs if a radial interval is inserted before
forming the profiles. Keep $r$ and $x=\cos\chi$ as independent polynomial
indeterminates and encode $r^i x^j$ at univariate index $Li+j$.
When every intermediate angular degree is below $L$, coefficient indices
are distinct and convolution adds the correct pair of exponents. The
module \texttt{Grothe/Upper/RadiusJet.lean} proves decoding, injectivity,
and compatibility with multiplication. A degree check is required before
every product; exceeding the stride must fail rather than carry silently.

At a point center $r_0$, exact binomial translation gives
\[
 P(r_0+h,x)=\sum_i h^i q_i(x),\qquad
 q_i(x)=\sum_{a\ge i,j}\binom ai a_{aj}r_0^{a-i}x^j.
\]
Lean proves the monomial translation and the finite complex-patch bound
\[
 \left|\sum_{i,j} b_{ij}(r-r_0)^i(x-x_0)^j\right|
 \le\sum_{i,j}|b_{ij}|R^i X^j
 \quad\text{if }|r-r_0|\le R,\ |x-x_0|\le X.
\]
Apply it to the omitted rows of the finite jet. This prices the whole
patch, with no empirical truncation or assumption about uncomputed
derivatives. It applies to complex centers and continuations. Rational
regularizer denominators still require separate exclusion of their poles.
These algebraic theorems do not prove the implementation or a Gaussian
integral enclosure. In particular, a useful enclosure on a toy patch does
not establish the response's compact integral or either endpoint.

\paragraph{An exact limitation of homogeneous norm transport.}
Suppose a score is quadratic under uniform dilation, and an anchor has
norm $N$ and score at least $0\le b\le N^2$. A corrected response of norm
at most one and distance at most $e$ from the dilation $aT$ necessarily
satisfies $aN\le1+e$. The generic full-energy comparison can then certify
the lower score
\[
 L=a^2b-2aNe-e^2.
\]
The module \texttt{Grothe/Upper/TransportLimit.lean} proves
\[
 N^2L\le b.
\]
Its scalar core is
$bt^2-M(2te+e^2)\le b$ for $0\le b\le M$, $t,e\ge0$ and $t\le1+e$.
Indeed $t^2\le1+2te+e^2$ under those conditions; multiply by $b$ and
use $b\le M$. Substitute $t=aN$ and $M=N^2$.
Thus a nearly unit-norm anchor leaves very little room for improvement
through this norm-only price, even if the correcting response is excellent.
The theorem bounds the certificate formula, not the true score. Retaining
the signed coefficient changes and pricing only the omitted tail avoids
this restriction. Quantitative Gaussian norm estimates remain separate.

\paragraph{A complete certificate for a smoothed signed update.}
Let $S:H\to K$ be a real linear contraction between Hilbert spaces, and
let $P_i:K\to K$ satisfy the finite Bessel inequalities. Given raw
responses $x,y$ and retained polynomial approximants $p,q$, suppose
\[
 \|(y-x)-p\|\le A,\qquad \|(y+x)-q\|\le B.
\]
For a finite retained set $I$, require $P_iSp=P_iSq=0$ outside $I$.
This is a support condition after smoothing; arbitrary coordinate
rotations or preservation of every individual block are not assumed.
Put $E_z(i)=\|P_iSz\|^2$ and
\[
 \nu_i=\begin{cases}E_y(i),&i\in I,\\ E_x(i),&i\notin I.\end{cases}
\]
Contractivity and the polarized approximation theorem give
$\sum_i|E_y(i)-\nu_i|\le AB$. Finite Bessel bounds also prove that all
energy tables here are summable.

For correlation polynomials $P,Q$ of Wiener norm at most one, denote the
existing bivariate coefficient map by $L$. Its l1 contraction gives
\[
 \|LE_y-L\nu\|_1\le AB,\qquad
 L\nu=L E_x+\sum_{i\in I}(E_y(i)-E_x(i))\,\operatorname{coeff}(\mathrm{term}_i).
\]
Thus the retained change is signed and finite before any absolute value
is taken. If $\widehat c$ approximates $L\nu$ to l1 error at most $\xi$,
the complete score satisfies
\[
 \boxed{\mathcal D(LE_y)\ge\mathcal D(\widehat c)-\xi-AB.}
\]
The reference allowance $\xi$ can be assembled from an inherited anchor
error and the finite retained-data price
$\sum_{i\in I}w_i|\Delta E_i-\widehat{\Delta E}_i|$, where
$w_i\ge\|\mathrm{term}_i\|_1$. Each error is charged once, including
its effect on the linear coefficient. No exact projection of either
response onto the retained polynomials is needed.

The module \texttt{Grothe/Upper/ResponseUpdate.lean} proves this chain.
If the actual radial kernel $LE_y$ is dominated and the displayed lower
score is at least $s>0$, its final theorem applies the existing transfer
inequality to obtain $K_G^{\mathbb C}\le1/s$. Domination is a
realizability condition on the actual bounded response, rather than an
assumed upper norm cap. For a concrete Gaussian application the smoothing,
block support, reference data, positive-error integrals and realizability
must still be supplied. This assembly theorem is not an accepted new
numerical certificate or endpoint.
