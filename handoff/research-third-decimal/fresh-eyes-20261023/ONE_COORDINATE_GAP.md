# A strict one-coordinate score gap from the Hankel certificate

23 October 2026. Paper proof with replayed Arb bounds. This is a restriction
theorem, not an improved bound for the full complex Grothendieck constant.

## Statement and precise class

Let `P_k` denote the ordinary one-complex-Gaussian-coordinate charge-one chaos
projection of bidegree `(k+1,k)`. Consider two arbitrary disk-valued charge-one
responses, scalar **linear Gaussian OU contractions** of those responses, and
probability mixtures of the resulting kernels. If `c_k` are the real mixed
coefficients, then their full reciprocal score satisfies

\[
D(c)=c_0-\sum_{k\ge1}|c_k|
\le\gamma^\dagger-\frac{3\pi}{102400}
<0.711866621.                                            \tag{1}
\]

The more accurate right side is `0.7118666209309906630...`. It is below the
current repository reciprocal upper-rounding score `0.711947153313208982` by
more than `0.0000805323822183`.

This theorem does **not** cover arbitrary nonlinear analytic preprocessing of
the final correlation variable. Such a composition mixes the final chaos
coefficients, so the rank-one perturbation below no longer commutes with it.
It also does not cover multiple original Gaussian coordinates, multigrade
latent channels, or general matrix preprocessing. A single scalar-valued
response depending on several latent graded Gaussians is outside this class.

Equivalently, the single-coordinate norm of the explicit admissible multiplier

\[
\lambda_k=(-1)^k\ (k\ne2),\qquad \lambda_2=199/200
\]

is at most the right side of (1). The claim about this multiplier also holds
for arbitrary bounded one-coordinate responses: the circle projection onto
charge one is an L-infinity contraction, and the multiplier is zero on all
other charge sectors. There is no extension of this norm bound to all Gaussian
dimensions. Using this restricted norm as an all-dimensional oracle would
suggest the unsupported lower endpoint `1.40475753546666...`.

## 2. One coefficient feature with a finite weighted norm

The normalized fifth-degree radial basis function is

\[
\psi_2(r)=\frac{r}{\sqrt3}L_2^1(r^2),\qquad
L_2^1(x)=x^2/2-3x+3.
\]

Define `u=mu_density*psi2/sqrt(V)` in `L2(dr)`. If `F=phase(z) f(r)` and
`G=phase(z) g(r)`, their `P_2` pairing is the rank-one form generated by `u`
after replacing `f,g` by `sqrt(V)f,sqrt(V)g`. Write

\[
u=\alpha h+b,\quad b\perp h,\quad
\zeta=\|u\|^2=\alpha^2+\|b\|^2.
\]

Direct Gaussian moments give

\[
\left|\int\psi_2\,d\mu\right|^2=\frac{3\pi}{256},\qquad
\alpha^2=\frac{3\pi}{256\gamma}.                       \tag{2}
\]

The positive integral representation of `C` gives

\[
C(r)\ge\frac{c}{(1+r)^3},\qquad
c=\sqrt{2/\pi}\,e^{-1/2}/3.
\]

Therefore

\[
\begin{split}
\zeta&=\frac23\int_0^\infty
 \frac{r^2e^{-3r^2/2}(L_2^1(r^2))^2}{C(r)}\,dr\\
&\le\frac{2}{3c}\int_0^\infty r^2(1+r)^3e^{-3r^2/2}
             (L_2^1(r^2))^2\,dr\\
&<12.469750581045< B:=25/2.                              \tag{3}
\end{split}
\]

The last integral is a finite signed sum of exact Gaussian moments; its
integrand is nonnegative. Arb enclosures handle the cancellation in the
expanded polynomial. No numerical quadrature or omitted tail is used in (3).

## 3. Rank-one perturbation with a certified operator norm

Put `t=1/400` and `Q_t=Q-2t u u*`. We prove

\[
\|Q_t\|\le1-t\alpha^2.                                 \tag{4}
\]

First, `Q>=-rho I`, so

\[
Q_t\ge-(\rho+2tB)I=-15I/16.
\]

For the upper inequality, decompose an arbitrary vector as `a h+y`, `y` in
`h`-perpendicular. Dropping the nonpositive `-2t|<b,y>|^2` term and bounding
the cross term by Cauchy gives the two-by-two upper matrix

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
1-2t\alpha^2&2t|\alpha|\|b\|\\
2t|\alpha|\|b\|&\rho
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

It is at most `(1-t alpha^2)I`: the first principal difference is
`t alpha^2`, and its determinant is nonnegative because

\[
1-\rho-t\alpha^2-4t\|b\|^2
\ge1-\rho-4t\zeta\ge1-\rho-4tB=0.
\]

Also `1-t alpha^2>=1-tB=31/32>15/16`, so this upper bound dominates the
absolute value of the negative lower bound. This proves (4), including
complex vectors and different responses.

## 4. Convert the operator norm to the full score

Let `Gamma` have multipliers `(-1)^k`. The multiplier
`T=Gamma-2t P_2` has the `Q_t` realization above. Since `|f|,|g|<=1`,

\[
|\langle F,TG\rangle|
\le(1-t\alpha^2)\|\sqrt V f\|\|\sqrt Vg\|
\le\gamma-t\frac{3\pi}{256}
=\gamma-\frac{3\pi}{102400}.
\]

All coefficients of `T` lie in `[-1,1]`, with first coefficient one. Thus
`D(c)<=sum lambda_k c_k<=|<F,TG>|`. Absolute summability follows from Cauchy
in the orthogonal coefficient sequence. This proves (1).

A scalar OU contraction preserves the response disk and charge, so it can be
absorbed into one of the responses before this argument. For a probability
mixture, apply the same linear multiplier to the combined signed coefficient
sequence and bound each branch by the same constant. Absolute values in `D`
are taken **after** summing the branch coefficients.

## What this changes in the research decision

The plain one-coordinate bilinear branch has a strict score gap, rather than
merely an unproved local ceiling at `gamma†`. It cannot match the incumbent
within the class stated here. Nonlinear preprocessing is a separate question;
this calculation explicitly avoids conflating it with OU contraction.

The new two-coordinate asymmetric quadratic-phase response engine is a
concrete next attack outside the excluded class. Its full signed tails and
mixture contribution must still be tested. No restricted-class theorem here
provides the missing all-dimensional lower-norm certificate.
